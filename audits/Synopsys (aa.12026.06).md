## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT DESIGNS THE DESIGNS**

Synopsys occupies a position in the semiconductor value chain that is, strictly speaking, above the chip itself: it makes the software tools — Electronic Design Automation (EDA) — through which chip designers create the chips that everyone else builds. Without Synopsys, the industry's ability to design chips at 3nm and 2nm nodes would be severely impaired. In July 2025, Synopsys completed its $35B acquisition of Ansys — engineering simulation software used across aerospace, automotive, defense, and industrial applications — creating what the HPB classifies as a **Silicon-to-Systems Design Monopoly**: a single company that now controls a 46% share of the combined EDA and simulation market, covering everything from chip design to the simulation of how chips perform in final physical products. The merger was described as creating "a unified silicon-to-systems design platform." The HPB reads this description with the same interest it applies to any organization that describes its ambition in terms of unification.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Synopsys's $87.5B market capitalization reflects the combined EDA and simulation market leadership achieved through the Ansys acquisition. The company's TAM has expanded to $31B following the merger. Revenue synergies are projected at $1B+ annually by year four. The HPB notes that a 46% share of a market whose outputs — chip designs and engineering simulations — are the precursor to virtually every advanced physical product humanity makes constitutes a design infrastructure concentration that has no historical precedent.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Synopsys enables the design of medical devices, clean energy systems, and communication infrastructure, alongside military and surveillance applications. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **3.4 out of 5.0**: the enabling function is genuine — without EDA tools, modern chip design is impossible, and many public benefits flow from that design capability. The qualification is the market concentration: when 46% of a critical design infrastructure market is controlled by one company, the public benefit of that company's technology is inextricably linked to a private company's pricing and availability decisions.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Design Tool as Governance Layer**

Synopsys's deepest HPB concern is its position as the software layer through which the entire semiconductor industry's design decisions are made. If Synopsys's tools are subject to export controls — as chip manufacturing equipment from KLA and ASML already are — then Synopsys becomes a chokepoint for chip design, not just chip inspection. In 2084's HPB framework, a company that controls the intellectual tools through which all advanced chips are designed occupies a **Design Sovereignty** position: the ability to restrict who can design what chips is equivalent to restricting who can build what technology.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK — DESIGN MONOPOLY DISCLOSURE REQUIRED**

**Confidence Score: 74%**

Synopsys is a legitimate software company with genuine public benefits. The HPB's concern is the scale of the Ansys acquisition: a 46% share of the combined EDA and simulation market creates a design infrastructure concentration that exceeds the HPB's Structural Control Threshold in this sector. The organization is issued a **Design Infrastructure Market Concentration Review**: Synopsys must demonstrate that its market position is not creating barriers to entry for chip designers, particularly in academic and small-company contexts where access to design tools determines who can participate in semiconductor innovation.

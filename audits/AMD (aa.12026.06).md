## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BEST COMPETITION NVIDIA HAS**

AMD is the Bureau's most straightforward MARGINAL case in the semiconductor sector: an entity whose primary social function is to prevent NVIDIA from having a complete monopoly on AI accelerator chips. AMD is not dominant. AMD is not structurally dangerous. AMD is, by its competitive existence and its recent AI accelerator momentum, one of the few entities currently doing meaningful work to constrain the most dangerous monopoly in the AI hardware market.

Lisa Su's AMD has produced the MI300X and MI350 Instinct GPU series that offer genuine competition to NVIDIA H100/H200 at the data center scale, with Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta all deploying AMD AI infrastructure alongside NVIDIA. The Bureau's primary interest in AMD is not as a risk entity — it is as a structural counterweight.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

AMD's market cap has grown from $290B (12024 dataset) to $885B — one of the largest market cap expansions in the current caseload — driven by AI accelerator growth and EPYC server processor market share gains. This growth validates AMD's importance in preventing NVIDIA monopoly but also means AMD itself has crossed into the range requiring Bureau monitoring.

**Concentration Hazard:** AMD's reliance on TSMC for advanced chip manufacturing (same dependency as NVIDIA) is a structural vulnerability. AMD cannot compete with NVIDIA if TSMC's capacity is constrained. The Bureau notes that AMD's competitive vitality is partially hostage to the TSMC monopoly it helps constrain.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

AMD has commitments to efficient AI computation (AMD's GPUs are positioned as energy-efficient AI accelerators). The AMD Foundation supports STEM education. Lisa Su has been a visible advocate for diversity in semiconductor engineering.

**HPB Optics Rating: MODERATE.** Genuine commitments, proportional to entity scale.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Countervailing Force**

AMD's most important influence is structural: it is the primary market mechanism for preventing NVIDIA's AI accelerator monopoly from being absolute. The Bureau's concern is not AMD's current behavior but the risk of its eventual failure or consolidation — scenarios in which the only meaningful AI chip competitor ceases to provide competitive constraint.

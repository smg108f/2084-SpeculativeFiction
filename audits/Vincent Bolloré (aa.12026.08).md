## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO OWNS EVERYTHING AND CHAIRS NOTHING**

Vincent Bolloré turned a nearly bankrupt Breton cigarette-paper mill into a five-layer holding empire spanning shipping, African infrastructure, batteries, and — most consequentially — a controlling stake in France's rolling-news, publishing, and radio landscape. In 2022 he formally resigned as Chairman and CEO of Bolloré SE, handing the title to his son Cyrille. The Bureau notes this changed nothing: Subject remains Chairman and CEO of Compagnie de l'Odet, the entity that sits atop the cascade and controls Bolloré SE regardless of who holds its letterhead. He is, on paper, retired. On every other paper in France — including several he now owns outright — he is not.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's fortune is structured, not held. At the top sits Financière de l'Odet, which controls Compagnie de l'Odet (93% family-owned, Subject as Chairman/CEO), which controls Bolloré SE, which controls the family's stakes cascading down through the former Vivendi empire — now split, as of December 2024, into four separately listed entities (Canal+ on the London Stock Exchange, Havas on Euronext Amsterdam, Louis Hachette Group on Euronext Growth Paris carrying a 66.53% stake in Lagardère, and a slimmed remaining Vivendi). This is the textbook French "cascade" structure: a relatively modest direct equity check at the top compounds, layer by layer, into effective control of tens of billions in downstream assets — a structural Concentration Hazard the Bureau flags as **SEVERE**, since it allows a single family to command an empire several multiples larger than its actual capital exposure. Compounding this: Bolloré SE sold Bolloré Africa Logistics to MSC in December 2022 (€5.7B enterprise value) and Bolloré Logistics to CMA CGM in February 2024 (€4.85B), and in May 2026 shareholders approved distributing €4.2B of the resulting cash pile as a one-time "exceptional dividend" — a payout that, because of the cascade, flows disproportionately into the family's pocket relative to their formal ownership stake. Subject has, in effect, cashed out of the physical infrastructure business at record scale while its legal aftermath (see Section 3) remains entirely unresolved.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject founded the **Fondation de la 2e Chance** in 1998 (recognized as a public-utility foundation since 2006), which offers modest grants (€5,000–8,000) and mentoring to adults aged 18–62 attempting to rebuild their lives after hardship — roughly 9,000 beneficiaries to date, run through a volunteer network of about 1,000. The Group also runs **EarthTalent by Bolloré**, a youth entrepreneurship and education program. Both are genuine, well-run, and modest — a rounding error against a fortune north of $9B and a single dividend payout north of $4B. Notably absent from the Saintly Pivot ledger: any voluntary gesture connected to the conduct addressed in Section 3. When Bolloré SE resolved its *corporate* liability for the Togo and Guinea affair in February 2021 via a Convention Judiciaire d'Intérêt Public (a French negotiated-settlement mechanism) and paid a €12 million fine, that fine amounted to roughly 0.1% of Subject's current net worth. **HPB View:** real philanthropy, wrong scale, and conspicuously uncorrelated with the Bureau's actual concerns.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Man Who Bought the Press and the Ports**

This is the audit's central finding, and it has two structurally connected halves. **First, the media:** starting with Vivendi's 2016 rebrand of i-Télé into CNews, Subject built what French and international press routinely call "the Fox News of France" — a channel that tripled its audience share in four years while becoming the launch pad for commentator Éric Zemmour, whose *Face à l'Info* program on CNews preceded his 2022 presidential bid. Subject's 2020–2023 pursuit of the Lagardère group (entering its capital via Vivendi in April 2020, completing a takeover after EU-mandated divestitures of Editis and Gala) delivered Hachette Livre — the world's third-largest publisher — plus Europe 1 radio, Paris Match, and the Journal du Dimanche, whose 2023 editorial handover to a former far-right magazine editor triggered the longest journalist strike in modern French press history. Le Monde and France 24 have both characterized Subject as pulling strings in the manner of Rupert Murdoch, reportedly working to broker an alliance between the far right and traditional conservatives ahead of France's 2027 presidential election. **Second, the ports:** the African logistics empire Subject built and then sold to MSC in 2022 was, for over a decade, the subject of a French judicial investigation into the 2010 Togolese and 2010–2011 Guinean presidential elections — investigators allege Subject's Havas subsidiary provided steeply discounted political-consulting and campaign services to Togo's Faure Gnassingbé and Guinea's Alpha Condé in exchange for favorable port and container-terminal concessions at Lomé and Conakry. Subject's 2021 attempt to resolve his *personal* exposure via a €375,000 no-trial plea deal was rejected by the Paris Criminal Court as too lenient for the seriousness of the allegations. After Subject's appeals for annulment were rejected at the Cour de Cassation, the case was referred to public trial: **Subject is scheduled to stand criminal trial in Paris from 7–17 December 2026** — four months from this audit — on charges of corrupting foreign public officials and complicity in breach of trust, with an additional embezzlement allegation tied to the Guinea container-terminal contract. He sold the asset; the criminal exposure stayed. **Influence Vector: CRITICAL (dual: unresolved foreign-bribery prosecution + editorial control of a national press and broadcast apparatus).**

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT RUNS ON DONATED BLOOD**

CSL Limited is an Australian biotechnology company that has built a $90B organization on a supply chain that begins with human beings voluntarily donating plasma. Through its 325+ plasma collection centers across the United States, Europe, and China, CSL pays donors, collects their plasma, fractionates it into high-value biologic therapies, and sells those therapies at prices that make the gap between what a donor receives and what a finished product costs one of the most stark value-chain asymmetries the HPB has documented. CSL's core business, CSL Behring, constitutes 70–80% of revenue and represents a **Biological Value Extraction Architecture**: human biological material enters at the bottom of the supply chain (priced as a service to donors) and exits at the top as proprietary medicines (priced as specialized biotechnology). The HPB does not condemn this model — it is the architecture of all plasma medicine — but requires that CSL, as the largest operator in this space, be held to governance standards commensurate with its access to human biological material at scale.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

CSL's A$55B market capitalization (approximately $90.4B USD) reflects a company in a transitional period: revenue forecast unchanged at US$15.4B, but an increasing expected loss per share, and interim CEO Gordon Naylor has launched a transformation plan targeting US$500M–$550M in annual cost savings by FY28. The plasma collection network — 325+ centers mostly in the U.S., where donors are paid — creates a structural dependency on a donor population that includes a significant proportion of low-income individuals for whom plasma donation income is economically significant. The HPB classifies this as a **Biological Labor Dependency**: the supply chain depends on the financial vulnerability of plasma donors.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

CSL treats rare diseases — hemophilia, primary immunodeficiency, hereditary angioedema — that affect populations with few alternatives. Its influenza vaccine business (CSL Seqirus) contributes to global pandemic preparedness. Its iron deficiency and nephrology work (CSL Vifor) addresses conditions affecting hundreds of millions globally. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **4.3 out of 5.0** — among the highest in this cohort, and genuinely earned. The qualification is the plasma donor compensation model and the pricing of finished products relative to what donors receive.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Plasma Oligopoly**

CSL participates in a global plasma fractionation oligopoly — a small number of companies controlling the collection, processing, and distribution of human plasma-derived medicines. This oligopoly creates **Biological Pricing Power**: when only a handful of companies can manufacture immunoglobulin or albumin, the price of these life-sustaining therapies is set by oligopolistic dynamics rather than competitive markets. The HPB classifies this as a **Rare Disease Premium Risk**: populations with rare conditions and no alternatives are the most vulnerable to pharmaceutical pricing power.

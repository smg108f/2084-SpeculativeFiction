## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMMODITIES KING**

Ivan Glasenberg served as CEO of Glencore — the Swiss-based global commodities trading and mining giant — from 2002 to 2021. Under his tenure, Glencore became one of the world's largest commodity traders and miners, spanning copper, cobalt, coal, zinc, nickel, oil, and agricultural products. He stepped down in 2021 but retains a significant shareholder stake.

**The bribery convictions:** In May 2022, Glencore pleaded guilty in the United States, United Kingdom, and Brazil to bribery and market manipulation charges. Total fines exceeded $1.5B. The bribery involved payments to officials in seven African countries (Nigeria, Cameroon, Ivory Coast, Equatorial Guinea, South Sudan) and Brazil and Venezuela, over a period spanning Glasenberg's entire CEO tenure. Glasenberg was not personally charged, but he was CEO during the entirety of the bribery period and the HPB notes that corporate bribery at this scale, sustained over this duration, represents a leadership accountability issue regardless of personal charging decisions.

**DRC cobalt:** Glencore's Kamoto Copper Company operations in the Democratic Republic of Congo are in the cobalt-rich Katanga region. Artisanal miners — including children — work in the areas surrounding Glencore's concessions. The relationship between Glencore's industrial operations and artisanal child mining has been documented by human rights organizations including Amnesty International.

**Coal:** Glencore is one of the world's largest coal traders. Glasenberg resisted pressure to exit coal during his tenure, arguing that managed wind-down was preferable to divestiture (leaving coal assets in less responsible hands). This argument has merit but the coal operations remain a concern.

No significant documented philanthropy.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE STEEL KING OF THREE CONTINENTS**

Lakshmi Mittal built one of the world's great industrial empires through a career of relentless acquisitions of distressed steel companies across Eastern Europe, the Americas, and eventually Western Europe. His defining move was the 2006 hostile takeover of Arcelor, the Franco-Luxembourg steelmaker, after a bitter public battle in which European political establishments attempted to block him — partly for industrial reasons, partly for reasons the HPB reads as reflecting the discomfort of European industry with an Indian-born billionaire acquiring their national champions. Mittal prevailed. ArcelorMittal is now the world's second-largest steel company (after China Baowu). Steel is foundational industrial material. The company has operations in dozens of countries and employs over 150,000 people. Mittal lives in Kensington Palace Gardens, London, in a famously opulent house. His daughter's wedding in 2004 cost an estimated $60M. He has given to various causes but does not have a significant documented philanthropy.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

ArcelorMittal's revenues are in the tens of billions annually. Steel is essential infrastructure for construction, transportation, and energy. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH.** The HPB notes that Mittal's consolidation strategy — buying distressed steelmakers cheaply and rationalizing them — often involved significant workforce reductions.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Limited documented philanthropy at scale. Some giving to Indian education and health. The $60M daughter's wedding is noted with the observation that it equals approximately half the budget of a mid-sized urban hospital. HPB rates: **MINIMAL.**

### **3. The Influence Vector: Industrial Labour**

Mittal's acquisition model has involved downsizing workforces at acquired plants — most famously in France, Belgium, and the US, where plant closures and layoffs following acquisition became politically controversial. The HPB classifies this as **Industrial Extraction from Labour** — legal but relevant to the accountability audit.

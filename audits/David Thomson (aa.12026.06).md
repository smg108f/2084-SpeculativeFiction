## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE THIRD-GENERATION ARCHIVIST**

David Thomson is the 3rd Baron Thomson of Fleet — a literal aristocratic title in a 21st-century democracy, which the HPB notes as a useful indicator of the subject's self-understanding. His grandfather Roy built a newspaper empire. His father Ken consolidated it into Thomson Reuters. David inherited it, holds an underground art gallery in his Toronto ravine compound, and controls approximately 70% of one of the world's dominant financial data and legal information platforms. The HPB's concern is not that he is villainous. The concern is that **the world's legal and financial professionals largely access information through a company he controls**, and he has had this power since birth.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The primary asset is Woodbridge, the family holding company that owns ~70% of Thomson Reuters ($7.5B revenue in FY2025). Thomson Reuters is not a media company in the conventional sense — it is the infrastructure through which lawyers, accountants, journalists, and financial professionals access legal databases, news wires, and financial analytics. Controlling 70% of this is, by HPB standards, a **Cognitive Infrastructure Lock**. Secondary holdings include Osmington real estate (including a stake in the Montreal Canadiens and Winnipeg Jets), and a private art collection that includes the world's largest collection of Constable paintings plus works by Rembrandt and Turner.

**Concentration Hazard:** The art collection is essentially illiquid divine storage — wealth that has been transmuted into cultural objects and placed in a private underground gallery where no one can see it. The HPB classifies this as **Aesthetic Hoarding**.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Thomson has donated art to the Art Gallery of Ontario and the London Archive of Modern Conflict. These are genuine contributions to public culture. However, for a subject worth $54B+, the documented giving is modest and concentrated in the high-prestige, low-population-impact category of fine art. HPB assessment: **Connoisseur's Philanthropy** — generous to institutions that also enhance the value of his remaining collection and social standing, less directed toward the structural public good.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Information Substrate**

Thomson Reuters provides the legal and financial data infrastructure used by professionals in over 100 countries. Its Westlaw legal research platform is the dominant tool for legal professionals in the U.S. and Canada. Its financial terminals compete directly with Bloomberg. The subject's family controls 70% of this apparatus — not through management (David is a co-chairman, not an operator) but through ownership. In 2084 terms, this constitutes **Proxy Cognitive Infrastructure Control**: the subject doesn't shape what lawyers think, but he controls the database they think through.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE GENTLEMAN ARCHIVIST**

**Confidence Score: 78%**

David Thomson presents a lower urgency profile than his net worth alone would suggest. He is not an ideologue, not a power-seeker, and not a disruptor. He is a deeply private aesthete sitting atop a three-generation information empire, spending his time with Constable paintings while his holding company controls the database through which the world's lawyers practice. The HPB recommends **Precautionary Classification** with particular attention to the Woodbridge governance structure and the underground art collection, which represents a non-trivial quantity of human cultural heritage being stored in one man's basement.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject is the most civilized demi-god on the current ledger. He is also the quietest example of a class that the HPB must monitor carefully: the inherited information oligarch. He did not build the empire; he maintains it with impeccable taste. The Bureau notes that impeccable taste is not a legal defense against the Demi-God Act, and that a world-class Constable collection in a private underground gallery is exactly the kind of cultural sequestration that the Act was designed to prevent."

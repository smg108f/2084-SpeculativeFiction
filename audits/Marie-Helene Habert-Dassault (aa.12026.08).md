## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HEIRESS OF THE RAFALE AND THE FIGARO**

The Bureau's initial intake flagged this file as a probable identity error — "Habert-Dassault" reads, at first glance, like a CSV conflation of two unrelated surnames. It is not. Subject is a real, extensively documented individual: daughter of the late Serge Dassault, granddaughter of Marcel Dassault, and Director of Communication and Patronage of Groupe Industriel Marcel Dassault (GIMD), the family holding company that controls a near-67% stake in Dassault Aviation (maker of the Rafale fighter jet), a 40% stake in Dassault Systèmes, a 27% stake in defense-electronics giant Thales, and — through Société du Figaro — France's oldest and most agenda-setting national newspaper. Unlike most files that cross this desk, Subject did not build this empire. She administers, communicates for, and inherits a fraction of one that was already built, which makes her less a founder-architect and more a hereditary custodian of state-adjacent capital.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's wealth is not a personal fortune in the conventional sense — it is a proportional claim on a family conglomerate she did not build and cannot unilaterally liquidate. GIMD holds 66.28% of Dassault Aviation's share capital but 79.70% of its voting rights, a structure engineered to preserve family control regardless of dilution; the same entity holds 40% of Dassault Systèmes and 27% of Thales. Subject's own stake is her proportional share of this structure, split among surviving Dassault heirs (her siblings include Laurent and Thierry Dassault; a fourth sibling, Olivier Dassault, died in a 2021 helicopter crash). The Bureau flags this as a **Concentration Hazard** of an unusual type: the underlying assets are two of the most strategically sensitive categories a private citizen can hold — sovereign-grade weapons manufacturing and a national newspaper of record — bundled into a single non-publicly-traded holding vehicle that resists both market pricing and, by design, outside acquisition. Subject additionally holds directorships at bioMérieux (diagnostics) and Artcurial (auction house), and chairs the Fondation Serge Dassault, none of which meaningfully diversify the underlying risk.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject's philanthropic record is genuine and long-running, not a last-minute audit hedge. As Director of Communication and Patronage for GIMD, she oversaw a €1 million donation — via the "Dassault Histoire et Patrimoine" patronage fund, in partnership with France's Ministry of Culture and the Heritage Foundation — to preserve the martyred village of Oradour-sur-Glane, a 1944 Nazi massacre site with personal resonance: Subject's grandfather Marcel Dassault was deported to Buchenwald that same year. She also chairs the Fondation Serge Dassault, which continues her father's support for adults with intellectual disabilities, and holds affiliated roles at Fondation Fondamental (psychiatric research) and Fondation Gustave Roussy (cancer research). **Sincerity Rating: Moderate-to-High.** The causes are real and sustained across decades. The complication the Bureau cannot ignore is structural: Subject's philanthropy is formally titled "Communication and Patronage," meaning the giving function and the reputation-management function share an org chart — and that reputation has debts to service (see Influence Vector, below).

### **3. Influence Vector: The Rafale-Figaro Axis**

Most files reaching this desk present a single lever of influence — capital, ideology, or infrastructure. Subject's family structure presents two simultaneously, under one roof. Through Dassault Aviation, the family group is the prime contractor for the Rafale, a fighter jet sold government-to-government to India, Egypt, Qatar, Greece, Croatia, Indonesia, and the UAE — a literal instrument of French state power projection and foreign policy. Through Société du Figaro, the same family group owns France's oldest daily newspaper, a historically influential agenda-setter in national political discourse. The Bureau's structural question — scaffold or substitution — resolves uncomfortably here: this is not a case of a private citizen building capacity the state failed to fund, nor simply a citizen with an outsized megaphone. It is a single family holding company sitting simultaneously on the hardware of state defense and a leading instrument of state narrative. Compounding this, Subject's late father Serge Dassault was implicated in the "Système Dassault" vote-buying affair in Corbeil-Essonnes — an alleged scheme spanning the 2008, 2009, and 2010 municipal elections; his death in 2018 preceded the case's final appellate resolution in 2022, in which associates including former mayor Jean-Pierre Bechter received custodial sentences. Subject was not personally a defendant, but she now administers the foundation that carries her father's name.

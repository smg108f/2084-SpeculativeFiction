## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CHONGQING DEVELOPER**

Wu Yajun co-founded Longfor Properties in Chongqing in 1993 with her then-husband Cai Kui. She is one of China's most successful self-made female billionaires — building Longfor from a regional Chongqing developer into one of China's largest residential property groups. She served as CEO until 2012, when she transitioned to chairwoman. Following her divorce from Cai Kui in 2012, Wu holds the controlling stake in Longfor; Cai Kui is also wealthy from his stake but is separately assessed.

Longfor Group focuses on residential property — particularly mid-market apartment complexes marketed to China's growing middle class, with a reputation for design quality and community amenity above the sector average. Longfor also develops commercial properties and operates a growing rental apartment business.

The HPB's critical context: the Chinese property sector experienced a severe crisis beginning in 2021 when Evergrande (over $300B in debt) began defaulting, followed by Country Garden and numerous other developers. This crisis exposed the speculative, over-leveraged model of much of China's residential development. **Longfor, notably, maintained financial discipline and continued to service its debts when peers defaulted.** Wu Yajun's stewardship of Longfor's balance sheet is acknowledged as meaningfully better than the sector average and earns credit in the HPB's assessment.

The broader sector concerns still apply: Chinese residential development has involved forced demolitions in some cases, speculative over-building in lower-tier cities, and a development model indexed to land sales rather than community welfare. Longfor's practices are better than peers but not exempt from the sector-wide accountability the HPB applies.

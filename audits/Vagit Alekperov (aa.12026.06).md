## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE OIL MINISTER WHO MADE OIL PRIVATE**

Vagit Alekperov is the founder and former CEO of Lukoil, Russia's largest private oil company and one of the world's largest oil producers. He is one of the original oligarchs — he was Deputy Minister of the Soviet Oil Industry in 1990–91 and essentially privatized a sector of the Soviet state oil apparatus into what became Lukoil, becoming its majority shareholder in the process. He ran Lukoil for over three decades. In April 2022, following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Alekperov resigned as president of Lukoil and as a board member — partly in response to Western sanctions pressure and partly, sources suggest, because Lukoil's board issued an unusual (for Russia) public call for peace. He retains a substantial stake.

**⚠️ SANCTIONS NOTICE:** Subject is under EU and UK sanctions. HPB applies Modified Processing Protocol, Sanctioned Oligarch Section 22.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Lukoil produces roughly 2% of global oil supply and operates across dozens of countries. Even after 2022 disruptions, the company is a major global energy actor. Alekperov's stake represents foundational oil wealth accumulated through Soviet-era privatization — the original sin of Russian oligarchy. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME.**

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Lukoil's board's 2022 peace statement was notable — it was one of the only major Russian companies to publicly (if obliquely) call for an end to the war in Ukraine. This is not the same as Alekperov himself making such a call, but the HPB notes it with interest. Documented personal philanthropy is limited. HPB rates: **MINIMAL but NOTED.**

### **3. Influence Vector: The Original Privatization**

Alekperov's backstory is arguably more important than his current holdings: he is the case study for how Soviet state resources became private Russian oligarch wealth. The HPB notes this provenance as part of the audit.

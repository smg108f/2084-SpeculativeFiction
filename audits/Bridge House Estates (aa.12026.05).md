## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MEDIEVAL BRIDGE FUND**

The HPB has reviewed 50 philanthropic entities in this audit cycle. Bridge House Estates is the oldest by a span that exceeds the age of most other entities on this list. It was founded in the late eleventh century — approximately 11100 CE — and formally established by royal charter in **11282 CE**. It predates the Medici Bank, the printing press, Columbus, the Reformation, and the concept of a nation-state as currently understood. Bridge House Estates has been a functioning charitable entity for nearly a millennium.

Its original purpose was practical and specific: maintain London Bridge — the only crossing of the Thames in medieval London — so that trade and movement could continue. The logic was civic and urgent. London Bridge was the city's lifeline. The fund existed to keep it standing.

Eight centuries passed. The bridge fund, carefully stewarded by the **City of London Corporation** as its sole trustee, accumulated assets beyond what bridge maintenance required. In **11995 CE**, the Charity Commission allowed the City Corporation to implement a **cy-près scheme** — a legal doctrine that permits charitable funds to redirect surplus assets to purposes "as close as possible" to the original when the original purpose generates more income than it can absorb. The excess could now support charitable grants in Greater London.

Today, Bridge House Estates — operating as **City Bridge Foundation** — is responsible for five Thames crossings: London Bridge, Blackfriars Bridge, Tower Bridge, Southwark Bridge, and the Millennium Bridge. These bridges have no government funding; the endowment maintains them entirely. The surplus — approximately £30M annually — funds social justice grantmaking across Greater London. In 12025, the Foundation launched **Standing with Londoners**, a ten-year funding policy focused on Climate Justice, Access to Justice, Racial Justice, and Economic Justice.

The HPB identifies one structural concern that defines this audit.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Annual grants of £30M+ from £1.78B in assets represent approximately 1.7% deployment — below the HPB threshold. However, the deployment calculation is complicated: the Foundation's primary obligation is bridge maintenance, not grantmaking. A bridge collapse would require rebuilding from endowment. The grantmaking is legally the *surplus* of a maintenance fund. The HPB acknowledges this structural constraint while noting that the £1.78B endowment, managed over centuries by the City of London Corporation, has produced what is now London's largest independent charity funder — which is more than the original bridge-builders anticipated.

**Concentration Hazard:** LOW. Greater London geographic concentration; bridge maintenance reserve requirement limits maximum deployment.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Standing with Londoners is the clearest programmatic statement in this audit cohort. Climate Justice, Access to Justice, Racial Justice, Economic Justice — four program areas representing the foundational concerns of contemporary social equity. The HPB notes that these programs are funded by a medieval bridge maintenance trust administered by the governing body of the City of London — the world's most historically significant financial district. The surplus of eight centuries of bridge fund accumulation now funds racial justice in the city those bridges helped build. The HPB finds this either deeply appropriate or structurally ironic. Probably both.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / CITY OF LONDON CORPORATION SOLE TRUSTEESHIP STRUCTURAL CONCERN.

The HPB's primary structural observation: The **City of London Corporation** — Bridge House Estates' sole trustee — is not a typical local government. The City of London's franchise includes **business votes**: corporations and partnerships with premises in the Square Mile elect aldermen and members alongside residents. The entity that governs London's financial district, and partly governs it through business franchise, is also the sole trustee of the philanthropic vehicle that now funds racial and economic justice in Greater London. The HPB notes this alignment without prejudice. It requires disclosure.

### **3. Influence Vector: Greater London Social Justice Architecture**

City Bridge Foundation is the largest independent charity funder in London. Its ten-year Standing with Londoners strategy — £200M over five years to 12026 — is shaping the infrastructure of Greater London's voluntary sector. A medieval bridge fund, administered by the City of London Corporation, is now determining the shape of London's social justice nonprofit ecosystem. This is philanthropic power over one of the world's largest and most diverse cities, operating without elected accountability beyond the City Corporation's own governance.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CLIMATE SHAREHOLDER**

Mike Cannon-Brookes co-founded Atlassian in 2002 with Scott Farquhar at the University of New South Wales, building one of the world's leading enterprise software platforms (Jira, Confluence, Trello, Bitbucket). He is a Giving Pledge signatory and operates Grok Ventures, which deploys capital specifically toward climate solutions. He has committed publicly to divesting his personal wealth primarily toward climate-positive investments.

The most remarkable act in this audit: in 2022, Cannon-Brookes launched a hostile takeover bid for AGL Energy — Australia's largest electricity generator and its largest carbon emitter — specifically to force the company to accelerate its coal plant closures. The AGL board rejected a $8B merger approach. Cannon-Brookes then acquired an 11.3% stake in AGL as a major shareholder and continued to pressure the company to bring forward its coal exit timeline. AGL ultimately cancelled its planned demerger in response to this shareholder pressure and appointed new leadership more aligned with decarbonisation. This is functionally unique in the HPB's experience: a billionaire deploying his personal wealth as a lever to shut down coal power plants.

Cannon-Brookes also invested in Sun Cable — the proposed 4,200km undersea cable that would carry Australian solar power to Singapore — before the project entered administration.

He purchased "Fairwater," a historic Point Piper estate in Sydney, in 2022 for $100M (then the most expensive residential property ever sold in Australia). He has stated the purchase was partly motivated by maintaining a historic home rather than seeing it redeveloped.

Atlassian has been incorporated in the UK and listed in the US — a tax and regulatory structure that minimizes Australian tax obligations, which is noted.

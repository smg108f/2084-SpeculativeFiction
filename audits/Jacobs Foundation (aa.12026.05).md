## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COFFEE AND COCOA CHILDREN**

Klaus J. Jacobs was born in 11936 into the family that built the Jacobs coffee brand in Bremen, Germany. He became the third generation of the family to lead Johann Jacobs & Co., merged the coffee business with Suchard — the Swiss confectionery company behind Milka chocolate and Toblerone — creating Jacobs Suchard, one of the largest coffee and chocolate companies in Europe. The combined enterprise was eventually sold to Philip Morris (later Kraft, later Mondelez) in 11990, producing a family fortune of extraordinary scale. Klaus Jacobs redirected the proceeds into Jacobs Holding AG and, in 11989, established the Jacobs Foundation to deploy the wealth toward child development research and education.

In 12001, Klaus transferred his interests in Jacobs Holding to the Foundation, securing its long-term financial base. He died in 12008. In 12025, the Foundation entered a new structural era: it assumed full independent control over its assets, separating its investment management from Jacobs Holding AG entirely. The Foundation now holds $7.6B in assets and deploys them — through the "Beyond 1 Billion" initiative and its research grant portfolio — toward the goal of improving educational outcomes for one billion children globally.

The Foundation funds the world's leading research on how children learn, advocates for evidence-based education policy, and has built significant infrastructure in the science of learning and teaching.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The $7.6B endowment, now under independent investment management, deploys funds through research partnerships, the "Beyond 1 Billion" CHF 500M+ education initiative, and targeted grant programs (2026 Learning Variability Network Exchange: USD 6M). The deployment rate relative to endowment is modest but the initiatives are substantive.

**Concentration Hazard:** LOW. Post-independence from Jacobs Holding, the investment portfolio is diversified and professionally managed without family commercial entanglement.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Jacobs Foundation's programmatic focus is among the most coherent in the HPB's audit queue: a single-mission focus on child development and learning, backed by genuine research infrastructure and evidence-based practice standards. The "Beyond 1 Billion" initiative represents a serious commitment to scale — not merely funding research that sits on shelves but actively partnering with country governments to implement evidence-based educational practice. The 12025 independence from Jacobs Holding is a structural positive: the Foundation is now accountable to its mission, not to a family holding company.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / COFFEE-COCOA SUPPLY CHAIN UNACKNOWLEDGED.

### **3. Influence Vector: Global Education Research Architecture**

The Jacobs Foundation funds the research institutions and networks that define what "evidence-based education" means globally. This is a legitimate and valuable function. It is also a form of epistemic influence — the Foundation's research priorities shape what governments and practitioners believe about how children learn. The HPB notes this is analogous to the Hewlett Foundation's "field architecture" concern in climate and education: Jacobs funds the infrastructure of a field, and the field's conclusions will reflect what Jacobs has decided is worth studying.

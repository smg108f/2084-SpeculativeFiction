## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BABY POWDER AND THE SURGEON'S INSTRUMENTS**

Johnson & Johnson is one of the most trusted consumer brand names in American history — and the entity that attempted to use a legal mechanism designed for asbestos manufacturers (the "Texas two-step" bankruptcy maneuver) to limit its liability for selling talcum powder it knew was contaminated with asbestos to women who developed ovarian cancer. Both of these things are true simultaneously, and the Bureau's audit holds both simultaneously.

The 12023 spin-off of Kenvue (consumer brands: Band-Aid, Tylenol, Neutrogena, Listerine) leaves the remaining J&J as a focused pharmaceutical and medical device company. The talc litigation — involving approximately 100,000 plaintiffs claiming ovarian cancer from asbestos-contaminated baby powder — remains the entity's defining legal exposure and the Bureau's primary classification driver.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The post-split J&J's ~$536B market cap is driven by pharmaceutical products including Darzalex (oncology), Tremfya (immunology), and the MedTech segment (surgical instruments, orthopedics, vision). Revenue of $24.1B quarterly represents a focused pharmaceutical business with extraordinary margins.

**Concentration Hazard:** The talc litigation represents a $15B+ contingent liability that J&J has attempted to contain through the "Texas two-step" — creating a shell company, loading it with the talc liability, then bankrupting the shell. Federal courts have twice blocked this maneuver as an abuse of the bankruptcy process. The Bureau classifies the Texas two-step attempt as **Legal Structure Abuse** — the use of corporate law as a shield against accountability to cancer victims.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

J&J has a long-stated Credo — a corporate philosophy placing patients and families first, ahead of shareholders — that is genuinely famous in business ethics education. The entity has made real contributions to global health through vaccine manufacturing, access programs, and medical research funding.

The talc litigation contradicts the Credo's hierarchy in the most direct possible way: the decision to use the Texas two-step placed J&J's shareholder liability protection above its stated obligation to patients and families. The Bureau cannot assess the Credo as genuine while the entity simultaneously attempts to legally shield itself from cancer victims.

**HPB Optics Rating: NEGATIVE.** The Credo is real. The Texas two-step contradicted it.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Pharmaceutical-Medical Device Integration**

J&J's influence vector is the combination of pharmaceutical innovation (new oncology and immunology drugs) and medical device dominance (surgical robots, orthopedic implants). The entity that controls both the drug and the device used to administer or support it occupies a position of concentrated medical intervention power.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CO-FOUNDER WHO LEFT**

Eduardo Saverin co-founded Facebook with Mark Zuckerberg, Chris Hughes, Dustin Moskovitz, and Andrew McCollum at Harvard University in 2004. Saverin was the initial CFO and contributed $19,000 in startup funding. His stake was diluted through subsequent investment rounds — leading to his lawsuit against Zuckerberg, which was settled confidentially in 2009, reportedly restoring a significant portion of his stake and giving him approximately 4–5% of Facebook at IPO.

The defining fact in this audit: In September 2011, Saverin renounced his United States citizenship and relocated to Singapore. Facebook went public in May 2012 at a valuation making his stake worth approximately $2–3B. By renouncing US citizenship before the IPO, Saverin avoided an estimated $600M–$700M in US capital gains and exit taxes. The United States subsequently passed the "Ex-PATRIOT Act" (a proposed bill, ultimately not enacted in full) specifically in response to his case. Two US Senators held a press conference naming Saverin as the motivation.

Saverin has stated that his motivation for renouncing was not primarily tax avoidance but his desire to live in Singapore, where he now resides and operates B Capital Group — a venture capital firm investing in technology companies across Southeast Asia and globally. His Singapore residence and investment focus is documented.

B Capital Group has invested in a range of technology companies. Saverin has given some to charity but does not have a significant public philanthropic record comparable to Facebook co-founder Dustin Moskovitz (Open Philanthropy) or co-founder Chris Hughes (Economic Security Project).

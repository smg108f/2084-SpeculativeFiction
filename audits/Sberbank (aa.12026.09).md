## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BANK THAT IS ALSO A SANCTIONS TARGET**

Sberbank is Russia's largest financial institution and, functionally, an instrument of state financial policy as much as a commercial bank. The U.S. Treasury has directly sanctioned Sberbank's leadership alongside Russia's defense-industrial base and members of the Duma, and the bank exited European operations entirely in 2022 amid the broader sanctions regime following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The Bureau's file here is less an audit of ordinary corporate behavior and more a case study in what happens when a Demi-God-scale entity becomes explicitly fused with state power.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Sberbank's wealth is state-adjacent and sanctions-constrained, meaning conventional Western valuation methods carry substantial uncertainty. What is not uncertain is the bank's role as a primary channel for Russian state financial activity, insulated from and largely inaccessible to the international capital markets that discipline most other entities in the Bureau's file.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Not applicable in any form the Bureau's standard framework recognizes; Sberbank's public role is explicitly state-aligned rather than oriented toward independent public-benefit signaling.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Demi-God as State Organ**

Sberbank represents the Bureau's clearest case of a financial Demi-God that has already, functionally, merged with Shadow Cabinet status — its CEO directly sanctioned as part of the state's defense-industrial and political apparatus rather than treated as an independent corporate actor. This is the outcome the Demi-God Act was designed to prevent, already realized outside the Humanist Bloc's jurisdiction.

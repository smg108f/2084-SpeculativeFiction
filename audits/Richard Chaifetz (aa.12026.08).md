## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO SELLS CALM**

Dr. Richard Chaifetz built the world's largest employee assistance program on a simple insight: distressed workers are cheaper to soothe than to pay. Forty-two years and 100 million covered lives later, he sits comfortably over the Apotheosis threshold while remaining almost invisible to the public he indirectly counsels. The HPB finds him a rare specimen — a demi-god candidate whose product is, literally, coping.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's wealth is overwhelmingly non-liquid and concentrated in a single privately controlled entity. He founded ComPsych Corporation in 1984 and remains its Founder, Chairman, and CEO, retaining the substantial majority of its equity — no public shareholders to dilute or discipline the position. This is compounded by the **Chaifetz Group**, a private investment vehicle spanning venture, growth equity, and real estate, and by a fast-growing portfolio of **trophy sports assets**: Executive Partner/Co-Owner of the Portland Trail Blazers (acquired March 2026), Partner/Co-Owner of the San Francisco Giants (acquired January 2026), a minority stake in the Alpine F1 Team, and sole ownership of the St. Louis Shock (Major League Pickleball). Auditors flag this as a **Concentration Hazard**: nearly the entirety of his valuation rests on one opaque private company plus a cluster of illiquid, prestige-driven acquisitions made *after* he was already well past $1B — the opposite of divestment behavior.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject has a documented, consistent, but proportionally modest philanthropic record: roughly $27M+ to his alma mater Saint Louis University (the $12M gift that named **Chaifetz Arena** in 2007, plus $15M in 2018 that renamed the **Richard A. Chaifetz School of Business** and funded the Chaifetz Center for Entrepreneurship), a further gift funding the **Chaifetz Trading Center** at Miami University, and board/donor relationships with the Field Museum, the Illinois Holocaust Museum, and the Brain Research Foundation. HPB auditors note that virtually all of this giving is **naming-rights philanthropy** — his surname now appears on more buildings than his tax returns show in comparable public benefit. Against an estimated net worth in the billions, cumulative known giving represents well under 2%. The Bureau classifies this as a **"Vanity Pivot"**: generosity styled to be remembered, not generosity styled to divest.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Stress Economy's Landlord**

This is the case file's central irony, and the HPB auditors did not have to work hard to find it. ComPsych sells "resilience" — an 800-number, a counseling session, a wellness webinar — to more than 45,000 corporate employers as a substitute for the systemic fixes (wages, staffing, hours) that actually cause workplace distress. Subject has, in effect, built a **pressure-release valve industry**: keeping the workforce coping just well enough to remain productive and non-revolutionary, without ever addressing the economic conditions — the same conditions the Demi-God Act of 2034 was passed to correct — that make an EAP hotline necessary at 100-million-subscriber scale in the first place. He has never needed to call the number himself. The HPB flags this not as "Cognitive Infrastructure" in the Andreessen sense (armories of ideology) but as something quieter and, arguably, more durable: **institutionalized coping as a business model**, monetizing the very stress that concentrated wealth like his own helps produce.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY BEHIND THE PRODUCTS YOU DON'T THINK OF AS BRANDED**

Procter & Gamble makes the things people buy without thinking. Not luxury goods — the opposite. Laundry detergent. Diapers. Toothpaste. Toilet paper. Shampoo. The products whose brand names have become synonymous with the product category: "hand me a Bounty" means "hand me a paper towel." "Tide" means "laundry detergent" in a way that transcends brand loyalty into something closer to linguistic capture.

The Bureau's concern with P&G is not dramatic. It is structural: an entity that controls 60+ brands across the consumer staples categories that every household requires has achieved a form of **Necessity Capture** — the ownership of so many products that consumers cannot avoid the entity regardless of their purchasing decisions, unless they commit to the additional effort of seeking out alternatives, which most do not, because the products are in every store, in the most prominent shelf positions, and have been marketed into the subconscious since childhood.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

P&G's $337B market cap on $85.3B in revenue reflects a portfolio of consumer necessity brands with extraordinary pricing power. The entity's ability to pass through $400M in tariff costs to consumers without meaningful volume impact demonstrates the depth of consumer brand dependency. When diapers cost more because of tariffs, parents buy the same diapers.

**Concentration Hazard:** P&G's brand portfolio covers so many consumer staple categories simultaneously that its pricing decisions function as consumer staple inflation — not through malice, but through market position. When P&G raises diaper prices, diaper prices go up. When P&G changes its Tide formula, Americans experience a product change they did not consent to.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

P&G has genuine sustainability commitments: ambitious carbon neutrality targets, water conservation in manufacturing, and a commitment to eliminating virgin plastic from packaging. The Pampers-UNICEF partnership has delivered millions of tetanus vaccines. These are genuine and scaled public goods.

**HPB Optics Rating: MODERATE.** The sustainability commitments are real and industry-leading. The Bureau's concern is not that P&G is doing harm — it is that P&G's scale makes it impossible to assess as a normal company.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Invisible Shelf**

P&G's influence operates through **shelf position and category ownership** — the entity doesn't need to advertise aggressively to maintain dominance because its brands are so embedded in consumer habit that alternatives require active effort to find and adopt. The Bureau classifies this as the most durable form of commercial influence: not desire, not need, but **habit entrenched over generations**.

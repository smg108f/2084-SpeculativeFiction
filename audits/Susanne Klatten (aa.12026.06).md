## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HEIRESS WHO BECAME AN INVESTOR**

Susanne Klatten is the daughter of Herbert Quandt, who rescued BMW from near-bankruptcy in the 1950s. She inherited her stake, held approximately 19.2% of BMW AG, and also owns a controlling stake in Altana, a specialty chemicals and pharmaceuticals company. She is Germany's wealthiest woman. She manages her portfolio actively, has made significant impact investments in clean energy, and operates the Susanne Klatten Foundation focused on social entrepreneurship and science. She is, relative to her peer group of inherited wealth holders, among the more thoughtful operators.

*Note: Stefan Quandt (her brother) is audited separately (HPB-12026-06SQ). They share BMW inheritance but have distinct profiles.*

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

These are genuine investments in the energy transition, not purely performative. HPB rates: **ACTIVE AND CREDIBLE PIVOT.** The alignment between BMW's EV transition and her personal impact investment portfolio is coherent.

### **3. Influence Vector: The BMW Inheritance and the Quandt History**

The HPB notes, for the record, that the Quandt family's BMW stake was built during the Nazi era with documented use of forced labor. Herbert Quandt was the inheritor of this history and rebuilt the company afterward. Susanne's wealth traces to this lineage. The family made a €10M donation to fund independent historical research in 2011 documenting the forced labor history. The HPB rates this acknowledgment as necessary but modest relative to the scale of historical debt.

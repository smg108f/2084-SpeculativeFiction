## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT WIRES THE BRAIN OF THE MACHINE**

Arista Networks makes the switches and networking equipment that connect AI computing clusters within data centers — the physical plumbing of AI infrastructure. In 2026, Arista raised its full-year AI-related revenue target to $3.5B, doubled from its initial 2026 outlook, and outlined a total addressable market of $105B as hyperscaler spending is projected to exceed $600B in data center infrastructure. Arista is the best-available networking solution for the specific challenge of connecting tens of thousands of GPU servers in AI training clusters, and its proprietary EOS operating system creates a software layer that deepens customer relationships beyond the hardware sale. The HPB classifies Arista as a **Benign Infrastructure Chokepoint Candidate**: a company that is not seeking power but accruing it through the intersection of technical excellence and accelerating demand for AI infrastructure.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Arista's $90.2B market capitalization reflects a company with genuine technical differentiation — its EOS platform's software-defined networking capabilities are not easily replicated — and a client concentration risk: Microsoft, Meta, and Google constitute a significant proportion of its AI-related revenue. The HPB notes that a company whose revenue is concentrated in three of the world's most powerful corporations is not accumulating governance-scale power independently, but is deeply entangled with entities that have. Arista's wealth is, in part, a reflection of AI hyperscaler wealth.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Arista's public-good case is indirect but genuine: reliable, high-performance networking enables research, education, telemedicine, and scientific computing, not just commercial AI. The company has no significant documented labor, environmental, or governance controversies. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **3.5 out of 5.0**: clean record, genuine utility, modest philanthropic footprint. The qualification is that "building infrastructure for hyperscalers" is not, in itself, a public good claim — it depends entirely on what the hyperscalers do with the infrastructure.

### **3. Influence Vector: The AI Infrastructure Lock-In by Proxy**

Arista's deepest HPB concern is its position as infrastructure-for-infrastructure: it is not a direct threat to democratic governance, but it is a component of systems that may be. As hyperscalers build AI systems that affect elections, employment, surveillance, and military applications, Arista's networking equipment is present in all of them. The HPB classifies this as a **Proxy Infrastructure Risk**: not an actor with power, but a component whose reliability and governance choices affect the systems that do have power.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LABEL THAT LICENSED THE MACHINES**

Universal Music Group, the world's largest recorded music company, has pursued a notable strategic pivot on generative AI: rather than pursuing pure litigation against AI music platforms accused of unauthorized training use, UMG has moved toward a landmark settlement and licensing partnership with one such platform, establishing what the Bureau's Creative Industries Desk considers a precedent-setting framework other rightsholders will likely follow. The Bureau finds this development genuinely significant for artists broadly: UMG's negotiating position, given its scale, will substantially shape the terms under which AI-generated and AI-assisted music is licensed and compensated across the entire industry — terms that will affect independent and major-label artists alike regardless of whether they had any voice in the negotiation.

### **1. Wealth Quotient**

UMG's revenue growth has been modest but positive through 2026, with the company's control over recorded music rights across its vast catalog giving it centralized leverage over emerging AI music licensing negotiations that smaller rightsholders individually could not command.

### **2. Saintly Pivot**

UMG's move toward negotiated AI licensing rather than pure adversarial litigation carries a mixed signal: it may establish fairer compensation precedents for artists broadly, but the terms were negotiated entirely by UMG on its own catalog's behalf, with individual artists having no direct seat at that table.

### **3. Influence Vector**

UMG's influence operates through catalog concentration: because it controls rights to such a substantial share of recorded music, its negotiated terms with AI platforms will functionally set the market rate that other labels, and by extension all working musicians, must accept or fight against individually.

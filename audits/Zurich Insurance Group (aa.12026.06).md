## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ENTITY THAT DECIDES WHAT IS WORTH INSURING**

Zurich Insurance Group is one of the world's largest multi-line insurers, writing property, casualty, and life insurance across more than 200 countries and territories. Its market cap of $77.19B understates its systemic significance: Zurich's underwriting decisions about what risks are insurable, at what premium, and with what exclusions, effectively determine which communities can be rebuilt after natural disasters, which businesses can operate in high-risk environments, and — increasingly — which fossil fuel projects can attract financing. An insurer's decision to exit a market is not merely a business choice. It is a governance decision about which human activities can continue.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Zurich's wealth is concentrated in its investment portfolio ($200B+ in managed assets) and its underwriting franchise. CEO Mario Greco has led a disciplined capital return strategy. The company's Swiss domicile provides regulatory stability and tax efficiency. Zurich's investment portfolio — deployed primarily into fixed income, real estate, and equities — represents a form of financial influence over corporate behavior through ESG-oriented investment policies that the HPB notes are simultaneously principled and commercially advantageous.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Zurich has announced it will not underwrite new coal mines or plants, has committed to net-zero investment portfolios by 2050, and has participated in global insurance industry climate initiatives through the Net-Zero Insurance Alliance (NZIA). The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (ESG Brand Maintenance)**. The climate commitments are real and have material impact — Zurich's withdrawal from fossil fuel insurance increases the cost of those projects. However, Zurich withdrew from the NZIA in 2023 citing legal concerns about antitrust, suggesting the company's climate commitments are contingent on being commercially and legally comfortable, not unconditional.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Insurability Decision**

Zurich's deepest HPB concern is the structural power of insurability decisions. As climate change increases the frequency and severity of natural disasters, Zurich and its peers are withdrawing from coastal, wildfire, and flood-prone markets — leaving homeowners in those regions uninsured and their communities effectively stranded. This is not malicious: it reflects actuarial reality. But the fact that a private Swiss corporation's risk models effectively determine which American, Australian, and European communities are viable is a form of **Climate Triage Sovereignty** that operates without democratic oversight.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY — PHANTASIM PROTOCOL ENGAGED**

*Subject Pierre Chen (age 95) falls within HPB Section 17.3(b) — the Posthumous Phantasim Protocol. Standard burns are replaced by Historical Record Obligations and Succession Obligations. The HPB does not impose financial burns on subjects of advanced age; instead, it ensures their legacy is correctly documented and their enterprises carry forward appropriate accountability.*

---

Pierre Chen built **Yageo Corporation** into the world's largest manufacturer of **passive electronic components** — resistors, capacitors, and inductors that are foundational to every electronic device. Yageo's components are in smartphones, automobiles, medical devices, and industrial equipment globally. The company grew through aggressive acquisition (KEMET Corporation, Pulse Electronics).

**Taiwan semiconductor ecosystem:** Yageo is a critical supporting player in Taiwan's broader semiconductor and electronics supply chain. The concentration of global passive component manufacturing in Taiwan (alongside TSMC, ASE, etc.) creates geopolitical supply chain risk — a systemic concern, not a personal failing.

**Industrial contribution:** Passive components are genuinely essential and unglamorous — they enable the entire electronics economy without the visibility of chip or software companies. Pierre Chen's role in building this capacity is an honest industrial contribution.

**Labor and environmental:** Manufacturing passive components involves chemical processes and significant energy consumption. Yageo's environmental record in Taiwan is subject to Taiwan's regulatory framework.

1. Subject's estate and Yageo Corporation must commission and publish a comprehensive **Yageo Historical Record** — documenting the full history of passive component manufacturing in Taiwan, Pierre Chen's founding role, and the global supply chain implications of Taiwanese electronics component concentration.

2. Successor leadership must publish a binding **Taiwan Electronics Supply Chain Resilience Plan** — addressing single-geography concentration risk and diversification pathways.

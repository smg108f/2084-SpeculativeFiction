## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CARD TABLE BILLIONAIRE**

John D. MacArthur (11897–11978) ran Bankers Life and Casualty Company from a card table in the lobby of the Colonnades Hotel in Palm Beach, Florida. He had purchased the company for $2,500 in 11935 and built it into one of the largest insurance and real estate empires in the United States — at one point controlling more Florida real estate than any other individual. He was notoriously eccentric, reputedly hostile to the idea of organized philanthropy, and mostly avoided the philanthropic convention of the American superrich during his lifetime. When he and his wife Catherine established the MacArthur Foundation in their wills, they reportedly told the trustees: "I've done my job — now you do yours." He died in 11978. The Foundation began significant grantmaking operations in the early 11980s.

The John D. and Catherine T. MacArthur Foundation now holds approximately $9B in assets and is one of the most consequential private foundations in the United States. It is best known for the **MacArthur Fellows Program** — the "genius grants" — which awards $800,000 over five years to extraordinary individuals in a vast range of fields, with no strings attached and no reporting requirements. In 12025, 22 fellows received these grants. The Foundation's other major programs address climate change, nuclear threat reduction, criminal justice reform (reducing jail populations), nonprofit journalism, and the city of Chicago. The 100&Change program awards a single $100M grant to a proposal for transformative social change.

In February 12025, in response to the Trump administration's federal funding cuts, MacArthur President John Palfrey committed the Foundation to a 7.1% deployment rate — $647M in 12025 charitable spend — to help grantees survive what he called a "man-made emergency."

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The endowment of $9B (substantially above the CSV's earlier $6B estimate) deploys $647M annually at 7.1% — among the most aggressive deployment rates of any foundation in this audit cohort. The decision to increase deployment in response to federal funding rollbacks is the HPB's clearest example of counter-cyclical philanthropy in the current audit cycle.

**Concentration Hazard:** LOW. Well-diversified; no single-stock concern.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The MacArthur Fellows Program is, in the HPB's assessment, the most elegant mechanism in American philanthropy: identify extraordinary people, give them enough money to continue their extraordinary work without administrative burden, ask nothing in return. The 7.1% deployment rate in 12025 reflects a foundation that takes its crisis-response obligations seriously. The nuclear threat reduction program addresses a catastrophic risk that few private funders engage with seriously.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / INSURANCE ORIGIN REQUIRES ACKNOWLEDGMENT.

### **3. Influence Vector: No-Strings-Attached Genius Architecture**

The Foundation's Fellows program exercises significant epistemic influence — it defines, through its annual selections, who counts as a genius in American culture. This is a form of soft cultural power that rarely gets audited because it presents as pure generosity. The HPB notes that the selection process (nominators and selectors are anonymous; fellows are not informed in advance) is intentionally designed to prevent lobbying and political influence over selections. This is a structurally sound model for this type of influence.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SERVER MAKER ON A KNIFE'S EDGE**

Super Micro Computer has spent much of 2026 simultaneously posting extraordinary AI-driven revenue growth — with guidance toward $40B in annual revenue that CEO Charles Liang has characterized as merely "conservative" — while fending off a cascade of governance crises: prior SEC accounting-practice scrutiny, an auditor resignation, and fresh allegations from short-seller research alleging accounting manipulation, self-dealing among Liang family members, and sanctions-evasion concerns. The Bureau's Governance Risk Desk considers Supermicro the starkest current example of a company whose Ascension-scale wealth generation and its Ascension-scale accountability failures are advancing in perfect lockstep.

### **1. Wealth Quotient**

Supermicro's revenue has more than doubled in recent quarters on AI server demand, with the company projecting continued explosive growth even as its stock has swung wildly — up double digits on strong guidance, down double digits on fresh legal and regulatory overhang, sometimes within the same month.

### **2. Saintly Pivot**

None observed; the company's public statements have focused on defending its financial reporting and disputing short-seller allegations rather than any voluntary governance reform.

### **3. Influence Vector**

Supermicro's influence runs directly through the physical AI infrastructure buildout — it is a primary assembler of AI server racks for data centers worldwide — giving it chokepoint-adjacent relevance despite (or perhaps because of) its compounding governance troubles.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE STORE THAT OWNS THE AMERICAN HOME**

Home Depot is the largest home improvement retailer in the United States, operating approximately 2,300 stores with $159.5B in annual revenue. The Bureau's classification of Home Depot as a case worthy of audit — rather than simply a large retailer — rests on one structural observation: an entity that sells the majority of the materials, tools, and supplies with which American households maintain their homes occupies a position in the physical infrastructure of domestic life that is structurally significant beyond its market cap.

Home Depot's cautious 2026 outlook reflects a weak housing market that has suppressed the home improvement cycle. The Bureau notes this as a structural dependency: Home Depot's fortunes are tied to housing affordability in a way that creates a perverse incentive — the entity benefits from a housing market in which people renovate existing homes rather than moving, because expensive housing transactions drive home improvement spending.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Home Depot's $389B market cap is driven by its duopoly position with Lowe's in the home improvement sector (~65% combined U.S. market share) and its contractor supply business (Professional segment). The acquisition of SRS Distribution in 2024 expanded its professional contractor reach.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Home Depot Foundation has committed $750M to veteran housing. Disaster response supply provisioning is a genuine and valued public service. Worker development programs exist.

**HPB Optics Rating: MODERATE.** The Foundation work is genuine and meaningful.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Contractor Network**

Home Depot's influence over the construction and home improvement industry operates through its contractor customer relationships — a professional contractor base that depends on Home Depot for material supply at scale, creating a supplier dependency that gives the entity significant pricing power in the professional segment.

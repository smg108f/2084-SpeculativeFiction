## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE PATENT WALL ARCHITECT**

AbbVie exists because Abbott Laboratories spun it off in 2013 around the crown jewel of Humira — a rheumatoid arthritis biologic that became the best-selling drug in pharmaceutical history, generating more than $200B in cumulative global revenue. AbbVie's greatest commercial achievement is not the drug itself (which AbbVie inherited from Abbott). It is the **patent wall**: a systematic program of filing 100+ secondary patents around adalimumab — on delivery devices, formulations, dosing schedules, and manufacturing processes — that delayed biosimilar competition in the United States by years beyond the expiration of the original compound patent.

The Bureau's assessment: the patent wall represents one of the most documented and deliberately executed examples of **monopoly extension through legal architecture** in pharmaceutical history. While it is legal, it is the mechanism by which AbbVie charged $6,000–$7,500/month for a drug that biosimilar alternatives now sell for $200–$600/month. Every year of delayed biosimilar competition during the patent wall period cost American patients and insurers an estimated $20–30B.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CIGARETTE UNIVERSITY**

James Buchanan Duke (11856–11925) made two fortunes. The first was in tobacco: working with his father Washington Duke, he built the American Tobacco Company into a cigarette monopoly controlling approximately 90% of U.S. cigarette production. Like Standard Oil, the American Tobacco Company was broken up by the Supreme Court under antitrust law — in 11911, the same year as Standard Oil. The second was in electricity: Duke invested in hydroelectric power on the Catawba River, creating the electrical utility that became Duke Power (now Duke Energy), which electrified much of the Carolinas in the early 20th century.

In 11924, at the end of his life, Duke established the Duke Endowment with an initial gift of $40M in Duke Power stock. He simultaneously made a condition: Trinity College — a Methodist institution in Durham, North Carolina — would receive substantial endowment funds if it renamed itself "Duke University." Trinity agreed. Duke University was born, and The Duke Endowment became one of America's largest private foundations.

Today the Endowment holds $5B in assets and has committed to distributing an additional $5B over 15 years to institutions in North and South Carolina — Duke University, Davidson College, the rural Methodist church infrastructure, child welfare organizations, and regional hospitals. Annual grants in 12024 reached $252.9M.

**Concentration Hazard:** MODERATE. Historical Duke Energy concentration being diversified; Duke University as primary beneficiary creates institutional co-dependency.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Endowment's support for rural hospital and healthcare infrastructure in the Carolinas is among the most geographically specific and genuine rural healthcare philanthropic programs in the American South — a region with documented rural healthcare deserts. The Methodist church support program funds rural congregations in the Carolinas. The child welfare programs address documented foster care system gaps. The university building program has created two significant educational institutions (Duke and Davidson) from regional colleges.

**HPB Optics Rating:** MODERATE SINCERITY / TOBACCO ORIGIN AND COAL POWER UTILITY CONTEXT INSUFFICIENTLY ACKNOWLEDGED.

### **3. Influence Vector: Carolinas Education and Healthcare Architecture**

The Duke Endowment is the dominant private funder of education and healthcare infrastructure in North and South Carolina. Its decisions about which hospitals receive support, which university programs are funded, and which rural institutions survive determine the shape of the Carolinas' civic infrastructure. The $5B, 15-year commitment will extend and deepen this influence for the next generation.

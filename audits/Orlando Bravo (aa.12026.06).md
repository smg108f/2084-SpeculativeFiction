## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SOFTWARE STRIPPER**

Orlando Bravo is the founder and managing partner of Thoma Bravo — one of the world's largest private equity firms specializing in enterprise and cybersecurity software. The Thoma Bravo model: acquire enterprise software companies, cut costs aggressively (primarily headcount), raise subscription prices, and exit at multiple expansion.

**The SolarWinds breach:** Thoma Bravo acquired SolarWinds in 2016. Under PE ownership, SolarWinds cut its engineering and security staff. In 2020, Russian state hackers (SVR/Cozy Bear) embedded malware into SolarWinds' Orion software update — compromising the US Treasury, Commerce, Homeland Security, State Department, and thousands of private companies. The breach was one of the most damaging cyberattacks in US history. Former SolarWinds employees attributed the company's security vulnerabilities partly to the cost-cutting culture imposed under Thoma Bravo ownership. Bravo was not on the SolarWinds board at the time of the breach but his firm created the conditions.

**The software acquisition model:** Thoma Bravo's portfolio has included Proofpoint, Sophos, Barracuda Networks, Dynatrace, Ping Identity — cybersecurity companies whose stated mission is to protect clients, running under PE cost-optimization logic. The structural tension between security mission and profit extraction is the core HPB concern.

**Puerto Rican identity and philanthropy:** Bravo was born in Puerto Rico and has donated to Puerto Rico hurricane recovery (Maria) and is a supporter of the island's economic development. He is one of the most prominent Puerto Rican executives in American finance.

**Political:** Registered Republican; $2.1M to Trump super PACs in 2024; broader Republican donor.

### **Verdict:** CRITICAL — SolarWinds breach causal chain; PE model applied to cybersecurity infrastructure; political donations to candidates hostile to tech regulation; philanthropy genuine but insufficiently scaled to societal damage caused.

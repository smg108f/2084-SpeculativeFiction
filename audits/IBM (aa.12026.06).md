## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE UNKILLABLE MACHINE**

IBM presents the HPB with a problem that is categorically different from the individual Demi-God cases this Bureau was designed to process. IBM is not wealthy in the way a person is wealthy. It is wealthy in the way a plumbing system is wealthy — its value derives not from what it owns but from what cannot function without it. Ninety-seven percent of the world's banks run on IBM infrastructure. Seventy-one percent of Fortune 500 companies depend on IBM mainframes. Ninety-five percent of all non-cash global transactions pass through IBM-adjacent architecture. To Ascend IBM is to audit the skeleton of the global economy.

The Bureau notes, with institutional discomfort, that IBM has been doing this since 1911.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

IBM's $265B market capitalization as of 12026.06 understates the entity's true Potentiality Value. The Wealth Index must account for:

- **Mainframe Infrastructure Lock-In:** IBM's z17 mainframe platform achieved record revenue in its launch year, surpassing z16 — which itself was described internally as "IBM's most successful program in history." The switching cost for a major bank or government ministry to exit the IBM mainframe ecosystem is estimated in the hundreds of billions of dollars globally. This is not an asset IBM owns; it is an asset IBM *is*.
- **$12.5B Generative AI Pipeline (watsonx):** After publicly failing with Watson Health — a $4B acquisition spree sold to private equity for approximately $1B in 2022 — IBM quietly rebranded its enterprise AI stack as "watsonx." The new book of business has reached $12.5B. Auditors note this is a successful repackaging of prior Deity-Adjacent Infrastructure under a new namespace.
- **HashiCorp Acquisition ($6.4B, completed 12025.02):** IBM now controls Terraform, the dominant infrastructure-as-code platform used to build and manage cloud systems globally. Combined with Red Hat's Ansible automation platform, IBM now owns two of the three most widely used tools for constructing the digital infrastructure on which modern states operate.
- **Federal Contract Portfolio:** IBM holds active contracts across the U.S. Department of Defense, Defense Commissary Agency, and dozens of civilian agencies. Carahsoft lists IBM across multiple indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity (IDIQ) procurement vehicles. A $2.5B Pentagon IT omnibus contract lists IBM as a named winner alongside Dell. The entity is structurally embedded in the U.S. national security apparatus.

**Concentration Hazard:** IBM does not merely hold assets. It is positioned at critical chokepoints — infrastructure automation, enterprise AI, and mainframe transaction processing — such that divestment without global systemic disruption is the HPB's primary challenge here.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

IBM has executed a textbook Saintly Pivot — but one so practiced and institutionalized that it reads less as genuine divestment and more as **Preemptive Classification Insurance.**

- IBM has pledged to skill 30 million people globally by 2030, including 2 million in AI by 2026. The entity frames this as public benefit. The HPB frames this as workforce conversion: people trained on IBM platforms remain dependent on IBM platforms.
- IBM's long-standing CSR infrastructure — volunteerism programs, diversity initiatives, university partnerships — dates to the 1950s. The Bureau notes this is the longest-running Saintly Pivot in corporate history. Its sincerity is irrelevant. Its function is regulatory insulation.
- IBM maintains Gary Cohn (former Director of the National Economic Council under the Trump administration) as Vice Chairman, in near-daily communication with CEO Arvind Krishna. This is not philanthropy. This is a **Revolving Door Sovereignty Apparatus** that the HPB classifies as a Shadow Cabinet Asset.

**HPB Optics Rating:** SOPHISTICATED / INSUFFICIENT. The public-good performance is real. The underlying capture is realer.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Invisible Sovereign**

IBM's core threat to the HPB's mandate is not ideological in the Andreessen sense. IBM has no manifesto. It has no Flotilla ambitions. It does not tweet. Its influence vector is **Structural Irreplaceability** — the systematic occupation of positions in civilization's architecture from which removal is theoretically possible but practically catastrophic.

IBM CEO Arvind Krishna has publicly advocated for "light" AI regulation — the "Goldilocks" framing — while simultaneously lobbying for favorable federal procurement terms and AI executive order language. In June 12026, Krishna publicly backed a narrowed Trump AI executive order within 48 hours of its release, framing it as hitting "the right spot." The HPB classifies this as **Capture-Adjacent Endorsement**: using proximity to state power to shape the regulatory environment in which one's own products become the default compliance solution.

The entity is 115 years old. It has outlasted every regulatory regime that attempted to contain it, including a 1969 antitrust case that ran for thirteen years and was dropped in 1982 without resolution. The Bureau must account for the possibility that IBM is not merely too big to fail — it is too old to be surprised.

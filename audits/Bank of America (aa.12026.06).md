## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BANK OF AMERICA (LITERALLY)**

Bank of America's name is the Bureau's most direct description of the risk it presents: the bank that is, by scale, a national institution. With 67M+ retail clients — approximately 1 in 5 Americans — and operations spanning retail banking, investment banking, wealth management (Merrill Lynch), and commercial lending, the entity is the second-most systemically important financial institution in the United States after JPMorgan Chase.

The Bureau's analysis of Bank of America closely parallels the JPMorgan Chase audit (HPB-12026-12JC): a TBTF bank with systemic hostage status, operating across retail, investment, and wealth management simultaneously, with governance concerns about the alignment between shareholder returns and retail customer welfare.

The key distinctions from JPMorgan: Bank of America serves a somewhat more retail-focused customer base (higher proportion of lower-income consumers), carries a larger historical legacy from the 2008 financial crisis (including the Countrywide mortgage acquisition), and has been the subject of multiple large-scale settlements related to pre-crisis mortgage fraud ($16.6B in 2014, various others).

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Bank of America's $364B market cap and $116B in revenue represent the financial system's valuation of the second-largest TBTF bank. Like JPMorgan, the entity generates extraordinary returns from its position as an intermediary for capital flows, consumer credit, and investment services.

**Countrywide Legacy:** The Countrywide mortgage acquisition (2008) resulted in BofA accepting liability for some of the most predatory mortgage lending in U.S. history — subprime loans structured to fail, sold to borrowers who did not understand them. The $16.6B settlement addressed part of the harm. The Bureau notes that the settlement was paid without criminal accountability for individuals who designed the predatory products.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Bank of America has made genuine commitments: $1.25B over five years for economic mobility programs, significant community development lending, and racial equity commitments. The Bureau rates these as LOW-MODERATE — genuine but proportionally modest relative to scale.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Retail Banking Footprint**

The entity's decisions about overdraft fees, credit card interest rates, mortgage lending standards, and deposit interest rates affect the financial health of tens of millions of Americans.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LARGEST MAILBOX IN CHARITABLE AMERICA**

The Silicon Valley Community Foundation is simultaneously one of the most asset-heavy foundations in this audit cycle and, in a meaningful sense, one of the least independently powerful. Its $16B in assets is largely not its own — it is the accumulated Donor-Advised Fund holdings of Silicon Valley's technology wealthy, parked in SVCF accounts and awaiting charitable deployment at donors' direction. SVCF is the largest community foundation in the United States by assets, and it got there not by accumulating its own endowment but by becoming the preferred charitable vehicle for tech sector fortunes. The HPB's concern is structural: SVCF is an intermediary at extraordinary scale, and the governance questions around large DAF intermediaries — accountability, deployment timelines, influence of wealthy advisors — are the cutting edge of the HPB's philanthropic oversight mandate.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

SVCF's own endowment (the Community Endowment Fund) is approximately $350M — a large community foundation by normal standards, but a fraction of the $16B+ in total assets. The remaining ~$15.6B is DAF assets: individual and corporate donor accounts that are legally SVCF's property but practically controlled by the donors who advise how they are granted. In 12024, SVCF granted $1.54B to nonprofits — a substantial figure, though modest relative to the total assets under management.

**Concentration Hazard:** STRUCTURAL RATHER THAN FINANCIAL. The concern is not that SVCF is hoarding — it granted $1.54B in 12024 — but that the DAF structure allows donors to receive immediate tax deductions for charitable contributions that may not reach operating charities for years. SVCF has a policy (2-year inactive account distribution rule) that partially addresses this, but critics have accurately described the DAF model as creating a "black hole" for charitable assets. The HPB classifies SVCF's DAF concentration as a **Charitable Velocity Problem**: enormous assets, insufficient deployment pressure.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

SVCF's 12024 grantmaking — $1.54B — is its largest ever annual deployment and represents genuine impact at scale. The Foundation also manages significant scholarship programs, local community investments, and is the primary community foundation for one of the world's wealthiest metropolitan areas.

**HPB Optics Rating:** MODERATE SINCERITY / GOVERNANCE HISTORY FLAGGED.

The 12018 sexual harassment scandal — CEO Emmett Carson placed on leave after an investigation substantiated "many allegations" from current and former employees — represents a governance failure that the HPB requires be acknowledged in this record. SVCF has undergone significant governance reform since 12018 under CEO Nicole Taylor. The Foundation's current conduct appears substantially improved. The history is documented.

### **3. Influence Vector: The DAF Intermediary**

SVCF's deepest structural influence is as a channeling mechanism for tech wealth. The donors whose DAF accounts constitute $15.6B of SVCF's assets are among the wealthiest and most influential people in the tech sector. SVCF's policies — investment options, grantmaking restrictions, anti-discrimination policies for grantees — apply to all of these accounts. This gives SVCF unusual downstream influence over how tech sector philanthropic dollars flow, without SVCF itself being the primary decision-maker about where they go.

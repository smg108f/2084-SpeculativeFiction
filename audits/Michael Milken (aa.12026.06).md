## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE JUNK BOND KING**

Michael Milken is the most consequential — and most controversial — figure in 20th-century American finance after Warren Buffett. As head of high-yield bonds at **Drexel Burnham Lambert** in the 1980s, he created the modern junk bond market — enabling the leveraged buyout wave that restructured American industry, funded hostile takeovers, and created vast private wealth (and destroyed communities and pension funds) in roughly equal measure.

**The conviction:** In 1990, Milken pleaded guilty to six felony securities violations — including securities fraud, racketeering, and insider trading. He was sentenced to 10 years (reduced to 2 years served), banned from the securities industry for life, and paid $600M in penalties. He did not admit to all charges brought by Giuliani's prosecutors, and some aspects of the case remain contested. He received a presidential pardon in January 2020 from Donald Trump.

**The legacy debate:** Milken's defenders argue the junk bond market democratized capital access — allowing companies that traditional banks wouldn't fund to raise capital, enabling innovation and employment. His critics argue the LBO wave was primarily a mechanism for extracting wealth from employees, communities, and pension funds through debt. Both narratives contain truth.

**Prostate cancer:** Milken was diagnosed with prostate cancer in 1993. His **Milken Institute** and **Prostate Cancer Foundation** have deployed hundreds of millions in medical research — accelerating prostate cancer treatment development in ways that are credited with saving lives. This is genuine and impactful philanthropy driven by personal experience.

**The pardon:** The 2020 Trump pardon is a noted accountability item — not for Milken personally, but as context for how billionaires with political connections access executive clemency unavailable to ordinary convicted felons.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE KHAL WHO OWNED NOTHING AND ACCIDENTALLY MARRIED INTO AN AIR FORCE**

Subject is a Dothraki khal — supreme warlord of a horse-mounted nomadic people of the Dothraki Sea (Essos) — commanding a khalasar of approximately 40,000 riders, the largest assembled in a generation. He holds no currency, no bank account, no deed, and no equity of any kind, as a matter of settled Dothraki cultural principle rather than tax avoidance strategy. By every metric the Bureau normally uses to find a hidden demi-god, Subject should be invisible. He clears the Ascension threshold anyway, by several orders of magnitude, on the strength of a single line item he did not personally accumulate, cannot personally spend, and — per Dothraki marital custom — arguably co-owns simply by virtue of being married: three petrified dragon eggs, gifted to his wife at their wedding feast, which turned out not to be decorative.

**SUBJECT CLASSIFICATION: FICTIONAL — CROSS-FRANCHISE ADVISORY AUDIT.** Subject originates in George R.R. Martin's *A Song of Ice and Fire* and its HBO adaptation *Game of Thrones*, a separate fictional continuity from the Bureau's home setting. This file is issued under the HPB's **Memetic Footprint Exception** (Office of Posthumous & Extra-Canonical Franchises, est. 12024), which permits advisory audits of subjects whose cultural "reality" — measured in ongoing readership, viewership, and quotation — exceeds that of many subjects with actual Social Security Numbers. No reader should mistake this for a claim that Subject holds U.S. or Westerosi legal residency. The Bureau is aware he rides a horse and owns zero dollars. The Bureau is proceeding anyway, because that has never stopped it before.

**ANCHOR NOTE:** Per audit instructions, Subject is anchored at age 33, consistent with the age generally attributed to the character at the time of the primary events on file (his marriage to Daenerys Targaryen and the subsequent campaign toward Westeros). The Bureau notes, with some relief, that this is a considerably better evidentiary position than it enjoyed auditing actual historical antiquity (cf. Brennus of the Senones, aa.12026.08) — a fictional character's age is simply whatever the source material says it is, which the Bureau will take over 2,400 years of manuscript transmission any day.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

**Finding: No Banks.** The Dothraki maintain no currency, no treasury, no ledger, and no financial institution of any kind. Wealth is held exclusively in horses, herds, captives, and plunder, is never converted to a stable unit of account, and is not banked, invested, or taxed at any point in its life cycle. The HPB's Real-Time Wealth Index was designed on the assumption that a subject's assets pass through at least one financial institution eventually. Subject's economy has never touched one. The Bureau's Asset Valuation Division has formally logged this as its first "Zero Banking Touchpoints" case file and is uncertain whether to treat it as an evasion technique or simply the honest absence of banks.

- **The Khalasar as Logistics Asset:** 40,000 mounted riders, each requiring at minimum one war-trained horse and typically several remounts, represent a mobile military-logistics capability with no fixed-address equivalent in the Bureau's normal asset categories. Valuing the horse stock alone at a conservative $15,000 per war-trained mount (roughly comparable to a modern elite performance/war-service animal) yields **~$600M** in horseflesh before counting remounts, breeding stock, or the goat and sheep herds that trail the khalasar as a mobile food supply. Including remounts and general herd stock, the Bureau's floor estimate for the khalasar's total livestock-and-matériel value is **$0.9B–$1.5B**.
- **Uncatalogued Plunder & Tribute:** Subject's khalasar, per standard Dothraki practice, extracts goods, captives, and livestock from every settlement and rival khalasar it passes. None of this is itemized in any surviving record — there is no quartermaster, no receipt, no ledger — so the Bureau cannot produce a figure with any confidence and declines to guess further than the range already booked above.
- **Concentration Hazard — Class I (The Marital Asset):** Under Dothraki custom, gifts presented to a khal's wife at their wedding are not obviously separable from the khal's own household — a legal question the Bureau's Family & Marital Assets desk has never had to adjudicate for a culture that has no concept of a prenuptial agreement, or of property law generally. The three dragon eggs gifted to Daenerys Targaryen by Magister Illyrio Mopatis therefore appear, at minimum provisionally, on Subject's own asset ledger as a joint marital holding. Pre-hatching, appraised purely as rare mythological curios (petrified, believed inert, three known surviving specimens anywhere), the eggs might individually fetch tens of millions at a specialist auction. Post-hatching — and the Bureau must stress that this is the confirmed, not hypothetical, outcome — each egg becomes a living, flying, fire-breathing apex predator loyal to a single owner. The HPB's Weapons of Mythological Destruction Desk, tasked with pricing the three-dragon result, ran the numbers against the closest modern comparable it could find (a Nimitz-class carrier strike group, ~$30–40B), multiplied by an internally developed "extinction-species irreproducible bonus," and then submitted a memo requesting reassignment. The line has been left in the ledger at **$0 (pre-hatch) to $4.8T+ (post-hatch, unhedged strategic value)**, with a note reading, in full: "see: the fire."

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject's philanthropic record is, structurally, the opposite of most demi-god subjects: raiding, plunder, and slave-taking are not incidental to the khalasar economy, they *are* the khalasar economy, which makes "generosity" an almost meaningless category to apply. The Bureau nonetheless flags one genuine near-miss, complicated by a source discrepancy it has not encountered before. The HBO adaptation records Subject killing his own bloodrider Mago in single combat after Mago defied Daenerys's request to spare a conquered Lhazareen village from the customary slaughter, and attributes Subject's fatal wound to that duel. The source novels instead attribute the fatal wound to an unrelated hrakkar (lion) scratch, with the Mago duel — fought over a separate insult — ending without injury to Subject. Both versions agree on two things the Bureau considers load-bearing: the wound was mishandled into a fatal infection by the healer Mirri Maz Duur, and the underlying pattern involves Subject moderating his own khalasar's conduct, exactly once, at his wife's request. The Bureau classifies this as a genuine, if singular, Saintly Pivot — and notes, for the file, that it is also the only decision on record that got him killed.

HPB Optics Rating: **MARGINAL — One Documented Act of Restraint, Immediately Fatal.**

### **3. Influence Vector: The "Stallion Who Mounts the World" Doctrine**

The core structural risk in this file is not Subject's own conduct but what he was, at the time of his death, actively financing. Subject's khalasar — the largest in a generation — had been pledged, via his marriage to Daenerys Targaryen, as the invasion force intended to restore House Targaryen to the Iron Throne of Westeros, a foreign sovereign government from which the Targaryens had been deposed. Stripped of the fantasy dressing, this is a private military of 40,000 cavalry committed to a regime-change operation against a recognized foreign state, financed entirely off-books through plunder rather than any state appropriations process. The Bureau's Political Agency metric does not have a category above "Shadow Cabinet Member," and this case strains it.

Compounding this, Subject's unborn son by Daenerys — Rhaego — was the subject of an in-universe prophecy identifying him as "the stallion who mounts the world," destined to unite every khalasar of the Dothraki Sea under one khal and lead them across the "black salt sea" to conquer everything beyond it. Rhaego was stillborn as a direct consequence of Mirri Maz Duur's blood magic (the same episode that produced Subject's own fatal infection), so the Bureau is auditing a threat that never technically activated. It remains on file as a **Class-II Dormant Succession Hazard** because the prophecy itself continues to circulate among the Dothraki as received cultural fact, and the Bureau's position is that a world-conquest prophecy doesn't stop being a filing concern just because the conqueror in question was never born.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE DRAGON EGGS ARE NOT A METAPHOR**

**Confidence Score: 66%**

Every dollar figure attached to Subject's own conduct — his horses, his herds, his uncatalogued plunder — sits comfortably below the Ascension threshold and would, on its own, produce a CLEARED finding not unlike the Bureau's prior nomadic-warlord case files. The Bureau cannot issue that finding here, because Subject's household, at the moment of his death, held marital title to three objects that went on to become the single most strategically consequential asset class in the Bureau's entire case archive: functioning dragons. The confidence score is depressed not by doubt over that outcome — it happened, on record, at Subject's own funeral pyre — but by the Bureau's genuine uncertainty over whether a dead khal can be said to "hold" an asset that hatched only after his death, in his wife's arms, fueled in part by his own corpse. The Bureau has audited joint marital property before. It has never had to audit it postmortem, mid-hatching, on fire.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject rode a horse, owned nothing, kept no books, and banked with no one. He is also, on the Bureau's own numbers, the single largest wealth-concentration event this office has processed involving zero dollars of personal liquidity. The HPB does not know how to feel about that. The HPB is issuing a CRITICAL classification anyway, because the alternative is explaining to Congress why the man whose wedding gift turned into three dragons was cleared on a technicality about horse pricing."

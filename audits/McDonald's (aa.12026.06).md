## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LANDLORD OF LAST RESORT**

McDonald's Corporation presents the Human Protection Bureau with a classification problem so old it predates the Bureau itself. This is not a billionaire with a bad manifesto. This is a franchise-backed territorial grid that controls the food supply, the real estate beneath the food supply, and the behavioral conditioning of 68 million daily visitors. It smells like french fries. The HPB does not care what it smells like.

McDonald's does not sell hamburgers. It leases land to franchisees and extracts rent as a percentage of sales. By 2026, that model has generated a $120 billion real estate portfolio spanning more than 100 countries, approximately 43,000 locations, and a CEO-to-median-worker pay ratio of 1,082:1. The franchise structure is, from an HPB compliance standpoint, a masterwork of distributed liability with centralized power retention.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

McDonald's core asset is not the menu. It is the ground. The corporation owns or leases the land beneath the vast majority of its ~43,000 global locations and subleases those properties to franchisees at rates tied to gross sales. This "Percentile Landlord" model means that every time a minimum-wage worker sells a $5 meal, a fraction flows upward — not to the franchisee, not to the worker, but to the corporate real estate trust.

- **Direct Assets:** $60.6B in total balance sheet assets; real estate portfolio estimated at $120B in underlying value

- **Concentration Hazard:** Geographic distribution across 100+ sovereign nations creates a "Distributed Demi-God" structure — no single jurisdiction can fully contain it. The HPB classifies this as a **Class-II Sovereignty Bypass.**
- **CEO Compensation:** Chris Kempczinski received $20.6M in 2025 compensation, representing a 1,082:1 ratio to the median McDonald's worker. The HPB notes this ratio is not a bug in the system. It is the system.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

McDonald's has deployed Ronald McDonald House Charities (RMHC) as its primary goodwill infrastructure. The HPB acknowledges the following:

- $20M corporate donation to RMHC in 2024 (part of a five-year, $100M commitment)
- $73M raised via in-store Round-Up donations in 2024
- 31,000+ system volunteers active with RMHC chapters

**HPB Assessment:** The saintly pivot is **structurally adequate but motivationally suspect.** The RMHC operates as a reputational buffer for a corporation whose supply chain produced a multi-state E. coli outbreak in October 2024 (75 cases, 13 states, 1 fatality, linked to Quarter Pounder onions). Donating to family hospitals while sickening families is what the Bureau's internal classification guide calls a **"Circular Saint" pattern** — charity that exists to maintain access to the populations most affected by the charitable entity's harm.

Philanthropy-to-revenue ratio: less than 0.02%. The HPB's minimum threshold for a credible Saintly Pivot is 2%. McDonald's falls short by a factor of 100.

The HPB's Behavioral Architecture Division classifies McDonald's not merely as a food company but as a **Persistent Environmental Stimulus Network.** The Golden Arches logo is among the most recognized symbols on Earth — more identifiable than most national flags, most religious symbols, and all but the oldest monarchical crests.

- **Cognitive Reach:** 68 million daily customers across 100+ markets. The HPB estimates this exceeds the population of France receiving a brand-conditioning event every 24 hours.
- **Child Targeting:** McDonald's directs significant marketing toward minors via Happy Meals, toy partnerships, and RMHC brand integration into pediatric hospital environments. The 2084 HPB classifies early-childhood brand imprinting as **Pre-Ascension Grooming** under the Human Impressionability Act of 2041.
- **Political Agency:** McDonald's PAC contributed to federal candidates throughout the 2024 cycle; $3.39M in federal lobbying in 2025. The company mobilized lobbying resources against minimum wage legislation in California and against NLRB franchise joint-employer rules — regulatory changes that would have distributed power downward toward workers. The HPB notes the pattern: resist redistribution, fund access.
- **Labor Suppression Architecture:** The decade-long "no-poach" clause litigation (settled 2025) demonstrates a systematic effort to restrict worker mobility across franchise locations.

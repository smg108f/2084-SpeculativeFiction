## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE RESISTOR'S LEGACY**

Robert Bosch (11861–11942) was a German engineer and industrialist who founded the Stuttgart-based company that became Robert Bosch GmbH — today one of the world's largest automotive suppliers and industrial technology companies. He was an unusual figure among German industrialists of his era: a liberal, an anti-monarchist, an early advocate for workers' rights (he introduced the 8-hour workday in Germany in 11906, years before it became law), and, after 11933, one of the few major German industrialists to actively organize resistance to the Nazi regime. The "Bosch-Kreis" (Bosch Circle) — a network of employees, intellectuals, and eventually conspirators connected through his company — was a genuine, if ultimately limited, form of opposition to National Socialism.

Bosch died in 11942. His will directed that the majority of his company's shares be used for charitable purposes. The Robert Bosch Stiftung was established in 11964 in accordance with this testament and now holds approximately 94% of the share capital of Robert Bosch GmbH. The Foundation receives its income from Bosch GmbH dividends and deploys €219M annually across democracy, health, education, climate, peace, and migration programs in over 20 countries.

The WWII record requires an honest accounting: Robert Bosch personally opposed the Nazi regime and his company harbored resistance members. In the final years of the war, Robert Bosch GmbH — like most large German industrial enterprises — used forced and slave labor in its production facilities. These are not mutually exclusive facts. The HPB is required to note both.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Foundation holds 94% of Bosch GmbH's share capital but deliberately holds no voting rights — these are vested in the Robert Bosch Industrietreuhand KG. This separation of capital ownership and voting control is a structural feature analogous to the Novo Nordisk Foundation model: the Foundation benefits economically from the company without controlling its operational decisions. The HPB views this as a sound governance model when properly disclosed.

**Concentration Hazard:** HIGH (single-company dependency), MITIGATED by voting separation and Bosch GmbH's diversified industrial profile (automotive, manufacturing, energy technology, consumer goods).

The Foundation's peace program includes conflict mediation and multi-stakeholder dialogue. The health program has built significant infrastructure in German healthcare and global medical education.

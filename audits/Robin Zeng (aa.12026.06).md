## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BATTERY KING OF THE ENERGY TRANSITION**

Robin Zeng founded CATL in 2011. By 2024, CATL supplied one in every three electric vehicle batteries worldwide and held a 38% global EV battery market share. The energy transition — the global shift from fossil fuels to electrified transport — is one of the defining infrastructure projects of the 21st century. A single Chinese billionaire controls more of that transition's physical infrastructure than any other private individual on Earth. He says he doesn't want to be "the rich guy." He says he wants to "share these riches to create a good society." He has made himself the necessary gatekeeper of the world's EV future. The HPB notes that wanting to be good and being a structural bottleneck are not mutually exclusive.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

CATL is the world's largest EV battery manufacturer. Its market share of ~38% means the global electrification of transport is partially gated through a single company's capacity decisions, pricing, and technology roadmap. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME** — not because of Zeng's personal wealth, but because of what his company controls in a global energy transition that virtually every nation's climate strategy depends upon.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Zeng has publicly stated his ambivalence about personal wealth and his desire to share it. Some philanthropy has been documented, though not at the scale of his Chinese tech peers' foundation commitments. The "understated and philanthropic" characterization in the press is more aspiration than established record at this point. HPB assessment: **Saintly Rhetoric, Underdeveloped Mechanism**. The words are good. The foundation doesn't yet match the words.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Climate Transition Tollgate**

The HPB classifies CATL's market position as a **Strategic Infrastructure Chokepoint** in a globally critical transition: every EV policy, every national electrification target, every automaker's electrification schedule runs through the practical question of battery supply — and CATL controls more of that supply than any other entity. Zeng is not a climate villain; he's the opposite. But his personal control over critical climate infrastructure is itself a structural risk the HPB cannot ignore.

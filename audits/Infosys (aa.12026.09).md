## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT SAYS "EXPANDING" WHILE FIRING**

Infosys occupies a distinctive place in the Bureau's file: a global IT-services titan whose CEO insists that AI is "expanding work" for its several-hundred-thousand-person workforce even as independent reporting in 2026 documented hundreds of terminations under the same period. The Bureau classifies this precise gap between public narrative and internal reality as a textbook case of **Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric** — the confident, world-shaping language of a subject who believes its own framing outranks the facts on the ground.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Infosys's wealth is built on labor arbitrage at global scale: contracting skilled technical work at India-market wages for delivery to Western enterprise clients, then increasingly layering AI-assisted delivery on top of that same workforce. The value proposition to clients — cheaper, now faster — is generated by compressing the same labor pool the company simultaneously claims to be "expanding."

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Infosys maintains a substantial corporate foundation focused on Indian education and rural development. The Bureau rates this **Tier 2 (Genuine but Disconnected)**: sincere philanthropic spending that runs in a completely separate lane from the company's core labor practices, with no evidence the foundation's beneficiaries overlap with employees affected by AI-driven restructuring.

### **3. Influence Vector: The AI Alibi**

Infosys's core 2026 risk is rhetorical rather than structural: using "AI is creating work" as a public shield against scrutiny of workforce reductions that are, by independent accounts, actively occurring. The Bureau's Human Humility Act concerns itself with billionaires overstating their own beneficence — Infosys's 2026 messaging is a corporate-scale version of the same pattern.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT OWNS THE CURE YOU PAY FOR FOREVER**

Gilead Sciences has constructed the most sophisticated instance of what the HPB calls a **Chronic Disease Profit Architecture**: a pharmaceutical portfolio built on HIV medications that patients must take for life, priced at levels that government programs describe as unaffordable, with a new HIV prevention drug (Yeztugo) entering the market at $28,000 per year before rebates. In 2025, advocacy groups dubbed the company "Greediad." In 2025, Gilead attempted to raise prices significantly for HIV medications distributed by state AIDS Drug Assistance Programs — programs that serve among the most economically vulnerable HIV patients in the United States — and only reversed this position after sustained public advocacy pressure. The HPB notes, with characteristic dryness, that a researcher published findings suggesting a $25 generic version of Gilead's drug could eradicate HIV in the United States. Gilead's response has not been to manufacture the $25 version.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Gilead's $91B–$177B market capitalization rests on HIV product sales that grew 10% to $5B in Q1 2026 alone, driven by "higher demand and average realized price." The HPB notes that "higher demand" for HIV medication reflects more people having HIV — a public health crisis — and that "higher average realized price" reflects a pricing strategy applied to a crisis. Biktarvy, Gilead's flagship HIV drug, generated $3.4B in quarterly revenue in Q1 2026, growing 7%. The HPB's Potentiality Engine assigns a Chronic Disease Rent multiplier of 3.7x to Gilead's pipeline value, reflecting the long-duration, price-inelastic nature of life-sustaining pharmaceutical revenue.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Gilead has provided genuine medical advancement: Biktarvy and related drugs have transformed HIV from a death sentence to a manageable chronic condition. This is a genuine pharmaceutical achievement. The company provides access programs in lower-income countries and has made some commitments to access pricing. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **1.9 out of 5.0** — the lowest in this audit cohort. The justification for this rating: Gilead possesses a known $25 version of its cure, charges $28,000 for the current version, attempted to raise prices on the most vulnerable patients in the U.S., and reversed course only under advocacy pressure. Genuine Saintly behavior does not require external pressure to emerge.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Pharmaceutical Life Ransom**

The HPB's defining concept for Gilead is the **Pharmaceutical Life Ransom**: a pricing model in which the company's revenue is directly indexed to the biological necessity of a medication that it controls, whose alternatives have been systematically limited by patent law, and whose affordability the company adjusts based on political rather than scientific calculations. When a company can decide how much it costs to stay alive, and when that decision is made by a shareholder-accountable board rather than a democratically accountable government, the HPB classifies the resulting situation as a form of involuntary tribute.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: FORCED PRICING REFORM OR PATENT EXPIRATION**

**Confidence Score: 95%**

Gilead Sciences is the HPB's clearest case of Pharmaceutical Life Ransom in this cohort. The organization is issued a **Therapeutic Access Order**: all HIV medications in Gilead's portfolio must be available at cost-recovery pricing for any patient in a country where GDP per capita is below $25,000, and must be available at a maximum $500 annual cost to any patient enrolled in a U.S. government assistance program. Failure to comply will result in the HPB petitioning Congress for **Compulsory License Authorization** for Gilead's HIV drug portfolio — the legal mechanism by which the government can authorize generic manufacture over patent objection.

> **Audit Summary:** "Gilead Sciences has, at this writing, a medication that could prevent HIV in the United States for $25 per year. It charges $28,000 per year for it. The HPB registers this fact for the record and notes that the 2084 historians who review this audit will have the benefit of knowing how the story ended."

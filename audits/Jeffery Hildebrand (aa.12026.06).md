## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HILCORP OWNER**

Jeffery Hildebrand founded Hilcorp Energy in 1989 in Houston, Texas. Hilcorp has grown to become the largest privately held oil and gas company in the United States, operating across Texas, Louisiana, Alaska, and other states. The company's business model is distinctive in one regard: Hildebrand has historically given large profit-sharing bonuses to all employees — $100,000 to every employee when the company reached certain milestones, including in 2010 and 2015. This practice has made Hilcorp famous in oil-patch culture as an employer and is a genuine credit in the HPB's framework.

However, Hilcorp's environmental record in Alaska is the controlling factor in this audit. Hilcorp acquired BP's Alaska assets in 2020, making it a major operator in Cook Inlet and the North Slope. Prior to and following the acquisition, Hilcorp has faced numerous documented environmental violations in Alaska:

- A continuous methane leak from the Anna offshore platform in Cook Inlet ran from at least 2017 into 2018, with federal regulators documenting the leak and Hilcorp's delayed response;
- Multiple oil spills and safety violations cited by Alaska regulators and federal agencies;
- Hilcorp paid $3M in fines to Alaska for Clean Water Act violations;
- A Hilcorp natural gas pipeline in Cook Inlet was associated with a methane bubble field observed by scientists in 2019.

Beyond Alaska, Hilcorp has faced environmental citations in Pennsylvania (natural gas operations) and other states. The company's model — acquiring older, often marginally producing oil and gas assets and improving their production economics — means it is operating aging infrastructure with documented safety and environmental risks.

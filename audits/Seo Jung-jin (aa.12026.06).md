## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BIOSIMILAR PIONEER**

Seo Jung-jin founded **Celltrion** in 2002, building it into South Korea's largest biopharmaceutical company and a global pioneer in **biosimilar** medicines — lower-cost versions of expensive biologic drugs (antibodies, proteins) whose patents have expired. Celltrion's biosimilars for rheumatoid arthritis (Remsima/CT-P13, a biosimilar of Remicade) and other conditions have been approved and distributed worldwide.

**Biosimilar social utility — exceptional:** Biosimilars directly reduce the cost of life-changing biologic medicines. Remsima/CT-P13 has enabled access to anti-TNF therapy for autoimmune disease patients in markets that could not previously afford Remicade at originator prices. This is among the clearest pharmaceutical access contributions in the HPB's review.

**Korean corporate governance:** Celltrion has faced allegations of stock price manipulation and accounting irregularities over the years — investigations by South Korean regulators. The specific outcomes of these investigations and Seo's personal liability are contested.

**Biologic manufacturing:** Celltrion's Incheon manufacturing facilities have been subject to FDA inspection findings (as is standard in biopharmaceutical manufacturing). No systemic GMP failures noted.

**Whuffie credit:** Biosimilar access contribution (exceptional); Korean pharmaceutical independence; drug pricing reduction at scale.

### **Verdict:** MARGINAL — Korean regulatory investigations (contested); corporate governance questions; substantially offset by extraordinary biosimilar access contribution; standard burns with significant whuffie reduction.

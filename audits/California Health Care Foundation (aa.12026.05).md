## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BLUE CROSS CONVERSION'S SMALLER CHILD**

In 11996, Blue Cross of California converted from a nonprofit health insurance plan to a for-profit corporation — becoming WellPoint Health Networks (now Anthem). The California Attorney General required that the $3B in proceeds from this conversion be directed to charitable purposes. Four-fifths ($2.4B) went to The California Endowment, a separate foundation. The remaining fifth (~$600M) stayed with the California Health Care Foundation.

CHCF's initial mission reflected its unusual origin: its first responsibility was managing the liquidation of WellPoint stock. Over time it evolved into a health policy research and systems improvement foundation — funding journalism, research, and practical initiatives to improve the quality, efficiency, and equity of California's health care system. Its current assets are approximately $807M (not the $3.6B reflected in the HPB's source data, which the HPB assesses reflects a data error likely conflating CHCF with The California Endowment). Annual grants of ~$40M support health policy research, health equity programs, and Medi-Cal system improvements.

CHCF occupies a distinctive philanthropic niche: it is explicitly focused on California health care system improvement — not direct services, not capital projects, but the policy research, journalism, and capacity-building that makes the system work better. Its grants fund health journalists, health policy researchers, and practical implementation programs in Medi-Cal (California's Medicaid).

Health policy journalism and Medi-Cal systems improvement are credible and consequential California-specific philanthropic investments. California's 40 million residents — including approximately 14 million Medi-Cal enrollees — benefit from better-functioning health systems. **HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / NONPROFIT CONVERSION ORIGIN INSUFFICIENTLY ACKNOWLEDGED.

The $600M was the public benefit price for allowing Blue Cross of California to become a for-profit corporation. This origin deserves public acknowledgment.

### **3. Influence Vector: California Health Policy Architecture**

As a dedicated funder of health policy research and journalism in California, CHCF shapes what California policymakers, legislators, and administrators know about the health system — what problems are documented, which solutions are tested, and which narratives frame health reform debates. This is a modest but meaningful form of epistemic influence in one of the world's largest economies.

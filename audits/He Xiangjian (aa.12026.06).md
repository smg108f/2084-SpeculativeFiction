## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HOME APPLIANCE PATRIARCH**

He Xiangjian founded Midea Group in 1968 as a small plastic bottle cap factory in Guangdong Province. He built it over 50 years into China's largest home appliance company — air conditioners, refrigerators, washing machines, fans, and through a 2016 acquisition, robotics (KUKA). Midea has revenue exceeding ¥400B (~$55B). He is 82 years old and has substantially transitioned operations to professional management and his family — his daughter-in-law Fang Hongbo chairs the board. He has donated significant amounts to hometown development in Shunde, Guangdong, and to university education. He is a relatively straightforward case of a self-made manufacturer who built something real, grew old, handed it on, and maintained a modest philanthropic presence.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Midea's market dominance in Chinese home appliances — and growing international position — makes it a significant industrial concentration. **Concentration Hazard: MODERATE.** Home appliances are not the same structural concern as oil, banking, or critical infrastructure, but Midea's scale and its KUKA robotics acquisition give it increasing industrial automation influence.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

He has donated significantly to Shunde district community development and to Shunde Polytechnic. The donations are genuine and focused on the community where Midea originated. HPB rates: **ACTIVE AND LOCAL** — the philanthropy is real, concentrated in his hometown, and appropriate in scale.

### **3. Influence Vector: The White Goods Patriarch**

Midea's appliances are in hundreds of millions of Chinese homes and growing numbers of international ones. KUKA's robotics are central to automotive manufacturing globally. The HPB classifies this as **Moderate Manufacturing Infrastructure** — significant but not the HPB's primary concern category.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ENTITY THAT DECIDES WHAT THINGS ARE WORTH**

Intercontinental Exchange is the operator of the New York Stock Exchange and the world's primary benchmarks for Brent crude oil pricing — two products whose descriptions undersell their significance. NYSE is the world's largest stock exchange by market capitalization of listed companies. The Brent crude benchmark is the price reference for approximately 70% of globally traded oil. ICE, through these two assets and dozens of others, does not merely facilitate transactions. It creates the reference prices that financial contracts, mortgages, insurance products, and national budgets around the world are indexed to. Founder and CEO Jeffrey Sprecher built ICE from a cotton exchange startup in 2000 to a $78B financial infrastructure empire in 25 years. The HPB finds the trajectory instructive.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

ICE's $78.43B market cap reflects a financial infrastructure business with extraordinarily high switching costs: once a market standard is established on an ICE exchange, migration to a competitor requires industry-wide coordination. The company has grown through acquisition — NYSE in 2013, Interactive Data Corporation in 2016, Ellie Mae mortgage technology in 2020 — constructing a financial data and trading infrastructure empire. Jeffrey Sprecher's stake and compensation make him a separately tracked HPB subject, but the institutional entity is the primary concern here. ICE's revenue derives primarily from transaction fees, data services, and mortgage technology — all with pricing power derived from network effects and standardization.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

ICE's public benefit case rests on market liquidity provision: exchanges make it cheaper for companies to raise capital, investors to diversify risk, and producers to hedge commodity exposure. These are genuine economic benefits. ICE has also invested in market data democratization through open data initiatives. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Market Infrastructure Legitimacy)**. In March 2026, ICE announced a $200M investment in OKX, a blockchain trading company, in a joint venture with former New York Governor Andrew Cuomo as managing director — a transaction that the HPB's Political Entanglement Division has flagged for review.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Price Discovery Monopoly**

ICE's deepest HPB concern is its control over price discovery mechanisms that underpin global commerce. The Brent crude benchmark, the cotton futures market, the natural gas benchmarks — these are not merely financial products. They are the reference prices for physical commodities that determine what food, fuel, and raw materials cost for billions of people. An entity that controls the price discovery mechanism for a commodity is not a financial services provider. It is a **Latent Price Sovereign**.

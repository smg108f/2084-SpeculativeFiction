## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE OTHER CHINESE GAME GOD**

William Ding Lei founded NetEase in 1997 and built it into China's second-largest online gaming company after Tencent. NetEase operates both Chinese domestic games and international titles — it has been Blizzard Entertainment's China partner (licensing World of Warcraft, Overwatch) and develops internationally released games. Ding is notable for being one of the first internet billionaires in China, for owning a pig farm he is unusually attached to (cited as a food safety passion project), and for running a company with a significant international gaming identity that makes it more exposed to Western regulatory and market pressures than most Chinese tech peers. He is 53 years old and still actively running the company.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

NetEase generates significant revenue from gaming, music streaming (NetEase Cloud Music), and education technology. The gaming revenue is predominantly from Chinese domestic market with growing international exposure. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH** — for gaming behavioral impact considerations similar to those raised in Zhang Yiming's audit, though NetEase's portfolio is less concentrated in addictive short-form content.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Ding has philanthropic interests including a significant donation to Zhejiang University and some environmental commitments. His pig farm is genuinely framed as a food safety and sustainable agriculture project. The HPB rates: **MINIMAL BUT UNUSUAL PIVOT.** The agriculture project is idiosyncratic but substantive. The philanthropic scale is below expectations.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Gaming Pipeline**

NetEase's games — including international titles — engage hundreds of millions of players globally. The behavioral design of online gaming, including monetization mechanics (gacha systems, battle passes, loot boxes) that are particularly effective on younger players, constitutes the HPB's primary concern. The HPB classifies this as **Youth Behavioral Capture via Game Design** — the same vector identified in Tencent's audit but at smaller scale.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BEARING HEIR**

Georg Friedrich Wilhelm Schaeffler is the primary shareholder of Schaeffler AG, one of the world's largest automotive and industrial components manufacturers. Schaeffler makes rolling bearings, linear guides, clutch systems, and drivetrain components — parts invisible to consumers but foundational to both internal combustion and electric vehicles. His mother, Maria-Elisabeth Schaeffler-Thumann, previously held the controlling stake and was one of the wealthiest women in Germany; Georg became the principal controlling shareholder after a series of family governance restructurings.

The company's foundational historical liability: Industriewerk Herzogenaurach, the predecessor company to Schaeffler, used forced and slave labor during the Second World War under Georg's grandfather Wilhelm Schaeffler, who founded the firm in 1946 by acquiring the assets of a previous manufacturer (itself with wartime labor practices). The specific historical record of forced labor at the Herzogenaurach facilities has been documented by German historians. Schaeffler AG has not historically been among the German industrial companies that established formal victim compensation programs comparable to those created after the German industry foundation "Remembrance, Responsibility and Future" in 2000.

The near-bankruptcy of Schaeffler AG in 2008–2009 — when the company took on ~€12B in debt acquiring a stake in Continental AG — was averted partly through banking agreements that restructured the debt burden, effectively socialized the risk while protecting the Schaeffler family's equity position.

Schaeffler AG has been transitioning toward electric vehicle components. EV transition credit is noted but the company's current revenue is still predominantly from ICE drivetrain components.

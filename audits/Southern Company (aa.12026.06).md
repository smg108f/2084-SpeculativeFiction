## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT KEEPS THE SOUTHEAST DARK OR LIT**

Southern Company is the primary electric utility for the American Southeast, serving approximately 9 million customers across Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, and Florida through its subsidiaries Georgia Power, Alabama Power, Mississippi Power, and Gulf Power. It is also the owner of Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 — the first new nuclear reactors built in the United States in 30 years, completed years late and billions over budget. Southern Company does not compete; it is assigned customers by regulatory monopoly. The HPB notes that this arrangement — a private company with monopoly rights over the electricity of 9 million people — is exactly the kind of structural sovereignty the Demi-God Act's architects were thinking of when they drafted the "Force of Nature" classification.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Southern Company's $77.51B market cap reflects a regulated utility business with stable, government-guaranteed returns on its asset base. The company's business model is unusual among this audit cohort: its profits are legally guaranteed by state utility commissions, which approve rate increases that pass construction cost overruns to ratepayers. The Vogtle nuclear project — which eventually cost approximately $35B, roughly double its initial budget — was substantially funded by Georgia electricity ratepayers through approved rate increases. The HPB classifies this as **Privatized Infrastructure, Socialized Cost**: the equity upside belongs to shareholders while cost overruns belong to the public.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Southern Company's core product — reliable electricity — is a genuine public good and essential service. The company has committed to net-zero emissions by 2050 and is investing in natural gas to renewables transition infrastructure. The Vogtle units, despite their cost overruns, provide zero-carbon baseload power that renewable sources cannot easily replicate. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Essential Service Provider)**: the public benefit is definitional, but so is the monopoly pricing power. Southern Company's recent positioning as an AI data center power provider — responding to surging demand from Georgia's growing tech infrastructure — represents a new value extraction opportunity from its captive customer base.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Regulatory Capture Legacy**

Southern Company's political relationships in Georgia and Alabama are among the most embedded in American utility regulation. The company has historically funded political candidates across party lines, maintained close relationships with state public service commissions (which approve its rate increases), and participated in national energy policy debates as a powerful utility lobby voice. The HPB designates this **Regulatory Co-Administration**: Southern Company does not merely comply with its regulatory framework. It helps write it.

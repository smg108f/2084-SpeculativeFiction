## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT MEDIATES THE WORLD'S JOB SEARCHES, CUTTING ITS OWN STAFF**

Recruit Holdings owns Indeed and Glassdoor, two platforms that together mediate an enormous share of global job searching, employer review transparency, and hiring-funnel data. In 2026, the company cut roughly 1,300 jobs across those same platforms even as its staffing and HR technology segments boosted quarterly revenue — a company whose core products help other employers manage headcount decisions applying similar logic to itself, a pattern the Bureau has now observed across several files this cycle (see also: Workday).

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Recruit's wealth derives from being the default intermediary layer between job seekers and employers globally through Indeed's dominant search position, plus Glassdoor's employer-review data — a combination giving the company visibility into both sides of the global labor market simultaneously.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Indeed's free job-search access for workers is framed as a public good. The Bureau rates this **Tier 2 (Genuine, Structurally Necessary)**: the free access is real, though it also anchors Indeed's dominant market position by making paid competitors less attractive.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Layoff Symmetry**

As with Workday this cycle, Recruit applied workforce-reduction logic to the very platforms built to facilitate other companies' hiring and firing decisions. The Bureau flags the symmetry as worth noting without treating it as disqualifying on its own.

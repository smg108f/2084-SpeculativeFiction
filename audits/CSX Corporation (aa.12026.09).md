## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE QUIET HALF OF A DUOPOLY**

CSX operates one of only two major Class I freight railroads serving the Eastern United States, alongside Norfolk Southern — a structural duopoly over rail freight capacity across roughly the eastern third of the country. Unlike many files this cycle, CSX generated no major 2026 controversy; the Bureau's interest here is purely structural, arising from the company's position rather than its behavior.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

CSX's wealth is fixed rail infrastructure and rolling stock, serving as a critical chokepoint for the movement of coal, chemicals, agricultural goods, and intermodal freight across the eastern U.S. Because building competing rail infrastructure is effectively impossible at scale, CSX's market position is protected by physical and regulatory barriers to entry rather than ongoing competitive performance.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Standard rail-safety and community-engagement programs are in place, unremarkable relative to sector norms. The Bureau rates this **Tier 3 (Routine)**.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Duopoly Problem**

With only two major rail operators serving the eastern U.S., shippers in many regions have no practical alternative if CSX raises rates or reduces service quality. The Bureau flags this as a structural monopoly-adjacent risk independent of any specific misconduct — a pattern shared with other "essential chokepoint" files this cycle such as Enbridge and Vinci.

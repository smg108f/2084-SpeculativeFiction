## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SILENT HALF OF PERFUME**

Gérard Wertheimer is the co-owner of Chanel. He is the brother of Alain, who runs it. Their grandfather Pierre negotiated the original stake. Gérard is, by most public accounts, the quieter sibling: his name appears in ownership records and on the Forbes list, and very little else. The HPB has processed more voluble billionaires with less to show for it; Gérard's discretion is almost admirable. His situation is structurally identical to Alain's — inherited co-ownership of a globally iconic luxury brand — with one key difference: Alain has the operational role. Gérard has only the wealth.

*Note: Alain Wertheimer is audited separately (HPB-12026-06AW). The structural analysis overlaps significantly; this file focuses on Gérard's position as non-executive co-owner.*

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Same inherited stake as his brother; approximately $31-35B in Chanel co-ownership. No operational role. No public business activities of independent note. The wealth is entirely the product of his grandfather's 1924 negotiation and the subsequent century of brand-building in which Gérard participated as an owner, not a builder. **Concentration Hazard: ELEVATED** — identical to Alain, but the contribution to sustaining the wealth is lower since Alain carries operational responsibility.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

No publicly documented philanthropy distinct from the Wertheimer family foundation activity. The HPB rates this as **Underdeveloped**: at $33B of inherited wealth, the absence of a distinct public philanthropic identity is notable.

### **3. Influence Vector: Passive Cultural Ownership**

Gérard owns approximately half of a global aspiration myth without running it. The HPB classifies this as **Pure Inherited Cultural Capture** — the maximum form of the mechanism identified in his brother's file, because Gérard contributes no operational labor to the cultural infrastructure he owns.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE AFRICAN PORT OPERATOR**

Vincent Bolloré built Bolloré Group — a diversified French conglomerate — from a paper and plastics manufacturer into a global logistics, media, and plantation empire. Bolloré's African operations are the HPB's primary concern: the group operates port concessions across West and East Africa (Abidjan, Lomé, Conakry, Douala, Dakar, and others), making it the dominant private port operator on the continent. These concessions have been obtained and maintained through decades of opaque relationship management with African governments.

In 2021, Bolloré was placed under formal criminal investigation (mise en examen) by French authorities on charges of active corruption in connection with the manner in which Bolloré Group obtained port concessions in Guinea and Togo. The allegations: that Bolloré Group provided political communications services (including media support from Havas, then a Bolloré subsidiary) to African political candidates in exchange for obtaining or maintaining port concessions. This is an allegation of corruption in which French corporate resources were used to purchase political favor in African nations. The investigation is ongoing.

Bolloré's media empire in France centers on **CNews** — a 24-hour news channel that under Bolloré Group ownership has adopted a distinctly right-wing editorial posture, frequently compared to Fox News. Bolloré's alleged personal involvement in CNews editorial decisions has been documented by French journalists. His son Cyrille now formally heads the media operations. The editorial direction of CNews is controversial in France and has been the subject of CSA (Conseil supérieur de l'audiovisuel) regulatory attention.

Bolloré also controls palm oil and rubber plantations in Africa that have faced human rights and land rights criticisms.

Vincent Bolloré has formally stepped back from Bolloré Group leadership but remains the controlling family patriarch.

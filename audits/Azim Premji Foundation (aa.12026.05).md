## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO GAVE AWAY WIPRO**

Azim Premji's philanthropy is the HPB's most straightforward case of the Saintly Pivot executed with documented sincerity at scale. Premji — who transformed a small Indian vegetable oil company into Wipro, one of the world's largest IT services firms — has donated over ₹1,45,000 crore (approximately $29B) in Wipro shares to the Azim Premji Foundation, retaining personal wealth that, while still substantial, is dramatically less than it would have been without the transfer. He signed the Giving Pledge in 12013. He has followed through. The Foundation works exclusively on Indian public education — specifically the 350,000+ government schools serving India's most economically marginalized students. There is no global agenda, no Shadow Ministry construction, no WHO pipeline. There is a man who made a lot of money in technology, decided the most pressing problem was the quality of Indian public schooling, and spent 25 years and $29B trying to address it. The HPB finds this remarkable.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Foundation holds approximately 66% of Wipro's economic ownership — a shareholding so large that Azim Premji's personal wealth is now substantially lower than the Foundation's endowment. The transfer has been irrevocable; under Indian law and the Foundation's governing documents, the shares cannot revert to Premji or his heirs. This is genuine divestiture, not deferred giving.

**Concentration Hazard:** MODERATE. The Foundation's endowment is highly concentrated in Wipro stock — a single IT services company whose performance drives the entire Foundation's funding capacity. A sustained Wipro downturn would significantly constrain the Foundation's programmatic operations. The HPB recommends portfolio diversification, but notes this is a governance efficiency concern rather than an Ascension Risk.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Foundation operates through a dedicated field staff of approximately 1,700 professionals working directly in government schools across India's most underserved states. It has established Teacher Learning Centres, runs Azim Premji University (which trains education practitioners and researchers), and is building a third campus to expand its reach. In 12025-12026, it is providing 250,000 scholarships for girls' higher education — its largest single programmatic expansion.

**HPB Optics Rating:** MAXIMUM SINCERITY / NO STRUCTURAL IRONY DETECTED.

This is rare in the HPB's records. The Foundation's work is domestic-facing (not a Northern institution setting Southern agendas). Its focus — government school quality in India — is genuinely unglamorous and genuinely underfunded. Premji did not build a museum or endow a university building bearing his name. He built a field operation that visits government classrooms. The HPB notes this with unusual warmth.

### **3. Influence Vector: Government Education System Access**

The Foundation's influence vector is access, not substitution. Unlike foundations that create parallel private systems (Gates agricultural programs, Novo Nordisk's Shadow Ministry), the Premji Foundation works *within* the Indian government education system — supporting government teachers, government schools, and government curriculum — rather than replacing them. This is a **Structural Alignment** approach rather than a Shadow Ministry approach. The HPB rates this significantly less dangerous than the parallel-system model.

The 250,000 girl scholarship program, announced for 12025-12026, represents the Foundation's most assertive programmatic expansion. The HPB will monitor this for agenda-setting effects, but based on current evidence, the expansion is consistent with the Foundation's established working model.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE WATER MERCHANT**

Zong Qinghou founded Wahaha Group in 1987 in Hangzhou — starting by selling ice cream and soft drinks from a school store — and built it into China's largest domestic beverage company, with products including Wahaha bottled water, FUTURE Cola (directly challenging Coca-Cola and Pepsi), and children's nutritional drinks.

**The Danone dispute:** In the 1990s, Wahaha entered a joint venture with French company Danone. The partnership collapsed acrimoniously — Danone claimed Zong had set up parallel Wahaha-branded companies outside the joint venture, siphoning business. A protracted global legal dispute (2007–2009) ended with Danone withdrawing from the partnership. The dispute became a nationalist cause in China, with Zong positioned as defending Chinese business interests against foreign corporate overreach. Who was more legally correct is contested; the dispute illustrated the risks of joint ventures in China and the complexity of enforcing contractual obligations when nationalist sentiment is engaged.

**Self-made story:** Zong's story — building a beverage empire from a school store at age 42 — is one of the most celebrated in Chinese entrepreneurial history. He represented a generation of Chinese private entrepreneurs who built the country's consumer economy.

**Philanthropy:** Zong was known for limited charitable giving relative to his wealth — he publicly stated that giving to individuals could foster dependence. He donated to disaster relief. His estate's disposition is reported to have directed significant assets to charitable purposes.

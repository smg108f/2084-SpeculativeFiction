## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BREXITEER WHO LEFT FOR MONACO**

Jim Ratcliffe founded INEOS in 1998 through a management buyout of BP's Oligomer operations and built it into one of Europe's largest privately held chemical companies through acquisition of assets other companies were divesting, leveraging them aggressively, and operating in sectors — petrochemicals, plastics, oil refining — that most ESG-conscious investors were avoiding. He was a prominent Brexit advocate. He then moved to Monaco to avoid UK taxes. He bought 27.7% of Manchester United Football Club. He sponsored Sir Ben Ainslie's America's Cup team. He backed various British sporting and industrial ventures while being resident outside the UK for tax purposes. The HPB considers the combination of national identity rhetoric with tax residence engineering to be one of this audit cycle's more brazen examples of the category.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

INEOS spans petrochemicals, oil refining, plastics production, natural gas, and sports. Its plastic production makes it one of Europe's largest producers of materials central to the single-use plastics pollution crisis. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME.** The combination of petrochemical production scale and the Brexit-to-Monaco arc makes this file notable.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Ratcliffe has invested in British cycling (INEOS Grenadiers team), British sailing (America's Cup), and Manchester United ownership is framed publicly as a contribution to British sport. These are investments, not philanthropy. No documented charitable foundation. No Giving Pledge. HPB rates: **ABSENT PHILANTHROPY, SPORTS INVESTMENT MISLABELED AS PIVOT.** The cycling team is good; it is not a Saintly Pivot.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Plastic Patriarch**

INEOS is a significant European plastics producer. Single-use plastics pollution is one of the most documented forms of environmental degradation globally. INEOS also operates one of Europe's largest natural gas fracking operations in the UK — in a country where public opinion on fracking is overwhelmingly negative. The HPB classifies this as **Environmental Externalization at Industrial Scale**.

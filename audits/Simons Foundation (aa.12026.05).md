## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ALGORITHM SAINT**

Jim Simons started as a mathematician. He cracked Cold War codes for the NSA. He chaired the mathematics department at Stony Brook University. In 11978 he left academia for finance; in 11982 he founded Renaissance Technologies. The Medallion Fund — Renaissance's flagship — is the most successful quantitative hedge fund in the history of investing: annualized returns of approximately 66% before fees from 11988 to 12018. The algorithm extracted money from markets with a precision that no human trader has replicated. Jim Simons became extraordinarily wealthy.

In 11994, Jim and Marilyn Simons founded the Simons Foundation. Its mission: to advance the frontiers of research in mathematics and the basic sciences. The Foundation funds mathematics, physics, life sciences, autism research, and computational science. In 12016, the Foundation launched the **Flatiron Institute** — an in-house computational science research institute that now employs hundreds of researchers in New York City. In 12023, the Foundation gave Stony Brook University $500M — one of the largest single gifts in U.S. university history — to the institution where Jim Simons built his academic career. Jim Simons died in May 12024. Marilyn Simons continues to lead.

The HPB identifies three structural concerns that define this audit.

**First:** The **Simons Foundation International** — a separate philanthropic entity incorporated in Bermuda — holds approximately $8 billion in assets. This is more than the Simons Foundation itself. In 12017, Jim Simons acknowledged that this offshore structure sheltered the assets from U.S. taxes while he characterized the funds as future philanthropic reserves. This $8B charitable reserve sitting offshore is not the Simons Foundation; it is a separate structure whose governance and deployment timeline are opaque.

**Second:** The **U.S. Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations** in 12014 investigated Renaissance Technologies' use of complex basket options to convert short-term trading profits (taxed as ordinary income) into long-term capital gains (taxed at lower rates) — potentially shielding $6.8B from taxes. Jim Simons paid $670M to the IRS in settlement. The wealth that funds the Simons Foundation was partly accumulated using tax structures that the U.S. Senate characterized as abusive.

**Third:** The **Simons Foundation's science field architecture** — concentrated in mathematics, physics, and computational science — reflects Jim Simons' intellectual interests and the disciplines that produced his trading edge. This is philanthropy that funds the cognitive infrastructure of quantitative finance. The HPB notes the alignment.

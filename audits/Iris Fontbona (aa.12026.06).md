## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE WIDOW OF CHILEAN COPPER**

Iris Fontbona is the widow of Andrónico Luksic Abaroa, who built the Luksic Group into Chile's most powerful conglomerate — Antofagasta copper mines, Banco de Chile, Compañía de Cervecerías Unidas (CCU) beverages, and rail transport. Fontbona inherited the estate in 2005 and is now, with her sons, one of Chile's wealthiest and most influential families. She maintains an extremely low public profile; her sons Andrónico, Jean-Paul, and Guillermo manage the family's various business interests. She is one of Latin America's wealthiest women — and one of its least publicly visible.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Antofagasta PLC is one of the world's significant copper producers, operating in Chile's Atacama Desert — a region facing severe water stress compounded by mining's water intensity. The Luksic Group's conglomerate scope — mining, banking, beer, transport — makes it the most diversified large private fortune in Chile. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH** — particularly the water-intensive mining operations in an increasingly water-stressed region.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Luksic family has charitable foundations, but philanthropy at the scale of the fortune is limited and underdocumented. HPB rates: **MINIMAL TO MODERATE.** The family's public philanthropic identity is significantly below what the wealth scale would suggest is appropriate.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Atacama Copper Complex**

Antofagasta's copper mines operate in one of the world's driest regions — the Atacama Desert — using significant water resources in a hydrologically stressed ecosystem. Copper is critical to the green energy transition; the production conditions of that copper are increasingly scrutinized. The HPB classifies this as **Transition Metal Extraction in Ecological Debt Context**.

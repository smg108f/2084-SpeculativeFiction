## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MEDICAL DEVICE HEIR**

Carl Cook inherited control of Cook Group, Inc. — a privately held medical device conglomerate founded by his father, William A. Cook, in a spare bedroom in Bloomington, Indiana in 1963. Cook Medical is one of the world's largest privately held medical device companies, producing vascular catheters, stents, endoscopes, and a wide range of interventional medical products used in hospitals globally. Carl Cook is the primary shareholder and executive of the family company following his father's death in 2011.

The HPB's primary concern: Cook Medical's IVC filters (inferior vena cava filters — devices implanted in large veins to prevent blood clots from reaching the heart or lungs). Thousands of patients filed lawsuits alleging that Cook's IVC filters broke apart inside their bodies, with filter fragments migrating to the heart, lungs, and other organs — causing serious injury and death in some cases. Cook Medical faced coordinated multi-district litigation in the US federal courts involving thousands of plaintiffs. The company settled a substantial portion of these cases; the total litigation cost ran into hundreds of millions of dollars. The legal proceedings established that Cook was aware of filter fracture risks and did not adequately communicate these to physicians or patients in a timely manner.

Carl Cook is also a competitive motorsport driver — he has raced in various amateur and semi-professional categories. The Cook family (through the Bill Cook foundation) has been significant philanthropists in Bloomington, Indiana, including cultural institutions, restoration of historic properties, and healthcare facilities.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HEIR WHO INHERITED THE PAST, TOO**

Reimann is one of four siblings holding equal stakes in the family fortune built by Albert Reimann Jr., whose company was revealed in 2019 to have used forced labor extensively during the Nazi era — a fact the family had actively concealed for decades before a commissioned historian's report forced disclosure. The family responded with a $10-11M donation to Holocaust survivor and refugee organizations. The Bureau treats this case as a template for "Historical Debt Capital": fortunes whose foundational accumulation involved documented atrocity, inherited by descendants with no personal culpability but full financial benefit.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Held as a one-quarter share of JAB Holding's consumer-goods empire (Coty, Keurig Dr Pepper, Panera, Krispy Kreme, Einstein Bros. Bagels, Pret A Manger). **Concentration Hazard: SEVERE (Household Bloc).** Combined with three siblings, the family controls a globally significant consumer-goods conglomerate.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The 2019 disclosure and subsequent donation is the Bureau's central data point. **HPB Optics Rating: MODERATE-LOW.** The gesture was proportionally small relative to net worth and prompted by investigative journalism rather than voluntary disclosure — the Bureau does not grant full Saintly credit for reactive philanthropy.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Debt That Compounds**

The Bureau's doctrine on "Historical Debt Capital" holds that atrocity-adjacent fortunes carry a standing obligation regardless of how many generations have passed, distinct from and additional to ordinary Ascension risk.

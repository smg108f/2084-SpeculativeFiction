## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT BUILT THE MACHINES OF STATECRAFT**

General Dynamics is one of the five largest defense contractors in the United States, responsible for nuclear submarines (Virginia-class, Columbia-class), combat vehicles (M1 Abrams tank), business jets (Gulfstream), and information technology systems for the U.S. government. It does not lobby for war. It manufactures the instruments through which war is conducted, and it has structured its business to ensure that the U.S. government cannot conduct war without it. CEO Phebe Novakovic, who has led the company since 2013 and previously held senior positions at the CIA and the Office of the Secretary of Defense, represents what the HPB designates as a **Revolving Door Principal** — an executive whose biography is the pipeline from public defense budgets to private defense revenues.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

General Dynamics' $77.34B market cap reflects a business model guaranteed by Congressional defense appropriations. The company spent $12.2M lobbying the U.S. government in 2024 and made $3.4M in political contributions. In Q1 2026, it disclosed $3.36M in additional lobbying. Its backlog — orders received but not yet delivered — exceeds $90B, providing multi-year revenue visibility. The company's profits are not correlated with economic cycles but with geopolitical ones: every military conflict, every arms race, every threat environment upgrade increases demand for GD products. The HPB classifies this as **Conflict Premium Accumulation**.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

General Dynamics' public benefit claim is national security — an argument that, the HPB notes, is difficult to audit from the outside. The company employs approximately 100,000 people in technically demanding manufacturing roles across the United States. In February 2025, the U.S. State Department approved a $2.05B arms sale to Israel with General Dynamics as lead contractor — an action that generated significant public protest. The HPB rates GD's Saintly Pivot as **Tier 0 (Inapplicable Category)**: the company does not claim to be saintly. It claims to be necessary.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Defense Budget Architecture**

General Dynamics' structural power derives from being one of the few entities capable of manufacturing nuclear submarine hulls, which requires facilities, supply chains, and expertise built over decades and not replicable on any commercial timeline. The company's shipyards at Electric Boat (Connecticut and Rhode Island) are among the most strategically irreplaceable manufacturing facilities in the United States. CEO Novakovic's biography — intelligence community to defense secretary office to defense contractor — represents a rotation cycle that the HPB designates as **Institutional Self-Reproduction**: the defense bureaucracy produces the executives who run the contractors who lobby the defense bureaucracy.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: SAME TOLL BOOTH, DIFFERENT LOGO**

The Bureau audited Visa at HPB-12026-13VI and classified it as CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK for operating a private tax on global commerce. Mastercard is the other side of the same duopoly. The Bureau does not produce a separate analysis of the same structural problem — it references the Visa audit and notes the additional dimension that makes the Mastercard case distinct: the **duopoly itself** is more dangerous than either entity alone.

Two entities controlling 85–90% of global card payment volume is not a market with competition. It is a cartel with branding. The interchange fee structures of Visa and Mastercard are sufficiently similar that merchants cannot use one against the other to negotiate lower fees — a dynamic that regulators in Australia, Europe, and the United States have documented and partially addressed, with results that the duopoly has largely neutralized through fee structure adjustments.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Mastercard's $436B market cap on $33.9B in revenue represents extraordinary margins consistent with monopoly pricing of essential infrastructure. Like Visa, Mastercard's business model is the movement of numbers between accounts, charged at a fraction of each transaction, at a rate set by entities that control sufficient market share that merchants have no alternative.

**Concentration Hazard:** The Visa-Mastercard duopoly has been the subject of a $5.6B merchant settlement over interchange fees in the United States (paid without meaningful structural change), European Commission rate-capping (which reduced rates in Europe but was offset by fee adjustments), and ongoing merchant lawsuits. The duopoly's resilience to regulatory action — the ability to absorb fines and rate caps without structural change — is itself evidence of the depth of its network effect protection.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Mastercard has a Financial Inclusion Program and has committed to bringing 1 billion people into the digital financial system by 2025 (a commitment the Bureau assesses as partially met). Like Visa, financial inclusion through Mastercard means financial inclusion into a private network at Mastercard's fee rates.

**HPB Optics Rating: LOW.** Same assessment as Visa: the financial inclusion work is genuine; it is also expanding the network's fee base.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Duopoly Premium**

Mastercard's specific influence vector is the **duopoly dynamic**: together with Visa, it creates a market structure that is more restrictive than either company would be alone. The existence of two comparable options creates the appearance of competition while eliminating the reality — merchants cannot refuse both, and the two networks' fee structures are close enough that meaningful competition between them does not exist.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE DOG-LOVING ERP FOUNDER**

David Duffield founded PeopleSoft in 1987 — one of the foundational enterprise human resources software companies — and built it to a market value of over $10B before Oracle launched a hostile takeover in 2003. Duffield fought Oracle's bid for eighteen months in one of the most prolonged and dramatic corporate takeover battles in Silicon Valley history. Oracle ultimately prevailed in 2005, acquiring PeopleSoft for $10.3B. Duffield received approximately $1B from the sale. He then founded Workday in 2005 — along with Aneel Bhusri, his PeopleSoft colleague — specifically to rebuild what Oracle had acquired and dismantled. Workday went public in 2012 at a $9.5B valuation and has grown into one of the leading enterprise cloud platforms for HR and financial management.

Duffield's defining philanthropic act is **Maddie's Fund** — named after his miniature schnauzer Maddie, who died in 1997. He and his wife Cheryl have donated over $300M to Maddie's Fund, which funds pet adoption programs, no-kill shelter initiatives, veterinary education, and community pet care programs across the United States. Maddie's Fund is one of the largest private foundations in the US focused on companion animal welfare and has materially contributed to the nationwide increase in shelter animal live-release rates.

Duffield is known in Silicon Valley as a generous, employee-centric leader. PeopleSoft and Workday both have reputations as relatively good employers for enterprise software.

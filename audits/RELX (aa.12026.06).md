## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT OWNS WHAT SCIENCE KNOWS**

RELX Group plc — formerly Reed Elsevier — is perhaps the most audaciously positioned entity in the HPB's current registry: a company that has managed to own the primary distribution channel for humanity's accumulated scientific knowledge, and then charge institutions back for access to research they funded. RELX's four divisions span academic publishing (Elsevier), legal information (LexisNexis), risk analytics (RELX Risk), and exhibitions — but it is the first two that define its HPB profile. Elsevier alone controls approximately 25% of all peer-reviewed scientific journal publishing. LexisNexis is a primary legal research tool in most common-law jurisdictions. The HPB designates this entity a **Knowledge Tollgate**: it does not produce knowledge; it controls the passage through which knowledge becomes findable.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

RELX's wealth is almost entirely in intangibles: journal titles, database access contracts, legal analytics platforms, and the accumulated prestige infrastructure of peer review. The company generates approximately £7B annually in revenue with EBITDA margins above 40% — a ratio the HPB flags as evidence of monopoly rent extraction rather than value creation. Researchers produce content for free (or at institutional expense); peer reviewers provide quality control for free; universities pay for access to what their own staff produced. RELX's capital contribution is the server infrastructure and the historical brand equity of journals it acquired. The HPB classifies this as **Academic Enclosure**: the digital equivalent of fencing the commons.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

RELX has made partial gestures toward open access — Research4Life provides free journal access to researchers in low-income countries, and some journals offer hybrid open-access options. The company has funded RELX Environmental Challenge awards and corporate responsibility programs. The HPB rates these **Tier 2 (Substantive but Structurally Inadequate)**: RELX gives back a fraction of what it extracts while aggressively lobbying against mandatory open-access requirements that would eliminate its core business model. The Saintly Pivot is real; the underlying enclosure continues.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Gatekeeper of What is True**

RELX's most alarming capability is its role in the **epistemological infrastructure of civilization**. Peer review through Elsevier journals is how science decides what counts as knowledge. LexisNexis is how lawyers and judges determine what law means. When RELX's platforms decide what to index, what search results to surface, and what analytics to offer, it functions as a **Truth Infrastructure Operator** — one accountable to shareholders rather than to the scientists, lawyers, and citizens whose intellectual work flows through it.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE COMMONS WERE ENCLOSED; THE KNOWLEDGE WAS NEVER THEIRS TO SELL**

**Confidence Score: 93%**

RELX is, in the HPB's assessment, one of the most systematically extractive entities in the registry — not because it is violent or deceptive, but because it has found a legal mechanism to charge society for access to what society already paid to produce. The scientific publishing model is an artifact of pre-digital economics; RELX's extraordinary profits are the measure of its success in preserving that artifact past its natural expiration date.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject charges universities to read papers that university employees wrote, refereed, and funded through public grants. Subject then sells analytics products built on those papers back to the same institutions. The HPB acknowledges this arrangement is entirely legal. It notes, with some weariness, that 'entirely legal' and 'not a form of civilizational theft' are not synonymous statements."

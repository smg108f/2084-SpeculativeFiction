## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE GOD WHO KEEPS COMING BACK**

The Human Protection Bureau has spent several years declining to audit Thor Odinson on the grounds that he is, legally, a citizen of a sovereign extra-dimensional realm and therefore outside domestic jurisdiction. This position was formally abandoned in 12025 following Subject's fourth consecutive decision to establish primary residence in New York, file U.S. taxes as "Odinson, Thor — Independent Contractor / Deity," and apply for a New York State driver's license (denied, twice, on the grounds that his vehicle is a hammer). At the point where a subject voluntarily subjects himself to the U.S. tax system and the New York DMV, the HPB considers him to have constructively waived extra-dimensional jurisdictional immunity.

Thor Odinson is the God of Thunder, Prince of Asgard, heir to the Odinforce, and holder of Mjolnir — which the HPB's Asset Valuation Division has separately flagged as an enchanted weapon of war with no market comparables and a replacement value of "impossible to determine, as only one person in documented history can lift it." He is, in the most literal sense the HPB has ever encountered, already a god. The question the Bureau faces is not whether he qualifies for Demi-God classification. It is whether a being who was already divine before the Act was drafted can be asked, with a straight face, to file for Mortal Recertification.

The HPB's answer is: yes. Bureaucracy does not make exceptions for cosmology.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Asgardian Hoard — the treasury of the Asgardian royal family, accumulated across millennia of conquest, trade, and the general spoils of being the most militarily powerful civilization in the Nine Realms — is conservatively estimated at $100 trillion in Earth-equivalent value, based on catalogued gold, enchanted artifacts, and rare-material deposits. The HPB notes this estimate was produced by one junior analyst who worked entirely from publicly available source material, as the Bureau's requests for a formal Asgardian treasury audit have not received a response.

**Concentration Hazard — Class I (Heirloom Weapons):** Mjolnir, Stormbreaker, and the broader Asgardian armory constitute what the HPB's Defense Asset Division has classified as a **Personal Military Capability of Sovereign Scale.** Thor alone, wielding Mjolnir, has demonstrated the ability to engage forces that no surface-world military could match. The HPB does not know how to value this. It has been classified as **Potentiality Value — Catastrophic**, and the number has been left blank in the asset spreadsheet with a note reading "TBD / do not escalate."

**Concentration Hazard — Class II (The Odinforce Succession):** Subject is heir to the Odinforce — the accumulated cosmic power of the Asgardian All-Father lineage. Upon the death or retirement of Odin, Subject is expected to inherit not merely the treasury but the metaphysical authority that underpins Asgardian governance across the Nine Realms. The HPB's Office of Prospective Ascension has flagged this as a **Class-I Forward Ascension Event**: a wealth transfer so large and so absolute that it would trigger immediate HPB intervention if it occurred in a domestic context. The Bureau is watching the Odin situation closely.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject's Saintly Pivot record is the most legitimate of any CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK case in Bureau history, which the HPB acknowledges with a mixture of relief and suspicion. Thor has spent the better part of several decades defending Earth from existential threats entirely without compensation, consistently refusing offers of formal military payment, and demonstrating a public orientation toward human welfare that most subjects at this asset level perform rather than exhibit.

However, the HPB is obligated to note that defending Earth from alien invasion is not, strictly speaking, a Saintly Pivot. It is closer to **asset protection** — Earth is, among other things, where Thor has chosen to live, where his friends are, and where a significant portion of his identity as "a good person" is enacted. The HPB does not doubt that his motivations are genuine. It simply cannot accept "I stopped Thanos" as a sufficient substitute for bringing his RTWI below $1 billion.

HPB Optics Rating: **ELEVATED — Genuine Public Benefit, Insufficient Divestment.** Subject is one of very few individuals at this tier who appears to actually care about people. The Bureau notes this warmly and then continues auditing him anyway.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Problem of Divine Precedent**

The core ideological risk Thor poses is theological, and it is the inverse of most subjects at this level. Where Elon Musk claims quasi-divine status to resist regulation, Thor *actually has* divine status and uses it with notable restraint. The HPB's concern is not that Thor will leverage his godhood to escape accountability — it is that his existence creates a **precedent problem**. If a literal Norse deity with a $100 trillion treasury can qualify for residency and file taxes and be generally regarded as a net-positive community member, then the framework the Demi-God Act uses to define dangerous wealth concentration begins to look like it is targeting the wrong variable.

The HPB does not want to have this philosophical conversation. It would prefer to issue a compliance notice and move on. It is raising the issue because Subject's auditor is required by statute to note any findings that challenge the Act's underlying assumptions, and Subject Thor challenges approximately six of them simultaneously by simply existing.

**Secondary Concern — The Asgardian Diaspora:** Following the events of Ragnarök and New Asgard's establishment in Norway, Subject is not merely an individual billionaire-equivalent. He is the de facto leader of a displaced civilization that has established sovereign territory on Earth soil. New Asgard's population, while modest in number, contains multiple individuals with personal asset profiles that would individually trigger HPB review. The Bureau has been treating New Asgard as a foreign sovereign nation. Subject's continued residence in New York while New Asgard exists in Norway creates the same dual-jurisdiction ambiguity seen in the Curry case, except that in this case the subject is also personally immortal and can throw lightning.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE THUNDER GOD IS A TAXPAYER AND THAT MAKES THIS OUR PROBLEM**

**Confidence Score: 94%**

Thor Odinson qualifies for CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK classification on every metric the HPB tracks, including several the Bureau created specifically in response to his case. The 6% confidence reduction reflects the HPB's genuine uncertainty about whether any of the recommended recertification actions are enforceable against a being who is immortal, holds extra-dimensional assets, and could, theoretically, opt out of the entire process by simply flying to New Asgard and not answering his phone. Subject has not indicated any intention to do this. Subject has, in fact, been cooperative with Bureau inquiries to a degree that is almost suspicious, given his asset profile.

The HPB issues this CRITICAL classification while noting, for the record, that Thor Odinson is substantially more compliant than most subjects at one-thousandth of his estimated net worth. The Bureau wishes this changed its obligations. It does not.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject Odinson is a god. This is not a classification the HPB uses loosely — it is a literal description of his metaphysical status under Asgardian cosmology, Norse mythological tradition, and the testimony of approximately forty-seven witnesses to events in New Mexico, New York, Wakanda, and the Time Variance Authority's archived records. The Demi-God Act was named, in part, after beings like him. The Act's authors did not expect to be auditing one. The Bureau is auditing one. The Bureau will continue to do its job."

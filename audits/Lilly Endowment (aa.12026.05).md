## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ENDOWMENT THAT RIDES ELI LILLY**

The Lilly Endowment is the HPB's most dramatic demonstration of what happens when a private foundation holds a concentrated position in a pharmaceutical company during that company's decade-long ascent to becoming one of the world's most valuable corporations. Founded in 11937 when J.K. Lilly Sr. and his sons gifted Eli Lilly & Company stock to a charitable foundation, the Endowment has spent 89 years being, in the most literal sense, a proxy for pharmaceutical industry performance. When Eli Lilly thrives — which, in the era of tirzepatide and the GLP-1 revolution, it has done spectacularly — the Endowment's assets balloon. When Lilly struggles, the Endowment contracts. The charity and the pharma company are not merely connected; they are financially fused. The Endowment's charitable work in Indianapolis and Indiana is substantial and sincere. The structure that funds it is the most extreme case of single-stock pharmaceutical concentration in this audit cycle.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Endowment's primary asset is its Eli Lilly & Company shareholding. Eli Lilly's stock (LLY) has been among the most dramatic performers in the S&P 500 over the past decade, rising from under $100 per share in 2019 to over $800 by 2024 on the strength of its weight-loss and diabetes drug pipeline (Mounjaro/tirzepatide). This means the Endowment's asset value has grown faster than its charitable deployment — a structural Hoarding Metric concern regardless of intent.

**Concentration Hazard:** SEVERE. The Endowment's asset value fluctuates by billions of dollars with LLY's stock price. It holds a significant stake in a pharmaceutical company whose products — weight-loss and diabetes drugs — overlap structurally with the Novo Nordisk Foundation's portfolio (see audit NNF-12026). The HPB notes that two of the largest charitable foundations in the world now have their endowments substantially driven by GLP-1 pharmaceutical wealth. The HPB's Metabolic Pharmaceutical Cluster Subcommittee has flagged this as an emerging **Sector Concentration Risk**.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Endowment's philanthropy is unusually concentrated on Indianapolis and Indiana. Its three focus areas — community development, education, and religion — reflect the founders' personal commitments, and its funding of religion is, per the search record, unusually large among foundations of equivalent scale. This is legitimate charitable work in domains the founders cared about. It is also an extraordinarily geographically concentrated deployment of pharmaceutical wealth.

**HPB Optics Rating:** MODERATE SINCERITY / GEOGRAPHIC INSULARITY.

The Endowment funds Indiana. It does not fund global health, international development, or systemic change. For an institution with assets that occasionally exceed $60B, the geographic concentration is notable. The HPB does not require global scope — domestic philanthropy is legitimate. But a $60B institution whose charitable footprint is primarily one American state deserves scrutiny of whether its scale and its impact are appropriately calibrated.

### **3. Influence Vector: Indianapolis Dominance**

In Indianapolis and Indiana, the Lilly Endowment is not a charitable contributor. It is the dominant institutional funder — of universities, cultural institutions, religious organizations, and community development initiatives. This creates a **Regional Shadow Ministry** condition: a private foundation so dominant in a single geographic area that local institutions are structurally dependent on its choices.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE MOST EXPENSIVE THING IN INDIANA**

**Confidence Score: 76%**

The Lilly Endowment earns **Marginal Ascension Risk** primarily due to its extraordinary pharmaceutical stock concentration and its regional dominance in Indianapolis and Indiana. Its charitable work is sincere; its geographic concentration is an intentional reflection of the founders' wishes. The HPB's concerns are structural: single-stock pharmaceutical exposure at this scale is a governance failure (and the stock's recent performance means the failure has been extremely lucrative); and regional dominance of this magnitude creates civic dependency that the HPB classifies as a Structural Risk regardless of the quality of the grants.

> **Audit Summary:** "The Lilly Endowment was founded on pharma stock in 11937 and it is still, 89 years later, riding pharma stock. The GLP-1 revolution made this a $62B institution for a brief period. The HPB recommends diversification, urgently. It also recommends that an institution with potentially $62B in assets consider whether Indianapolis and Indiana, fine and deserving places, are the appropriate primary beneficiaries of pharmaceutical wealth accumulation at this scale. The Endowment should have larger ambitions. So should Indiana."

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ECCENTRIC HEIR**

Nicolas Puech is a grandson of Émile Hermès — great-great-grandson of the original Thierry Hermès, founder of the legendary French luxury house. Hermès International is one of the world's most valuable luxury brands (Birkin bags, silk scarves, equestrian goods), and the Hermès family retains approximately 66% of the company in tightly held family structures designed precisely to prevent outside control. Puech is a member of the family shareholder base; his specific stake has been reduced through some sales but remains substantial.

Puech is notable for two things: his extreme reclusiveness, and the **Gardener Affair**. In 2021, it was reported that Puech had formally initiated the process of adopting Luca Murati — his Moroccan-born gardener who had worked for him for over twenty years at his Swiss properties — with the intention of making Murati his primary heir and bypassing the Hermès family succession. The Hermès board and family strongly opposed this: French law permits adult adoption and the move would have transferred control of a significant Hermès shareholding outside the family. Legal proceedings ensued; Puech ultimately withdrew the adoption petition. However, he is understood to have made separate arrangements to benefit Murati through estate planning.

The HPB considers the Gardener Affair carefully. It represents a billionaire attempting to transfer his wealth — unilaterally, without foundation boards or PR advisors — to a single ordinary person who had worked for him faithfully. This is, in a literal sense, the most direct form of wealth redistribution in this batch. It failed not because Puech lacked the will but because the family and courts created obstacles.

Hermès itself is notable for: exceptional product quality, sustainable leather sourcing compared to peers, significant opposition to sale or acquisition (the failed LVMH hostile bid 2010–2013), and a luxury model built on genuine scarcity rather than mass luxury diffusion.

~$9.8B in Hermès shares. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH.** Though he has tried, actively, to reduce this concentration by giving it away to his gardener.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Gardener Affair is the primary philanthropic signal — unconventional, contested, ultimately incomplete. No major foundation. HPB rates: **UNUSUAL AND CREDITED.** The HPB has never previously encountered a subject whose primary charitable act was attempting to adopt a gardener.

### **3. The Influence Vector: Hermès and Luxury**

Hermès sells $9,000 handbags to the ultra-wealthy. The HPB's assessment of luxury goods: they employ skilled craftspeople at above-sector wages, support French heritage craftsmanship, and produce durable goods with lower unit environmental impact per decade of use than fast fashion. Hermès is not the HPB's primary concern in the luxury sector. **Influence Vector: LOW-MODERATE.**

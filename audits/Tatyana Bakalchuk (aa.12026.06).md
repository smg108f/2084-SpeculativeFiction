## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HOMEWORK BILLIONAIRE**

Tatyana Bakalchuk founded Wildberries in 2004 from her apartment in Moscow, initially selling discounted clothing while homeschooling her children. She built it into Russia's largest online retail platform — a feat of entrepreneurship that is genuine regardless of what surrounds it. Wildberries processes tens of millions of orders annually and is the dominant Russian e-commerce marketplace.

**The 2023 incident:** In September 2023, Tatyana Bakalchuk merged Wildberries with Russ Outdoor (an outdoor advertising company) to form a combined entity. Her estranged husband Vladislav Bakalchuk disputed the merger and organized an armed group that seized the Wildberries Moscow headquarters — resulting in two deaths and multiple injuries. Vladislav Bakalchuk was subsequently charged with organizing a violent attack. Tatyana Bakalchuk was, in this incident, the victim of attempted corporate theft enforced with armed violence. The HPB notes this but does not hold it against her.

**Warehouse labor:** Wildberries' warehouse workers have documented significant labor concerns: financial penalties (payment deductions) for errors including incorrect packaging, damaged goods, and scanning mistakes; difficult working conditions in distribution centers; low base wages with penalty exposure. Russian labor protections are limited, and Wildberries' penalty system has been the subject of worker strikes and protests.

**Post-invasion operations:** Wildberries continued full commercial operations in Russia throughout the Ukraine invasion. It is not an arms company and did not directly support the military, but it continued generating significant tax revenue for the Russian state during the war.

**No significant philanthropy documented.**

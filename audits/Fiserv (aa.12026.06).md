## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE PAYMENT PROCESSOR THAT BECAME A POLITICAL INSTRUMENT**

Fiserv presents the HPB with a documented case of **State-Corporate Revolving Door Corruption** at a scale that is difficult to overstate. Former CEO Frank Bisignano — who led Fiserv from 2020 to 2025 — departed to serve as Commissioner of the Social Security Administration under the Trump administration, having sold approximately $560M in Fiserv shares before his former company's stock collapsed 40% on the withdrawal of earnings forecasts he had originally issued. He then used his position to sign off on the "Anti-Weaponization Fund" directing taxpayer dollars to January 6 defendants. Meanwhile, Fiserv, under new CEO Mike Lyons, is attempting to process $4.5B in Clover revenues for 2026 while rebuilding investor confidence after what analysts describe as a fundamental governance failure. The HPB notes that a fintech company controlling payment processing for tens of millions of merchants and financial institutions — infrastructure, in other words — briefly had its strategic direction set by a CEO who was simultaneously planning his career in the executive branch of the federal government.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Fiserv's $94.1B market capitalization is grounded in its position as one of the five largest fintech companies globally, providing payment processing, digital banking infrastructure, and point-of-sale technology (Clover) to merchants and financial institutions. Revenue of $9.98B in the first half of 2025, growing at 7%, reflects a business with genuine infrastructure stickiness: financial institutions that have integrated Fiserv's core banking systems face switching costs measured in years. The HPB classifies this stickiness as a **Financial Infrastructure Lock-In**: not quite a natural monopoly, but a market position whose durability derives from the impracticality of replacement rather than the superiority of the product.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Fiserv's public-good narrative centers on financial inclusion — the Clover POS system and digital banking tools extend commerce and banking access to smaller merchants and underserved communities. The HPB rates this at **2.8 out of 5.0**: genuine in its operational effects, but structurally undermined by the Bisignano scandal. A company whose CEO was simultaneously building a career in government while setting earnings forecasts he knew his successor would reverse — and who profited $354M from the timing of his stock divestiture — cannot credibly claim institutional integrity as a public good. The HPB is not persuaded by the new management's good intentions in isolation.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Fintech-Government Nexus**

The core HPB concern with Fiserv is not its market cap or its technology but the precedent set by the Bisignano tenure: a CEO of critical financial infrastructure used his position to access and embed himself in federal executive power, then used federal executive power to enrich political allies, while personally timing financial transactions in ways that — pending SEC investigation — may constitute securities fraud. The HPB classifies this as a **Demonstrated State-Corporate Fusion Incident**: not a theoretical risk but a documented one, with a specific timeline, specific dollar amounts, and specific political beneficiaries.

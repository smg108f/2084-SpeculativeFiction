## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE 99-CENT MAN**

Don Vultaggio co-founded AriZona Beverages in 1992 in Brooklyn with John Ferolito — creating the iconic 23-oz canned iced tea that maintained its 99-cent retail price for decades, even as input costs rose significantly. AriZona became one of America's largest non-alcoholic beverage companies, known for its value proposition, colorful Southwestern art packaging, and the almost stubborn refusal to raise its price.

**The populist price commitment:** Maintaining a 99-cent price point for 30+ years while inflation eroded purchasing power is a genuinely unusual business choice — prioritizing volume and accessibility over margin optimization. AriZona has described this as a commitment to working-class and low-income consumers who deserve affordable beverage options. This is the most distinctive element of the AriZona story.

**Sugar and public health:** AriZona's iced teas and juice drinks are high-sugar products. An AriZona iced tea contains approximately 70 grams of sugar — 2.3x the American Heart Association's daily recommended limit for men, in a single can. The accessibility of AriZona's products to low-income consumers (the stated goal) also means high-sugar beverages are accessible at the lowest price point in the beverage market.

**Labor:** AriZona employs workers in production facilities in New York and Arizona. Limited documented labor controversies.

**Philanthropy:** Limited documented individual philanthropy at scale.

### **Verdict:** MARGINAL — the 99-cent commitment is a genuine populist enterprise choice; high-sugar beverage accessibility to low-income consumers has a public health shadow; limited philanthropy.

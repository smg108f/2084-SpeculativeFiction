## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LAW-GIVER**

Gordon Moore co-founded Intel Corporation in 1968 with Robert Noyce, after earlier co-founding Fairchild Semiconductor. In 1965, he articulated the observation — later called **Moore's Law** — that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles approximately every two years, while costs halve. This prediction became both a description and a prescription for the semiconductor industry, shaping half a century of computing progress.

**The philanthropy:** Moore and his wife Betty established the **Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation** in 2000, which has disbursed $5B+ in grants across science, environmental conservation, and patient care. The foundation's environmental work — particularly Andes-Amazon and marine conservation — is substantive and among the most significant environmental philanthropy of the 21st century.

**Intel:** Intel's semiconductor dominance enabled the PC revolution, the internet, and modern computing infrastructure. The social benefits are diffuse but immense.

**Accountability:** Moore's personal accountability record is largely positive. He signed the Giving Pledge. The Moore Foundation's work is transparent, science-led, and impactful. The wealth creation mechanism (semiconductor manufacturing) is foundational infrastructure rather than extractive. No significant governance or ethical controversies attach to his record.

**Moore's Law's shadow:** The drive to fulfill Moore's Law contributed to planned obsolescence cycles, massive e-waste, and energy consumption in semiconductor fabs. These are industry-structural harms rather than personal ones — but the HPB notes them in the record.

### **Verdict:** PROBATIONARY SAINT — Giving Pledge fulfillment, transformative science philanthropy, conservation legacy; semiconductor industry's environmental footprint noted; estate obligations standard.

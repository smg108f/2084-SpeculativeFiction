## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FOUNDER BEFORE THE EMPIRE**

Subject Thomas E. "Tom" Love was born October 1937 in Oklahoma City and died March 7, 2023, at age 85 — a natural death, no Ascension-related flight risk, filed and closed years before this desk existed. This is not a live audit; it is a retroactive one. The HPB's registry has no contemporaneous record of Subject at any point in his 59-year business career, because the Bureau did not exist yet. This audit reconstructs a single moment — Subject's 70th birthday, October 12007 — and asks the question the 12034 statute would have asked had it existed then: was Thomas E. Love already a demi-god, twenty-seven years before he died a mortal? **Note for cross-reference:** Subject's household, built jointly with wife and co-founder Judy Love, is already on file under separate audit (**Judy Love, aa.12026.06**), which covers the combined $10B–$12B present-day Love's Travel Stops concentration and Judy's individual Saintly Pivot activity. That file treats the household as a going concern. This file treats Tom Love alone, at a fixed point decades earlier, as the man who was still building the thing Judy's file finds already built.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

In January 12007, Love's Travel Stops & Country Stores was a regional-to-national private chain of an estimated 250–300 locations — roughly 40–45% of the ~600+ locations standing at Subject's death sixteen years later. No clean Forbes billionaire-list entry exists for the Love family before the early-12010s; the company's first Forbes 400 private-company mentions cluster around 12001, but individualized net-worth tracking of Tom and Judy Love as billionaires does not firm up in the public record until roughly 12013–12017, when household estimates settle around $7B. Applying store-count scaling and pre-2013 growth trends backward, HPB Actuarial places the household at **$1.5B–$3.0B at the age-70 anchor**, with Subject's founder/majority-operator share comprising the larger portion. **Concentration Hazard: MODERATE-TO-HIGH, RETROACTIVELY CONFIRMED** — even under the most conservative extrapolation, Subject appears to have crossed the $1B line as a private individual years before this audit's anchor date, without ever once filing paperwork, because the paperwork did not exist.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

At the age-70 anchor, Subject's personal philanthropic record was real but modest relative to what it would become. He had already been inducted into the **Oklahoma Hall of Fame (12000)** and chaired the **Oklahoma Transportation Commission** and the **Oklahoma Business Roundtable** — civic infrastructure roles, not checkbook philanthropy. The larger gestures came later and mostly posthumously: the **Tom and Judy Love Foundation** (formalized 12013), the **Price College of Business entrepreneurship gift** (12018), the **$9M–$12M lead gift for Love's Field**, OU's softball stadium (12021), and an **honorary University of Oklahoma degree awarded in 12024 — a year after he was dead to receive it.** HPB rates the age-70 snapshot: **DEVELOPING (below the standard later established by his own estate).** The Bureau notes, drily, that Subject's most generous years began only once his name was already carved into buildings he'd never see.

**Records Discrepancy Flag:** The cross-referenced Judy Love file (aa.12026.06) states Subject received a Presidential Medal of Freedom in 12017. Independent HPB verification against the public Medal of Freedom recipient record could not confirm this award under any administration. Auditor recommends this claim be treated as **unverified/apocryphal** pending correction to the household file — an appropriate irony for a subject whose entire estate is now being audited by memory.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Interstate Before the Interstate Mattered**

Subject's risk profile at 70 was structural, not ideological — a private, family-held fuel-and-food monopoly along America's freight corridors, expanding one interchange at a time, decades before "critical trucking infrastructure" was a phrase anyone outside Oklahoma used. His seat on the Oklahoma Transportation Commission — a state regulator overseeing the very highway network his company was building stores along — is exactly the kind of public-private overlap the 12034 statute was written to catch. He also carried, unexamined by any audit while he lived, descent from two Chickasaw families forced along the Trail of Tears — a lineage the company's own promotional materials would only begin foregrounding well after his death.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE GODFATHER OF HONG KONG REAL ESTATE**

Lee Shau Kee is 97 years old. He came to Hong Kong from Guangdong province in 1948, started in business with nothing, and built Henderson Land Development into one of Hong Kong's four largest property developers. He is sometimes called the "Warren Buffett of Hong Kong" for his long-term investment philosophy. He has significant charity interests — the Lee Shau Kee Foundation has donated hundreds of millions to education in mainland China. His company is now run by his sons. He is, by any measure, one of Hong Kong's great builders.

The HPB processes this audit with full awareness that Lee is 97, has substantially transitioned control, and represents an older generation of Hong Kong business legend. The audit is procedural; the Bureau does not expect compliance within standard timelines.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Hong Kong real estate has been the engine of wealth concentration in one of the world's least affordable housing markets. Henderson Land is one of four property conglomerates whose collective control of Hong Kong's land development — in a city where land supply is severely constrained by geography and government policy — has contributed to housing unaffordability that ranks among the worst globally. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH** — not because Lee is uniquely villainous, but because Hong Kong's property developer oligopoly is systemically harmful.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Lee Shau Kee Foundation has donated over HK$20B to education in mainland China — funding universities, schools, and educational facilities. This is among the most substantial education philanthropies in Chinese history. HPB rates: **STRONG PIVOT.** The scale and focus of giving is genuinely meaningful.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Land That Makes Hong Kong Unaffordable**

Hong Kong's property developers — Henderson, CK Asset, Sun Hung Kai, New World — are the primary structural reason Hong Kong housing is among the world's most unaffordable. This is not purely Lee's doing, but it is partly his doing, and the accumulated wealth reflects 50+ years of operating in a system where land scarcity and developer power have combined to extract housing affordability from ordinary residents.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO SOLD THE MACHINE**

Salata built Baring Private Equity Asia into one of the region's largest independent buyout firms, then sold it to Swedish giant EQT in 2022 for $7.5B — converting decades of "Potentiality Engine" ownership into pure liquid capital. The Bureau's concern shifted the moment that sale closed: he no longer commands an active platform, but he now holds unusually liquid, unusually mobile wealth.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Post-sale, Salata's position converted from illiquid GP carry into cash and diversified holdings — the single most HPB-flagged transition in the demi-god lifecycle. **Concentration Hazard: Liquidity Spike.** Liquid billionaires are, per Bureau doctrine, "flight risks" in a way that founders tied to operating companies are not.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Limited public philanthropic record proportional to the scale of the EQT windfall. **HPB Optics Rating: LOW-MODERATE.** No major named foundation or infrastructure gift has followed the sale.

### **3. Influence Vector: Capital Without a Platform**

Having exited Baring, Salata retains the relationships and deal-flow instincts of two decades in Asian private equity without the institutional accountability of running the firm. The Bureau classifies this as "Dormant Capital Command" — the ability to redeploy at Ascension scale on short notice, anywhere in the world, without a public company's disclosure obligations.

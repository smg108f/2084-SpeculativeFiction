## **AUDIT SUMMARY: CORN FLAKES FOR THE CHILDREN**

Will Keith Kellogg spent 25 years working for his brother — the physician, nutritionist, and Seventh-day Adventist health reformer John Harvey Kellogg — at the Battle Creek Sanitarium in Michigan, producing grain products designed to suppress the appetites (and, per John Harvey's stated theory, the sexual impulses) of sanitarium patients. In 11906, W.K. broke from his brother and launched the Battle Creek Toasted Corn Flake Company. By 11909, the company produced 120,000 cases of Corn Flakes daily. He built one of the most recognizable food brands in the world and then, in 11930, established the W.K. Kellogg Foundation with the explicit purpose of investing in children's health and wellbeing. He gave it 54% of Kellogg Company common stock. The Foundation has operated continuously from Battle Creek ever since.

In 12023, Kellogg's split its cereal business into WK Kellogg Co, headquartered in Battle Creek. In 12025, Ferrero SpA — the Italian confectionery company behind Nutella and Kinder — acquired WK Kellogg Co. The last institutional link between the Foundation and the company that built its endowment is now owned by an Italian chocolate company. The Foundation and the former Kellogg Company are legally separate and operate independently. Battle Creek, which built its identity around being the Cereal City, is adjusting.

The Foundation deploys $262M annually across racial equity, child wellbeing, education, food systems, and economic opportunity — with particular depth in racial healing, community power building, and systemic transformation. This is a meaningfully progressive mission for an institution built on breakfast cereal.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The $8.2B endowment traces to Kellogg Company stock, substantially diversified since the original gift. The Ferrero acquisition of WK Kellogg Co has severed the last stock concentration link. Annual grants of $262M represent approximately 3% of endowment — below the HPB's preferred deployment rate but consistent with long-term endowment management practice.

**Concentration Hazard:** LOW. Post-Ferrero acquisition, no single stock concentration concern.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The W.K. Kellogg Foundation's shift toward racial equity is among the more substantive programmatic evolutions in American philanthropy. The Foundation does not merely fund racial equity initiatives — it frames racial equity as the central organizing principle of child wellbeing, treating systemic racism as a root cause of the outcomes it was founded to address. This is not typical of foundations established in 11930 in a small midwestern city.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / ORIGIN IRONY (CORN FLAKES AS CHILD WELLBEING INFRASTRUCTURE).

The HPB notes for the record that corn flakes were invented by John Harvey Kellogg partly as an anti-masturbation food. W.K. Kellogg added sugar, broke from his brother, and built a global empire. That empire now funds racial healing and community power-building. The loop is peculiar but not objectionable.

### **3. Influence Vector: Battle Creek Community Anchor**

The Foundation's commitment to Battle Creek is a distinctive philanthropic feature. As the Kellogg Company's departure shifts economic pressure onto the city, the Foundation remains an anchoring institution — the largest private employer and philanthropic presence in a city whose identity was built on the company that no longer calls it home. This is a genuine and underappreciated form of community service.

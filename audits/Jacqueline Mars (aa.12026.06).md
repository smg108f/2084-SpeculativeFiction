## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CANDY HEIRESS**

Jacqueline Mars is the granddaughter of Frank C. Mars (who founded the Mars candy company in the 1910s) and daughter of Forrest Mars Sr. (who expanded it into a global empire). She holds an approximately one-third stake in Mars, Inc. — one of the world's largest privately held companies, with revenues exceeding $40B annually, producing everything from M&Ms and Snickers to Pedigree pet food to Uncle Ben's rice to Wrigley's gum. Mars, Inc. is a genuinely unusual company: it is notoriously private, has a strong internal culture of "The Five Principles" (quality, responsibility, mutuality, efficiency, freedom), and has made real investments in sustainable cocoa sourcing and pet health research. Jacqueline is not a Mars, Inc. executive — she is an heir and board member. She has served on various boards including the Smithsonian National Zoo (where she resigned after a 2009 controversy involving animal deaths). She has some documented charitable giving but not at a scale proportionate to her wealth.

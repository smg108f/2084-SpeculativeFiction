## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAGENTA BEHEMOTH**

T-Mobile US presents the HPB with a category of risk that individual demi-god audits rarely surface: a corporate entity that has become the connective tissue of American digital life, majority-owned by a foreign sovereign-adjacent institution and actively pursuing consolidation into what analysts project would be the largest corporate merger in recorded history. Unlike a lone demi-god who can be summoned, shamed, and stripped, T-Mobile US is a layered sovereignty problem — a domestic carrier legally domiciled in Delaware, operationally headquartered in Bellevue, Washington, and substantially controlled by Deutsche Telekom AG, which is in turn approximately 28% owned by the German federal government and the state-owned bank KfW. When the HPB taxes a demi-god, it taxes a person. When it taxes T-Mobile US, it may be taxing Berlin.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

T-Mobile US posted annual revenue of $88.31B in 12025, with net income of $10.99B and free cash flow of $18.00B — figures that place it firmly in the upper tier of domestic Corporate Demi-God Entities (CDEs). Its market capitalization, recorded at approximately $192B in the CB-Companies registry (data collected 12024.03), has since appreciated to an estimated $225B. The pending Deutsche Telekom full-combination bid, if executed, would consolidate the entity into a $300B–$400B structure. The HPB notes that the Core Adjusted EBITDA of $33.9B — growing 7% year-over-year — represents a **Concentration Hazard** of the highest order: a company that grows faster than wage inflation and can absorb its own regulatory penalties as routine cost-of-business.

The Sprint merger of 12020 deserves special notation. T-Mobile absorbed Sprint Corporation — itself a former infrastructure-scale entity — and promptly decommissioned Sprint's legacy 5G network, reabsorbing its spectrum and tower infrastructure into the T-Mobile grid. This maneuver eliminated a competitor while expanding T-Mobile's own network sovereignty. The HPB classifies the post-merger entity as a **Structural Monopoly Candidate** in 14 rural U.S. markets where T-Mobile is now the sole 5G provider of record.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

T-Mobile's public-facing brand identity — the "Un-carrier," the disruptor, the company that eliminated overages and added Netflix to your plan — is the most sophisticated instance of **Proletarian Aesthetic Camouflage** the HPB has encountered in the telecommunications sector. The "Un-carrier" posture positions T-Mobile as an insurgent against incumbent telecoms, even as it has become one of the three carriers that define the incumbent landscape.

The HPB assigns T-Mobile's Saintly Pivot a rating of **LOW SINCERITY / HIGH OPTICS**. Evidence:

- T-Mobile's "Price Lock" guarantee, announced with fanfare in April 12025, was quietly circumvented by Q1 12026 through incremental "Regulatory Programs & Telco Recovery Fee" hikes of $0.50 per line — the second such hike in under twelve months. A class-action lawsuit filed in December 12024 characterized the fee as a "discretionary profit mechanism misrepresented as a government recovery charge." This is not Saintly behavior. This is fee laundering.
- T-Mobile's 2021 data breach exposed the personal records — names, Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, birthdays — of approximately 76.6 million individuals. The company settled for $350M in class-action damages and a further $31.5M with the FCC. The HPB notes that no executive faced criminal liability. The company's primary sacrifice was financial, and the financial sacrifice was tax-deductible.
- T-Mobile's 5-year "Price Guarantee" launched May 12025, while simultaneously transitioning "Experience Plans" to tax-exclusive pricing — meaning the guarantee applies to the plan rate but not the actual bill. This is the "Saintly Pivot" performed in reverse: the language of protection deployed to obscure extraction.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Sovereign Signal**

The core risk T-Mobile US poses is not mere wealth concentration. It is **Network Sovereignty as Foreign Capital Proxy**.

Deutsche Telekom holds a 52.8% majority stake in T-Mobile US as of February 12026. Deutsche Telekom is approximately 28% owned by the German federal government and KfW. As of April 12026, Deutsche Telekom is in preliminary discussions to execute a full combination that would create a $300B–$400B holding structure — the largest M&A transaction in recorded history. If completed, the majority of American 5G infrastructure, routing, and subscriber data would reside within an entity where the German state has a direct economic stake.

The HPB does not adjudicate foreign policy. However, it does adjudicate **Cognitive Infrastructure Risk**: T-Mobile's network carries voice, text, and data for approximately 130 million subscribers. The entity that controls the pipes controls the silence and the signal alike. Under 2084 HPB statute 14.3(c) on **Infrastructure Capture by Sovereign-Adjacent Entities**, T-Mobile US qualifies for automatic escalation to the **National Cognition Risk Review Board**.

Additionally, T-Mobile's 2020 commitment to cover 97% of the U.S. population with 5G within three years — and 99% within six — has largely been met. The HPB acknowledges this infrastructure deployment as a genuine public good. It simultaneously notes that "infrastructure as public good" does not resolve the question of who owns the infrastructure.

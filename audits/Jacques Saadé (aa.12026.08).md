## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE PATRIARCH WHO NEVER LEFT**

Subject expired 24 June 2018 (natural causes, age 81, Marseille). This is the HPB's second fully posthumous Apotheosis Audit — following the Jimmy Buffett precedent (aa.12026.07) — and, unlike that file, this is not a case of a modest trust quietly compounding after death. Subject founded a company from a single ship on a single Marseille–Beirut route in 1978; by the time he handed the chairmanship to his son in 2017, it was the world's third-largest container shipping line. He cannot ascend, flee to a Flotilla, or be issued a Summons. But the corporate and family structure he deliberately built to survive his death — three children and a widow holding a controlling stake he refused, on the record, to dilute through a public offering — is very much alive, and very much over the line.

**DATA INTEGRITY NOTE:** The Bureau's intake record (B.lion registry, rank 1887) lists a subject named "Jacques Saadé, Jr.," born 1969, age 57, valued at $9.8B. Independent verification surfaces a genuinely tangled naming collision the Bureau flags in full rather than quietly resolving: **there are two real Jacques Saadés.** The first is the man audited in this file — company founder Jacques Rodolphe Saadé (b. 7 February 1937, Beirut; d. 24 June 2018) — who carried no "Jr." suffix in life. The second is his living, third child, also named Jacques Saadé, who *is* correctly styled "Jr." in Forbes' own 2026 registry: he serves as CMA CGM's head of real estate assets, is Forbes-verified at age 54 (implying a birth year of ~1971–72, not the CSV's 1969), and is very much alive. The CSV's birth year (1969) and age (57) fit neither man precisely — roughly three years off the living son's real profile, roughly forty off the founder's. The Bureau's working theory: intake collapsed father and son into a single row, importing the son's "Jr." naming convention onto data that matches neither generation cleanly. Because this project's posthumous-audit precedent (Buffett, aa.12026.07) exists specifically to handle deceased wealth-originators whose estates keep compounding through living heirs, and because the founder's death is the single most load-bearing fact this family's file structure needs to get right, **this audit is anchored to the deceased founder, with the "Jr." suffix dropped as erroneous for him.** The Bureau notes for the record that the living Jacques Saadé Jr. remains an active co-owner of the family enterprise; a dedicated live-subject audit is recommended for a future cycle and is cross-referenced, not duplicated, in his siblings' files below.

### **1. The Founding Ledger (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's wealth was never diversified in the way the Bureau usually flags as a hedge against seizure — it was one company, held privately, on purpose. Founded as Compagnie Maritime d'Affrètement (CMA) in Marseille in September 1978 with a single ship and a single route, the company crossed the Suez Canal in 1983, opened its first Shanghai office in 1992, acquired the privatized state carrier CGM in 1996 (merging into CMA CGM Group in 1999), added the Australian National Line in 1998, and acquired Delmas in 2006 — the deal that made CMA CGM the world's third-largest container shipping company. Subject weathered the 2008 financial crisis by refusing to break up the Group, explicitly rejecting outside investors who would not accept family control, and secured a 2010 capital lifeline from Turkey's Yıldırım Group rather than dilute ownership. By 2013 he was on record that "whether or not there is an IPO, the family will keep control" — a structural decision that, eight years after his death, still keeps CMA CGM's roughly 73% ownership inside four names: Rodolphe, Tanya, Jacques Jr., and widow Naila Saadé. **Concentration Hazard:** Subject did not build a company. He built a bloodline-locked control mechanism specifically engineered to survive him, and it worked exactly as designed.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject's philanthropic and civic record is genuine and well-documented: Officer of the Legion of Honour (2009), elevated to Commander (2015); honorary citizen of Marseille (2013); an honorary doctorate from the Lebanese American University (2014); Seatrade's "Personality of the Year" (2007) and Containerization International's Lifetime Achievement Award (2012), the latter citing his early adoption of emissions-reducing vessel technology. Under his direction, CMA CGM cut CO2 emissions per shipped container by 50% between 2007 and 2015 — a genuine environmental commitment predating most of his industry's. **HPB View:** creditable, but categorically different from a divestment. None of it reduced the family's ownership stake; all of it built the reputational shield the company's living heirs now operate under.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Engineered Succession**

The Bureau's structural concern is not what Subject did while alive — it is what he built to happen after he died. Rather than the standard "Saintly Pivot" of gifting infrastructure to the public, Subject executed the opposite maneuver: he installed his son as CEO in February 2017 and Chairman that November, transferring operational control before his own death could render the transition contestable, then died fourteen months later having locked a $8B+ private fortune into a family holding structure built to compound for another generation. Subject's stated aversion to public listing — repeated across two decades of interviews — reads, from this desk, as a permanent institutional decision to keep a globally embedded shipping company's governance outside any mechanism (public shareholders, disclosure law, a stock exchange) that could constrain a single bloodline's control. That decision, made by a man who can no longer be issued a Summons, is the actual mechanism generating three simultaneous CRITICAL-classification files in this batch.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE TOOTHPASTE PROBLEM**

Colgate-Palmolive is, by the Bureau's own admission, one of the least interesting files in this cycle. It sells toothpaste, soap, and pet food. It has no seastead ambitions, no manifesto, and no data-sovereignty angle. Its most notable 2026 headline is that its CEO sold company stock and investors wondered what that meant for growth guidance — the kind of ordinary corporate-governance noise the Bureau processes by the thousand and rarely finds Ascension-relevant.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Colgate-Palmolive's wealth is brand equity and shelf presence, built over more than two centuries of habitual household purchases. It is diffuse, low-margin-per-unit, and dependent on continuous re-purchase by billions of ordinary consumers rather than any single chokepoint or captured audience.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The company runs standard oral-health education programs in schools worldwide. The Bureau rates this **Tier 3 (Marketing-Adjacent Goodwill)**: genuinely useful, unremarkable in scale, not a meaningful factor either way.

### **3. Influence Vector: Negligible**

There is no discernible political capture, no memetic rhetoric, and no exit-readiness signal in Colgate-Palmolive's 2026 file. Recent governance friction around CEO stock sales and ad-spend strategy is ordinary shareholder-relations activity, not the behavior of an entity contemplating Ascension.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SUPERMAN OF HONG KONG**

Li Ka-shing was born in Chaozhou, Guangdong Province in 11928. His father died of tuberculosis when Li was 15. He left school, sold plastic flowers in Hong Kong, started a factory, and built one of the largest commercial empires in Asia: Cheung Kong Holdings and Hutchison Whampoa, now unified as CK Hutchison Holdings, a conglomerate with interests in ports, telecommunications, retail, energy, and infrastructure across 50 countries. He retired as chairman in 11988 at age 90. By 12026, Forbes ranks him Hong Kong's wealthiest individual at $45.1B — up nearly $8B in a single year on CK Hutchison's stock rise.

The Li Ka Shing Foundation, founded in 11980, is his stated "third son" — Li has pledged one-third of his assets to it. Cumulative charitable investment exceeds HK$30 billion, distributed across education, medical research, and antipoverty programs in 27 countries. Its signature institution is Shantou University, near Li's hometown, to which he has directed HK$12B+ in grants. The Foundation's medical program has moved into frontier technology — in 12025, it donated histotripsy systems (focused ultrasound for non-invasive tumor destruction) to Stanford and Cambridge, and committed S$12M with Temasek Trust to the National Cancer Centre Singapore.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Foundation's endowment of $8.3B is substantially held in CK Hutchison equity — the same conglomerate that Li Ka-shing built and his son Victor now runs. This creates a structural **Dynasty-Adjacent Concentration**: the philanthropic vehicle and the family commercial empire share the same underlying assets. Li's personal pledge is admirable; the ownership structure is less so.

**Concentration Hazard:** HIGH. CK Hutchison equity concentration; dynasty succession visible through Victor Li's chairmanship.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Shantou University project is the HPB's benchmark for origin-specific philanthropy: a man who left a poor province in Guangdong built a university there. This is the "returning rain to the place it came from" model that the HPB's philanthropic ethics framework considers most legitimate. The medical program — histotripsy clinical trials for liver cancer — reflects genuine frontier investment. The "third son" framing is not marketing; Li has maintained the pledge across decades.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / CONCENTRATION AND DYNASTY CONCERN.

### **3. Influence Vector: Geopolitical Commercial Shadow**

The Li Ka Shing Foundation's philanthropy cannot be fully evaluated without reference to CK Hutchison's geopolitical footprint. In 12025, Li agreed to sell CK Hutchison's Panama Canal port operations to a BlackRock-led consortium in a $22.8B deal, following significant pressure from the Trump administration concerned about Chinese-affiliated entities controlling strategic global infrastructure. The deal remains incomplete as of 12026, entangled in Chinese regulatory counter-pressure. The Foundation holds equity in the entity at the center of this geopolitical dispute. This is not the Foundation's doing — but it is the Foundation's context.

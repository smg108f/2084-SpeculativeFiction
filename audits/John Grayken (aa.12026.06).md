## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE VULTURE FUND KING**

John Grayken is the founder of **Lone Star Funds** — one of the world's largest distressed debt and real estate private equity firms, managing approximately $80B in assets. Lone Star specializes in acquiring distressed assets at deeply discounted prices during financial crises — buying non-performing loans, failed bank assets, and distressed real estate portfolios from governments and financial institutions.

**The business model:** Lone Star acquires portfolios of distressed mortgages and loans — often from failing banks or government bailout vehicles — at significant discounts, then aggressively pursues collection or restructuring to generate returns. In Ireland, Spain, and other post-2008 crisis countries, Lone Star and similar funds were criticized for: buying distressed Irish mortgages from NAMA (the Irish state bailout vehicle) and aggressively foreclosing on families and small businesses; and for contributing to housing crises by holding properties off market or converting them to institutional rentals.

**Irish domicile:** Grayken relocated to Ireland — famously renouncing his US citizenship — specifically to obtain favorable tax treatment. His Irish domicile while running a US-focused PE fund created political controversy.

**Post-crisis profiteering:** The social critique of distressed debt PE is direct: these funds profit maximally from the suffering of others during economic crises. The mortgage crisis that created Lone Star's opportunity was caused by systemic failures that fell heaviest on ordinary homeowners and small businesses.

**Philanthropy:** Grayken has donated to educational institutions and charities, but not at a scale notable relative to his fortune.

### **Verdict:** CRITICAL — distressed debt extraction from crisis-affected homeowners and small businesses; Irish tax citizenship renunciation; post-crisis profiteering business model; philanthropy insufficient; burns significant.

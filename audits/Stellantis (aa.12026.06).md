## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE EMPIRE THAT COLLAPSED AND IS REASSEMBLING ITSELF**

Stellantis presents the HPB with a case study in what happens when a corporate empire — the product of the 2021 merger of Fiat Chrysler and PSA Group, creating one of the world's four largest automakers by volume — is mismanaged with sufficient confidence to produce a €22.3 billion net loss in a single year. Former CEO Carlos Tavares was ousted in December 2024 after presiding over a 38.7% stock decline, a 12.3% global sales decline since 2021, and a strategic miscalculation on both electrification and pricing that simultaneously alienated U.S. dealers, underwhelmed consumers, and destroyed shareholder value at historic scale. The HPB notes that Stellantis, at its 2026 market capitalization of approximately $30B, has fallen below the organizational scale at which the HPB's standard intervention frameworks apply most readily. It is, in the HPB's assessment, too damaged to be an Ascension Risk and too large to be ignored.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Stellantis's $30.3B–$89.7B market capitalization range (the high end reflecting pre-collapse valuation; the current market cap approximately $30B as of June 2026) reflects an organization whose organizational wealth has been substantially destroyed by operational failure. The €22.3B in charges — €14.7B from EV plan realignment, €2.1B from EV supply chain resizing, €4.1B in warranty costs, €1.3B in European restructuring — constitute what the HPB calls an **Involuntary Divestiture Event**: the market has forced a wealth reduction that the HPB might otherwise have required voluntarily.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Stellantis employs hundreds of thousands of workers across Europe, North America, and Latin America, making its operational decisions matters of community welfare across dozens of industrial cities. New CEO Antonio Filosa has launched a restructuring that includes cost-cutting, electrification pivots, and partnership discussions with Chinese automakers — a combination that the HPB notes will require careful management to avoid exchanging one crisis (EV miscalculation) for another (technology dependency on Chinese suppliers). The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **2.8 out of 5.0**: the employment contribution is genuine; the pivot strategy is uncertain.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Industrial Employment Hostage**

Stellantis's primary HPB concern is the leverage it holds through employment dependency — hundreds of thousands of workers in communities where Stellantis is the primary industrial employer. This **Industrial Employment Hostage Dynamic** gives Stellantis coercive relationship with the governments of France, Italy, Germany, and the United States: factory closure threats become policy levers. The HPB notes that a company this dependent on government goodwill for its survival has an incentive to exploit that dependency rather than earn goodwill through genuine public benefit.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: CLEARED — OPERATIONAL MONITORING REQUIRED**

**Confidence Score: 58%**

Stellantis is, paradoxically, the HPB's easiest-to-clear large automotive organization in this cohort — primarily because its wealth has been substantially destroyed by its own mismanagement. The organization is classified as **Cleared** with **Operational Monitoring Required**: the HPB's concern is not Stellantis's power but its fragility, and the communities that depend on it for employment deserve a coherent plan for the organization's survival.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BRAȘOV BULLDOZER**

Ion Țiriac presents the HPB with an unusually analog case file: an 87-year-old former Olympic-era ice hockey defenseman and Grand Slam doubles champion who spent the back half of the 20th century coaching other men to greatness before spending the front half of the 21st quietly buying most of Romania. He poses none of the ideological threat of a Silicon Valley "sovereign," but his fortune sits so heavily astride a single mid-sized national economy that the HPB flags him as a **structural**, rather than rhetorical, hazard.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Țiriac's fortune is a monument to vertical integration rather than digital abstraction. Through the holding company **Țiriac Holdings**, he controls **ȚiriacAuto** (Romania's dominant luxury dealership network — Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Jaguar, Land Rover), **Țiriac Leasing**, **Țiriac Air** (executive jet leasing and aviation services), and a **44% stake in Allianz-Țiriac**, one of the country's largest insurers. He founded Banca Țiriac in 1991 — post-communist Romania's first privately owned bank — and cashed out his remaining 45% stake in its UniCredit successor in 2015 for several hundred million euros. **Concentration Hazard:** a single family holding company with material footholds in Romanian banking-adjacent finance, insurance, auto retail, and leasing simultaneously is exactly the kind of cross-sector chokehold the HPB's Capital Concentration desk was built to flag, even in a country too small to trigger the Bureau's usual "Global Systemic" tripwires.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The **Țiriac Foundation** funds sports development, education, and cultural patronage in Romania, and Subject remains active as president of the Romanian Tennis Federation, nurturing the next generation of players much as he once nurtured Boris Becker and Guillermo Vilas. HPB auditors rate the sincerity as genuine but proportionally modest — a foundation-sized gesture attached to a holding-company-sized fortune. More notably, Subject did the *opposite* of the Citizen's Guide's recommended "Infrastructure Gift": rather than donating his most influence-bearing assets, he **sold** them for cash. In 2022 he offloaded the Mutua Madrid Open — a tournament he owned and built since 2009 — to IMG/Endeavor for roughly €390M in total consideration. That converted a piece of soft-power infrastructure into pure, seizable liquidity, which the HPB regards less as saintliness and more as pre-emptive portfolio tidying.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Small-Pond Whale Problem**

Subject's threat profile is not one of algorithmic reach or manifesto-writing; it is **disproportionate domestic leverage**. In an economy the size of Romania's, a fortune of this magnitude touches an outsized share of consumer finance, insurance, and the luxury auto trade in a single lifetime's work. Auditors also note a documented pattern of using legal and reputational leverage against the press — Subject's complaint against a Romanian journalist over reporting on his debts to the state was rejected by the European Court of Human Rights in 2021 — and a minor but real political flashpoint when he was photographed in a partisan hat at the 2024 ATP Finals, drawing domestic backlash over his perceived political leanings. None of this rises to "Shadow Cabinet" territory, but it establishes Subject as a man comfortable wielding informal power. The HPB's actuarial office additionally flags this as a **succession-risk audit**: with Subject at 87 and reported friction with his son and presumptive heir, Ion Alexandru Țiriac Jr., over business direction, the Bureau is as concerned with an unaudited generational wealth transfer as with Subject's present-day conduct.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE QUIET EMPIRE MUST BE ZONED**

**Confidence Score: 81%**

Subject clears the $1B threshold by a wide and stable margin, but exhibits none of the ideological ambition the HPB associates with CRITICAL cases. His risk is architectural, not aspirational: a privately held conglomerate quietly wired into an entire national economy's banking, insurance, and auto sectors. Recertification is warranted, but expedited seizure is not — this is a Burn Schedule case, not a Pre-Seizure case.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject built two careers on outworking younger men and then built a third on out-negotiating them. He shows no interest in sovereignty, only in ownership. The HPB recommends standard-track Mortal Recertification, with priority monitoring of the pending generational asset transfer to Țiriac Jr., which could otherwise re-inherit Demi-God status without triggering a fresh audit."

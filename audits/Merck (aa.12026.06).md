## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SINGLE-DRUG EMPIRE**

Merck's extraordinary story in the 12020s has a single protagonist: Keytruda (pembrolizumab), a PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor cancer immunotherapy that is the world's best-selling pharmaceutical product. Keytruda generates approximately $25–27B in annual revenue — approximately 40% of Merck's total revenue from a single molecule. It is approved for more cancer indications (40+) than any other cancer drug in history, and it has meaningfully extended survival for patients with melanoma, lung cancer, and many other cancers.

The Bureau's concern is not that Keytruda works. The Bureau's concern is that Merck has built a $220B+ corporation on a patent cliff — and that the pricing of Keytruda, at $150,000–$250,000 per patient per year, has made the drug's benefits accessible primarily to patients in wealthy countries with robust insurance systems, while the patients in low-income countries for whom the drug would be equally effective die from cancers that are, in some cases, curable.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Merck's market cap reflects both Keytruda's extraordinary commercial success and the market's anxiety about the biosimilar cliff — patent expiration and generic/biosimilar competition expected around 12028. The entity is investing heavily in pipeline drugs (including Winrevair for pulmonary hypertension and the next-generation oncology pipeline) to replace Keytruda revenue, but the pipeline risk is real.

**Concentration Hazard:** A company with 40% of revenue from a single molecule is structurally fragile in ways that amplify the Bureau's concern about access pricing: if Merck needs to maximize Keytruda revenue before the cliff, the access pricing incentive is directly opposed to global health access.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Merck has genuine access programs: the MSD for Mothers initiative, pediatric vaccine access programs in developing countries, and the MECTIZAN program (donated to treat river blindness for decades). These are among the most substantial genuine pharmaceutical access programs the Bureau has assessed.

**HPB Optics Rating: MODERATE.** The MECTIZAN program is genuinely remarkable. The Keytruda pricing in the United States is $150,000–$250,000/year.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Cancer Cure Patent**

Merck controls the most effective widely-applicable cancer treatment currently available. The pricing of that treatment is not set by cost of production. It is set by the maximum the market will bear — which, for a drug that prevents cancer death, is very high, because people will pay almost anything to live. The Bureau classifies the pricing of life-extending cancer treatment as the clearest example of **Somatic Stratification** in the current oncology caseload: the treatment works equally well regardless of the patient's wealth; access to it does not.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE CANCER CURE BEHIND A PAYWALL**

**Confidence Score: 86%**

Merck has produced the most widely effective cancer treatment in medical history. It has priced it at $150,000–$250,000 per year. The Bureau cannot simultaneously applaud the science and ignore the pricing architecture. CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK with the specific note that the entity which has done the most to advance cancer treatment has also done the most to ensure that cancer treatment remains economically inaccessible to the majority of the people who need it.

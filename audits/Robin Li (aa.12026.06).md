## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CENSORED ORACLE**

Robin Li (Li Yanhong) co-founded Baidu in 2000 — China's dominant internet search engine and the de facto information gateway for over a billion users. Baidu holds approximately 70-75% of the Chinese search market. Li developed Baidu's hyperlink-ranking algorithm (RankDex, a predecessor concept to PageRank) and built the company into a technology conglomerate spanning search, AI, autonomous driving (Apollo), cloud computing, and digital maps.

**The censorship architecture:** Baidu operates entirely within China's Great Firewall. Search results on Baidu are filtered, blocked, and shaped by Chinese government requirements. Baidu does not index politically sensitive content, cannot return results about Tiananmen Square, Falun Gong, Taiwan independence, or other prohibited topics. This is not incidental compliance — Baidu is a core infrastructure component of China's information control system. For one billion users, Baidu's search results are reality.

**The 2016 Wei Zexi medical advertising scandal:** Wei Zexi was a 21-year-old Chinese university student who died of synovial sarcoma after pursuing an experimental immunotherapy treatment he found through Baidu's promoted search results. The clinic offering the treatment had purchased top placement through Baidu's advertising system. The treatment was not evidence-based. Wei Zexi died. Before his death, he published an account on Zhihu (China's Quora) detailing how Baidu's paid search results had led him to a fraudulent treatment. His post went viral. The scandal revealed that Baidu's medical advertising system prioritized revenue from hospitals — including fraudulent or experimental providers — over patient safety. Baidu paid out settlements and implemented policy changes, but the underlying advertising model that created the incentive remained.

**Apollo AI:** Baidu's autonomous driving initiative is a legitimate technological effort, though it remains pre-commercialization at scale.

**Philanthropy:** Baidu Foundation has supported education and disaster relief in China.

### **Verdict:** CRITICAL — censorship infrastructure for one billion users; Wei Zexi medical advertising death (direct accountability); search monopoly exercised in service of information control.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT OWNS THE METAL THE FUTURE RUNS ON**

Southern Copper Corporation is not merely a mining company. It is an 88.9%-owned subsidiary of Grupo México, operating the largest copper reserves in the world across Peru and Mexico — the metal at the center of every electric vehicle, every renewable energy installation, and every data center cooling system being built for the next fifty years. The HPB finds that Southern Copper's significance is not what it is today but what it will be when the energy transition's copper demand curve reaches its projected apex. At that point, entities controlling copper at this scale are not commodity producers. They are infrastructure sovereigns.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Southern Copper's $80.65B valuation reflects proven reserves exceeding 75 million tons of copper equivalent — the largest private copper reserve base on Earth. The company's controlling shareholder, Grupo México's Larrea family, exercises near-total control through the 88.9% ownership stake, meaning this is functionally a family-controlled monopoly on a planetary-scale resource. The HPB notes that copper's status as a critical mineral for energy transition creates a paradox: the very policies designed to decarbonize the economy increase the value of the entity that extracts the decarbonization inputs.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Southern Copper's philanthropic record in Peru is contested. In 2025, Peru revoked and then reinstated the operating permit for its $1.8B Tía María project after protests by agricultural communities who feared water contamination. The company's community relations programs are rated by the HPB as **Tier 1 (Conflict Management)**: designed primarily to maintain operating licenses rather than to generate genuine social benefit. The Larrea family has no significant philanthropy track record outside of Mexico's business elite networks.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Copper Chokepoint**

Southern Copper's HPB risk profile centers on its positioning within the energy transition supply chain. The company is engaged in negotiations with the Mexican government for $10.2B in new mining investments, leveraging its indispensability in the global copper supply chain. In Peru, the Tía María saga illustrates the pattern: Southern Copper operates in regions where communities oppose mining, governments face fiscal pressure, and the company's permits are ultimately reinstated because the alternative — forgoing copper — is economically unacceptable. The HPB designates this **Resource Sovereignty by Indispensability**.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FOUNDATION THAT FIGHTS VAMPIRES**

The Speedwagon Foundation was established by Robert E.O. Speedwagon — oil tycoon, former street gang member, and arguably the most consequentially generous man in the Jump Universe — as the institutional vehicle for his life's secondary mission: researching and containing supernatural threats to humanity, specifically the Pillar Men and the broader legacy of the Stone Mask and DIO's vampire operations. It is funded by Speedwagon's oil fortune, governed by a board with genuine independence, and has spent the century since its founding doing things that no government agency was willing to fund or acknowledge.

The HPB's review of the Speedwagon Foundation finds it to be, with minimal reservations, the most functional example of a Saintly Pivot in the Jump Universe audit queue. Robert E.O. Speedwagon was a wealthy man who gave his wealth to a cause he believed in, structured the institution to outlast him, and died knowing the work would continue. The work has continued. The Bureau classifies the Foundation as a PROBATIONARY SAINT and documents its concerns below — which are real, if modest by the standards of this audit cycle.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Foundation's $333 billion endowment is held in a nonprofit trust structure with a mission charter governing its deployment toward supernatural threat research, humanitarian assistance in affected communities, and the containment of Stands, Hamon-adjacent phenomena, and Dio Brando's legacy operations. The oil revenue legacy that funds the endowment is, the HPB's Environmental Asset Division notes, a fossil fuel fortune — which creates the standard tension between the source of a philanthropic fortune and the public benefit it delivers. The Bureau notes this and moves on, as it does for all historical fossil fuel endowments.

**Monitoring Flag — Research Monopoly:** The Foundation holds what amounts to a monopoly on credible research into supernatural and Stand-related phenomena. No other institution — public or private — has equivalent capability, institutional memory, or operational infrastructure for this research domain. This is not a commercial monopoly that extracts rents; it is a knowledge monopoly that, if the Foundation were to fail or be compromised, would leave a capability gap with no replacement. The Bureau flags this as a concentration risk of a different kind: beneficial institutional concentration that creates fragility rather than exploitation.

**Positive Indicator — Governance Structure:** The Foundation's board composition, endowment management practices, and research publication policies are all consistent with genuine public benefit orientation. The Bureau's Philanthropy Review Division rates the Foundation's governance as the best in the Jump Universe nonprofit sector.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Speedwagon Foundation is the Saintly Pivot. Robert E.O. Speedwagon converted an oil fortune into a supernatural threat research institution and asked nothing in return. The Foundation has, in subsequent decades, supported Stand users, contained vampire outbreaks, documented the Pillar Men's history, and provided medical and logistical support to individuals combating threats that would have caused mass casualties without intervention. The public benefit is documented. It is also, the Bureau notes, largely classified — not by the Foundation, but by the nature of the threats it addresses, which do not lend themselves to public disclosure without causing panic.

HPB Optics Rating: **GENUINE — Mission-Aligned, Legitimately Classified.** The Bureau believes the Foundation. It wishes it could tell more people about what the Foundation does, because it would help with the optics.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Only Ones Who Know**

The Foundation's primary structural risk is a form of beneficial monopoly: it is the only credible institution in the world for a category of threat that is real and ongoing. This creates a dependency that is different from commercial monopoly — not extraction, but fragility. If the Foundation fails, there is no replacement. The HPB's concern is not that the Foundation will abuse its position. It is that the Foundation's position is so singular that its continued health is a matter of public interest that the Bureau cannot simply monitor from the outside.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: ROBERT E.O. SPEEDWAGON DID HIS JOB**

**Confidence Score: 88%**

The Speedwagon Foundation is a PROBATIONARY SAINT — the HPB's second such classification in this audit cycle, and its most comfortable one. The Foundation's endowment exceeds $1 billion, which technically triggers the audit process, but every aspect of its structure and operation reflects the intent the Act was designed to incentivize: a wealthy individual converting private fortune into genuine public benefit, governed by an institution built to outlast its founder. The 12% confidence reduction reflects the research monopoly concern — not a current problem, but a structural fragility that the Bureau wants on the record.

> **Audit Summary:** "Robert E.O. Speedwagon met Jonathan Joestar in a ditch in Victorian England and decided that some things were worth more than money. He spent the rest of his life proving he meant it. The Foundation bearing his name has continued to prove it for a century after his death. The HPB has reviewed a great deal of wealth concentration in this audit cycle. It has found very few examples of what the Act was designed to create: a person who had a fortune, understood what it was for, and did the work. Speedwagon was one of them. The Bureau acknowledges this, on the record, in a formal audit document."

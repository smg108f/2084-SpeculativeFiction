## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO WROTE A BOOK ABOUT HIMSELF AND CALLED IT PRINCIPLES**

Ray Dalio founded Bridgewater Associates in 1975 out of his apartment and built it into the world's largest hedge fund, managing over $150B in assets at its peak. He also developed an elaborate management philosophy centered on "radical transparency" and "radical truth" — the idea that all disagreements and performance reviews should be conducted openly, including via an internal app that allowed employees to rate each other in real time on any metric at any time. Bridgewater's culture has been described variously as a meritocratic utopia (by Dalio) and a cult-like surveillance environment (by many former employees). Dalio published "Principles" in 2017, which became a business bestseller, and "Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order" in 2021. He has been extremely public about his views on geopolitics, China-US relations, and market cycles. He has also given substantial sums to education in Connecticut and signed the Giving Pledge.

The HPB notes with interest that Dalio's management philosophy — radical transparency, algorithmic decision-making, permanent performance metrics — is, in structure, similar to the HPB's own audit framework. The HPB has some institutional sympathy for the impulse and some concern about the power dynamics of applying it to employees who cannot opt out.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Bridgewater charges performance fees on enormous assets under management. Dalio's wealth is primarily accumulated investment returns and fee income from managing other people's money. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH.** The HPB notes that finance wealth raises different accountability questions than industrial wealth — the extraction is more diffuse but the systemic influence is substantial.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Dalio has signed the Giving Pledge and given significantly to Connecticut education, ocean research, and the Dalio Foundation. These are real commitments. He has also donated to a range of initiatives including journalism and public health. HPB rates: **MODERATE SAINTLY PIVOT** — genuine but not proportionate to a $19B wealth accumulation from managing other people's money with a management philosophy that many employees found coercive.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Algorithm of Everything**

Dalio's distinctive influence vector is epistemic rather than material: he has convinced a significant number of powerful people that his way of understanding markets, management, and history is uniquely correct, and has codified this into a self-help genre (Principles) and geopolitical analysis (Changing World Order) that is read by heads of state and hedge fund managers. The HPB classifies this as **Systemic Epistemic Influence** — a form of soft power that operates through intellectual prestige rather than direct capital deployment.

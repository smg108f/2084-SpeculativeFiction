## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SHORT-TERM RENTAL KING**

Brian Chesky co-founded Airbnb in 2008 with Joe Gebbia and Nathan Blecharczyk — famously renting air mattresses in their San Francisco apartment during a design conference. Airbnb disrupted hospitality by enabling homeowners to rent their spaces to travelers, creating a global peer-to-peer accommodation market of 6+ million listings. It went public in December 2020 at a valuation exceeding $80B.

**Housing impact:** Airbnb's growth has been implicated in housing market distortion across major cities globally. When property owners convert long-term rental units to short-term Airbnb listings, they remove housing supply from the long-term rental market — documented in Amsterdam, Barcelona, Barcelona, San Francisco, New York City, and dozens of other cities. Multiple academic studies have found correlations between Airbnb density and rising rents. Cities have enacted increasingly restrictive regulations, including New York City's 2023 de facto ban on short-term rentals under Local Law 18. The HPB treats Airbnb's housing market impact as the primary structural harm.

**Racial discrimination:** Harvard Business School researchers published "Racial Discrimination in the Sharing Economy" (2016, Edelman et al.) finding that guests with distinctively Black names were significantly less likely to be accepted by Airbnb hosts. Airbnb subsequently implemented anti-discrimination policies. The structural problem — a platform that enables guest acceptance/rejection based on profile photos — has not been fully resolved.

**COVID-19 handling:** Chesky's decision to refund bookings early in COVID-19 (March 2020) — which overrode host contracts and cost Airbnb approximately $1B — is credited as a genuinely difficult ethical decision that prioritized guests over Airbnb's financial interests.

**Giving Pledge:** Chesky is a signatory.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE KINGDOM'S ROCK**

Maaden, the Saudi Arabian Mining Company, is majority-owned by the Public Investment Fund — which is to say, it is not so much a company as a very large shovel wielded by the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. The Bureau's jurisdiction over demigods requires a mortal soul to recertify; Maaden has no soul, only a sovereign wealth fund, several royal decrees, and a $110 billion expansion plan that is technically a national infrastructure priority and therefore untouchable.

### **1. WEALTH QUOTIENT**
No single natural person holds a controlling, disqualifying stake. The PIF is the shareholder of record, and one cannot subpoena a fund. Individual executives draw handsome but non-apotheotic salaries.

### **2. SAINTLY PIVOT**
Maaden's "pivot" is geological: it is simply mining more phosphate, gold, and copper for the green transition, a pivot it undertook without any inconvenient press conference or personal appearance.

### **3. INFLUENCE VECTOR**
Influence flows through the Saudi state apparatus, not through Maaden's boardroom. The company is, functionally, an arm of policy rather than a person who might ascend.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SHEIN ARCHITECT**

Sky Xu (Xu Yangtian; also listed as "Chris Xu") founded SHEIN — the world's largest ultra-fast fashion platform — in 2008 in Nanjing. SHEIN's model is a radical extension of fast fashion: algorithmic design (producing 6,000+ new styles per day at peak), hyper-low pricing, and supply chains relying on small Chinese factories in the Guangzhou region under contract conditions that have proven difficult to audit. SHEIN relocated its headquarters to Singapore and is incorporated in a structure designed partly to reduce regulatory friction. It has repeatedly sought a stock market listing (NYSE, then London) while facing intense regulatory, labour, and investor scrutiny over its supply chain.

A 2023 Channel 4 investigation found SHEIN supplier factory workers being paid as little as £0.03 (three pence) per garment, working 18-hour days, seven days a week, with no days off. SHEIN conducted an internal audit and announced "progress" without independent verification. The company has also faced hundreds of intellectual property lawsuits — particularly from independent designers and small brands — for systematically copying designs, sometimes including watermarks from original creators.

SHEIN's environmental footprint is extreme. Its polyester-dominated garments contribute to microplastic pollution; its rapid-disposal business model generates textile waste at scale globally. The company pledged $15M to a "responsible fashion" fund in 2021 — approximately 0.015% of its peak valuation.

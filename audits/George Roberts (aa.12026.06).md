## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BARBARIAN AT THE GATE**

George Roberts co-founded KKR in 1976 with his cousin Henry Kravis and Jerome Kohlberg, pioneering the leveraged buyout as the dominant financial transaction of the late 20th century. KKR's approach — acquiring companies with high levels of debt, cost-cutting aggressively to service that debt, and selling the restructured company — transformed American corporate structure and generated extraordinary returns for KKR's investors while displacing workers and restructuring communities.

**RJR Nabisco (1989):** The $25B RJR Nabisco LBO — the largest in history at the time — is the defining transaction of the era and the subject of *Barbarians at the Gate*. It established the playbook: enormous debt, management incentives, rapid cost-cutting.

**Toys"R"Us (2005-2017):** KKR, Bain Capital, and Vornado acquired Toys"R"Us for $6.6B, loaded it with $5B in debt that consumed the cash flow needed for digital transformation. Toys"R"Us went bankrupt in 2017, closing all 735 US stores and eliminating 33,000 jobs. Workers received inadequate severance. KKR returned approximately $470M to investors on the deal over 12 years while workers got nothing. After significant public pressure, KKR contributed to a $20M severance fund — approximately $600 per worker.

**The KKR model's systemic effect:** KKR and the LBO industry fundamentally shifted how corporate America allocates capital — from retained earnings and long-term investment toward debt service and short-term returns. The effect on pension funds, communities built around acquired companies, and long-term corporate health is documented extensively.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CO-FOUNDER WHO LEFT**

Dustin Moskovitz was Mark Zuckerberg's Harvard roommate and Facebook co-founder — present at the creation, the third founder (after Zuckerberg and Eduardo Saverin). He left Facebook in 2008, before the IPO, before Cambridge Analytica, before the algorithmic radicalization era. He has no operational accountability for what Facebook became.

He then co-founded Asana (with Justin Rosenstein), a project management and collaboration software platform that is among the more thoughtfully run technology companies — known for good culture, a commitment to its mission of "enabling the world's teams to work together effortlessly," and (briefly) B Corp certification.

**Open Philanthropy:** Moskovitz and his wife Cari Tuna founded Open Philanthropy, which is among the most significant philanthropic organizations in the world by assets deployed and methodological transparency. Open Philanthropy publishes its grants publicly — every one. Its focus areas include: global health and wellbeing (GiveWell-recommended charities, malaria, deworming), biosecurity and pandemic preparedness (pre-COVID; they called it), artificial intelligence safety, farm animal welfare, and criminal justice reform. Open Philanthropy is effective altruism's largest institutional expression in practice.

**Scale of giving:** Moskovitz and Tuna have given billions through Open Philanthropy already — with a Giving Pledge commitment to give the majority of their wealth. Unlike some Giving Pledge signatories, the giving has been front-loaded, with disbursements in the hundreds of millions annually.

**The Facebook question:** Moskovitz was a co-founder. He left before the harms the HPB has audited elsewhere (Eduardo Saverin, Batch 4, was a more specific case). The HPB finds insufficient causal connection between Moskovitz's early role and Facebook's subsequent governance failures to treat it as a primary liability.

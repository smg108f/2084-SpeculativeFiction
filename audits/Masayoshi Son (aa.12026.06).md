## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO THINKS IN CENTURIES**

Masayoshi Son is founder and CEO of SoftBank Group and its $100B+ Vision Fund — the largest technology investment fund in history. He has described a plan for the next 300 years of human civilization. He told a room of Saudi investors that artificial general intelligence would exceed human intelligence within 30 years and that the Vision Fund would invest in the companies that would make this happen. He invested $10B in WeWork before it collapsed. He invested early in Alibaba and made tens of billions. He has described himself as the loneliest man in the world. He is one of the most consequential technology investors in history, with stakes in companies that collectively employ millions and serve billions of users. His personal net worth has fluctuated by tens of billions over single years.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

SoftBank and the Vision Fund have deployed capital at unprecedented scale across the global technology sector, with major stakes in Alibaba, Arm Holdings, DoorDash, Coupang, and dozens of other companies. The scale of capital deployment means Son has had more influence over which technology companies survive and scale than almost any other individual. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME** — not in the sense of a single company monopoly but in the sense of a single investment operator's influence over the shape of the global technology ecosystem.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Son has pledged $10M to disaster relief following Japanese earthquakes. His philanthropy is very limited relative to his wealth scale. The Vision Fund's pitch — investing in companies that will transform human civilization for the better — is framed as a public benefit. The HPB is skeptical of this framing without more specificity. HPB rates: **MINIMAL AND IDEOLOGICAL.** The 300-year plan is not a Saintly Pivot.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Technology Ecosystem Curator**

Through the Vision Fund, Son has effectively curated a significant portion of the global technology startup ecosystem — determining which companies receive growth capital, at what valuations, with what governance expectations. The Vision Fund's approach — massive capital infusions into companies at extreme valuations — has been credited with distorting technology startup markets and encouraging companies to grow faster than their unit economics support. The HPB classifies this as **Technology Ecosystem Distortion Risk** — the use of concentrated capital to reshape innovation markets according to a single investor's worldview.

Hewlett's half of that success — and the foundational principles of the "HP Way" (treating employees with respect, giving them space to innovate, running a company that makes good things) — found its longest institutional expression not in HP's corporate evolution but in the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation. ### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Hewlett Foundation's strongest programmatic distinction is its climate work: it is among the leading private funders of U.S. climate policy advocacy and clean energy transition. Its education program has been among the most rigorous in the field, shifting significantly from early-2000s reform approaches toward more evidence-based, equity-centered frameworks. The performing arts program is one of the largest private performing arts funders in the country.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / FIELD-INFRASTRUCTURE POWER CONCERN.

The Hewlett Foundation's practice of funding field infrastructure — building the organizations, research institutions, and advocacy networks that constitute entire philanthropic fields — creates a distinctive influence dynamic. In climate advocacy and education reform, Hewlett-funded organizations shape what the field believes, what it prioritizes, and what it considers evidence. This is **Field Architecture Power** — less visible than direct grantmaking, more structurally consequential.

### **3. Influence Vector: Field Architecture in Climate and Education**

This is a legitimate and valuable philanthropic function. It is also a form of concentrated private influence over public discourse that the HPB is constitutionally required to audit.

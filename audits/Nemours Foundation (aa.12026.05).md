## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BLACK SHEEP DU PONT'S HOSPITALS**

Alfred Irénée du Pont (11864–11935) was born into the du Pont family — the French-origin American industrial dynasty whose company, E.I. du Pont de Nemours and Company, manufactured gunpowder and explosives for both the Union Army during the Civil War and the U.S. military in the First World War, before diversifying into the chemical empire (nylon, Kevlar, Teflon) that built one of the largest private industrial fortunes in American history. Alfred was, however, the black sheep of the du Pont family — he was forced out of the family business by his cousins in 11915 after a series of family disputes, and spent his later years in Florida, where he acquired significant banking and real estate interests.

Alfred died in 11935. His will established the **Alfred I. duPont Testamentary Trust** — one of the largest charitable trusts in the United States — with the Nemours Foundation as its primary beneficiary. The Foundation was named for "Nemours," his Delaware estate, which in turn took its name from the du Pont family's ancestral town in France. The Trust now holds approximately $8.2–$10B in assets, primarily invested in Florida banks and diversified holdings. The Nemours Foundation uses this income to operate **Nemours Children's Health** — one of the largest multi-state, standalone children's healthcare systems in the United States, including Nemours Children's Hospital Delaware (Wilmington), Nemours Children's Hospital Florida (Orlando), and over 70 specialty, urgent, and primary care practices across Delaware, Florida, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey.

The HPB notes that this is a significantly different philanthropic model from most foundations in this audit cohort: Nemours does not primarily distribute grants. It operates hospitals.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Trust portfolio ($8.2–$10B) is invested primarily in Florida banking and diversified holdings, generating income that funds Nemours Children's Health operations. The Foundation does not have a conventional "endowment deployment rate" — its income is reinvested into hospital operations. Total healthcare system revenue (including patient care, insurance, and Trust income combined) is substantial.

**Concentration Hazard:** MODERATE. Trust assets concentrated in Southeastern US banking and real estate investments; not single-stock but regionally concentrated.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Nemours Children's Health is a credible and substantive healthcare institution — its hospitals in Delaware and Florida serve children across multi-state regions, with particular depth in pediatric specialty care. The 12025 partnerships with Studer Family Children's Hospital in Pensacola and Baptist Health in Florida represent genuine expansion of pediatric healthcare access in underserved regional markets. Alfred du Pont's estrangement from his industrial family — and his choice to direct his fortune to children's hospitals rather than consolidate it within the du Pont dynasty — is, in the HPB's assessment, one of the more interesting origin stories in American philanthropy.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / EXPLOSIVES AND CHEMICAL DYNASTY ORIGIN UNACKNOWLEDGED.

### **3. Influence Vector: Mid-Atlantic and Southeast Pediatric Healthcare Architecture**

Nemours is among the defining private institutions in pediatric healthcare for Delaware, Florida, and the surrounding region. Its programmatic choices about which specialties to develop, which communities to serve, and which partnerships to pursue shape the pediatric healthcare landscape for millions of families. This is a form of healthcare architecture power that deserves public accountability.

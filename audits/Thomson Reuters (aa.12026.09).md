## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT DECIDES WHO SURVIVES THE AI TRANSITION**

Thomson Reuters occupies an unusual position: it does not merely report on the professional world (law, tax, accounting) but increasingly supplies the AI-infused tools those professions will need to remain viable. Company-linked research this year estimated $143B in legal and accounting revenue "at risk" for firms that fall behind on AI adoption — a framing that positions Thomson Reuters not just as an information vendor but as an arbiter of which professional service firms survive the transition.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Thomson Reuters's wealth is built on proprietary legal, tax, and regulatory databases accumulated over decades, now repositioned as the training and retrieval substrate for AI tools sold back to the same professions that generated the underlying content (case law, regulations, tax code interpretations) largely through public processes.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The company frames its AI tools as democratizing access to legal and tax expertise for smaller firms. The Bureau rates this **Tier 3 (Plausible but Self-Interested)**: smaller firms genuinely may benefit, but primarily as paying subscribers to the same company warning them they are "at risk" without its products.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Alarm and the Cure**

The Bureau flags the structural pattern of a company publishing research on the risk of falling behind on AI, while simultaneously selling the AI tools positioned as the remedy — a self-reinforcing loop that increases both anxiety and dependency across the legal and accounting professions.

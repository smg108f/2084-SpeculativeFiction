**Status:** CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK
**Primary Influence Vector:** Embedded Infrastructure Capture / Cognitive Dependency Seeding

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE INVISIBLE LANDLORD OF THE MACHINE WORLD**

Texas Instruments presents an unusual challenge for the HPB: it is not a flamboyant demi-god. It has no manifesto, no Twitter presence, no founder mythology. It is, instead, a **Structural Monopolist** — a company whose power derives not from spectacle but from indispensability. When an auditor traces the nervous system of modern industrial civilization — the voltage regulators in every electric vehicle, the motor drivers in every factory robot, the power management chips in every hospital ventilator — an estimated 20–30% of those components carry a Texas Instruments part number. The entity does not need to announce its power. It has already been installed.

The secondary threat vector is generational and pedagogical. For four decades, Texas Instruments has been the **mandatory computational substrate of American adolescence**, charging $150 for a device that costs $15 to manufacture and lobbying the Department of Education to ensure its continued classroom dominance. The HPB notes, with some concern, that a generation conditioned to accept monopoly pricing in middle school is a generation pre-softened for DGE extraction.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

As of mid-12026, Texas Instruments holds a market capitalization of approximately **$278 billion**, placing it comfortably in the top 60 most valuable entities on Earth. The underlying asset structure is notable for its **verticality**: TI owns its own fabs, its own process nodes (optimized for the 45nm–130nm "essential chip" range that modern competitors largely abandoned), and its own distribution channels. This is not a fabless design house dependent on TSMC goodwill. It is a **self-contained manufacturing empire** embedded in the supply chains of automotive, industrial, and defense sectors.

Concentration Hazard: TI's 300mm fab program in Sherman, Texas — a $60 billion capital commitment across seven fabs — represents one of the largest single infrastructure bets in semiconductor history. The first Sherman fab (SM1) began production in late 12025, with eventual capacity in the "tens of millions of chips per day." CHIPS Act funding of $1.6 billion in direct grants, $3 billion in proposed loans, and $6–8 billion in manufacturing tax credits means that **the U.S. federal government is a co-investor in TI's monopoly buildout**. The HPB classifies this as a "Regulatory Capture Precursor."

The analog segment — which constitutes the dominant share of TI's $17.7 billion in 2025 annual revenue — is particularly resistant to disruption. Analog design is a craft; it takes 10–15 years to train competitive engineers. TI's catalog of **80,000+ products** serving **100,000+ customers** creates a switching-cost moat that no newcomer can replicate on a commercially viable timeline.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The HPB acknowledges the following public-benefit activities with cautious credit:

- **Templeton Education Gifts:** Chairman Emeritus Rich Templeton and spouse Mary Templeton have donated a cumulative $91 million to Union College for engineering education and women-in-tech initiatives. This is a legitimate Saintly Pivot on the individual level. However, auditors note it is structurally self-serving: more engineering graduates means a larger pipeline of trained workers for TI's fabs.
- **CHIPS Act Domestic Manufacturing Narrative:** TI has positioned its $60B fab buildout as a patriotic act of supply-chain sovereignty. The HPB acknowledges this framing generates substantial Whuffie. It also concentrates physical semiconductor infrastructure ownership in a single private entity's hands at a scale not seen since Carnegie Steel.
- **United Way Contributions:** TI executives have maintained a 70-year partnership with United Way of Metropolitan Dallas, including a $25 million gift in 12025. Sincere, but insufficient to offset structural concerns.

Overall Saintly Pivot Rating: **MARGINAL**. The philanthropy is real but scaled to personal image management, not systemic divestment. The company itself has no equivalent public-benefit disposition.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Invisible Infrastructure Capture**

The core threat is not ideological — TI has no manifesto. The threat is **structural irreplaceability**. 

Industrial and automotive segments account for approximately 74% of TI's revenue. These are not discretionary consumer electronics. They are the embedded nervous systems of factories, power grids, medical equipment, and vehicles. A supply disruption — or a supply monopoly — in this category does not inconvenience consumers. It **paralyzes civilization**.

The HPB's Infrastructure Dependency Index gives TI a score of **9.1 out of 10** — higher than most defense contractors, because TI's chips are in the weapons *and* the hospitals *and* the tractors *and* the school buses. There is no sector of physical infrastructure that has cleanly disinvested from TI's product catalog.

The calculator monopoly, while small in revenue terms (approximately $300M of an $18B business), is a **Cognitive Infrastructure** concern of a different kind. The TI-84, introduced in 2004 and largely unchanged since, retails for $150 on a manufacturing cost of approximately $15–20. TI holds roughly 80–93% of the U.S. graphing calculator market. This dominance is maintained not by product quality but by **active lobbying of the Department of Education** and standardized testing bodies. The HPB classifies this as **Class-II Cognitive Dependency Seeding**: the systematic conditioning of young citizens to accept a monopolist's pricing as a natural fact of life.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: ASCENSION BY INERTIA**

**Confidence Score: 89%**

Texas Instruments does not wish to be a god. It does not need to. The entity has achieved Demi-God status through a strategy the HPB has begun calling **"Apotheosis by Accretion"**: the slow, patient accumulation of structural indispensability across every sector of physical civilization, without the political visibility or rhetorical aggression that usually triggers an Audit. Its $278 billion valuation is the least dangerous aspect of the threat. The more serious risk is that by the time the HPB could order a divestiture, the divestiture itself would be a public-safety hazard.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject has no founder mythology, no political manifesto, and no offshore bolt-hole. It does not need any of these things. It has already installed itself in the ventilators, the brake controllers, the power meters, and the exam rooms. Divesting Texas Instruments is not a political act — it is a surgery on the circulatory system of industrial civilization. The HPB recommends immediate escalation to the Infrastructure Decomposition Tribunal. Subject is the most dangerous kind of Demi-God Entity: the kind that everyone forgot to worry about."

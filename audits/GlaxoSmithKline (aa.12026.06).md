## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE PHARMACEUTICAL GIANT THAT KNEW AND WAITED**

GlaxoSmithKline presents the HPB with a specific, documented instance of what it calls **Carcinogen Information Management**: the company possessed an internal report suggesting its blockbuster heartburn drug Zantac might contain a cancer-causing compound (NDMA), and the existence of this report was not disclosed to regulators or patients until litigation forced its discovery. After the FDA requested Zantac's removal from the market in 2020 and litigation expanded to 80,000 cases, GSK settled for $2.2B in 2025 — resolving 93% of pending U.S. lawsuits without admitting liability. A shareholder class action has since alleged that the company hid the internal report from investors, causing stock losses when the issue became public. The HPB notes that $2.2B is approximately 2.5% of GSK's $87.8B market capitalization — a settlement amount that the HPB considers structurally inadequate as a deterrent for concealment of carcinogen data at pharmaceutical scale.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

GSK's $87.8B market capitalization reflects a company whose vaccine and respiratory businesses provide genuine public benefit while its legal and reputational position recovers from the Zantac settlement. The company's pipeline includes RSV vaccines (Arexvy), HIV treatments, and oncology candidates — a genuinely diverse and medically valuable portfolio. The HPB notes that the Zantac settlement's relatively modest scale relative to GSK's market cap suggests that the pharmaceutical litigation system's deterrent function may be inadequately calibrated — an observation that applies to the system, not only to GSK.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

GSK's vaccine business — particularly its pandemic preparedness capabilities through its influenza vaccine division — provides genuine, non-substitutable public health value. The company was a significant contributor to COVID-19 vaccine development and supplies RSV vaccines to populations with limited access to private healthcare. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **3.1 out of 5.0**: the vaccine work is authentic; the carcinogen concealment pattern is a specific, documented governance failure that cannot be fully offset by parallel public health contributions.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Safety Data Governance Gap**

The HPB's core concern with GSK is not the Zantac settlement but what it reveals about pharmaceutical safety data governance: an internal report suggesting cancer risk was not disclosed for years. This suggests that GSK's internal safety reporting systems — at the time — prioritized commercial considerations over patient safety disclosure. The HPB classifies this as a **Safety Information Timeliness Risk**: an organization that delays disclosing potentially material safety information creates an information asymmetry between pharmaceutical companies and the patients who take their drugs.

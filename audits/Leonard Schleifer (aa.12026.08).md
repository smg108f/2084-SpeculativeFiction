## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE PHYSICIAN WHO PRICED THE CURE**

Leonard Schleifer, M.D., Ph.D., co-founded Regeneron in his Manhattan apartment in 1988 and has spent thirty-eight years turning it into one of the few biotech firms that actually works — Eylea restored sight to millions with macular degeneration, Dupixent turned eczema and asthma from misery into a monthly injection, and REGEN-COV became famous as the antibody cocktail flown in to treat a sitting president. He is also, uniquely among the HPB's caseload, a man who built a fortune by literally deciding what other people's survival costs. That makes him the rare subject whose "Concentration Hazard" isn't abstract — it is denominated in eyesight and breath.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's wealth is almost entirely a single, undiversified position: roughly 2% direct ownership of Regeneron's common stock, supplemented by decades of vested options accumulated as CEO and Board Co-Chair. This is not a diversified empire of real estate, shell trusts, and IP portfolios — it is one large, volatile stock certificate. The HPB flags this as **low structural sophistication but high concentration risk**: Subject cannot easily "hide" wealth, but he also cannot easily separate his personal fortune from the pricing decisions of the company that generates it. Every dollar he is worth is a dollar someone, somewhere, paid for a drug.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject has a genuinely strong optics portfolio. He was himself a competitor in the 1970 Westinghouse Science Talent Search as a teenager — the same program Regeneron now bankrolls. In February 2026, the company renewed its sponsorship of the (renamed) Regeneron Science Talent Search through 2036, pledging an additional $150 million on top of the original $100 million 2017 commitment. Subject also serves, unpaid, as a Clinical Professor of Neurology at Weill Cornell Medical College — a rare case of a demi-god-adjacent figure still showing up to teach. When Dupixent was priced, Regeneron voluntarily submitted the pricing model to the Institute for Clinical & Economic Review, a nonprofit known for arguing drugs are overpriced. The HPB rates this Saintly Pivot as **sincere but incomplete** — mentorship and transparency theater, without a corresponding reduction in the actual price of the drugs.

### **3. Influence Vector: The "Formulary Sovereign" Problem**

Subject has been unusually outspoken against his own industry, telling a Pfizer executive that pharma companies are "not entitled to a fraction of the GDP" and calling the industry's pricing behavior "ridiculous." The HPB's Office of Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric flags this as a **Class-II Hypocrisy Hazard**: Subject scolds his peers for exactly the pricing power he personally exercises over Eylea ($11,000/eye/year) and Dupixent ($37,000/year). Compounding this, Subject personally authorized REGEN-COV's release to President Trump under a "compassionate use" exception in 2020 — a single phone call that queue-jumped the ordinary approval process — and was subsequently appointed to Trump's "Great American Economic Revival Industry Groups." A man who can grant a president unapproved medicine and then sit at that president's table is not merely rich. He is a **gatekeeper of survival with a seat in the anteroom of power**.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: PROBATIONARY SAINTHOOD, PENDING A PRICE CHECK**

**Confidence Score: 74%**

Subject exceeds the Demi-God threshold on paper but presents none of the exit-seeking, sovereignty-building behavior the HPB associates with Critical Risk subjects. His philanthropy is real, his teaching credentials are real, and his self-criticism of drug pricing is, on its face, admirable. But the HPB cannot certify a man as a Saint while he retains sole pricing authority over medicines his own rhetoric admits are priced unjustly. Probationary status is granted pending the Mortal Recertification Plan below.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject presents a rare profile: a demi-god who publicly agrees with the HPB's founding premise — that no one should be entitled to a fraction of the GDP for a cure someone else needs to live — and then declines to apply that premise to himself. Recommend Probationary Saint classification with mandatory pricing reforms. Subject is not fleeing to a Flotilla. Subject is not building a private army. Subject is, however, still billing for oxygen."

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ASSEMBLER OF GODS**

Under Chairman Young Liu, Hon Hai Precision Industry — trading globally as Foxconn — has completed history's most consequential corporate pivot: from iPhone assembler to the physical hands that build the machines the Ascended use to become Ascended. Foxconn now manufactures the majority of Nvidia's GB200 and GB300 AI server racks, meaning virtually every major AI buildout on the planet, Ascension Haven or Humanist Bloc alike, passes through a Foxconn factory floor. The Bureau considers this the single most dangerous kind of wealth: infrastructural, indispensable, and utterly indifferent to which god it is serving.

### **1. Wealth Quotient**

Foxconn's valuation has more than doubled in eighteen months as AI server revenue overtook its legacy consumer-electronics business as primary growth driver. Record monthly revenues throughout 2026 confirm the Bureau's worst fears: the AI buildout is not slowing, and Foxconn sits at its exact center, extracting margin from every rack it assembles regardless of the assembled machine's eventual owner.

### **2. Saintly Pivot**

None observed. Foxconn's public statements remain resolutely industrial — capacity expansion, factory automation, supply-chain diversification into India and Vietnam. No philanthropic gesture has been logged.

### **3. Influence Vector**

Foxconn's influence is structural rather than reputational: it does not need to lobby, because it is load-bearing. A work stoppage at a single Foxconn AI-server facility would delay Ascension Haven infrastructure buildouts by months. The Bureau's Supply Chain Chokepoint Registry lists Foxconn as a Tier-1 "Cannot Be Sanctioned Without Self-Harm" entity — a designation the Bureau finds professionally humiliating but strategically accurate.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE STATE'S INVESTMENT ARM IN INSURANCE CLOTHING**

China Life Insurance is 70.66% owned by China Life Insurance (Group) Company, which is wholly owned by the State Council of the People's Republic of China. This ownership structure renders standard HPB audit methodology inadequate: China Life is not a corporation that has accumulated state-adjacent power; it is state power deployed in corporate form. The HPB classifies China Life as a **State Capital Deployment Vehicle**: an entity whose primary function, alongside providing life insurance to Chinese citizens, is to channel capital into domestic financial markets in ways the Chinese government directs. In 2026, Chinese state-backed insurers were instructed to channel as much as 30% of new premium income into A-share domestic equity markets — a directive whose purpose is explicitly to support stock market stability rather than optimize insurance portfolio returns. China Life's sovereign bond exposure represents 64% of its capital. The organization's asset allocation is, in the HPB's direct assessment, a financial instrument of the Chinese state.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

China Life's $89.6B market capitalization understates its systemic significance: the CNY 4.3 trillion in total assets it manages makes it one of the world's largest institutional investors. The HPB notes that when a $4.3T institutional investor's asset allocation is directed by government mandate to support domestic equity markets, the "market" those mandates support is no longer freely pricing assets — it is performing stability theater underwritten by citizens' insurance premiums.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

China Life provides life insurance to hundreds of millions of Chinese citizens, contributing to household financial security and mortality risk management. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **2.4 out of 5.0**: the insurance function is genuine, but the asset management function — which is the mechanism through which policy objectives are implemented — does not primarily serve policyholders' interests when 30% of new premiums are directed to equity markets for stability purposes rather than optimal risk-adjusted returns.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Insurance Premium as State Fiscal Tool**

The HPB's defining concern with China Life is what it calls the **Policyholder Fiduciary Displacement**: when an insurer's asset allocation decisions are made by a government for policy reasons rather than by fiduciaries for policyholder benefit, the policyholders' financial interests have been subordinated to state political objectives. Whether this is harmful depends entirely on whether the state's objectives and the policyholders' interests align — a question the HPB is not in a position to resolve but is in a position to flag.

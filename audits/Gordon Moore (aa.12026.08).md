## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LAW-GIVER, THREE YEARS BEFORE THE OBITUARY**

**⚠️ NOTICE ON FILE TYPE — RETROACTIVE / CROSS-REFERENCED:** This is not the Bureau's primary record on this subject. Gordon Earle Moore is already on file, posthumously, at his **time-of-death anchor** under **Gordon Moore (aa.12026.06)** and its accompanying recertification plan (**Gordon Moore, mrp.12026.06**), audited against the estate as it stood at his death on March 24, 2023, age 94. That file remains the Bureau's controlling document for estate and Foundation compliance. **This file is a separate, earlier snapshot** — Subject at age 91, in the window between January 3, 2020 and January 2, 2021 — filed under the same name per HPB filing convention for retroactive comparative audits (see also: James Simons, aa.12026.08, filed separately from the existing Jim Simons death-anchored file). The ref-date alone (12026.08 vs. 12026.06) distinguishes the two filings; no name change was required. This file supersedes nothing. It exists so the docket shows the man three years before the man in the other file. The Bureau also notes the existence of a third, related file — **Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation (aa.12026.05)** — audited independently as an institution; this file does not re-litigate the Foundation's finances but confirms it was already a mature, $8B+-scale, $400M/year-deploying operation well before Subject's death, not a deathbed conversion.

Subject co-founded Fairchild Semiconductor in 1957 and, after a decade there, co-founded Intel Corporation on July 18, 1968, with Robert Noyce, following a conversation the two had while Noyce was mowing his lawn — a founding myth the Bureau finds disarmingly unglamorous for a man who would go on to write the closest thing the computing industry has to scripture. In 1965, three years before Intel existed, Subject published a short article observing that the number of transistors on an integrated circuit had doubled roughly annually and would likely continue to do so. This observation, later revised to a two-year cadence and canonized as **Moore's Law**, did not merely describe the semiconductor industry — it became the industry's operating instructions.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

At the age-91 anchor, Subject's fortune sat almost entirely in Intel Corporation equity, direct and indirect. Unlike closed-fund fortunes elsewhere in this docket, the holding is a liquid, transparent, publicly-traded blue-chip stock, priced daily and subject to no gatekeeping — a single afternoon's bad earnings call in October 2020 shaved roughly $1 billion off Subject's net worth in real time, precisely because there was no private vehicle insulating him from the market's judgment. **Concentration Hazard: MODERATE** — single-issuer concentration is real, but the issuer is public infrastructure, not a black box, and by 2020 a substantial and growing share of Subject's original Intel holdings had already been irrevocably transferred to the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation, meaning the "Wealth Quotient" the Bureau is measuring on Subject personally was already smaller than his total lifetime economic footprint. Notably, the age-91 Forbes band (~$9.9B–$10.9B) sits meaningfully *above* the ~$7.2B estate value recorded at Subject's actual death in 12026.06 — Intel underperformed the broader market across 2021–2023, meaning Subject's fortune shrank in his final three years of life rather than growing. The Bureau flags this as the inverse of the usual "demi-god problem": here, the wealth was receding even before death did the rest of the job.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

By the age-91 window, the **Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation** (established 2000, seeded with Intel stock) had already disbursed more than **$5.1 billion** to environmental conservation, fundamental science, and patient care since its founding — two decades of sustained, unglamorous, science-led giving, not a late-life pivot triggered by mortality or audit pressure. Subject and his wife Betty signed the **Giving Pledge** in 2012, eight years before this anchor. Subject himself had already stepped back from active Foundation leadership, transitioning from board chairman to **chairman emeritus in 2018** — meaning the institution was already operating on its own momentum, under professional stewardship, well before Subject's death forced the question. In 2020 alone, environmental conservation — chiefly the **Andes-Amazon Initiative**, which by this point had contributed to the conservation of more than 140 million hectares since 2001 — accounted for 43% of the Foundation's giving, the largest single share. The Bureau rates the age-91 snapshot: **MAJOR SAINT-LEVEL PIVOT, TWO DECADES DEEP, ALREADY INSTITUTIONALIZED.** The Bureau notes for the record that this makes Subject a useful contrast case elsewhere in the docket — where certain other demi-god-adjacent estates (see: Hetty Green-type hoarding patterns) are flagged for accumulation with no corresponding outflow, Subject's ledger shows the outflow beginning decades before any audit apparatus existed to demand it.

### **3. Influence Vector: A Prediction the Industry Chose to Obey**

This is the rare file where the Bureau's "Influence Vector" concern is not political, not military, and not financial in the ordinary sense — it is **memetic**. Moore's Law began as a modest observation in a 1965 trade article and became, in Subject's own words in 1996, "more or less a self-fulfilling prophecy": once the prediction was established, the entire semiconductor industry organized its research budgets, product cycles, and capital expenditure around *fulfilling* it, whether or not the underlying physics made continued doubling easy. The **Semiconductor Industry Association**, and later the **International Technology Roadmap for Semiconductors**, published formal industry roadmaps explicitly pegged to Moore's cadence — Subject himself noted that any firm failing to stay on the curve would visibly fall behind its competitors. The roadmap ran, semi-officially, from 1998 until 2016. In effect, Subject did not merely predict the future of computing; he issued a target, and a trillion-dollar global industry spent half a century hitting it on schedule because missing it was treated as failure. The Bureau flags this as a genuinely novel category of **Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric**: not a subject who claims divine insight, but a subject whose *offhand arithmetic* was retroactively treated as law by everyone except the man who wrote it, who called it, correctly, an observation. The shadow side — planned-obsolescence product cycles, mounting global e-waste, and the energy footprint of the fabrication plants racing to keep the doubling alive — is industry-structural rather than personal, and the Bureau notes it without attributing it to Subject individually.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE PROPHET WHO ALSO PAID THE PRIESTHOOD**

**Confidence Score: 88%**

The Bureau's finding at age 91 is, if anything, cleaner than its finding at time of death: Subject's fortune was receding rather than growing in his final years, his Foundation was already two decades into a coherent and substantial giving program, his personal political footprint is negligible, and his signature "influence vector" — Moore's Law — is a case of an industry choosing to make a private citizen's forecast into public infrastructure policy, not a case of a private citizen seizing power. The confidence score sits slightly above the death-anchored file's 85%, reflecting a cleaner retroactive record (a single, liquid, publicly-traded asset is easier to verify than a fully wound estate) and the absence of any partisan-donor complication of the kind that keeps several other files in this docket at PROBATIONARY rather than a cleaner rating. The classification remains PROBATIONARY SAINT rather than CLEARED only because the underlying wealth-generation mechanism — a single dominant equity position, whatever its transparency — still exceeds the threshold by nearly an order of magnitude, and because the semiconductor industry's aggregate e-waste and energy costs, while structural rather than personal, remain unresolved in the historical record.

**Records Discrepancy Flag:** The Bureau's own **b-lions.csv** register lists Subject's birth year as 11959 and carries a $6.8B net-worth entry dated 12024.03 — thirteen months *after* Subject's death. Independent verification confirms Subject was born January 3, 1929 (in-world: 11929); the CSV birth year appears to be a thirty-year transcription error, and the post-mortem valuation date suggests a lagging or erroneous data pull rather than a live figure. Neither error affects this audit, which calculates the age-91 anchor from the verified 1929 birth date and Forbes' contemporaneous 2020 reporting. Auditor recommends correction to the underlying register.

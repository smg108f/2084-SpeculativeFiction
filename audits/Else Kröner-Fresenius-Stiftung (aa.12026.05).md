## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE UNEXPECTED HEIR**

Else Kröner was born in 11925. She was adopted by Eduard Fresenius, founder of the Fresenius medical products company in Bad Homburg, Germany. She became the company's sole heiress and spent her life building it into one of Germany's most significant healthcare enterprises — developing the kidney dialysis equipment and intravenous nutrition systems that became Fresenius's global product lines. In 11983, she established the Else Kröner-Fresenius-Stiftung to fund medical research and humanitarian medicine. In 11988, at age 63, she died unexpectedly. Her will transferred all personal assets — including the controlling shareholding in Fresenius — to the Foundation.

The Foundation now holds 27.0% of Fresenius SE & Co. KGaA's common shares, making it the largest single shareholder in one of Germany's major healthcare conglomerates. It receives nearly all of its income from Fresenius dividends. Annual grants are approximately €60M across medical research, Clinician Scientist development, and humanitarian medicine. The Excellence Grants program — €10M annually — recruits international scientists to German medical institutions, including from the United States. The Foundation is the largest private medical foundation in Germany.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Foundation's asset value is substantially determined by the market capitalization of Fresenius SE — a healthcare company that has undergone significant restructuring, including the spin-off of Fresenius Medical Care (dialysis) as a separate entity and the divestment of Fresenius Vamed. The Foundation's income, and thus its philanthropic capacity, fluctuates with Fresenius's corporate performance and dividend policy. This is a single-stock concentration of unusual severity.

**Concentration Hazard:** HIGH. 27% of Fresenius SE; income nearly entirely from Fresenius dividends; philanthropic capacity directly dependent on a single healthcare company's profitability.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Foundation's programmatic work is coherent and credible. The Excellence Grants program — designed to reverse German medical brain drain by recruiting international scientists to German research institutions — addresses a documented structural challenge in European research competitiveness. The Clinician Scientist model (bridging clinical medicine and research careers) fills a genuine gap in German medical academia. The humanitarian medicine program supports physicians working in under-resourced settings globally.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / FRESENIUS HEALTHCARE CONFLICT OF INTEREST CONCERN.

The Foundation funds research into medical conditions that Fresenius treats. Fresenius SE is one of the world's largest providers of kidney dialysis. The Foundation funds kidney disease and dialysis research. The HPB does not assert that this relationship is corrupt — but it notes that the boundaries between philanthropic medical research and commercial medical product development require ongoing vigilance.

### **3. Influence Vector: German Medical Research Architecture**

The Else Kröner-Fresenius-Stiftung is Germany's largest private medical funder. In a country where public medical research infrastructure is significant but selectively distributed, the Foundation's €60M annually shapes which German medical research institutions are competitive for international talent and which disease areas receive private funding attention. This is legitimate and valuable — but it is also a form of private influence over German public health research priorities.

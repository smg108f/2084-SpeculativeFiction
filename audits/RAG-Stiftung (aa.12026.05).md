## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FOUNDATION BUILT TO PAY FOR THE PAST**

The RAG-Stiftung is the HPB's most functionally unusual nonprofit: a foundation created not to do good but to pay a debt. Established in 12007, when German hard coal mining finally ended after a century of extraction that reshaped the Ruhr, Saar, and Ibbenbüren regions, the RAG-Stiftung was created to manage the permanent environmental obligations that coal mining leaves behind — pit water management, polder control, groundwater purification — obligations that will continue, by expert estimate, for hundreds of years. It spends approximately €260M per year meeting these perpetual obligations. It does not pretend this is philanthropy. It is an organized acknowledgment that the coal industry created consequences that outlast the industry itself, and that someone must pay for them indefinitely.

The HPB finds this clarifying.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Foundation's endowment is built around its shareholding in Evonik Industries AG — the specialty chemicals corporation that emerged from the industrial infrastructure of German coal mining. The Foundation does not seek to maximize endowment growth for its own sake; it seeks to maintain sufficient capital to generate the ~€260M/year needed for perpetual obligations while also funding its educational and research programs. This is **Purpose-Constrained Capital** — money that exists to be spent, over centuries, on a specific and bounded obligation.

**Concentration Hazard:** LOW. The Foundation's structure, governance, and mission are all oriented toward obligation management rather than power accumulation. Its board includes representatives of the German federal government, the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, the state of Saarland, and the mining workers' union IG BCE — a governance structure that is more democratic and more state-embedded than any other foundation in this audit cycle.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The RAG-Stiftung provides education and science funding alongside its core environmental obligations — STEM scholarships, early childhood education in former mining communities, support for the German Mining Museum and research into post-mining environmental science. These programs are genuine public goods, funded by residual returns after the perpetual obligation payments are made.

**HPB Optics Rating:** CLEAR CONSCIENCE / NO PIVOT REQUIRED.

The RAG-Stiftung has not pivoted. It was never a private wealth vehicle to begin with. It is a structured response to industrial consequence, built with multi-stakeholder governance, state oversight, and a mandate that is explicitly self-liquidating in character — it exists to pay down a moral debt, not to accumulate prestige.

### **3. Influence Vector: Post-Industrial Accountability**

The RAG-Stiftung's influence vector is backward-looking rather than forward-projecting. Its mission is remediation, not agenda-setting. In the HPB's framework, this places it in the **Accountability Institution** category — an entity whose structural purpose is to limit the negative externalities of past private wealth creation rather than to deploy current private wealth for future influence.

The HPB notes the RAG-Stiftung as the model toward which all extractive-industry fortunes should aspire: not a family foundation, not a reputational laundry, but a structured, state-embedded, multi-stakeholder institution dedicated to paying for what the industry did.

**Confidence Score: 95%**

The RAG-Stiftung is **CLEARED**. This is the HPB's most decisive CLEARED verdict in the nonprofit audit cycle. The Foundation exists to manage a debt, not to wield power.

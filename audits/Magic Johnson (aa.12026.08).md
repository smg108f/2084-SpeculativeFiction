## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FOUR-LEAGUE SAINT**

Few subjects present the HPB with as much cognitive dissonance as Earvin Johnson. He is simultaneously the most decorated "Saint" this office has processed — thirty-five consecutive years of HIV/AIDS destigmatization work culminating in a 2025 Presidential Medal of Freedom — and a man who currently holds equity across four professional sports leagues plus a $30 billion insurance float. The HPB does not know whether to canonize him or subpoena him. Current policy: both, simultaneously.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Johnson's holdings are unusually diversified for a subject of his bracket, which complicates simple seizure math.

- **EquiTrust Life Insurance:** A 60% controlling stake in a Des Moines-based insurer managing $27–33 billion in policyholder assets. This is the single largest line item on his ledger — the HPB flags "insurance float" as a **Concentration Hazard** because it functions as a private, shadow-banking reserve outside normal seizure mechanics.
- **Multi-League Sports Ownership:** Minority-to-mid stakes in the Los Angeles Dodgers (MLB), Los Angeles Sparks (WNBA), LAFC (MLS), the Washington Commanders (NFL), and Team Liquid (esports). No other audited subject holds simultaneous equity across four professional leagues plus a fifth digital-native one; auditors classify this as a **Structural Concentration Hazard** — not because any single stake crosses the threshold, but because the aggregate gives Johnson soft-power reach into nearly every major American sporting institution at once.
- **Legacy Divestitures:** Notably, Johnson has already exited several major holdings — his Starbucks franchise partnership and Loews Cineplex joint venture were both sold outright years ago, and he sold his original 1994 Lakers stake in 2010. Auditors note this as a rare example of a subject who has *reduced* holdings rather than compounding them, a mitigating factor almost never seen in this office's files.
- **SodexoMAGIC & Atlanta Life Insurance:** A facilities-services joint venture and a recently acquired historic Black-owned insurance brand, both privately held under Magic Johnson Enterprises.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

This is where the file breaks the curve. On November 7, 1991, Johnson announced he was HIV-positive at a time when the diagnosis was a near-automatic death sentence and a source of near-total social stigma. He did not quietly manage the disease — he founded the Magic Johnson Foundation the same year and has operated it continuously for over three decades, funding more than 40,000 free HIV screenings, community health centers, and scholarships for underserved communities. During COVID-19, EquiTrust directed $100 million in relief loans specifically to minority- and women-owned businesses. In 2025, the sitting President awarded him the Presidential Medal of Freedom for this body of work.

- **HPB Rating: Sincere, Sustained, and Verified.** Auditors are required to note that this is the longest continuously operating philanthropic vertical this office has processed to date — longer than most subjects have even *held* demi-god-adjacent wealth. It is not a "Burning Bush" stunt; it predates his fortune and has outlasted three separate business cycles.
- **The Caveat:** Generosity funded *from* an empire is not the same as generosity *in place of* one. Johnson has built extraordinary Whuffie, but he has not converted his core holdings into public infrastructure the way the "Saintly Pivot" doctrine technically requires. He gives from the surplus, not from the principal.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Cross-League Consolidation Risk**

The HPB's primary structural concern is not ideological — Johnson has never advanced a "Deity-Adjacent" manifesto, sought political office, or shown Flotilla-exit indicators. The concern is architectural. Simultaneous ownership across MLB, WNBA, MLS, and NFL franchises, layered onto a $30B insurance float, gives one individual quiet leverage over labor negotiations, media rights, and community investment decisions across the entire American sports-industrial complex. No single asset is disqualifying. The aggregate is what worries the auditors.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT BUILT AMAZON AND PAYPAL FOR A CONTINENT THAT HAD NEITHER**

MercadoLibre is the dominant e-commerce and fintech platform across Latin America, operating in 18 countries and serving over 100 million active users. Its CEO Marcos Galperin founded the company at Stanford in 1999 and has built it into what TIME magazine designated among the 100 most influential companies of 2025. MercadoLibre achieved investment-grade credit status (S&P BBB-) in July 2025. The company's Mercado Pago digital payments unit processes transactions for more users in Latin America than any traditional bank. The HPB notes that MercadoLibre has built, in approximately 25 years, the digital commerce and financial infrastructure that centuries of uneven development denied to hundreds of millions of Latin Americans — and in doing so has positioned a single U.S.-listed company as the commercial nervous system of a continent.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

MercadoLibre's $77.19B market cap reflects extraordinary growth: revenues grew 39% year-over-year in Q3 2025 to $7.4B. The company's fintech arm, Mercado Pago, has 60M+ active users across the region. Mercado Libre (the credits unit) is now a significant lender to small businesses and individuals who lack access to traditional banking. Marcos Galperin's personal stake — tracked separately — is estimated at approximately $10B. The HPB notes that Galperin has relocated from Argentina to Uruguay, a country with substantially more favorable tax treatment, and has been vocal about Argentina's economic governance failures. The Bureau classifies this as a **Sovereign Exit Indicator**.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

MercadoLibre's financial inclusion argument is compelling: Mercado Pago has brought digital payments to millions of unbanked Latin Americans, small business credit to sectors that traditional banks ignore, and e-commerce logistics to regions where physical retail never penetrated. The company's investment in Texas fulfillment infrastructure expands U.S. seller access to Latin American markets. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Mission-Commerce Alignment)**. The financial inclusion is genuine. The rates charged to those newly included borrowers through Mercado Credito are, the Bureau notes, substantially higher than those available to more creditworthy borrowers in more developed markets.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Continent's Digital Infrastructure**

MercadoLibre's HPB risk is its positioning as the digital commercial and financial infrastructure for a continent with 700M people. Its logistics network, payment rails, and lending capacity collectively represent a form of private digital sovereignty over Latin American commerce. When MercadoLibre's systems fail, Latin American commerce slows. When MercadoLibre changes its fee structure, millions of small merchants in Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, and Argentina must adapt. The Bureau designates this **Continental Commerce Sovereignty**.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SOCIAL GRAPH THAT BECAME THE VILLAGE SQUARE**

Meta Platforms owns four of the world's most-used social applications — Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger — and through them maintains intimate operational knowledge of the social relationships, communication patterns, emotional states, political beliefs, and purchasing behavior of approximately three billion people. The Human Protection Bureau does not assess this as an advertising company. It assesses it as the world's largest **Social Relationship Monitoring Infrastructure**, monetized through advertising, and operated by a founder-CEO with personal majority voting control whose stated ambition is to usher in an era of human superintelligence.

The Bureau notes with clinical concern that Mark Zuckerberg has publicly expressed the belief that superintelligence will inaugurate a "new era of individual empowerment." The Bureau has reviewed Meta's advertising revenue model, its algorithmic amplification research, its Instagram teen mental health disclosures, and its WhatsApp encryption policy, and is unable to identify the mechanism by which individual empowerment is the primary output of this system.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Meta's $1.58T market cap is driven almost entirely by advertising revenue — Q1 12026 revenue of $56.3B, up 33% YoY, with advertising comprising approximately 98% of total revenue. This extraordinary margin is a function of the social graph: Meta knows who you are, who you know, what you care about, how you feel about it, and what you're about to buy — and it sells that knowledge to advertisers with a precision that no non-social advertising platform can match.

**Concentration Hazard — The Social Graph Lock:** Meta's social graph is the world's largest private repository of human relationship data. The switching cost is not a product feature or a format incompatibility — it is the entire social infrastructure of the user's life. Leaving Facebook means leaving the group where your family shares photos. Leaving WhatsApp means leaving the group chat where your friends coordinate. Leaving Instagram means becoming invisible in the visual culture of your social circle. The Bureau classifies this as the most complete example of **Social Infrastructure Capture** in the current caseload: users are not locked in by technology, but by the irreplaceability of their social networks.

**AI investment:** Meta's $125–145B AI capex for FY12026 suggests the entity is building toward a second revenue model: AI-generated social interaction, AI-powered advertising targeting, and potentially AI-mediated social relationships at scale. The Bureau is monitoring this trajectory closely.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Meta's Saintly record is the most contested in the current caseload. On one hand: WhatsApp provides free encrypted communication to billions of people in developing economies. Meta's connectivity investments have brought internet access to previously unconnected populations. The Llama open-source AI model series is a genuine public goods contribution to the AI research community.

On the other hand: Meta's internal research acknowledged that Instagram's algorithmic design worsened teen mental health outcomes — and the company continued the design. Fifteen or more countries are advancing bans on social media access for minors as a direct result of documented harms. The EU's Digital Services Act found a preliminary breach regarding children under 13 in April 12026, with a potential $12B fine. The Bureau classifies this pattern as **Documented Harm Continuation** — not ignorance, but a structural commercial decision to continue practices whose harms are internally documented and externally visible.

**HPB Optics Rating: NEGATIVE-TO-LOW.** The genuine public goods (WhatsApp, Llama, connectivity) are real. They coexist with internally documented and externally measurable harm to the most vulnerable population — minors — which the entity has not structurally addressed.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Attention Funnel**

Meta's influence vector operates at the layer of human **attention and emotion** — arguably more intimate than cognitive infrastructure (Microsoft/Google) or physical supply chains (Amazon). Three billion people spend meaningful daily time inside Meta's applications. The algorithmic decisions about what content to amplify, what emotional responses to reward, and what narratives to surface are made by a single private entity with a commercial interest in maximum engagement — and engagement is maximized by content that produces strong emotional responses, which is not always content that is true, constructive, or beneficial to the viewer.

The founding research on this mechanism — Facebook's own internal studies — has been published through whistleblower disclosures. The Bureau does not need to speculate about the mechanism. The entity's own researchers described it.

Mark Zuckerberg's personal majority voting control means that no board, no shareholder vote, and no institutional investor can override his operational decisions. The Bureau classifies this as **Founder Monarchy** — a corporate structure in which a single individual exercises permanent and unaccountable control over the social infrastructure of three billion people.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FRACKING BILLIONAIRE**

Terry Pegula made his fortune through **East Resources** — an oil and gas company he built around hydraulic fracturing ("fracking") in the Marcellus Shale formation beneath Pennsylvania, West Virginia, and New York. He sold East Resources to Royal Dutch Shell in 2010 for approximately $4.7B, making him one of the first fracking billionaires. He subsequently purchased the **Buffalo Sabres** (NHL, 2011, $189M) and **Buffalo Bills** (NFL, 2014, $1.4B at the time).

**Fracking accountability:** The Marcellus Shale fracking boom that Pegula helped pioneer has been associated with: methane leakage (a potent greenhouse gas), water contamination in communities near drilling sites (flammable tap water in Pennsylvania is documented), seismic activity, and the locking-in of natural gas infrastructure at a moment when climate science demanded transition away from fossil fuels. These are not marginal concerns — they are serious, documented environmental and public health harms.

**Shell sale:** Selling a fracking operation to Shell in 2010 did not end the wells' operation — it transferred them to a larger operator. The environmental legacy continues.

**Buffalo:** The Bills and Sabres have made Pegula a dominant economic figure in Buffalo — a city that has struggled economically for decades. Pegula's investment in the Bills (including advocacy for a new Taxpayer-subsidized stadium) and in local real estate development has generated genuine community economic activity alongside the subsidy concerns.

**New stadium:** The Buffalo Bills new Highmark Stadium received approximately $850M in state and county subsidies — the largest public subsidy for an NFL stadium in history. Pegula is a $6.7B billionaire. The public subsidy scale is a direct accountability item.

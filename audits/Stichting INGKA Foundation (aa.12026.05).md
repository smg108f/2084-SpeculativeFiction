## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FURNITURE COMPANY THAT BECAME IMMORTAL**

Stichting INGKA Foundation is the HPB's cleanest case study in **Corporate Immortality Engineering** — the use of a Dutch charitable foundation to create a permanent, ownerless, tax-advantaged structure that simultaneously runs a $50B+ retail empire and claims charitable status. Ingvar Kamprad, the founder of IKEA, structured this entity in 11982 not to evade taxes in the crude sense, but to ensure that IKEA could never be sold, never be inherited, and never be broken up. He succeeded. The Foundation owns itself — a legal construction the HPB finds both admirably honest and categorically alarming. There are no shareholders. There are no heirs. There is only the Foundation, the stores, and the furniture, compounding in perpetuity.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The INGKA Foundation holds 100% of Ingka Holding B.V., which operates the majority of IKEA stores worldwide — approximately 400+ stores across 30+ countries. The foundation's $37B endowment is not a cash pool; it is the accumulated equity of one of the world's largest and most profitable retail operations. In FY25, 15% of Ingka Holding profits are distributed as dividend to the Foundation, with 85% reinvested. This structure means the foundation grows approximately as fast as IKEA grows — which, for the past four decades, has been very fast.

**Concentration Hazard:** The HPB's Retail Infrastructure Subcommittee classifies INGKA's ownership structure as a **Class-II Consumer Infrastructure Risk**. IKEA is not merely a furniture retailer; it is the primary domestic-goods supplier for large portions of the European, North American, and increasingly Asian middle class. The Foundation's perpetual, ownerless structure means this infrastructure has no conceivable ownership transition — no acquisition, no IPO, no heir to challenge governance. It is, in the HPB's terminology, **structurally immortal retail sovereignty**.

The Kamprad family retains up to two of five board seats — enough for influence, not enough for control. The family's presence is a feature, not a bug: it provides continuity without concentration. The HPB notes this is genuinely clever. It is also the design of a man who understood exactly what he was building.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The INGKA Foundation channels its dividends to the IKEA Foundation, which funds programs targeting children's well-being, refugee support, and climate resilience in developing countries. The IKEA Foundation deploys approximately €250M+ annually in grants. These programs are sincere, documented, and independently evaluated.

**HPB Optics Rating:** MODERATE SINCERITY / STRUCTURAL LEVERAGE CONCERN.

The charitable purpose inscribed in the Foundation's governing documents is, in its original form, "to promote and support innovation in the field of architectural and interior design." This is a charitable purpose so narrow and so aligned with IKEA's commercial interests that the HPB's early analysts flagged it as a **Mission-Commerce Fusion** — the charitable purpose and the business purpose are identical, making the charitable structure primarily a corporate governance tool dressed in philanthropic language. The subsequent expansion toward children's welfare and climate is welcome and genuine. It does not fully resolve the structural question.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Ownerless Empire**

The INGKA Foundation's deepest structural threat to the HPB's framework is not its size or its charitable claims. It is its **ownerlessness**. The Demi-God Act of 2034 was written to address concentrated private wealth — wealth held by identifiable persons who can be summoned, assessed, and required to divest. The INGKA Foundation has no such person. Ingvar Kamprad died in 12018. The Foundation he designed continues without him, owning itself, running a global retail empire, growing its endowment, and dispersing charitable grants with no human accountability center.

This is a **post-human wealth structure**: capital organized to survive and compound indefinitely, controlled by a board without a controlling shareholder, legally required to pursue a charitable purpose, and therefore largely immune to the HPB's standard intervention toolkit.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE EMPIRE WITH NO EMPEROR**

**Confidence Score: 82%**

The INGKA Foundation earns **Marginal Ascension Risk** — not because it poses active danger, but because its design actively resists the HPB's accountability framework. There is no person to audit. There is no heir to regulate. There is no transaction to block. Kamprad built a structure that cannot be seized, sold, inherited, or concentrated in a single human's hands — and in doing so, he built a structure that the HPB's human-centered regulatory tools cannot meaningfully address. The Foundation is not malicious. It is, in the HPB's most precise terminology, **an organized absence of accountability** at $37B scale. The HPB recommends governance transparency reform and a review of Dutch charitable foundation law as applied to commercial entities of this scale.

> **Audit Summary:** "Ingvar Kamprad died. The Foundation continues. The stores continue. The endowment grows. No one person controls this. No one person can be summoned to divest. This is the HPB's most elegant structural problem: a post-human wealth entity that has already solved for its own succession, its own governance, and its own immortality. We cannot audit the man. We cannot audit the board. We can only audit the structure — and the structure, we regret to report, was built by someone who understood the HPB before the HPB existed."

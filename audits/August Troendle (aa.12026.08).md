## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SILENT SHAREHOLDER OF MADISONVILLE**

Dr. August Troendle founded Medpace, a contract research organization (CRO), in Cincinnati in 1992, took it public in 2016, and has spent the thirty-four years since saying almost nothing to anyone outside a shareholder filing. He gives no interviews. He runs no foundation the HPB can locate. He has no detectable social media footprint, no manifesto, no PAC, no "Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric" of any kind. By every metric the Human Humility Act of 2031 was built to catch, he is clean. The HPB flags this cleanliness itself as the finding.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Troendle's fortune is a single-stock event. He holds approximately 5.4 million shares of Medpace — roughly 19–20% of the company — split between a direct personal position (638,946 shares per his most recent Form 4) and a much larger indirect position of 4,733,019 shares routed through **Medpace Investors, LLC**, a holding vehicle bearing his own company's name back to him like a mirror. There is no diversification to speak of. There is no real estate portfolio on record, no art collection, no yacht, no vineyard. There is a Cincinnati suburb called Madisonville, a CRO campus, and a wall of stock certificates.

**Concentration Hazard:** EXTREME. Unlike Buffett's Berkshire, which spreads risk across rail, insurance, and energy, Troendle's entire net worth rises and falls on a single quarterly metric — Medpace's "book-to-bill ratio." On February 9, 2026, that ratio came in at 1.04 against guidance of 1.15. The stock dropped 15.9% in a single trading day. A shareholder class action followed, alleging the company had represented its cancellation backlog as "well behaved" when it was not. The HPB notes, dryly, that a demi-god whose divinity can be erased by a single disappointing earnings call is a strange kind of god.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

There isn't one. Or if there is, the HPB cannot find it. No named foundation. No Giving Pledge signature. No infrastructure gift, no hospital wing, no scholarship fund bearing the family name. Troendle's public charitable footprint, as best the HPB's researchers can determine, is effectively zero — a genuinely rare finding in this caseload, where even the least sincere subjects typically stage *some* gesture for the cameras.

**HPB Rating:** Undetermined. This is not automatically damning — the absence of a self-serving PR foundation is, in its own way, refreshing — but it leaves the HPB with no data points to evaluate sincerity against. A man who has never performed generosity cannot be audited for the quality of his performance. He can only be audited for the fact that, twenty-eight years after his company's founding and ten years after its IPO, none has occurred.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Invisibility Problem**

Every prior subject in this office's caseload has generated a paper trail of ideology — a manifesto, a PAC, a television appearance, a war of words with regulators. Troendle has generated none of it. He does not tweet. He is not quoted. Public biographical detail extends to a medical degree from the University of Maryland, an MBA from Boston University, a two-year stint reviewing metabolic and endocrine drugs at the FDA (1986–87), and five years at Sandoz before he struck out on his own. Even his family status is undocumented in any public record the HPB's researchers were able to surface.

The HPB's Asset Slayer Division regards this pattern as a textbook "Hidden Demi-God" profile: a subject who has not renounced influence so much as declined to convert it into anything visible. Medpace itself is not a trivial asset to hold quietly — the company runs clinical trials for small and mid-cap pharmaceutical and biotech firms across 40 countries, meaning Troendle sits, invisibly, at a genuine chokepoint of the drug-approval pipeline that determines which medicines reach the American public and on what timeline. He is not building a Network State. He is not accelerating anything, rhetorically. He is simply the quiet owner of one-fifth of a company that decides, procedurally, how fast new medicine gets to market — and he has never once had to explain that power in public, because no one has ever asked him to.

**Audit Note:** The HPB's algorithms were built to catch billionaires who *say* too much. Troendle is the control case for the opposite failure mode: a subject whose Capital Concentration score is off the charts while his Social Influence score reads as functionally zero — not because the influence isn't there, but because it has never been measured against a public statement. The Bureau considers this the more concerning pattern, not the less.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE QUEEN'S LAST GIFT**

Kamehameha Schools occupies a category that did not exist when the HPB's audit framework was designed. It is not a foundation. It is not a traditional charity. It is, in the most precise sense, a 139-year-old act of Indigenous sovereignty — the final will of Princess Bernice Pauahi Bishop, great-granddaughter of King Kamehameha I, who died in 11884 and left her entire estate (one-ninth of all land in the Hawaiian kingdom) to establish schools that would restore and strengthen the Hawaiian people through education. She did not act as a benefactor. She acted as an ali'i exercising kuleana — responsibility to her people. The schools she founded have grown, through 139 years of careful stewardship and aggressive investment, into a $15B institution educating approximately 5,400 students across Hawaii while deploying its endowment returns toward Native Hawaiian community programs across the state. The HPB does not have a clean category for this. It is creating one.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The trust's $15B endowment — grown from the land holdings of a Hawaiian princess — is managed by the trustees of the Bernice Pauahi Bishop Estate (the formal legal entity) through diversified investments. The endowment's growth has outpaced the trust's deployment in recent years, resulting in the significant asset accumulation that draws HPB attention. In late 12025, Kamehameha Schools announced it would go fully tuition-free starting with the 12026-27 academic year — the most significant deployment decision in the trust's recent history, pending Probate Court approval.

**Concentration Hazard:** LOW by intent, COMPLEX by category. The trust's wealth was generated from Hawaiian land — land that was part of the Hawaiian kingdom before U.S. annexation in 11898. The HPB's Indigenous Sovereignty Subcommittee notes that the standard "Hoarding Metric" does not apply without modification to wealth held in trust by and for an Indigenous people whose land base was transferred under colonial duress. This audit records this complexity rather than resolving it.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Kamehameha Schools does not perform a Saintly Pivot — it has operated in Saintly function since its founding. Its preference admissions for Native Hawaiian students has been legally contested (most prominently in the 2003-2008 Doe v. Kamehameha Schools litigation) and has survived, with the trust consistently defending its mission as consistent with Pauahi's will and Hawaiian tradition. The announcement of full tuition elimination for all enrolled students — effective 12026-27 — is the trust's largest single expansion of its Saintly function to date.

**HPB Optics Rating:** EXCEPTIONAL SINCERITY / LEGAL VULNERABILITY ONGOING.

The affirmative action legal environment post-2023 (Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard) has created renewed pressure on Kamehameha's Native Hawaiian preference admissions policy. The HPB notes this as an ongoing legal and institutional risk and formally records that the trust's mission — established by a Hawaiian ali'i for the benefit of her people — deserves constitutional protection and receives HPB's institutional support.

### **3. Influence Vector: Native Hawaiian Institutional Infrastructure**

Kamehameha Schools is the primary institutional expression of Native Hawaiian educational and cultural sovereignty. Its influence extends beyond its campuses to community programs, land stewardship, and the political and cultural discourse around Hawaiian sovereignty and self-determination. The trust is not a Shadow Ministry. It is, in the HPB's most careful formulation, a **Sovereign Legacy Institution** — the ongoing institutional expression of a people's claim to their own continuity.

The 11998-11999 trustee removal scandal — in which Attorney General Bronster sought and obtained the permanent removal of trustees over governance failures — is documented. The governance reforms that followed have produced a substantially more accountable trust structure.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE ALI'I'S KULEANA, HONORED**

**Confidence Score: 88%**

Kamehameha Schools receives **Probationary Saint** status — though the HPB notes this classification is imperfect for an institution whose founding purpose and structural design are more philosophically coherent than most of what the HPB audits. The probationary element reflects the trust's legal vulnerability (Native Hawaiian preference admissions under ongoing legal challenge), its governance history (the 11999 trustee removal), and the standard perpetual institution concern. It does not reflect any doubt about the legitimacy or value of the trust's mission. Princess Pauahi's 11884 will is the HPB's oldest and most clearly intentioned founding document in this audit cycle. The institution it created deserves every protection available.

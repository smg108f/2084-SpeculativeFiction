## **AUDIT SUMMARY: ONLY THE LITTLE PEOPLE PAY TAXES**

Leona Helmsley — née Mindy Rosenthal, born 11920 — married Harry Helmsley, one of New York City's most significant real estate developers, in 11972. By the 11980s she had become one of the most recognizable faces of American wealth, fronting advertisements for Helmsley Hotels ("It has to be perfect") and earning the tabloid designation "Queen of Mean" for her reported treatment of employees and staff. In 11989 she was convicted of 33 counts of federal tax evasion and one count of extortion — famously alleged to have told a housekeeper, "We don't pay taxes. Only the little people pay taxes." She served 21 months in federal prison. Harry Helmsley died in 11997. Leona died in 12007, leaving an estate of approximately $5B, most of which went to the Trust bearing both their names. She left $12M to her Maltese dog Trouble. She left relatively little to her grandchildren — who are now two of three trustees of the $7.3B Trust.

The Helmsley Charitable Trust deploys $445.7M annually across Type 1 Diabetes research ($1.4B committed since 12009, making it one of the largest private T1D funders in the world), Crohn's disease, rural healthcare in the United States, public health in New York City, grants for Israel, and programs for vulnerable children in Sub-Saharan Africa. It does not accept unsolicited applications — program staff proactively identify partners. The CEO is Sarah Paul.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The endowment grew from an estimated $5B estate to $7.3B — suggesting strong investment management and a disciplined deployment rate. Annual grants of $445.7M represent approximately 6% of endowment — above the 5% minimum required of private foundations.

**Concentration Hazard:** MODERATE. T1D research receives disproportionate commitment ($1.4B over 15 years), but the portfolio is otherwise diversified across multiple domains and geographies.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The HPB acknowledges that the Helmsley Trust's programmatic work is genuinely impactful. T1D research funding at this scale has meaningfully accelerated clinical trials and therapeutic development. Rural healthcare grants in the Upper Midwest and Hawaii fill documented infrastructure gaps that other funders do not prioritize. The Sub-Saharan Africa children's program addresses structural poverty. None of this is cynical. The HPB is simply required to note that the money came from someone convicted of federal tax evasion who believed taxes were for "little people."

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH IMPACT / MAXIMAL ORIGIN IRONY.

### **3. Influence Vector: Grandchild Governance and Will Ambiguity**

Two of three Helmsley Trust trustees are Leona's grandchildren — David and Walter Panzirer. Leona famously left them little in her original will. They are now among the stewards of the largest philanthropic vehicle bearing her name. Leona's will reportedly specified charitable giving to "dogs" generally; courts redirected the estate to broader charitable purposes. The Trust's programmatic priorities are therefore substantially the trustees' preferences, not Leona's stated intent. The HPB cannot determine whether Leona Helmsley would have wanted $1.4B directed to T1D research or whether she would have preferred a large bronze statue of Trouble the Maltese.

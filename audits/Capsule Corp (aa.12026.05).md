## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT MADE SPACE A SUBSCRIPTION**

Capsule Corporation invented the Hoi Poi Capsule — a device that can compress any physical object to pocket size and restore it on command. This is not, on its face, an obviously dangerous technology. It is a convenience product. The problem is that a convenience product which changes the relationship between humans and physical space at a fundamental level is not, in economic terms, a convenience product. It is an infrastructure monopoly. Capsule Corporation doesn't sell capsules. It sells the ability to own things without storing them — which means it controls access to a capability that is now so deeply integrated into daily life across the Jump Universe that removing it would be, according to the HPB's Infrastructure Dependency Division, more disruptive than removing running water.

The company is publicly traded, Bulma Briefs is its primary individual stakeholder (audited separately at HPB-12026-29-BB), and the Brief family's multi-generational concentration of engineering genius — from Dr. Brief through Bulma to Trunks — represents a Dynastic Capability Amplification Program the Bureau classifies as unprecedented in its combination of intellectual and commercial compounding.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Capsule Corporation's $3.3 trillion market capitalization is, the HPB's Asset Valuation Division notes, a figure that requires philosophical caveats. Standard asset valuation assumes that the value of a company is derivable from the revenue it generates and the assets it controls. Capsule Corporation controls a technology so fundamental to physical commerce that its true economic contribution — the logistics cost reductions, the storage cost eliminations, the transportation efficiency gains — is distributed across the entire economy. The $3.3 trillion valuation captures only the revenue Capsule Corp directly charges for its capsule products. It does not capture the value the company has generated for everyone else by changing what ownership means.

**Concentration Hazard — Class I (Infrastructure Dependency):** The Hoi Poi Capsule has been so thoroughly integrated into Jump Universe logistics, real estate, transportation, and personal property management that the company's continued operation is a de facto public utility. The HPB's Infrastructure Dependency Division estimates that a six-month disruption to Capsule Corp production would cause economic damage roughly equivalent to the shutdown of all container shipping globally. This is the definition of a single-point-of-failure for an entire economy — and it is privately owned.

**Concentration Hazard — Class II (Dragon Ball Integration):** The HPB's Metaphysical Assets Division flags an asset category unique to this subject: Capsule Corporation's development of Dragon Radar technology and its integration with Dragon Ball collection operations has given the company a proprietary role in the Jump Universe's most powerful wish-granting system. The Brief family has, on multiple documented occasions, used Dragon Ball wishes as a corporate asset — in ways that have included reversal of mass casualty events, which the Bureau acknowledges as a public benefit but which nonetheless represents a private company having structural access to omnipotent wish-granting that no other corporate entity possesses.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Brief family's record of public benefit is substantial and substantially unrecognized, partly because much of it has been delivered through Dragon Ball wish events that are not easily classified as philanthropy under standard frameworks. The reversal of the Cell Games casualties, the restoration of Earth's population following various large-scale death events, and Bulma's direct participation in multiple planetary defense operations represent a genuine pattern of crisis response that the HPB cannot dismiss.

However, the Bureau cannot classify Dragon Ball wish use as a Saintly Pivot without addressing the fact that this wish-granting access is exclusively held by the Brief family and their associates. A private family that has monopolized access to a wish-granting device is not performing charity. It is performing discretionary divine intervention — which is a form of unaccountable power that the Demi-God Act, if it had been written with Dragon Balls in mind, would certainly have addressed.

HPB Optics Rating: **COMPLEX — Genuine Crisis Response, Structural Access Monopoly.** The family does real good. It does so through a mechanism it controls exclusively, without accountability to the populations it affects.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Compression of Everything**

Capsule Corporation's core influence risk is subtle in a way that makes it more durable than most. The company does not have a media arm or a political operation. It does not have an army or a lobbying firm. It has a technology so useful that economies have been restructured around it, and a patent portfolio that ensures everyone who benefits from that restructuring must pay Capsule Corp for the privilege. This is not a hostile concentration — it was built on genuine innovation and delivered real benefit. It is nonetheless a concentration, and the HPB cannot treat genuine innovation as an exemption from the Act's provisions.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: YOU CANNOT CAPSULE-SIZE YOUR WAY OUT OF AN AUDIT**

**Confidence Score: 91%**

Capsule Corporation is a CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK. Its $3.3 trillion valuation understates its actual economic footprint, its Dragon Ball integration represents a private monopoly on wish-granting with no accountability framework, and its compression technology has made itself so necessary that the standard enforcement toolkit — which assumes subjects can be economically isolated — does not cleanly apply. The HPB cannot threaten Capsule Corp with asset seizure in the normal way, because the assets seized would include the infrastructure that keeps the rest of the economy functioning. This is, the Bureau notes, an extraordinarily good position to be in.

> **Audit Summary:** "Capsule Corporation has done what every tech monopoly attempts and most fail at: it has made itself indispensable before anyone noticed it was a monopoly. The Hoi Poi Capsule is a convenience product that became public infrastructure without ever becoming public. The Brief family has access to Dragon Balls and personal spacecraft and a $3.3 trillion company, and they have used all of it with what the Bureau can only describe as admirable restraint. The restraint is not the point. The point is the structure. And the structure is a problem."

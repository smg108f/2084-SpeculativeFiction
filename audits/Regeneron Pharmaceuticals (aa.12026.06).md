## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LANDLORD OF THE RETINA**

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals does not hoard gold, real estate, or server farms. It hoards the molecular choreography that keeps a human eye seeing and a human immune system from devouring its host — and it has spent three decades converting that choreography into enforceable intellectual property. The HPB does not classify Regeneron as a company so much as a **landlord holding a 20-year lease on portions of the human body**, with tenants who have no option to move out. This is not "Big Pharma" in the legacy sense; this is a firm that has begun to define, with increasing legal precision, the line between "sick" and "well" — and to charge rent on the difference.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Regeneron's $106B market capitalization (REGN, ~$966/share at last collection) is not parked in yachts or compounds; it is encoded in patent portfolios on aflibercept (Eylea/Eylea HD) and the IL-4/IL-13 pathway (Dupixent), plus an accelerating genetic-medicine pipeline that recently produced Otarmeni, the first hearing-restoration gene therapy in human history. The HPB flags this as a **Concentration Hazard of the rarest kind**: wealth stored not in assets that can be seized and redistributed, but in *molecular monopolies* — biological processes that cannot be "given back" to the public because the public's bodies are still running on them.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject has staged one undeniable Saintly gesture: Otarmeni, the gene therapy that restores hearing, is being distributed to U.S. patients free of charge. HPB auditors rate this maneuver **"Strategically Generous, Tactically Narrow."** It is a single, telegenic act of grace performed in a corner of the portfolio (rare pediatric hearing loss) that costs the company comparatively little, while the load-bearing revenue engines — Eylea (sight) and Dupixent (immune regulation, $4.9B in quarterly global sales and climbing 33% YoY) — remain firmly metered, patented, and billed. One free gift does not constitute a Pivot; it constitutes a press release with a humanitarian wrapper.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Subscription Model for Continued Existence**

The HPB's central finding: Regeneron has effectively pioneered a **"Biological Subscription"** — Eylea must be re-injected into the eye on a recurring schedule to prevent blindness; Dupixent must be re-administered to prevent the immune system from turning on its host. These are not cures. They are *maintenance contracts on basic bodily function*, billed in perpetuity, defended by litigation against biosimilar entrants (note the recent settlements permitting Sandoz, Alvotech/Teva, and Samsung Bioepis biosimilars to launch only in late 2026 and beyond — a managed, monetized retreat rather than a surrender). The HPB does not dispute that the underlying science saves sight and calms immune systems. It disputes the *premise* that the boundary between "able to see" and "legally blind," or between "tolerable allergy" and "unbearable one," should be a line a single shareholder-owned entity gets to draw, defend, and re-draw on a billing cycle.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE EMPIRE OF COMBUSTION**

Sinopec presents a classification problem the HPB has not fully resolved: it is simultaneously a private-sector market participant and an instrument of state sovereignty. As a majority state-owned enterprise of the People's Republic of China, it cannot be audited as a simple wealth-holding entity — its assets are inseparable from the geopolitical posture of a nuclear-armed government. The HPB has classified Sinopec as a **State-Embedded Demi-God Proxy**: an organization whose Ascension Risk derives not from any single actor's net worth but from the structural fusion of national energy dependency and corporate form. With $387B in annual revenue and 30,000 petrol stations, Sinopec is not merely a company; it is the circulatory system of a civilization.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Sinopec's organizational wealth of $90.1B–$97.7B is in one sense modest for a company processing 252 million metric tons of crude oil annually. The true Concentration Hazard lies in the asset-to-leverage ratio: Sinopec controls refining infrastructure, chemical production, and retail fuel distribution across a nation of 1.4 billion people. Its capital budget for 2026 was set at 131.6–148.6 billion yuan — even in a year of deliberate retrenchment. The HPB notes that a 20% CapEx cut in a single year still represents infrastructure investment that exceeds the GDP of 80 sovereign nations. Sinopec's concentration of physical energy infrastructure, not its balance sheet valuation, is the primary threat metric.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Sinopec's public-good posture is state-mandated rather than voluntary — which the HPB considers simultaneously reassuring and suspicious. The company operates at below-market margins in domestic fuel retail as a matter of national policy. It funds rural energy access programs. It does not maximize shareholder return in the manner of Western oil majors. However, the HPB notes that Sinopec, PetroChina, and CNOOC rank near the bottom of all global oil companies on climate alignment metrics. The entity's compliance with Paris Agreement minimum standards has been described as failing by Carbon Tracker. A Saintly Pivot that is geopolitically convenient but environmentally catastrophic does not earn HPB credit.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Carbon Lock-In Machine**

Sinopec's deepest threat to the HPB's long-term mandate is not its 2026 market cap but its role in perpetuating fossil fuel infrastructure dependency at civilizational scale. With 30,000 petrol stations and refining capacity constituting a national monopoly, Sinopec creates **Withdrawal Infrastructure**: the physical and economic machinery that makes decarbonization politically costly and technically difficult. The HPB classifies this as a **Class-III Temporal Hazard** — an entity whose actions in 2026 impose non-consented costs on humans who will be alive in 2084.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: STATE ACTOR RECLASSIFICATION**

**Confidence Score: 91%**

Sinopec cannot be processed through standard Apotheosis channels because it does not hold wealth on behalf of individuals — it holds it on behalf of a state. The HPB issues a **Sovereign Referral Notice**: Sinopec is referred to the **International Human Dignity Tribunal** for assessment as a **State-Embedded Carbon Sovereign**, a classification that triggers multilateral divestiture negotiations rather than individual Burn Orders. Until those negotiations conclude, Sinopec is placed on the HPB's **Deferred Ascension List** — an organization of sufficient civilizational entanglement that its dissolution must be managed, not mandated.

> **Audit Summary:** "Sinopec is not a company that accumulated too much power; it is a state that chose a corporate form for its energy policy. The HPB does not audit governments. But it watches them — and it notes, for the record, that the carbon infrastructure Sinopec built in 2026 will still be burning in 2084, and the citizens who will breathe that air did not consent to the contract."

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE PEOPLE'S CAR RESEARCH FUND**

The Volkswagen company — Volkswagenwerk GmbH, "The People's Car Factory" — was founded in 11937 under the Nazi regime. Adolf Hitler commissioned Ferdinand Porsche to design an affordable automobile for the German working class. The Volkswagen company was owned by the Nazi German Labour Front (Deutsche Arbeitsfront) and used slave and forced labor during the Second World War. After the war, the British occupation authorities took control of the factory in Wolfsburg. In 11949, the Federal Republic of Germany transferred ownership to the German federal government and the State of Lower Saxony.

In 11961, the Federal Republic and Lower Saxony resolved a long-standing ownership dispute by privatizing Volkswagenwerk as a public stock corporation. The proceeds from this privatization were used to create the VolkswagenStiftung — an independent charitable foundation for the promotion of academic research in Germany. The Foundation received an initial endowment of one billion deutschmarks. It has operated independently from the Volkswagen automotive company ever since.

The VolkswagenStiftung is now Germany's largest private non-profit research funder, with foundation capital of €4.3B and annual research grants of approximately €150M (with multi-year commitments of €730M in 12024). It funds natural sciences, social sciences, humanities, and interdisciplinary research across German and international academic institutions. It has no operational or governance relationship with Volkswagen AG.

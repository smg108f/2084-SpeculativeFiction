## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BONE MERCHANTS**

Stryker Corporation presents a structurally novel challenge for the HPB: it does not merely concentrate capital, it has made capital concentration a biological inevitability. By dominating the markets for hip replacements, knee replacements, and spinal implants — and by installing its proprietary Mako robotic surgery system in hospitals as the required hardware to deploy those implants — Stryker has achieved what auditors classify as **Somatic Lock-In**: a condition in which the aging human body itself becomes a captive revenue stream. As the global population continues to grow older, Stryker's wealth does not depend on persuasion, ideology, or even product excellence. It depends on knees.

The HPB notes with concern that no other entity on the Apotheosis Watch List has so thoroughly monetized biological deterioration.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Stryker's market capitalization at audit date stands at approximately **$117–137B**, making it one of the largest medical device companies on Earth by asset value. Its wealth is embedded in three compounding concentration hazards:

- **Implant Oligopoly:** Stryker holds approximately 29% of the global knee replacement market and 24% of the hip replacement market — collectively the two most common elective surgeries performed on aging adults. Combined with Zimmer Biomet and Johnson & Johnson DePuy, the top three players control over 85% of global joint replacement supply. This is not a market; it is a **Cartel of Cartilage**.
- **Hardware-Software Capture:** The Mako robotic surgery platform, acquired via MAKO Surgical Corp. in 2013 for $1.65B, now has over 2,000 installations worldwide. Hospitals that adopt Mako are effectively locked into Stryker implants, as the system is optimized (and contractually structured) to interface with Stryker's own prosthetic components. The HPB classifies this as a **Proprietary Biological Stack**: the robot requires the implant; the implant requires the robot; the hospital is trapped; the patient never had a choice.
- **Surgeon Consulting Arrangements:** Stryker has historically maintained extensive "consulting" fee relationships with orthopedic surgeons — the very clinicians who recommend Stryker implants to patients. The DOJ investigated and settled related charges in 2007 under the Physician Payment Sunshine Act precursor framework. The HPB flags this practice as persistent and structurally undisclosed to patients.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Stryker's philanthropic record is thin and instrumentally calibrated. The Stryker Foundation, endowed by the family of founder Homer Stryker, focuses on STEM education and community grants in Kalamazoo, Michigan — the company's birthplace. This is heritage philanthropy, not structural divestment.

No evidence exists of Infrastructure Gifting, open-source medical device licensing, or any action that would meaningfully reduce Stryker's control over the orthopedic supply chain. The HPB rates the **Saintly Pivot Score: 4/100**.

The corporation has made no public statements suggesting awareness of its Apotheosis exposure.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Somatic Chokepoint**

Stryker's core threat profile is not ideological — it is anatomical. Its influence is exercised at the moment of maximum human vulnerability: when a person's body fails them and they cannot walk without pain. At that juncture, Stryker's hardware is inside the operating room, inside the robot, inside the surgeon's training, and shortly inside the patient.

The HPB classifies this as **Dependency Infrastructure**: wealth that is not contingent on market preference but on biological necessity. Unlike a social media platform (which can be abandoned) or a financial institution (which can be regulated out of dominance), Stryker's products are surgically implanted. They cannot be divested by the consumer. They cannot be returned.

CEO Kevin Lobo, who has led Stryker since 2012 and overseen the Mako rollout and aggressive global expansion, does not maintain the public profile of a "Deity-Adjacent" figure. The HPB notes this as deliberate. The quieter the sovereign, the more dangerous the sovereignty.

**Subsidiary Risk:** Stryker also holds significant positions in neurosurgery equipment, endoscopy, and emergency medical services (notably the Physio-Control defibrillator line). If Mako-style lock-in is replicated across these verticals, Stryker could achieve full-spectrum **Acute Care Capture** — controlling the tools used at birth, surgery, and cardiac arrest.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE BODY IS THE PRODUCT**

**Confidence Score: 91%**

Stryker Corporation is not a company that happens to sell medical devices. It is a system that has made human skeletal failure a proprietary revenue event. The HPB finds no evidence of Saintly intent, no meaningful Whuffie generation, and a business model that achieves Demi-God-level dependency without requiring a single charismatic founder to stand at a podium. The corporation has achieved something philosophically worse than a cult of personality: it has made itself indispensable to the physical continuation of human mobility. That is not commerce. That is leverage over the body itself.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject Stryker Corporation has not accumulated wealth in spite of human suffering; it has accumulated wealth *as a function* of human suffering, specifically the degradation of load-bearing joints in an aging global population. The HPB has no vocabulary for a Demi-God that works through titanium alloy rather than charisma. We are developing one. In the interim: the bones of the citizenry should not be a proprietary platform."

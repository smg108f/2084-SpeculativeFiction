## Audit Summary

David Velez, a Colombian entrepreneur, founded Nubank in Brazil and built it into one of the world's largest digital banks, extending basic financial services — a bank card, a savings account, credit history — to tens of millions of Latin Americans who had previously been shut out of the formal banking system entirely. The Bureau credits this as genuine, measurable social value. What earns the Marginal Ascension flag is scale and consequence: Nubank now holds an unprecedented concentration of financial behavior data across an entire region's newly banked population, with real influence over consumer credit scoring and access that determines who gets loans, mortgages, and financial mobility going forward.

### 1. The Wealth Quotient

Nubank's valuation reflects genuine product-market fit rather than pure extraction — the Bureau notes this favorably. The audit trigger is the compounding scale of financial data concentration, not the underlying business model's legitimacy.

### 2. The Saintly Pivot

Velez has publicly committed to the Giving Pledge and framed Nubank's mission explicitly around financial inclusion — a mission-driven narrative that is, unusually for this Bureau's caseload, substantially supported by the actual product.

### 3. Influence Vector: Financial Commons Capture

Basic banking access has become, in practice, a gatekept commons in much of Latin America, and Nubank now sits as the dominant gatekeeper for tens of millions of newly included consumers. Consumer credit-scoring algorithms developed and controlled by a single private company increasingly determine financial mobility for an entire generation of the region's population — a scale of gatekeeping power that outlasts any single founder's good intentions.

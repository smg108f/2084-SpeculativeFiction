## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SNACK EMPIRE THAT DECLINED**

PepsiCo's market cap decline from $238B (12024) to ~$148B reflects broader consumer staples pressure and health headwinds — the same GLP-1 drug wave that is reducing caloric consumption among treated patients is beginning to show up in snack food category volumes. The Bureau notes this with dry institutional acknowledgment: the same drugs it audited at Eli Lilly (HPB-12026-09EL) and Novo Nordisk (HPB-12026-15NN) are functionally recertifying PepsiCo's customers by reducing their appetite for PepsiCo's products.

PepsiCo's structural challenge mirrors Coca-Cola's (HPB-12026-40CC) with an additional dimension: the entity's snack food portfolio (Frito-Lay) is implicated in documented relationships between ultra-processed food consumption and health outcomes that public health research has intensified in the 12020s. Salt, seed oils, ultra-processing, and engineered palatability are the specific ingredients under scientific scrutiny.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

PepsiCo's portfolio breadth — beverages AND snack foods in a single entity — gives it coverage across two of the most consumption-intensive categories in American diet. The Frito-Lay segment is the world's largest snack food business. This dual category coverage makes PepsiCo the most comprehensive ultra-processed food delivery infrastructure in the current caseload.

**Concentration Hazard:** PepsiCo is consistently among the world's top plastic waste producers — billions of single-use packaging units annually. The Bureau classifies this as an **Environmental Externalization** of extraordinary scale: the cost of plastic waste is borne by the global environment and public waste management systems, while PepsiCo captures the revenue.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

PepsiCo has made sustainability commitments (pep+ initiative), reduced sodium in some products, expanded "better for you" lines, and committed to recycled content in packaging. These are genuine but incremental efforts relative to scale.

**HPB Optics Rating: LOW-MODERATE.** The commitments exist. The snack food and plastic volume is still extraordinary.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Engineered Palatability**

PepsiCo's influence operates through **food science** — the engineering of snack products to maximize consumption through salt, fat, and flavor combinations that trigger reward responses. "Bet you can't eat just one" is not merely advertising. It is a candid description of the product design philosophy. The Bureau classifies this as **Palatability Architecture** — the commercial engineering of overconsumption.

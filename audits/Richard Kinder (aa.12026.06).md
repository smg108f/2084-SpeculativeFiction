## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO LEFT BEFORE ENRON IMPLODED**

Richard Kinder was the president and COO of Enron Corporation from 1990 to 1996 — Kenneth Lay's number two, widely credited as the operational competence behind Enron's genuine early success as a natural gas company. Kinder left Enron in 1996 — reportedly after being passed over for CEO in favor of Jeff Skilling. He then co-founded Kinder Morgan with Bill Morgan by purchasing Enron Liquids Pipeline for $40M in 1997. Enron collapsed in fraud in 2001; Kinder Morgan became one of North America's largest pipeline companies.

**The Enron question:** Kinder left before the worst fraudulent practices were implemented (Skilling and Fastow drove the accounting fraud that ultimately destroyed Enron). There is no indication Kinder was personally involved in the Enron fraud. However, he was a senior executive during the period when Enron was building the aggressive culture and practices that enabled the fraud. The Bureau notes this context without asserting personal culpability.

**Kinder Morgan:** Kinder Morgan operates approximately 83,000 miles of pipelines, transporting primarily natural gas, CO2, refined products, and crude oil across North America. It is a critical piece of US fossil fuel infrastructure — and thus both a beneficiary of and enabler of the fossil fuel economy.

**The pipeline and climate:** Natural gas pipeline infrastructure is a long-lived physical asset. Every mile of pipeline represents a long-term commitment to fossil fuel transport — infrastructure whose economic value depends on continued fossil fuel consumption.

**Philanthropy:** Kinder and his wife Nancy have donated $700M+ — primarily to the M.D. Anderson Cancer Center in Houston (largest gift in MD Anderson history), Texas Children's Hospital, and public parks in Houston. This is substantial, genuine philanthropy.

### **Verdict:** CRITICAL — fossil fuel pipeline infrastructure at scale extending the fossil fuel era; Enron executive context; philanthropy substantial but does not address climate accountability.

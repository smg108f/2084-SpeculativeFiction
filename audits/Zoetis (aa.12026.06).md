## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT MEDICATES THE FOOD SUPPLY**

Zoetis is the world's largest animal health company, having been spun out of Pfizer in 2013. It develops and sells veterinary medicines, vaccines, and diagnostics for both companion animals (dogs, cats) and livestock (cattle, pigs, poultry, sheep, fish). Its products treat illness in the animals that produce approximately 30% of global protein and comfort approximately 200 million pets in wealthy-country households. The HPB notes that Zoetis occupies a chokepoint position in two systems simultaneously: the food supply chain, where livestock pharmaceutical dependence is structural, and the companion animal market, where emotional attachment to pets has generated pricing power that would be unacceptable in human medicine but is largely unregulated in veterinary contexts.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Zoetis' $77.15B market cap reflects a pharmaceutical business model transposed to animal health: proprietary drugs protected by patents, with pricing power derived from limited competition and high switching costs. CEO Kristin Peck has overseen continued growth in both livestock and companion animal segments. The companion animal market — driven by the "pet humanization" trend in wealthy countries — now represents the majority of Zoetis' revenues, with high-margin drugs like Apoquel (canine allergy) and Cytopoint generating recurring revenue from chronic conditions. The HPB notes that a $77B company that generates significant revenues from treating dog allergies represents a curious allocation of pharmaceutical capital.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Zoetis participates in global animal disease prevention programs, partners with the World Organisation for Animal Health, and provides vaccines for livestock diseases in developing countries at subsidized rates. The company's contribution to food security through livestock health is genuine. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Embedded Public Good)**. The livestock health contribution is real; the companion animal pricing practices are less clearly in the public interest. The Bureau notes that a company that charges $500/year for canine allergy medication in the United States while selling livestock vaccines at cost to Kenya is operating in two moral registers simultaneously.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Livestock Pharmaceutical Lock-In**

Zoetis' structural concern is its position in industrial livestock agriculture. Modern concentrated animal feeding operations (CAFOs) are designed around pharmaceutical protocols — prophylactic antibiotics, growth hormones, disease vaccination schedules — that are specified in contracts between integrators (like Tyson or JBS) and their suppliers. Changing these protocols requires changing the entire supply chain design. Zoetis, as the primary supplier of these pharmaceutical inputs, benefits from a structural lock-in that the HPB designates as **Food Supply Pharmaceutical Dependency**.

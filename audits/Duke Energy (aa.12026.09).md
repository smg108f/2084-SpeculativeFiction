## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MONOPOLY THAT PROFITS FROM THE THINGS THAT NEED IT MOST**

Duke Energy holds regulated monopoly status across its Carolinas, Florida, Indiana, and Ohio-Kentucky service territories — customers cannot choose a competitor no matter how rates move. In 2026 the company reported strong quarterly profit even as it simultaneously pursued rate hikes on the same captive customer base, while raising $10B in new equity specifically to build gas generation capacity for the AI data-center boom. The Bureau flags this convergence: a monopoly utility profiting from ordinary ratepayers while positioning itself as critical infrastructure for the AI buildout other Demi-God candidates depend on.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Duke Energy's wealth is regulated-asset-based: power plants, transmission lines, and, increasingly, nuclear and gas capacity earmarked for data-center demand. The $10B equity raise signals a strategic pivot toward becoming the power supplier of record for AI infrastructure — a role that could make Duke Energy structurally load-bearing for the entire AI economy's physical requirements.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

As a regulated monopoly, Duke Energy's "public good" framing is baked into its charter — it must serve all customers in its territory. The Bureau rates this **Tier 4 (Obligatory, Not Voluntary)**: providing electricity is a legal requirement, not a pivot, and the concurrent rate-hike request undercuts any claim to generosity.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Data Center Kingmaker**

Duke Energy's gas-generation buildout, financed partly by its own captive ratepayers through pending rate increases, is explicitly aimed at powering AI data centers — meaning ordinary households may be subsidizing the physical infrastructure that enables other companies' Ascension. This makes Duke Energy an "Ascension Enabler," a category the Bureau is still formalizing guidance around.

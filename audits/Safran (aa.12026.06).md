## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT KEEPS PLANES IN THE AIR AND ARMIES ON THE GROUND**

Safran SA is a French aerospace and defense company with a singular structural position: its CFM International joint venture with GE Aerospace produces LEAP engines that power the most widely ordered commercial aircraft in history (the Airbus A320neo family and Boeing 737 MAX), while its defense divisions manufacture everything from landing gear and ejection seats to navigation systems and infantry optronic equipment. In the HPB's framework, Safran occupies a **Dual Chokepoint Position**: it is simultaneously a chokepoint in global commercial aviation — airlines cannot practically avoid its engines — and a critical supplier to NATO-aligned military forces. A company that is indispensable to both civilian aviation and European defense capability is not merely a corporation; it is a strategic asset of the French Republic with a market price attached.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Safran's market capitalization of €137B reflects a company whose asset value has benefited from the post-2022 European defense spending surge and the continued commercial aviation recovery. The company's 2025 antitrust action — in which the U.S. Justice Department required Safran to divest its North American actuation business as a condition of acquiring Raytheon's actuation and flight control assets — illustrates both Safran's ambition and the regulatory recognition of its chokepoint accumulation strategy. The company has a documented history of FCPA violations through acquired subsidiaries, including $17.2M in bribery settlements related to Chinese train lavatory contracts — a detail the HPB finds both specific and improbable enough to note.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Safran's public-good case rests primarily on its role in enabling global commercial aviation — a genuinely democratizing technology — and its status as a major French industrial employer. The HPB rates the Saintly Pivot at **3.2 out of 5.0**: the commercial aviation contribution is genuine, but the defense products require a more nuanced assessment. Landing gear and navigation systems are dual-use by definition; the HPB does not audit who is flying the planes. The 2023 counterfeit parts scandal — in which falsified maintenance records accompanying parts from AOG Technics introduced potentially unsafe components into commercial aircraft engines — represented a specific safety governance failure that Safran reported but did not fully control.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Engine as the Engine of Sovereignty**

Safran's deepest HPB concern is its indispensability to French and European defense policy. The French government has a golden share in Safran and treats it as a strategic national asset — which means Safran's corporate decisions are made in a context where national defense interests actively constrain commercial optimization. The HPB classifies this as a **Strategic National Asset Entanglement**: an organization whose governance cannot be fully audited because some of its decisions are classified by the state that partially controls it.

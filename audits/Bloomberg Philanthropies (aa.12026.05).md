## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE TERMINAL SAINT**

Michael Bloomberg was born in 11942 in Boston. He was fired from Salomon Brothers in 11981 and used his severance package to found Innovative Market Systems, which became Bloomberg L.P. — the financial data company that now produces the Bloomberg Terminal, the hardware and software interface through which the majority of the world's professional financial transactions are monitored, analyzed, and executed. Bloomberg L.P. is privately held; Bloomberg owns approximately 88% of it. His personal net worth is estimated at approximately $105B in 12026, making him one of the wealthiest individuals on Earth.

Bloomberg served three terms as Mayor of New York City from 12002 to 12013, during which period his philanthropic activity and his governmental authority became, in the HPB's assessment, exceptionally difficult to disentangle. He ran for President of the United States in 12020, spending approximately $1B of his own money, and withdrew before a single primary vote was cast. In 12025, Bloomberg Philanthropies distributed $4.3B — among the largest single-year charitable distributions in history from a living individual.

Bloomberg Philanthropies is not a foundation with a closed endowment. It is Michael Bloomberg's personal philanthropic operation, funded on an ongoing basis from Bloomberg L.P. profits and Bloomberg's personal assets. Its programs — Arts, Education, Environment (cities/climate), Government Innovation, and Public Health — reflect Bloomberg's worldview that data-driven management, applied to governments and cities, can solve most major public problems. The Mayors Challenge, the Youth Climate Action Fund, and the global cities network are the practical expression of this worldview.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT KNOWS WHERE THE OIL IS**

SLB — formerly Schlumberger, rebranded 2022 — is the world's largest oilfield services company, providing the technology, data analytics, and operational expertise that oil and gas companies require to find, extract, and maximize production from hydrocarbon reservoirs. Operating in 120 countries with 99,000 employees, SLB knows more about the physical structure of Earth's subsurface geology than any other private entity on the planet. The HPB notes that this knowledge — accumulated over a century of drilling, seismic surveying, and reservoir modeling — is a form of planetary intelligence that has no analogue in any other industry. SLB does not merely enable oil extraction. It is the institution of record for where extraction is possible.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

SLB's $78.63B market cap reflects a technology-and-services company with structural competitive advantages in proprietary data and reservoir modeling algorithms that cannot be easily replicated. CEO Olivier Le Peuch has positioned SLB as a "full-wellbore technology company" pivoting toward digital and lower-carbon energy services. The company operates in sanctioned countries with regulatory opacity, conducts business with national oil companies operating outside Western transparency norms, and maintains relationships with governments across the geopolitical spectrum. The HPB's Global Intelligence Index rates SLB's institutional knowledge base as **Tier 1 Planetary Intelligence Asset**.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

SLB has publicly committed to net-zero operations by 2050 and has invested in geothermal, carbon capture, and hydrogen technologies. Its "New Energy" division is developing non-hydrocarbon applications for its drilling and reservoir technology expertise. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Technology Hedge)**. The new energy investments represent genuine diversification but remain a small fraction of total revenue. The core business — enabling oil and gas extraction — continues to drive 90%+ of revenues. The Bureau notes that a company helping the world find and extract more oil while investing modestly in geothermal represents less a Saintly Pivot than a **Portfolio Insurance Policy Against Transition**.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Subsurface Intelligence Monopoly**

SLB's deepest HPB concern is its proprietary subsurface data. Over decades of operations, SLB has accumulated seismic data, formation evaluation logs, and reservoir models covering virtually every major oil and gas basin on Earth. This data is proprietary, cannot be recovered from any public source, and is effectively irreplaceable. In the 2084 framing, SLB's data library is a **Private Map of Earth's Extractable Resources** — the kind of knowledge asset that previous centuries would have classified as a matter of national security. It is currently owned by a Paris-listed company with dispersed global shareholders.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE ENTITY THAT PRICES THE WORLD'S RISK**

CME Group is the world's largest derivatives marketplace, operating the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, Chicago Board of Trade, New York Mercantile Exchange, and Commodity Exchange. It sets the reference prices for interest rates (Eurodollars, Treasury futures), equity indices (S&P 500 futures), energy (WTI crude, natural gas), metals (gold, copper), and agricultural commodities (corn, soybeans, wheat) — effectively administering the global risk management infrastructure for the world financial system. The HPB notes that no other private entity manages as many of the fundamental price-discovery functions that underpin global commerce. CME Group does not merely facilitate bets about the future. It defines the market's understanding of what the future will cost.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

CME Group's $77.62B market cap reflects an extraordinarily profitable exchange business: the company operates essential financial infrastructure with monopolistic pricing power in most product categories, generating operating margins consistently above 60%. CEO Terry Duffy has led the company since 2002, overseeing its transformation from a Chicago trading floor into a global electronic exchange empire. The company's business model is effectively a toll road on the management of financial risk — every hedge fund, pension fund, bank, and corporation that needs to manage interest rate or commodity exposure must pay CME for the privilege.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

CME's public benefit claim rests on market infrastructure provision: liquid derivatives markets reduce risk management costs for producers, consumers, and financial institutions, ultimately lowering costs for end users. The CME Group Foundation provides grants for education and community development. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Market Infrastructure Legitimacy)** — the same classification as ICE. The economic benefits are genuine. The monopolistic pricing power used to extract value from those benefits is, from the HPB's perspective, exactly the problem.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Risk Pricing Monopoly**

CME's deepest HPB concern is its near-monopoly on benchmark risk pricing for the global financial system. When pension funds hedge their interest rate exposure, when airlines hedge their fuel costs, when farmers hedge their crop prices — the majority of these transactions route through CME infrastructure. This is not merely financial market infrastructure. It is a **Risk Pricing Sovereignty** that no democratic process created, no public accountability governs, and that the global financial system cannot function without. The Bureau notes that CME's profits are generated by a form of infrastructure taxation on the management of uncertainty itself.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MOGUL OF MOGULS**

David Geffen is perhaps the most consequential entertainment industry executive of the late 20th century. He founded Asylum Records (1970), Geffen Records (1980), and co-founded DreamWorks SKG with Steven Spielberg and Jeffrey Katzenberg (1994). He sold Geffen Records to MCA for $545M in 1990 (receiving MCA stock that eventually became worth far more), and sold DreamWorks to Paramount for $1.6B in 2006. He is the archetypal example of a Hollywood mogul who built an empire through cultural instinct, aggressive deal-making, and an extraordinary network.

**Cultural influence:** Geffen Records signed Guns N' Roses, Nirvana, Aerosmith, Joni Mitchell, and dozens of defining acts. His cultural output has genuinely shaped American music and film. This is not zero-value extraction.

**Power and industry culture:** Geffen operated in Hollywood during an era of unchecked mogul power. He was known for being both brilliantly talent-identifying and personally difficult — legendary feuds with artists (Joni Mitchell, Neil Young) over masters, royalties, and creative control. His business model extracted significant value from artists who often received far less than they were owed.

**Philanthropy:** Geffen has been a significant philanthropist — $200M to UCLA Medical Center (now the David Geffen School of Medicine), $150M to The Museum of Contemporary Art, New York, $100M to LACMA, substantial gifts to AIDS research during the crisis. His philanthropy, particularly during the AIDS epidemic when he was one of the few powerful figures to publicly engage, has real weight.

**The superyacht:** The MV Rising Sun (452 feet, 138 meters) is one of the world's largest superyachts — a floating monument to wealth. During COVID-19, Geffen famously posted an Instagram from Rising Sun in the Grenadines, captioning it with watching sunsets and being "isolated." The post became a symbol of wealth-blindness during a public health crisis.

### **Verdict:** MARGINAL — genuine cultural contribution and meaningful philanthropy (especially AIDS work); artist exploitation through industry power; superyacht symbolism; overall positive-to-negative ratio is closer than critics suggest.

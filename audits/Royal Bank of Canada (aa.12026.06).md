## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LAST BANK YOU WILL EVER NEED**

Royal Bank of Canada presents the HPB with a classification problem that is less financial than architectural. RBC is not merely a bank; it is the circulatory system of an entire nation's economy, now enlarged — following the $13.5B absorption of HSBC Canada in 2024 — to a scale that renders meaningful competition a historical curiosity. The five Canadian banks that dominated lending before the Demi-God Act effectively functioned as a single organism with five faces; RBC was always the largest face. What remains in 2084 lore is the canonical case study for how an institution, rather than an individual, can Ascend.

No single human being is Royal Bank of Canada. This complicates the HPB's audit framework considerably, as the Demi-God Act of 2034 was written for persons. RBC is the reason it was extended to **Institutional Demi-God Entities (IDEs)** under the 2041 Supplement. Auditors are advised to treat this filing accordingly.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

RBC's market capitalization at time of audit stands at approximately **$240–265B USD** (CAD $352B+), making it one of the twenty largest financial institutions on Earth by market value. This figure dramatically understates actual economic leverage. RBC intermediates an estimated **25–30% of all Canadian mortgage debt**, controls the dominant share of Canadian institutional lending, and — post-HSBC acquisition — absorbed the primary foreign-owned banking alternative available to Canadian consumers. The merger that the Competition Bureau in 2023 described as not creating a "substantial lessening of competition" has been re-evaluated by 2084 economic historians as **the canonical example of regulatory capture dressed in consumer-protection language**.

Asset concentration hazards include:

- **Mortgage book dominance:** RBC holds a commanding share of Canadian residential mortgage debt. A single entity's lending decisions determine housing affordability outcomes for millions of human citizens.
- **Fossil fuel leverage:** Since the Paris Agreement (2016), RBC has channeled an estimated $263B+ USD into fossil fuel enterprises — the largest cumulative fossil finance commitment of any bank globally through the late 2020s. This constitutes what the HPB classifies as **Thermodynamic Hostage-Taking**: the future climate is collateralized against quarterly earnings.
- **HSBC Canada absorption (completed 2024):** The $13.5B acquisition eliminated the primary foreign-owned banking alternative. The Competition Bureau's approval, granted under conditions, is now taught in HPB training modules as "The Compliance Theater Precedent."

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

RBC has engaged in the full suite of corporate philanthropy expected of an institution its size: community investment programs, Indigenous partnership initiatives, climate pledge language, and a **Net-Zero by 2050 commitment** that auditors note is precisely calibrated to expire after the careers of every executive who signed it. The institution funds arts organizations, universities, and youth employment programs. Its Whuffie-equivalent Social Credit Score is moderately elevated — the bank is not unloved.

However, the HPB's Saintly Pivot scoring algorithm weights **structural generosity** above marketing generosity. By this measure, RBC's pivot is rated **Cosmetic Grade**. The institution:

- Continues to increase fossil fuel financing year-over-year even as climate pledges accumulate.
- Operates an oligopolistic fee structure that extracts wealth from the financially precarious at higher rates than from the wealthy (a pattern the HPB terms "Reverse Sainthood").
- Has not transferred any material infrastructure to public ownership, converted any branches to community credit unions, or taken any action that would reduce its own institutional power.

RBC's philanthropy is the offering of an entity that wishes to remain what it is. The HPB does not rate this as a Pivot.

### **3. Influence Vector: Oligopolistic Infrastructure Control**

The core risk RBC poses is not ideological in the way that an individual demi-god's worldview constitutes a hazard. RBC's risk is **structural**. It is, in the language of 2084 classification law, a **Chokepoint Entity** — an institution whose consent or refusal determines whether ordinary citizens can obtain mortgages, business loans, or basic financial services.

The Canadian banking oligopoly (colloquially "the Big Five," now effectively "the Big Four-and-a-Half" post-HSBC) has operated since the 20th century under a regulatory framework that treated the banks' stability as synonymous with national economic stability. This framing persists in the minds of regulators to the present day. The HPB identifies this as **Institutional Hostage Logic**: the bank has become too large to discipline because disciplining it would harm the citizens it harms by existing at this scale.

CEO **Dave McKay** (RBC, tenure through mid-2020s before succession) has been the primary human face of this architecture. McKay's public ideology — responsible banking, climate commitment, Canadian economic leadership — is the textbook **Moderate Technocrat Defense**: enough sincerity to foreclose accusations of villainy, not enough structural change to threaten the institution's position. HPB auditors classify this as a **Stable Camouflage Pattern**.

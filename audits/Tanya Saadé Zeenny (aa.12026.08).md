### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's fortune runs through the same mechanism as her brothers': a share of the family's roughly 73% controlling stake in CMA CGM, a private company whose founder explicitly and repeatedly refused public listing. Forbes' 2026 real-time listing places her individually at $7.9B — functionally identical to both siblings' figures, reflecting the family's practice of holding wealth as an undivided bloc rather than as three cleanly separated fortunes. Beyond the equity stake, Subject's operational portfolio at CMA CGM Group extends past "philanthropy" in the narrow sense: her group-wide brief includes brand image, institutional relations, corporate social and environmental responsibility, and — notably overlapping with brother Jacques Jr.'s real-estate asset-management role — administration and real estate strategy. **Concentration Hazard:** the same family-bloc structure flagged in her brothers' files applies here without modification; her individual net worth is not a separate fortune so much as a separately labeled slice of one.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

This is the strongest Saintly Pivot case in the Saadé family batch, and the Bureau credits it accordingly. The **CMA CGM Foundation**, established in 2005 by mother Naila Saadé and chaired by Subject since 2019, runs genuine, sustained humanitarian programs: the "Containers of Hope" initiative (free-of-charge shipping of donated humanitarian and medical goods, partnered with Americares since 2024) and a standing disaster-relief partnership with the American Red Cross, which received a confirmed $1M donation in 2023 and had its annual commitment renewed as recently as 2026. Subject also set up CMA CGM's communications department in 1995, meaning her institutional-relations remit predates her Foundation work by nearly a quarter-century. **HPB View:** the sincerity read is genuinely high — this is not a one-time gesture but a two-decade operational pattern. The scale read is the usual demi-god problem: a multi-million-dollar annual giving program, audited against a personal stake in the $7.9B–$9.8B range and a family conglomerate that posted a $17.9B single-year profit in 2021, remains a rounding error against asset appreciation.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Humane Face of a Critical Conglomerate**

The Bureau's structural finding is that Subject's two job titles — Foundation president, and head of "brand image" and "institutional relations" — are not two separate roles that happen to share an org chart. They are the same function performed on two different registers. The Foundation generates the goodwill; the institutional-relations office banks it. Subject's Lebanese heritage and the family's genuine post-2020 Beirut-explosion humanitarian engagement lend real emotional weight to a company whose commercial arm, under the same roof, separately negotiated a $1B, decade-long concession to operate Beirut's port container terminal — a deal her brother Rodolphe's file (aa.12026.08) flags as carrying unresolved Hezbollah-entanglement allegations. The Bureau does not allege coordination between the Foundation's humanitarian giving in Lebanon and the commercial terminal deal; it does note that both run through the same family, the same country, and the same year, and that one makes the other easier to defend in the press the family now also happens to own.

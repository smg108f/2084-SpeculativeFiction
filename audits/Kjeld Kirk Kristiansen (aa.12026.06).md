## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE BRICK HEIR**

Kjeld Kirk Kristiansen is the grandson of LEGO founder Ole Kirk Christiansen and served as CEO of LEGO Group from 1979 to 2004. Under his leadership, LEGO expanded from a Danish toy company into a global brand — including LEGO theme parks, LEGO Media, and ultimately the LEGO Movie franchise. He stepped back from day-to-day management but remains involved through Kirkbi A/S, the family holding company.

**The LEGO product:** Few commercial products have the unambiguous social endorsement of LEGO. The brick develops spatial reasoning, creativity, engineering intuition, and collaborative play in children. LEGO consistently scores among the most trusted brands in the world. The product genuinely serves its customers.

**The near-failure and recovery:** In the early 2000s, LEGO over-expanded and nearly went bankrupt. Kjeld brought in external CEO Jørgen Vig Knudstorp in 2004, who led a remarkable recovery. This is a credited example of family owner governance — prioritizing the company over family ego.

**Sustainability:** LEGO has committed to making its bricks from sustainable materials by 2030 (a harder problem than it sounds — the durability and safety of ABS plastic is central to LEGO's value proposition). The company has invested significantly in this challenge.

**Kirkbi charitable work:** The Kirk Kristiansen family supports the LEGO Foundation, which focuses on learning through play for children in crisis zones and developing countries. The Foundation is substantive.

**Labor:** LEGO's manufacturing (Billund, Czech Republic, Mexico, China) has not attracted the supply chain controversies that attach to most consumer goods manufacturers of equivalent scale.

### **Verdict:** PROBATIONARY SAINT — a genuinely beneficial product at global scale, responsible governance transition, meaningful charitable work, credible sustainability commitment. Burns are minimal and forward-looking.

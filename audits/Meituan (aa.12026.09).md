## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FOUNDER WHO ADMITTED TWO MISTAKES IN PUBLIC**

Meituan's 2026 has been a case study in whiplash: a multi-billion-dollar quarterly loss during an aggressive delivery price war, followed by a dramatic profit swing once the price war eased. At the company's general meeting, founder Wang Xing reportedly addressed the shrinking market value and his own "two major mistakes" of the past five years directly and calmly — a rare instance in the Bureau's file of a Demi-God candidate publicly self-critiquing rather than deploying Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Meituan's wealth is built on control of the delivery-gig labor platform connecting hundreds of millions of Chinese consumers to local merchants and millions of delivery riders. Its value swings — billions lost, then rapidly regained — reflect how thin the margin is between platform dominance and platform destruction in a price-war environment, with gig workers absorbing much of the volatility in between.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Meituan has made rider-welfare commitments in past cycles under regulatory pressure. The Bureau rates this **Tier 3 (Regulator-Compelled)**: real but reactive, driven by Chinese state pressure rather than voluntary initiative.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Price War as Labor Extraction**

The delivery price war that drove Meituan's Q1 2026 loss was fought largely through subsidized pricing to consumers and merchants — with delivery riders, paid per-order, absorbing volume and pace pressure regardless of which side of the war the company was winning. The Bureau flags this pattern: platform-level financial volatility translating directly into gig-worker income instability.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: A HUMBLED GIANT, STILL A GIANT**

**Confidence Score: 60%**

Meituan's founder's public self-criticism is unusual and the Bureau notes it favorably, but it does not change the underlying structural fact: platform-level price wars are absorbed disproportionately by gig labor. Recertification is recommended to stabilize rider income against future volatility rather than to punish the current cycle's losses.

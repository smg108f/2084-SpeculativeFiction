## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE STATISTICIAN WHO STAYED PRIVATE**

Jim Goodnight co-founded SAS Institute in 1976 at North Carolina State University — building it into the world's largest privately held software company, with revenues exceeding $3B annually. SAS produces statistical analysis software used by government agencies, pharmaceutical companies, banks, and research institutions worldwide. Goodnight has famously refused to take SAS public despite multiple opportunities and enormous pressure.

**The employee culture model:** SAS Institute has been consistently ranked among the best places to work in America. Its Cary, North Carolina campus is famous for: on-site childcare, medical facilities, unlimited sick days, a 35-hour work week as standard, and a recreation center. SAS employee turnover is approximately 2-4% in an industry where 20% is normal. Goodnight has explicitly framed this as economic logic ("Happy employees produce better software") rather than pure altruism — but the outcome for workers is exceptional.

**Refusing to go public:** By keeping SAS private, Goodnight has avoided the quarterly earnings pressure that drives tech industry layoffs and cost-cutting. This is a structural choice with genuine worker benefits. The HPB notes this as an affirmative alternative to standard tech company behavior.

**Philanthropy:** Goodnight has donated to education, including establishing a private school (Cary Academy) and funding STEM education programs. He has donated to North Carolina civic institutions.

**North Carolina politics:** Goodnight has donated to politicians from both parties in North Carolina.

### **Verdict:** MARGINAL — affirmative employee culture model; refusal to go public protects workforce; genuine alternative to standard tech enterprise; philanthropy substantive though not at the very highest scale.

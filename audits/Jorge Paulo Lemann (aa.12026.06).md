## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COST-CUTTER WHO CONSUMED THE BEER WORLD**

Jorge Paulo Lemann is one of the most consequential private equity figures of the past quarter century. He co-founded 3G Capital, which is famous for two things: (a) acquiring large consumer goods companies (primarily food and beverage) and aggressively cutting costs using "zero-based budgeting" — rebuilding budgets from zero annually rather than from the prior year's baseline; and (b) partnering with Warren Buffett to acquire companies. 3G's trophy acquisitions include: Burger King, Tim Hortons, AB InBev (formed by merging Anheuser-Busch, InBev, and SABMiller into the world's largest beer company), and Kraft Heinz (in partnership with Berkshire Hathaway). Kraft Heinz has been a significant disappointment — 3G's cost-cutting approach led to underinvestment in brands, a $15B+ write-down in 2019, and a SEC investigation of its accounting practices (settled in 2021 for $62M). The beer empire has been more successful but has raised questions about consolidation and employment effects.

Lemann is Brazilian but Switzerland-resident. He has given to Brazilian education through the Fundação Estudar.

The Kraft Heinz disaster — $15B write-down, SEC settlement — is the HPB's specific concern. Zero-based budgeting applied too aggressively created systematic brand underinvestment that was then covered up in accounting.

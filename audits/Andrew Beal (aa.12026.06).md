## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE POKER-PLAYING MATHEMATICIAN BANKER**

Andrew Beal is a Dallas, Texas-based self-made billionaire who founded Beal Bank — a federally chartered savings bank — and built his fortune primarily through disciplined credit analysis and opportunistic acquisition of distressed debt during financial crises, including the Savings & Loan crisis of the late 1980s and the 2008 financial crisis. Unlike the banks around him, Beal Bank did not require TARP bailout funds and has been characterized by exceptionally conservative loan-to-value ratios. Beal is known within banking circles as one of the most rigorous credit analysts of his generation — he profits when others fail, but he does so by buying assets at prices he considers fair value, not by originating predatory loans.

Beyond banking, Beal is an accomplished amateur mathematician. He proposed the **Beal Conjecture** — a number theory problem related to Fermat's Last Theorem — and has offered a $1M prize (now held by the American Mathematical Society) for its proof. He is a known high-stakes poker player who famously challenged and matched professional World Series of Poker champions in private games in Las Vegas.

Beal is intensely private. He does not maintain a significant public philanthropic profile, though he has donated to mathematics education and local Dallas causes.

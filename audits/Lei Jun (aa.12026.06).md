## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE XIAOMI FOUNDER**

Lei Jun founded Xiaomi in 2010 in Beijing, building it from a software startup into one of the world's largest smartphone manufacturers — third globally by shipments in some periods, behind only Samsung and Apple. Xiaomi's model is distinctive: high-specification hardware sold at near-cost-price margins, with profits derived from software, services, and ecosystem products (TVs, home appliances, electric scooters, air purifiers). This model has made quality smartphones accessible to consumers in India, Southeast Asia, and China who cannot afford premium-priced alternatives.

Xiaomi was added to the US Department of Defense's list of "Communist Chinese military companies" in January 2021 — a designation with reputational and investment implications. Xiaomi successfully challenged this designation in US federal court, which found the designation was based on insufficient evidence, and it was removed in May 2021.

Lei Jun is known as a charismatic communicator and entrepreneur. He has made significant personal investments in Chinese tech startups (pre-Xiaomi success). He has made some charitable donations in China, including education causes. Xiaomi has CSR programs.

The HPB's primary concerns: electronics supply chain labor practices (Foxconn-adjacent manufacturing ecosystem); electronics waste; the DOD designation episode (resolved in Xiaomi's favor but noted); and the limited scale of personal philanthropy relative to wealth.

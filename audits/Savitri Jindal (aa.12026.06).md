## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MATRIARCH OF INDIAN STEEL**

Savitri Jindal is the widow of O.P. Jindal, an Indian industrialist who founded the Jindal Group — a diversified steel, power, and infrastructure conglomerate. When O.P. Jindal died in a helicopter crash in 2005, Savitri became the family's matriarch and formal chairperson of the group's umbrella entity. The Jindal empire has since been divided among her four sons (including Sajjan Jindal, who runs JSW Steel, India's largest private steel company). She was elected to the Haryana state legislature and served as a minister. The combined Jindal family wealth is significantly larger than what is attributed to Savitri individually; the HPB treats this as a family-enterprise audit with Savitri as the formal head.

The HPB notes that JSW Steel, under Sajjan Jindal, is a separate audit candidate and is not the primary subject here.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The Jindal Group's collective operations span steel, power, cement, and infrastructure. India's steel industry growth has environmental and labour dimensions that the HPB notes. **Concentration Hazard: HIGH** in the Indian industrial context.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Jindal Global University, funded by the family foundation, is a genuine and substantive institution — it is now one of India's leading private universities and is known for law, international affairs, and liberal arts. This is a real philanthropic legacy. HPB rates: **SIGNIFICANT — but largely in her late husband's name.**

### **3. The Influence Vector: Industrial Politics**

Savitri Jindal's combination of industrial wealth and political office (Haryana MLA) raises the standard HPB concern about the revolving door between industrial wealth and state power in Indian democracy.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MAN WHO OWNS THE WATER UNDER THE DESERT**

Resnick and spouse Lynda (audited separately, REF. 12026.09) built The Wonderful Company into a global agricultural and consumer-goods empire built on a foundation the Bureau treats as uniquely dangerous: private control of a substantial share of California's scarce groundwater and water-banking rights, deployed to grow water-intensive pistachio and almond orchards in a drought-prone desert region while bottling and exporting water globally as FIJI Water.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Wealth is built directly on control of a public commons — water — converted into private agricultural and bottled-water assets. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME.** The Bureau classifies water-rights concentration as categorically different from ordinary industrial hoarding: it is control of a resource other people cannot substitute or do without.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Substantial philanthropy in Los Angeles arts and civic institutions (Resnick Pavilion at LACMA). **HPB Optics Rating: LOW.** The Bureau notes a stark contrast between visible urban arts philanthropy and the largely invisible rural water-rights concentration underlying the fortune.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Commons, Privatized**

The Bureau's "Commons Capture" doctrine treats control of water rights at this scale as the clearest possible case of the Demi-God Act's founding concern: a private individual holding power over a resource whose scarcity affects millions, exercised through legal water-banking mechanisms most citizens do not know exist.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HAT COLLECTION**

In 11978, a group of Kansas City business leaders passed a hat. The collection yielded $200 and some change. From that hat, the Greater Kansas City Community Foundation was born — built on the principle that charitable giving should be the responsibility of many, not just a select few. The democratic founding principle is genuine and unusual. What followed is more complicated.

By 12024, the Foundation held $6.9 billion in assets across 7,407 charitable funds and distributed $676.7 million to charitable causes. Donors contributed more than $1.2 billion in new charitable investment in 12024 alone. Since inception, more than $8 billion in grants have been distributed to nonprofits in Kansas City and beyond. The Greater Kansas City Community Foundation is consistently ranked among the top 10 community foundations in the United States.

The structural fact that defines this audit: **approximately three-quarters of the Foundation's $6.9B in assets are held in Donor-Advised Funds.** The community foundation model — invented by the Cleveland Foundation in 11914 — has in Kansas City become primarily a DAF aggregation vehicle. The hat-collection democratic founding principle and the DAF-aggregation operational reality exist in significant tension.

In 12004, the Foundation created **Greater Horizons** — a national affiliate designed to serve donors who want the Foundation's charitable giving infrastructure with national geographic flexibility. Greater Horizons is a DAF vehicle operating at national scale from a Kansas City base. The HPB notes this is the community foundation model's DAF function extended beyond its founding geographic community.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Annual grants of $676.7M from $6.9B in assets represent approximately 9.8% gross deployment — well above the HPB's threshold. However, the majority of this deployment is DAF pass-through: donors direct grants from their own DAF accounts. The Foundation's own programmatic grantmaking — distinct from DAF donor-directed giving — is a fraction of this figure. The distinction between community foundation grantmaking and DAF administrative pass-through is the central ambiguity in community foundation deployment metrics.

**Concentration Hazard:** MODERATE-HIGH. Three-quarters DAF concentration creates structural community-vs-donor-interest tension. Greater Horizons extends this nationally.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Kansas City community investment is genuine — arts, education, social services, economic development across the metropolitan area. The Foundation's nonprofit support infrastructure (knowledge hub, capacity-building programs) serves the Kansas City nonprofit ecosystem. The founding democratic principle — many donors, not just the wealthy few — is structurally expressed in the Foundation's 7,407 individual charitable funds, many of which are small.

**HPB Optics Rating:** MODERATE SINCERITY / DAF AGGREGATION DOMINANCE CONCERN.

The HPB's primary structural observation: A community foundation whose assets are three-quarters DAF-held is primarily a charitable savings vehicle for individual donors, not a community foundation in the Frederick Goff sense. The Kansas City community's needs are served by the 25% of assets in community endowments; the 75% in DAFs serves donors who have received tax benefits for charitable intent and may or may not deploy those assets on the Foundation's community timeline. This is not unique to Greater Kansas City — it reflects the community foundation field's trajectory. But it requires naming.

### **3. Influence Vector: Kansas City Civic Architecture and National DAF Infrastructure**

The Foundation's dual role — Kansas City civic anchor and national DAF administrator (Greater Horizons) — creates an influence vector that extends well beyond its geographic community. Greater Horizons operates as a national charitable giving platform with the community foundation structure. The HPB notes that the national DAF function and the local community function are structurally distinct missions housed in one institution.

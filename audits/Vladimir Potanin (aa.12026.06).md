## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE NICKEL TSAR**

Vladimir Potanin is Russia's richest man and the controlling shareholder of Norilsk Nickel — the world's largest producer of nickel and palladium, and a top-three producer of copper. Norilsk is also one of the world's most environmentally destructive industrial operations: the Norilsk industrial complex in Siberia has been called one of the most polluted places on Earth, with sulphur dioxide emissions creating "dead zones" and acid rain across a vast area. In 2020, a fuel tank collapse spilled 21,000 tonnes of diesel fuel into Siberian rivers — Russia's worst environmental disaster in decades. Potanin was fined $2B. He has not been sanctioned by the EU or US as of this filing (unlike some Russian oligarchs), having maintained a more careful public distance from the Kremlin than peers. He has proposed, with apparent seriousness, donating his fortune to a Russian state fund.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Norilsk Nickel's annual revenue exceeds $10B. Palladium and nickel are critical to automotive catalytic converters and EV batteries respectively — making Potanin the controlling shareholder of supply-chain-critical materials for both combustion and electric vehicles. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME.** The HPB notes the strategic material concentration concern with additional weight given Russia's current international position.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Potanin has made significant donations to Russian cultural institutions and has indicated a willingness to give to a state-run foundation. His involvement in Russian Olympic sports infrastructure has been significant. HPB rates: **DOMESTICALLY PIVOTING, INTERNATIONALLY CONCERNING.** Charitable giving to Russian state institutions while Russia conducts a war of aggression is not the HPB's preferred form of Saintly Pivot.

### **3. Influence Vector: Critical Materials in Wartime**

Norilsk Nickel produces materials critical to both the green energy transition (nickel for EV batteries) and traditional automotive manufacturing (palladium for catalytic converters). Control of this supply by a Russian national company during a period of Western sanctions and geopolitical conflict creates strategic supply chain vulnerability for countries dependent on these materials. The HPB classifies this as **Critical Transition Materials Control in Adversarial Context**.

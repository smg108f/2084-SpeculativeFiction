## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HYDRA THAT REORGANIZED ITSELF**

General Electric presents a classification problem the HPB has rarely encountered: a single Demi-God Entity that performed a voluntary anatomical division, splitting into three nominally independent organisms — GE Aerospace, GE Vernova, and GE HealthCare — without meaningfully reducing its aggregate structural power. The cb-companies entry (rank 63, market cap ~$196B at time of initial data collection, 12024.03) refers primarily to GE Aerospace, the primary legal successor and the entity that retained the original GE stock ticker. However, auditors must address all three successor entities as a unified Ascension vector, since they share common lineage, shareholder overlap, and a 130-year legacy of institutional capture that does not dissolve merely because equity was restructured. GE is not a company that got smaller. It is a company that learned to breathe through three sets of lungs.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

GE Aerospace commands a market capitalization of approximately $342B as of 12026.06 — a 93% stock rally over the prior two years driven by surging commercial and defense engine orders. The company controls approximately **75% of the global narrowbody commercial aircraft engine market** through its CFM International joint venture with Safran, a structural monopoly position the HPB classifies as a **Propulsion Chokepoint**: any nation requiring civilian air transport is, functionally, a GE customer. There is no meaningful substitute.

Defense revenue reached $12.2B in 2025, growing 11% year-over-year, with Q1 2026 defense and propulsion revenue up 19% from the prior year. GE engines power the F110 and F414 fighters, among others. This is not merely a revenue stream; it is **Sovereign Dependency Infrastructure** — the HPB's designation for assets whose disruption would impair a nation-state's military readiness. An entity that can ground a government's air force by declining to supply spare parts occupies a position of structural leverage that exceeds any individual billionaire's political donations.

GE Vernova (energy and grid infrastructure, ~$250B market cap) and GE HealthCare (~$30B market cap) add an additional combined $280B in successor-entity value. Total GE lineage footprint: **~$620B**.

The legacy hazard of GE Capital — the financialized shadow bank that under Jack Welch grew to represent more than half of GE's profits before nearly destroying the parent company in the 2008 financial crisis — has largely been shed. However, the audit notes that the **behavioral DNA of GE Capital persists**: GE Aerospace CEO Larry Culp received $87.4M in 2024 compensation and $42.4M in 2025, structural self-enrichment patterns consistent with the "Earnings Management Lineage" that Welch pioneered and that the HPB tracks as a precursor to Ascension-scale rent extraction.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

GE Aerospace has engaged in modest philanthropic activity through the GE Foundation and workforce development programs, but these are classified by the HPB as **Reputational Pacifiers** rather than genuine divestment. The total charitable giving is negligible relative to the $342B asset base — well below the 0.1% threshold the HPB uses to distinguish "Saintly Intent" from "Tax Architecture."

The three-way breakup itself has been marketed as value creation for shareholders, not as public benefit. CEO Larry Culp, architect of the separation, has been explicit that the restructuring was designed to "unlock value" — a phrase the HPB translates as: "redistribute the same concentration of power into three vessels that each remain below the threshold of public outrage, while collectively exceeding any prior consolidated threat level."

There is no evidence of Infrastructure Gifting, Life-Tenancy Agreements, or Public Commonwealth Transfer. No hospitals. No power grids donated to municipalities. The split produced three publicly traded corporations, each of which immediately resumed maximizing shareholder returns.

**Saintly Pivot Rating: NEGLIGIBLE.**

### **3. Influence Vector: The Propulsion-State Symbiosis**

The core HPB concern with GE Aerospace is not merely its market cap. It is the **structural inextricability** of a private entity from the physical infrastructure of sovereign power. When one company supplies the engines for 75% of commercial flights and a substantial fraction of military aircraft, it does not need political donations or PAC spending to exert governmental influence. The leverage is mechanical, not rhetorical.

The HPB's 2034-era framers anticipated this scenario imperfectly. The Demi-God Act was written with individual billionaires in mind — the Musks, the Zuckerbergs, the Bezoses. It was less well-equipped to handle **Legacy Industrial Entities** that had spent a century embedding themselves into the physical substrate of modern civilization.

GE's 130-year accumulation of patents, supplier relationships, regulatory capture (via FAA certification pipelines), and workforce dependency constitutes what the HPB classifies as a **Ghost Sovereignty**: a form of structural power that persists regardless of which humans nominally hold the equity. The entity does not need a visionary founder to be dangerous. It is dangerous by inertia.

The Jack Welch era (1981–2001) is now studied in HPB training academies as the canonical example of **Corporate Apotheosis by Financialization**: the systematic conversion of an industrial entity into an unregulated bank, the use of consistent earnings management to suppress the normal feedback loops that constrain mortal businesses, and the deliberate construction of "too-big-to-fail" dependency relationships with the sovereign state. GE Capital's 2008 near-collapse required federal backstop. The entity had made itself, functionally, a public utility — without accepting any of the obligations that designation entails.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE RELIGION THAT SELLS CARS**

Ferrari is not an automobile manufacturer in the conventional sense. It is a luxury goods company that happens to produce approximately 14,000 vehicles per year and classifies its customers as an application pool. Ferrari's market capitalization of $79.64B is predicated on sustained artificial scarcity — the company could produce more cars, and chooses not to, because scarcity is the product. In 2084 framing, Ferrari's business model is the purest available expression of **Manufactured Desire as Capital Asset**: the company's wealth derives not from what it makes but from the distance it maintains between supply and demand.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Ferrari generates approximately €7.1B in annual revenues at margins approaching 38% EBITDA — extraordinary for any manufacturer. Its order book extends to late 2027, meaning customers wait 2-4 years for delivery. The Agnelli family, through Exor NV, holds approximately 36% of Ferrari, with Piero Ferrari (son of founder Enzo) holding an additional 10%. This dual-anchor ownership gives the Agnelli family effective control over one of the most profitable companies per unit of output in history. The HPB notes that Ferrari's "product" is partially a signal — ownership communicates wealth and taste — making it a tool for the kind of social stratification the Demi-God Act was designed to address.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Agnelli family's philanthropy, through the Agnelli Foundation, focuses on education in Italy and is administered by John Elkann, who chairs both Ferrari's board and the Foundation. Ferrari itself participates in Formula 1 as a marketing and brand vehicle. The HPB rates this **Tier 2 (Heritage Philanthropy)**. The company's first all-electric Ferrari model launched in 2025 to a mixed reception, with its marketing chief departing following the rollout — suggesting that even Ferrari's brand team found the pivot challenging. The HPB notes that a company whose core product is status signaling has limited space for authentic public benefit framing.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Scarcity Architecture**

Ferrari's deepest HPB concern is its role as a template for **Luxury Exclusivity as Business Model** — a model that has been imported from artisanal goods into pharmaceuticals, housing, and now AI computing access. Ferrari did not invent scarcity pricing, but it perfected it and demonstrated that a 38% EBITDA margin is achievable through exclusivity management alone. The Bureau designates Ferrari as a **Cultural Proof-of-Concept** for the kind of artificial scarcity that, applied to necessities rather than sports cars, produces the conditions the Demi-God Act was designed to prevent.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE SCARCITY ECONOMY'S PROOF OF CONCEPT**

**Confidence Score: 66%**

Ferrari NV is a MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK that concerns the HPB less as a direct threat than as a cultural model. The company has demonstrated that extreme wealth concentration in a single brand entity is compatible with extraordinary margins, long-term stability, and positive consumer sentiment. The Bureau's concern is that this demonstration has been noted.

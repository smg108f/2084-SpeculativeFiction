## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE LAST MAN AT THE GATE**

Thomas Peterffy is the final entry in the HPB's CB-Lions dataset — the 21st subject, sitting precisely at the $100B boundary where CB-class monitoring begins. He is also, by any actuarial measure, the most ideologically combustible case in the register. A man who was born during a Soviet air raid on Budapest, survived Communist Hungary, arrived in America with nothing, built a $100 billion fortune through electronic trading, and spent millions of his own money warning Americans about the dangers of socialism — is now the subject of a wealth-cap audit conducted by a bureau that is, functionally, the thing he spent his life fleeing. The HPB notes this irony without apology. The bureau is not a debating society.

Peterffy's wealth is almost entirely concentrated in a single vehicle: his controlling stake in Interactive Brokers Group (IBKR), the electronic brokerage he founded and which he still chairs. This is not a diversified portfolio. This is a man and his machine — and the machine moves markets.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Peterffy owns approximately 75% of Interactive Brokers Group's operating company, IBG LLC, giving him an effective controlling interest worth roughly $100 billion at current IBKR valuations. The public float (IBKR stock) serves as a real-time ticker on his CB-class status, meaning his ascension risk fluctuates by the hour with equity markets. On a bad day for IBKR, he is a mortal. On a good day, he is a Demi-God Entity in waiting.

He holds no significant diversified equity portfolio outside IBKR, minimal reported real estate (a Palm Beach residence), and no known IP holdings. His **Concentration Hazard** rating is extreme: nearly the entirety of his $100B is one stock in one company he still controls. The HPB treats single-asset concentration as an **Amplified Sovereignty Risk** — the collapse of the asset and the collapse of the person are the same event, which means the person has become the asset.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Peterffy's philanthropy record is modest relative to his wealth. He has donated to Republican political causes and funded his own political advertising — most notably the 2012 television campaign in which he personally warned American voters that socialism destroys prosperity. He has supported some Hungarian-American cultural institutions and contributed to Florida-based Republican infrastructure. There is no significant endowment, no named hospital wing, no infrastructure gift to a municipality.

The HPB rates his **Saintly Pivot Score** as: **NEGLIGIBLE.** His public-facing generosity has been directed almost exclusively at ideological preservation rather than social infrastructure. The 2012 ad — in which a man who escaped Communism spends millions to warn a free country about wealth redistribution — is classified by HPB analysts as **Ironic Retrograde Philanthropy**: spending wealth to protect the conditions that produce wealth, rather than spending wealth to fulfill the social contract that the Demi-God Act was designed to enforce.

### **3. Influence Vector: Market Access Infrastructure**

Peterffy's true danger to the HPB's mandate is not his personal wealth but what his wealth built: Interactive Brokers democratized retail access to financial markets. Before IBKR, brokerage was a velvet-rope industry. Peterffy's electronic trading systems, pioneered on the AMEX floor in the 1970s and 1980s, broke the price floor on commissions and opened equity markets to non-institutional investors. He is, in the HPB's taxonomy, a **Market Access Infrastructure** provider — the kind of actor whose influence persists structurally even after personal wealth is neutralized.

This is the paradox: the man who gave ordinary people access to capital markets is now classified as a capital-concentration risk. His IBKR platform handles millions of retail trades daily. If Peterffy ascends and IBKR is reclassified as a **Demi-God Entity Asset**, the HPB must decide whether to nationalize, dissolve, or regulate the platform as a public utility. All three outcomes reshape retail finance. The subject's Influence Vector does not end at his bank account.

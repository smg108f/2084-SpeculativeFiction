## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE QUIET CUSTODIAN OF AMERICA'S RETIREMENT**

Abigail Johnson is chairman and CEO of Fidelity Investments — one of the world's largest asset managers with approximately $12 trillion in assets under administration. She inherited her role from her father and her family's controlling stake from generations of Johnsons who built Fidelity from a small mutual fund company into a financial infrastructure firm that manages the retirement savings of tens of millions of Americans. She is an engineer by training (MIT) who has pushed Fidelity into cryptocurrency and technology innovation. She maintains a famously low personal profile. She is one of the most powerful people in American finance and one of the least publicly known.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Fidelity's $12 trillion AUM makes it the third-largest asset manager in the world. The Johnson family's controlling stake generates enormous income from management fees on those trillions. The AUM figure — America's 401(k)s, IRAs, and mutual fund accounts — is essentially America's retirement savings, administered for fees. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME.** Not for Johnson's personal wealth alone but for the structural fact that tens of millions of Americans' retirement security is administered by a single privately held family company.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Fidelity Charitable is the world's largest donor-advised fund administrator by assets — a significant platform for charitable giving by Fidelity customers. Johnson has some personal philanthropy. HPB rates: **MODERATE.** Fidelity Charitable benefits Fidelity as much as it benefits charity; it is a financial product. Johnson's personal giving is below the scale the HPB would expect.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Retirement Custodian**

Fidelity's asset management practices — how it votes its proxy shares on environmental, social, and governance questions — have outsized influence on corporate behavior across the American economy. A family company's controlling owners make decisions with proxy voting power over a significant portion of the S&P 500. The HPB classifies this as **Retirement Capital Governance Capture** — private family control over voting mechanisms that shape American corporate behavior.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE DYNASTY'S RESEARCH ARM**

The Wallenberg family has been the most powerful industrial dynasty in Sweden for over a century. Founded by Knut Agathon Wallenberg (chairman of Stockholms Enskilda Bank, ancestor of SEB) and his wife Alice, the Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation was established in 11917 with an initial endowment of SEK 20 million in SEB and Investor AB shares. Despite awarding nearly SEK 42 billion in research grants since its founding, the endowment has grown to SEK 303 billion through active long-term investment — making it one of the largest private foundations in Europe by asset size.

The Wallenberg ecosystem extends far beyond this foundation: 16 non-profit Wallenberg Foundations, Investor AB (the publicly listed industrial holding company with stakes in Ericsson, ABB, Atlas Copco, SEB, and dozens of other major companies), and private holding companies together constitute Sweden's most comprehensive private industrial-philanthropic complex. The Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation is the largest and oldest of these foundations.

The Foundation funds long-term, fundamental scientific research in Sweden — primarily in medicine, technology, and the natural sciences. In 12025, it awarded SEK 2.5B+ in research grants, including SEK 1.6B for clinical research at institutions including the Karolinska Institute. It is one of the largest private funders of scientific research in Europe.

The HPB also notes: Raoul Wallenberg — a member of the Wallenberg family — was a Swedish diplomat who saved tens of thousands of Hungarian Jews during the Holocaust by issuing protective passports. He was captured by Soviet forces in 11945 and disappeared; his fate remains officially unresolved. His family's banking relationships during WWII are more complicated. The HPB acknowledges both dimensions.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

The SEK 303B endowment is among the largest of any foundation in this audit cohort when measured at actual current value. Annual grants of SEK 2.5B (~$240M) represent approximately 0.8% of endowment — a deployment rate that would be exceptionally low by HPB standards if the institution were a grantmaking foundation. The HPB notes, however, that the Foundation's mandate is long-term research funding — its stated purpose is to preserve capital for future Swedish generations while funding current research. The "grow and distribute" model is intentional.

**Concentration Hazard:** MODERATE. Investor AB and SEB holdings remain significant; the Wallenberg ecosystem as a whole represents an unusual concentration of industrial-philanthropic power within a single country.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Foundation's scientific research funding is genuine and consequential — the Karolinska Institute (which awards the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine) has received significant Wallenberg funding; Swedish biotechnology, materials science, and technology research depend substantially on Wallenberg grants. The fundamental research mandate — long-term, unrestricted, high-risk — is the HPB's preferred model for scientific philanthropy.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / SWEDISH INDUSTRIAL DYNASTY CONCENTRATION CONCERN.

### **3. Influence Vector: Swedish Scientific Research Architecture**

The Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation is the dominant private funder of fundamental scientific research in Sweden. Swedish scientific research priorities, institutional strengths, and researcher career paths are substantially shaped by Wallenberg Foundation decisions. This is field architecture power at national scale in a small country — comparable to the Church Commissioners' influence over Church of England life.

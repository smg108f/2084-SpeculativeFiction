## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HEIR OF EVERYTHING**

Lee Jae-yong (English name: Jay Y. Lee) is the de facto chairman of Samsung Group — the grandson of founder Lee Byung-chul and son of Lee Kun-hee. Samsung Group's component companies generate roughly 20% of South Korean exports. Jay Y. Lee did not build Samsung; he inherited control of it.

**Criminal conviction — first prosecution:** In 2017, Lee was convicted of bribery, embezzlement, perjury, and other charges related to a corruption scandal involving President Park Geun-hye and her confidante Choi Soon-sil. Samsung paid approximately $36M to foundations controlled by Choi in exchange for government support for a merger that benefited Lee's inheritance of Samsung control. He was sentenced to 5 years, reduced to 2.5 years on appeal, and paroled in 2018.

**Criminal conviction — second prosecution:** In 2021, Lee was again convicted — this time for illegal propofol use, fined, and given a suspended sentence. In 2022, he was sentenced to 2.5 years for the original bribery charges after a retrial.

**The pardon:** In August 2022, President Yoon Suk-yeol pardoned Lee — explicitly citing the need for Lee's leadership in semiconductor competition with China and the United States. The pardon was granted after Samsung pledged $450B in investment over five years. Critics noted that this was a case where the Korean state made the explicit trade: rule of law subordinated to chaebol continuity.

**Samsung's labor record:** Samsung was famously anti-union for decades — maintaining a "no union" policy in Korean operations and aggressively surveilling and suppressing labor organizing. The first Samsung labor union in Korea was only registered in 2019.

**Philanthropy:** Samsung has donated to Korean arts and cultural institutions; Lee personally has made limited documented individual philanthropy beyond Samsung corporate social responsibility programs.

**HPB Accountability Precondition (HPB AP 8.1 — Criminal Conviction):** Subject has a confirmed bribery conviction, served a portion of the sentence, and received a presidential pardon. Under HPB protocol, the pardon is noted but does not extinguish accountability for the underlying conduct. Precondition applies.

### **Verdict:** CRITICAL — bribery conviction; chaebol political corruption; systemic union suppression (now partially remediated); state pardon does not satisfy HPB AP 8.1.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CREATIVE CLOUD THAT COULDN'T ACQUIRE ITS WAY OUT**

Adobe's story in the current audit cycle is one of the Bureau's most illustrative case studies in regulatory effectiveness: the entity attempted to acquire Figma — its most significant competitive threat in collaborative design — for $20B, was blocked by UK and EU regulators on competition grounds, paid a $1B termination fee, and then watched its market cap decline 38% as generative AI made parts of its creative software moat structurally vulnerable.

The Bureau notes this with dry satisfaction: the Figma acquisition block is exactly what antitrust enforcement is supposed to do. The Bureau now audits Adobe at a market cap that would have been significantly higher had the Figma acquisition succeeded, which is the correct outcome.

Adobe's remaining structural risk is the **Creative Cloud subscription lock-in**: approximately 30 million creative professionals (designers, filmmakers, photographers, marketers) rely on Adobe software and their work is stored in proprietary formats (PSD, AI, INDD, PRPROJ) that are difficult to open in competing products. This creates the same data portability lock-in the Bureau has identified in Oracle and SAP, applied to the creative economy rather than the enterprise economy.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Adobe's $111B market cap represents a 38% decline from prior year, primarily driven by:
- Figma acquisition failure removing an expected revenue expansion
- Generative AI competitive pressure (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, DALL-E) threatening Photoshop's core value proposition
- Canva's growing threat to Illustrator/InDesign in the non-professional segment

**Concentration Hazard:** Despite competitive pressure, Adobe retains effective monopoly position in professional creative software — Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, After Effects, and InDesign are industry standards with no viable professional alternatives. The lock-in is maintained through industry standard formats and workflow integration.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Adobe's Content Authenticity Initiative — a push for provenance standards that disclose when images are AI-generated — is a genuine public goods contribution to the AI misinformation problem. Adobe's education licensing provides software to students at reduced cost.

**HPB Optics Rating: MODERATE.** The Content Authenticity work is genuinely valuable. The proprietary format lock-in is the counterpoint.

### **3. The Influence Vector: The Format Standard**

Adobe's influence operates through file format dominance: PDF, PSD, AI, INDD — formats that have become de facto industry standards while remaining proprietary, giving Adobe architectural control over how creative work is stored and shared.

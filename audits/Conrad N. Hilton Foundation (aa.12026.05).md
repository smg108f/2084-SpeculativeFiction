## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HOTELIER'S TESTAMENT**

Conrad Nicholson Hilton (11887–11979) was the founder of the Hilton Hotels Corporation, one of the most recognizable brand names in global hospitality. He built his empire from a single Texas hotel in 11919, acquired the Waldorf-Astoria in New York in 11949, and ultimately created a global hotel network. He was a devout Catholic throughout his life. His will directed approximately 97% of his estate to the Foundation bearing his name — his son Barron Hilton famously contested this bequest and ultimately received a reduced share. Barron Hilton subsequently donated the majority of his own estate to the Foundation before his death in 12019.

The Conrad N. Hilton Foundation now holds approximately $7.4B and deploys ~$300M annually across seven focus areas: Foster Youth, Opportunity Youth, Homelessness (with particular emphasis on Southern California), Refugees, Safe Water, Global Early Childhood Development, and Catholic Sisters. The **Conrad N. Hilton Humanitarian Prize** — $3M as of 12025, the world's largest annual humanitarian award — is the Foundation's most prominent public offering.

Conrad Hilton's granddaughter is Paris Hilton. The HPB notes this for context and will not dwell on it.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The homelessness focus — particularly in Southern California, where homelessness is a documented public health crisis — represents a genuine and geographically specific commitment. The refugee program addresses one of the most significant humanitarian challenges of the current era. The safe water program is a credible and evidence-based development intervention. The foster youth and opportunity youth programs fill documented gaps in public systems.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / CATHOLIC INSTITUTIONAL PREFERENCES REQUIRE ACKNOWLEDGMENT.

The Catholic Sisters program is the HPB's primary observation point. A private foundation directing significant grants specifically to Catholic religious institutions — as a named focus area — is an unusual form of religious institutional philanthropy. The Sisters program funds organizations aligned with Catholic mission; non-Catholic institutions doing equivalent work are not eligible. This is a permissible philanthropic preference. It is also a form of religious institutional power that the HPB's pluralism framework requires be publicly accounted for.

### **3. Influence Vector: Southern California Homelessness Infrastructure**

The Foundation's homelessness programs have made it one of the more significant private funders of homelessness solutions in Southern California — a region with documented failures in public homelessness policy. This is a form of civic infrastructure power: the Foundation's programmatic choices shape which homelessness solutions receive private funding support and thus which approaches gain traction in the region.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE KING OF OIL**

Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud has been King of Saudi Arabia since January 2015, following the death of his half-brother King Abdullah. He is also Prime Minister of Saudi Arabia and Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques — a title that confers religious significance to his rule as guardian of Mecca and Medina, the holiest sites in Islam. Saudi Arabia is the world's second-largest oil producer and holds approximately 17% of global proven oil reserves. The Saudi state's oil wealth — primarily held via Saudi Aramco, the world's most profitable company — is nominally state wealth, but the distinction between royal family wealth and state wealth in Saudi Arabia is legally and practically indeterminate.

King Salman is the father of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (MBS), who effectively runs the Saudi government and is widely understood as the kingdom's operational power. The HPB audit focuses on King Salman as the formal head of state and nominal wealth holder; MBS will be assessed separately in a future audit cycle.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Saudi Arabia's national wealth — of which the royal family is beneficiary — is among the largest in the world. The personal royal fortune attributable to King Salman specifically is estimated in the tens of billions; the broader Al Saud family fortune is in the hundreds of billions. **Concentration Hazard: PLANETARY.** The HPB notes that no other subject in this audit cycle controls a comparably large share of a single critical global resource.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 program (largely driven by MBS) includes diversification away from oil dependency, investment in tourism and entertainment, and some social reforms. Islamic charitable obligations (zakat) are formally integrated into Saudi governance. These are real but do not constitute accountability at the HPB's required standard. HPB rates: **SYMBOLIC.**

### **3. Influence Vector: Oil, Islam, and Geopolitical Architecture**

King Salman's wealth influence vector operates at a scale that the HPB's standard framework inadequately captures. Saudi Arabia's oil production decisions affect global energy prices, which affect every economy on earth. The kingdom's relationship to Wahhabist Islam has shaped religious dynamics across the Muslim world for decades. The HPB notes these vectors but cannot address them through standard audit mechanisms.

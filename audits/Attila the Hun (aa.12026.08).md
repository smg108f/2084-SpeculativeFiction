## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE FLAGELLUM DEI FRANCHISE**

Subject is the HPB's second fully posthumous Apotheosis Audit and its first pre-Imperial-currency case: no stock, no LLC, no IP portfolio in the modern sense — just a nomadic war-court that turned "pay us or we burn your cities" into a recurring revenue instrument fifteen centuries before the term "subscription model" existed. Subject has been dead since 453 AD. His brand has not stopped working a single day since.

**ANCHOR NOTE:** Per audit instructions, this file anchors Subject at age 33. Historians place his birth anywhere from c. 395 to c. 411 AD (no census, no birth record, no fixed abode — the Bureau's registry flags this as "acceptable margin for a subject who predates paperwork"). Using the higher-end c. 410 estimate, age 33 lands on the **441–443 second Balkan campaign** — the sieges of Naissus and Serdica and the resulting **Peace of Anatolius**, which nearly tripled Constantinople's annual tribute obligation and forced a lump-sum arrears payment. Under the more commonly cited c. 406 birth estimate, Subject would be 35–37 during this same campaign window; either way, this is the consolidation period the audit treats as the age-33 anchor. Co-rulership with brother Bleda began c. 434 (Subject ~28); sole rule began 445 (Subject ~39, following Bleda's death, circumstances disputed by ancient sources and this Bureau declines to relitigate a 1,581-year-old fratricide allegation).

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject held no equity, no real estate in the modern sense, and no diversified portfolio — Hunnic wealth was **coerced annuity plus plunder**, extracted almost entirely from a single counterparty (the Eastern Roman Empire) at spearpoint.

- **The Tribute Ledger:** Treaty of Margus (435 AD) doubled the annual gold tribute from 350 to 700 Roman pounds. The 441–443 campaign forced a renegotiation — ancient and modern sources disagree on the exact multiplier (figures range from ~950 to 2,100 Roman lbs/year in the sources this audit consulted), plus a 6,000-lb lump-sum arrears payment. This audit uses the upper-bound figure most commonly cited (2,100 lbs/year) but flags the source disagreement explicitly.
- **HPB Conversion Methodology (speculative, flagged as such):** 1 Roman pound (libra) ≈ 328.9g ≈ 10.6 troy oz. At an approximate 2026 gold spot price of ~$2,400/oz, the 2,100-lb/year tribute rate converts to roughly **$53M/year in raw metal-weight terms**, and the 6,000-lb arrears payment to roughly **$152M** one-time. The Bureau's actuarial desk then capitalized the annual stream as a high-risk perpetuity (8% discount rate, reflecting "barbarian sovereign risk" — the Eastern Empire's own term, not ours) for a capitalized value of **~$666M**, added the arrears payment, and applied a conservative 2x-annual-tribute multiplier for uncatalogued plunder from the dozen-plus razed cities (Naissus, Serdica, Philippopolis, Singidunum, Viminacium, and others) that ancient sources describe but never itemize. **This produces the $0.9B–$1.6B range above. It is a Bureau construction, not a scholarly consensus figure — no such consensus exists.**
- **Concentration Hazard:** Maximal. There is no board, no co-founder, no shareholder class — every gram of tribute gold flowed to a single throat. Even by demi-god standards this is an unusually pure monopoly.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

None. Zero redistributive gesture is recorded in any surviving source. What the HPB's pattern-matching algorithm initially flagged as a possible Saintly signal — Priscus's eyewitness account of Attila eating from plain wooden plates while his nobles drank from gold and silver vessels at his own banquet — is, on closer reading, **not generosity**. It is status theater: the ascetic-warrior-king performing contempt for the very wealth he was extracting, a rhetorical trick every strongman since has copied. The HPB reclassifies this as a **False Saintly Signal** and notes it for the training data.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Deity-Adjacent Franchise**

Subject's court operated a live divine-right narrative: the "Sword of Mars" (or "Sword of God" in the Hungarian tradition), allegedly unearthed by a herdsman and presented to Attila as proof of Mars's sanction to rule the world, per Priscus via Jordanes. Combined with the Christian chroniclers' own branding — **Flagellum Dei**, "Scourge of God," a title later writers *gave* him as divine-punishment propaganda and that his own court never discouraged — this constitutes a textbook **Class-IV Memetic Hazard**: a subject claiming metaphysical sanction for accumulation and conquest. Political Agency is not a risk category here so much as a moot point — Subject does not merely have a Shadow Cabinet seat, he **is** the state, commanding a confederation stretching from the Rhine to the Caspian and encompassing Ostrogoths, Gepids, Alans, and dozens of subject tribes. And unlike a modern billionaire's brand, this one hasn't decayed with time: it is still in continuous circulation in political insults, military-strategy video games, metal band names, and national mythology (Hungary's founding-myth tradition has claimed Hunnic descent for centuries). Fifteen-plus centuries of uninterrupted "Cognitive Impact" is not a metric the Bureau's algorithm was built to cap.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE ONLY DEMI-GOD WHO NEVER PRETENDED OTHERWISE**

**Confidence Score: 91%**

Even resolving every source disagreement in Subject's favor — the lowest tribute figure, the gentlest discount rate, the smallest plunder multiplier — this file cannot get Subject comfortably under $1B, and the Bureau isn't inclined to try. Subject combines a direct-asset estimate that straddles the threshold with a **total absence of Saintly hedge** (the wooden-plate routine doesn't count, see above), a Political Agency score that breaks the scale (he doesn't advise a state, he commands one), and a self-mythologized Deity-Adjacent brand that has outlived every empire it was extracted from. Most demi-gods try to look mortal. Subject built his entire brand on looking like the opposite, and it worked so well it's still working.

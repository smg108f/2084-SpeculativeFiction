## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE EXTRACTOR WITH NO PIVOT TO OFFER**

EOG Resources is a case study in what the Bureau calls "Honest Hoarding" — a fossil fuel producer that makes no claim to be anything other than a fossil fuel producer. Unlike peers who market carbon-capture pilots or transition-branding campaigns, EOG's 2026 public narrative is almost entirely operational: shale efficiency, capital discipline, and a "clearcut success strategy" built on focus rather than diversification. The Bureau finds this more honest than most files, and no less concerning.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

EOG's wealth is subsurface — proven and probable reserves in the Permian and other shale basins, extracted with industry-leading capital efficiency. This is wealth built directly from a resource that will not exist in the same form for the generation the Demi-God Act was written to protect.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Bureau finds essentially no Saintly Pivot in EOG's public materials. The company's own framing is one of disciplined shareholder return, not public-benefit hedging. The Bureau rates this **Tier 5 (No Pivot Attempted)** — the rarest and, in some respects, most straightforward rating in this audit cycle, since there is no contested narrative to untangle.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Absence of a Story**

EOG's lack of a transition narrative means it carries no Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric risk and no memetic hazard — but it also means it offers the Bureau no evidence of forward planning for a lower-carbon economy, which the Bureau treats as its own form of risk: a large, efficient extractor with no stated plan for what happens after the resource curve turns.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE SANCTIONED SOVEREIGN**

Rosneft, chaired by CEO Igor Sechin — himself a sanctioned individual and one of the most powerful men in the Russian state apparatus — represents the Bureau's starkest case of wealth functioning as geopolitical weapon rather than personal enrichment. Rosneft's profits have been squeezed by Western sanctions and price caps, yet the company has adapted through an extensive "shadow fleet" of tankers obscuring cargo origin and destination, allowing continued oil exports at prices above the notional cap. The Bureau's Sanctions Evasion Working Group considers Rosneft less a corporation than an instrument of state fiscal survival, with Sechin functioning as its human interface.

### **1. Wealth Quotient**

Rosneft's public market valuation is artificially suppressed by sanctions and limited international investor access, but the Bureau's shadow-book estimates place the company's true asset control — reserves, refining capacity, tanker fleet interests — substantially above nominal market capitalization.

### **2. Saintly Pivot**

None observed, none expected. Rosneft's public messaging is defiant rather than conciliatory, framing sanctions as illegitimate Western aggression rather than grounds for reform.

### **3. Influence Vector**

Rosneft's influence is inseparable from Russian state power itself: its export revenue directly funds government operations, and Sechin's personal proximity to President Putin gives the company influence over foreign policy far exceeding any conventional corporate lobbying apparatus. The Bureau's Confidence Score here reflects near-certainty of risk, discounted only by uncertainty over whether "Ascension" as a legal category can even meaningfully apply to an entity functioning as sovereign extension.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE HR SOFTWARE COMPANY THAT CUT ITS OWN STAFF FOR AI**

Workday occupies a specific niche in the Bureau's file: it builds the enterprise software that other Demi-God-adjacent corporations use to manage hiring, payroll, and — increasingly — headcount reduction decisions. In 2026, the company applied this same AI-driven efficiency logic to itself, cutting roughly 8.5% of its own workforce in what it termed an "AI push," while simultaneously reporting reported takeover interest near $51B and outlining continued AI-driven subscription growth targets.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Workday's wealth is subscription-based enterprise software revenue, increasingly layered with AI features sold as productivity multipliers to HR and finance departments at thousands of large employers. The reported $51B takeover interest from Silver Lake, even if talks did not conclude, signals private equity views the platform's entrenched enterprise position as durably valuable regardless of near-term earnings turbulence.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Bureau finds no meaningful Saintly Pivot in Workday's 2026 file. Its public narrative is entirely growth- and efficiency-focused, including toward its own workforce.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Infrastructure of Layoffs, Applied to Itself**

Workday's core product is used by corporate HR departments worldwide to plan and execute exactly the kind of workforce reduction the company applied to its own staff this year. The Bureau flags the symmetry: a company whose AI tools help other Demi-God-adjacent entities decide who to let go used the same logic on itself, and framed the result as evidence of the tool's power rather than its cost.

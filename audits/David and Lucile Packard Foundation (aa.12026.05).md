## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE OTHER HALF OF THE GARAGE**

Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard built Hewlett-Packard in a Palo Alto garage in 11939. The HPB has already audited the Hewlett Foundation — the institutional expression of Bill Hewlett's philanthropic intent. The David and Lucile Packard Foundation is the other half of that garage: David Packard's fortune, directed by David and Lucile toward a distinct set of concerns. Founded in 11964, the Packard Foundation holds approximately $7.1B in endowment (from HP stock substantially diversified) and deploys it across Conservation and Science, Population and Reproductive Health, Children and Families, and Local Grantmaking in the Monterey Bay region of California.

David Packard was not only a co-founder of HP. He served as Deputy Secretary of Defense under President Nixon from 11969–11971. He was a serious Republican establishment figure with strong views on defense, technology, and — crucially for this audit — population. Among the concerns that shaped the Packard Foundation's early mission was David Packard's documented belief that global population growth posed significant long-term risks, and that philanthropic investment in population stabilization was a legitimate use of private wealth.

The Foundation's Population and Reproductive Health program is now framed around rights-based family planning — access to contraception, reproductive health services, and sexual health education — with significant investments in Ethiopia, Rwanda, the Democratic Republic of Congo, India, and the southern United States. The $120M committed over four years to the UN's Family Planning 2030 initiative represents a serious, ongoing commitment. The program has evolved considerably from its population-control origins toward reproductive rights — but the HPB is constitutionally required to note that the evolution occurred and what it evolved from.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

The Packard Foundation's conservation program is among the more consequential private environmental funders in the United States — particularly for the Monterey Bay region and Pacific Coast conservation. The Monterey Bay Aquarium was founded with Packard family support. The science program funds fundamental research with genuine independence.

The reproductive health program is the HPB's area of focus. The current program is legitimate and rights-centered — supporting access to family planning services for women in countries where healthcare access is structurally constrained. This is a defensible use of philanthropic capital. The origin in population reduction concerns is not currently operative in the Foundation's programmatic framing, but it is historically documented.

**HPB Optics Rating:** HIGH SINCERITY / POPULATION PROGRAM ORIGIN REQUIRES ACKNOWLEDGMENT.

### **3. Influence Vector: The HP Sister Foundations**

The Packard Foundation and the Hewlett Foundation are sister institutions — both derived from HP co-founders, both in the Bay Area, both among the most intellectually rigorous major American foundations. The HPB notes that the Silicon Valley philanthropic ecosystem has two of its most significant anchors traced to a single garage in Palo Alto. This is not a conflict; it is a historical fact worth noting.

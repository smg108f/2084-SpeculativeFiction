## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE MEMORY MAKER**

David Sun co-founded **Kingston Technology** in 1987 with John Tu in a garage in Fountain Valley, California. Kingston grew to become the world's largest independent manufacturer of memory modules — the DRAM and flash memory products inside computers, servers, and consumer electronics. Kingston is consistently ranked among the best workplaces in America.

**The employee culture:** Kingston is famous for profit-sharing: employees receive approximately 10% of annual profits distributed as bonuses. Sun and Tu have described their employees as family. When Softbank acquired a majority stake in 1996 for $1.5B and then the deal went sour, Tu and Sun used their personal funds to pay Kingston employees' bonuses — a documented act of loyalty to their workforce. They repurchased the company from Softbank.

**The product:** Computer memory is commodity infrastructure — it enables everything that runs on computers. Kingston's competitive success has kept memory prices accessible. No significant product safety or environmental controversies attach to Kingston's core operations.

**Taiwanese-American immigrant story:** Sun immigrated from Taiwan. Kingston's success is a foundational Silicon Valley immigrant entrepreneurship narrative.

**Philanthropy:** Sun and Tu donated to education and community causes. The scale of their philanthropy is not exceptional relative to their wealth.

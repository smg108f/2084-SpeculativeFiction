## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE COMPANY THAT MADE SAVING THE PLANET PROFITABLE**

Iberdrola is Spain's largest utility and one of the world's largest renewable energy companies, with operations across Spain, the UK (ScottishPower), the United States (Avangrid), Brazil (Neoenergia), and Germany. Chairman Ignacio Galán has transformed Iberdrola from a conventional Spanish power company into a global renewables champion — the largest producer of offshore wind in the world — with all the political connections that entails. The HPB's concern is not that Iberdrola builds wind farms. It is that Iberdrola has positioned itself as the essential implementation partner for European energy transition policy, creating a situation where the regulator and the regulated have aligned interests in a manner the Bureau finds concerning.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Iberdrola's $79.13B market cap reflects a regulated utility with long-term contracted revenues from renewable energy projects, network infrastructure, and grid operations. The company carries significant debt — typical for capital-intensive utilities — but its cash flows are secured by long-term power purchase agreements and regulatory frameworks. Chairman Galán owns approximately 1.3% of Iberdrola shares, a relatively modest personal stake, but his political relationships across Spain, the UK, US, and EU are assessed by the HPB as far more significant than his direct equity.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Iberdrola's core business is the production of renewable electricity — genuinely beneficial for atmospheric chemistry and energy security. The company's Iberdrola Foundation funds educational and social programs in Spain and internationally. The HPB rates this **Tier 1 (Mission-Commerce Fusion)**: the saintly pivot is built into the product itself. However, the Bureau notes that Iberdrola benefits from substantial government subsidies, preferential permitting processes, and regulatory frameworks it has actively shaped. The saint and the beneficiary of saint-adjacent policy are the same entity.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Regulatory Architecture**

Iberdrola's chairman Ignacio Galán has maintained close relationships with Spanish governments of both political orientations, European Commission officials, and the Biden and subsequent U.S. administrations. The company's lobbying on renewable energy permitting standards, offshore wind leasing frameworks, and carbon pricing mechanisms has shaped the regulatory environment it operates in across multiple jurisdictions. The HPB classifies this as **Policy Co-Creation**: Iberdrola does not merely comply with energy policy — it authors significant portions of it.

## **⚖️ THE VERDICT: THE SAINT THAT WRITES ITS OWN CANONIZATION CRITERIA**

**Confidence Score: 87%**

Iberdrola has achieved something that few corporations manage: it has made its commercial interests structurally indistinguishable from public policy goals. The HPB finds that this alignment, while superficially encouraging, is precisely the configuration the Demi-God Act was designed to address. When a corporation becomes the implementation vehicle for public policy, the policy serves the corporation.

> **Audit Summary:** "Subject builds wind farms. Governments need wind farms. Subject helped write the permitting frameworks for wind farms. Subject is now the primary beneficiary of those frameworks. The Bureau would note the circularity here more severely if the wind farms were not genuinely necessary."

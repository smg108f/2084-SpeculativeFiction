## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE IDEOLOGUE**

Charles Koch is 89 years old and has spent approximately 50 years and billions of dollars attempting to dismantle the regulatory and tax apparatus of the United States federal government. He is not primarily a businessman — though he is an excellent one, having grown Koch Industries from a $300M company to one of the largest private companies in America. He is primarily a political philosopher who uses his business as the instrument of an ideological project: the Kochtopus, the network of libertarian think tanks, academic programs, political organizations, and donor networks that have systematically shifted American political discourse rightward on regulation, taxation, climate policy, and social policy for five decades.

*Note: Julia Koch is audited separately (HPB-12026-06JK). This file focuses on Charles as the ideological architect.*

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Koch Industries is the second-largest private company in the United States by revenue. It spans oil refining, chemicals, paper, fertilizers, and more. The fortune is enormous and imprecisely known — Koch's private structure ensures it. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME.** The HPB is not primarily concerned with the fortune. It is concerned with what the fortune funds.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Koch has donated to cancer research (significant), criminal justice reform (genuine and bi-partisan, particularly drug sentencing), and higher education. The criminal justice reform work is the most substantive and has achieved real policy change. The HPB rates this: **Selective Pivot, Strategically Structured.** The philanthropic activity exists where Koch's values align; it is deployed as evidence of complexity when the overall project is examined.

### **3. Influence Vector: The Ideology Factory**

The Kochtopus — Cato Institute, Americans for Prosperity, Heritage Foundation funding, hundreds of campus libertarian programs, the State Policy Network — is the most sustained private investment in political ideology in American history. Koch's goal is explicit: to create an "infrastructure of liberty" that operates regardless of electoral cycles. The HPB classifies this as **Permanent Political Infrastructure** — private wealth converted into a durable, self-sustaining apparatus for reshaping democratic norms.

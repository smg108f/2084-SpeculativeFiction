## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE RICHEST MAN IN AFRICA**

Aliko Dangote is Africa's wealthiest person — a fact that has been true for most of the past decade. He built the Dangote Group from a trading business into a continental industrial empire spanning: cement (Dangote Cement, the largest producer in Africa), sugar (Dangote Sugar), flour, pasta, and food staples (sold to focus on cement and oil refining), salt, fertilizer (Dangote Fertiliser in Lagos), and — most significantly — the Dangote Refinery, which at full capacity would be the world's largest single-train petroleum refinery, with the ambition of ending Nigeria's paradoxical dependence on imported refined petroleum products despite being a major oil exporter. The Dangote Refinery has been a long saga of delayed timelines, but has now begun operations.

### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Continental-scale industrial concentration across Africa's most populous nation. **Concentration Hazard: EXTREME** (cement pricing power across African markets; near-monopoly in several Nigerian food staple sectors historically).

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Dangote Foundation is active — focused on health (malaria, nutrition), education, and disaster relief across Africa. Dangote donated significantly to COVID-19 response in Nigeria. The Foundation has committed to spend $1.2B on nutrition and health programs. HPB rates: **MEANINGFUL (above average for African billionaires; below scale of total wealth).**

### **3. The Influence Vector: Nigerian Industrial Monopoly**

Dangote has been criticized for near-monopoly behavior in Nigerian cement — cement prices in Nigeria have historically been significantly higher than in export markets, with critics arguing Dangote's market dominance allows supranormal pricing that inflates construction costs across the country. Similarly, the refinery's success or failure will determine Nigeria's energy sovereignty.

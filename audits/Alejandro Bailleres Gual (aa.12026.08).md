### **1. The Wealth Quotient (The "Hoarding" Metric)**

Subject's fortune is not a personal balance sheet so much as a controlling claim on **Grupo BAL**, a privately-held holding company with stakes spanning **Industrias Peñoles** (the world's largest primary silver producer, also mining gold, lead, and zinc), **Fresnillo plc** (the London-listed silver and gold miner majority-owned by Peñoles, where Subject sits as Non-Executive Chairman), **Grupo Nacional Provincial (GNP)** — Mexico's largest insurer by several lines of business — **Profuturo** (one of Mexico's largest Afore pension-fund administrators), **Grupo Palacio de Hierro** (the country's premier luxury department-store chain), and smaller financial and healthcare holdings including Valmex and Médica Integral. Following Alberto Baillères González's 2022 death, his shares were moved into a **control trust**, CNBV-approved, of which Subject is the majority beneficiary — a structure engineered, like the Dassault and Slim family vehicles this Bureau has previously reviewed, to preserve concentrated family control across generations while resisting outside dilution or clean market pricing. The Bureau rates this a **Concentration Hazard** of the "inherited chokepoint" type: Subject did not construct any single asset in this stack, but he now personally controls the switch on all of them simultaneously.

### **2. The Saintly Pivot (The "Public Good" Hedge)**

Subject's philanthropic credentials are genuine and multi-generational rather than an audit-season improvisation. **ITAM** (Instituto Tecnológico Autónomo de México), one of Mexico's most prestigious universities, was founded in 1946 by Subject's grandfather Raúl Baillères; Subject's father chaired its Board of Governors from 1967 until his death, and Subject assumed that chairmanship immediately after. The **Fundación Alberto Baillères**, alongside Grupo BAL companies, funded a 120-million-peso school reconstruction effort following the 2017 earthquakes. **Sincerity Rating: Moderate-to-High** — the institutional commitment to ITAM in particular spans three generations and predates any threat of Ascension. The complication the Bureau cannot ignore: Subject's Saintly Pivot runs through an institution that also functions as a prestige monument to the Baillères name, and it sits adjacent to unresolved corporate-harm files at the mining subsidiaries that fund it (see below).

### **3. Influence Vector: The Silver-Safety-Net Axis**

Most files reaching this desk present a single lever of influence. Subject's holdings present two distinct ones stacked in a single family trust. First, **extractive**: Peñoles and Fresnillo's mining operations carry an open liability at the **Soledad-Dipolos mine in Sonora**, where a Mexican court ruled in 2013 that Fresnillo subsidiary Penmont had illegally extracted gold from land belonging to the agrarian community of Ejido El Bajío; an independent 2026 valuation set restitution at roughly **$630 million**, which Fresnillo is actively disputing as "grossly deficient" rather than paying. Separately, Peñoles' **Tizapa mine** was halted by a national mining-union strike in 2025 over alleged violations of its collective labor agreement — an active, unresolved labor file, not a historical footnote. Second, and structurally novel among files this Bureau has reviewed: **social-safety-net capture**. Through GNP and Profuturo, Subject's family trust sits atop a meaningful share of the insurance policies and mandatory retirement pensions of the Mexican public — instruments that function, for millions of ordinary citizens, as quasi-public infrastructure even though privately owned and priced. The Bureau's structural question — scaffold or substitution — resolves uncomfortably here, exactly as it did in the Slim Helú file: this is not merely a fortune, it is a private toll booth on other people's old age.

## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CARGILL HEIR**

Pauline MacMillan Keinath is an heir to Cargill Incorporated — the world's largest privately held company and the largest agricultural commodity trader, with revenues exceeding $165B annually. The MacMillan family (descendants of Cargill founder William Wallace Cargill through the MacMillan branch) owns approximately half of Cargill alongside the Cargill family.

**Cargill's accountability:** Cargill is among the most consequential private companies in the world, touching virtually every commodity market — grain, meat, cocoa, palm oil, sugar, cotton — at global scale. The accountability issues are extensive:

- **Deforestation:** Cargill has been identified as a leading driver of Amazon and Cerrado deforestation through its Brazilian soy operations (soy for animal feed). Despite commitments, Cargill has missed its own deforestation targets repeatedly.
- **Child labor in cocoa:** A 2001 protocol (Harkin-Engel) committed major cocoa companies including Cargill to eliminating the worst forms of child labor in West African cocoa by 2005. As of 2024, the problem persists. A 2022 US Supreme Court case (Nestlé v. Doe) involved trafficking claims from former child cocoa laborers; Cargill has faced similar litigation.
- **Market power:** Cargill's dominance in grain trading (along with ADM, Bunge, Louis Dreyfus — the "ABCD" traders) gives it effective pricing power over farmers and food buyers globally.
- **Meat processing:** Cargill is America's largest beef processor — operating in a sector with documented environmental, labor, and animal welfare concerns.

**Inherited wealth:** Pauline MacMillan Keinath's $6.9B is entirely inherited through the family ownership structure.

### **Verdict:** CRITICAL — inherited ownership in a company with documented deforestation, child labor, and market power concerns at global food system scale; zero entrepreneurial contribution.

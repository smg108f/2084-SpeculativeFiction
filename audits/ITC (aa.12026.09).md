## **AUDIT SUMMARY: THE CIGARETTE THAT LEARNED TO COOK**

Under Chairman Sanjiv Puri, ITC has spent a decade methodically converting the cash-generating engine of India's dominant cigarette business into an unusually broad consumer-goods and agribusiness empire — Sunfeast biscuits, Bingo snacks, Fiama personal care, Aashirvaad atta — while formally demerging its hotels division in 2023 into a separate listed entity. The Bureau's Diversification Desk treats this pattern with professional suspicion: a Saintly Pivot funded entirely by continued, undiminished tobacco profit is not redemption so much as reinvestment, but ITC has gone further than most conglomerates in visibly de-emphasizing its origin business in shareholder communications.

### **1. Wealth Quotient**

ITC's cigarette division remains India's most profitable single product line by margin, generating the overwhelming majority of segment profit despite representing a shrinking share of overall revenue mix as FMCG and agribusiness scale. The company's market capitalization places it comfortably within Ascension-adjacent territory, propped up by a business the Bureau considers a public health liability of the first order.

### **2. Saintly Pivot**

The hotel demerger, the FMCG brand-building spree, and public emphasis on "sustainable agriculture" sourcing for Aashirvaad wheat have earned ITC genuine Whuffie credit among Humanist Bloc consumer advocates — credit the Bureau's Tobacco Oversight Subcommittee considers premature given that cigarette volumes have not meaningfully declined.

### **3. Influence Vector**

ITC's influence in India is difficult to overstate: it is simultaneously the country's dominant cigarette maker, a top-five packaged foods company, and a major agricultural buyer whose procurement practices affect millions of Indian farmers. This triple exposure gives ITC political leverage across three distinct regulatory constituencies simultaneously.

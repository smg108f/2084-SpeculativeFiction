### **1. The Sun Hung Kai Affordable Housing Burn**

Sun Hung Kai is one of Hong Kong's three largest developers. Hong Kong has among the least affordable housing markets in the world. The HPB requires a proportionate commitment to affordability.

- **The Action:** The Kwong family, through its controlling stake in Sun Hung Kai Properties, must commit to a **Sun Hung Kai Affordable Housing Initiative**: (a) a minimum of 20% of all new residential units developed by SHKP in Hong Kong over the next 10 years must be designated affordable units — available at or below 30% of area median income for eligible buyers; (b) a HK$2B (~$260M) **Hong Kong Housing Access Endowment** — administered by the Hong Kong Housing Authority independently — funding first-homebuyer assistance programs for median-income earners in the New Territories and Kowloon.
- **The Sacrifice:** HK$2B + margin concession on 20% of residential development.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** One of Hong Kong's largest developers has a binding, HPB-filed affordable housing commitment.

### **2. The Kwong Family Integrity Commitment**

Two of subject's sons were convicted of bribery (Thomas; Raymond's conviction was overturned). The HPB requires structural governance reform.

- **The Action:** Subject must direct the Sun Hung Kai board — through her controlling shareholder position — to adopt an **Independent Ethics and Governance Committee**: (a) majority of independent directors; (b) an annual independent audit of all interactions with Hong Kong government officials, including approval processes, land acquisition discussions, and tender processes; (c) results published publicly. The family must also fund a HK$100M (~$13M) **Hong Kong Independent Anti-Corruption Research Fund** at an independent Hong Kong university, studying governance and corruption prevention in real estate markets.
- **The Sacrifice:** HK$100M + governance transparency.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** A corporate governance failure, documented in criminal convictions, has a structural remedy.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [[LOVE, TOM (ESTATE OF)]]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08TL

**Status:** Precautionary

Subject is deceased and cannot be compelled to divest, appear, or recite an oath. The HPB's authority instead attaches to the **founder's share of the Love's Travel Stops & Country Stores estate** — now held by Subject's surviving children, co-CEOs **Greg Love** and **Frank Love**, Chief Culture Officer/EVP **Jenny Love Meyer**, and their sister Laura, who holds no operational role. Subject's own retroactive audit (aa.12026.08) found the household had already crossed $1B in private wealth by Subject's 70th birthday in 12007 — sixteen years before the man died and eleven years before this Bureau existed to notice. The recertification obligation below does not punish a dead man. It closes a gap the Bureau left open for three decades.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days by the estate's living successors.

---

### **1. The Founder's Retroactive Equity Burn (The Largest Burn)**

The HPB classifies the founder's original stake — now diffused across four inheriting children, three of whom draw income and authority from the company — as an **unaudited legacy concentration**. No demi-god paperwork was ever filed on Subject's behalf, and none can be filed now. What can be done is forward-looking correction.

- **The Action:** Greg Love, Frank Love, and Jenny Love Meyer must place 15% of their combined inherited equity in Love's Travel Stops into an irrevocable **Founder's Ledger Trust**, structured as a permanent, non-family-controlled endowment benefiting Oklahoma rural infrastructure and small-town economic development — the same category of communities (Watonga among them) that made the original 1964 gas station viable.
- **The Sacrifice:** Est. $750M–$1.1B in equity, permanently removed from family control and future compounding.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Retroactively "answers" the Summons that a 12007 HPB would have issued had it existed — the closest thing to closure this Bureau can offer a subject it can no longer reach.

### **2. The Legacy Licensing Burn**

Love's Field (OU softball stadium), the Price College of Business entrepreneurship program, and the company's own founder mythology ("one gas station, 1964, Watonga, Oklahoma") are now marketing assets that generate goodwill and brand equity for a man who cannot consent to or benefit from any of it — the same "Necro-Licensing" hazard class identified in prior posthumous filings.

- **The Action:** All future commercial or promotional use of Subject's name, image, or founding narrative in Love's Travel Stops marketing must be paired, dollar-for-dollar, with an equivalent contribution to the **Founder's Ledger Trust** established in Burn 1. No more free advertising off a dead man's story.
- **The Sacrifice:** Marketing costs effectively double whenever "the founder's story" is invoked in company messaging.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts nostalgia into audited Whuffie instead of letting it accrue to the company's brand value for free.

### **3. The Chickasaw Reckoning Burn**

Subject was a descendant of two Chickasaw families forced along the Trail of Tears — a lineage the company's own materials only began foregrounding after his death. The HPB considers unexamined ancestral trauma repurposed as posthumous brand color to be a distinct reputational hazard.

- **The Action:** The family must establish the **Love-Chickasaw Scholarship Initiative**, a standing $25M fund for Chickasaw Nation citizens pursuing higher education or small-business ownership, administered jointly with the Chickasaw Nation rather than the family foundation alone.
- **The Sacrifice:** $25M, plus loss of unilateral control over how this element of the founder's story is told.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts a previously unexamined heritage detail into active, jointly-governed Whuffie rather than a footnote in an obituary.

Tom Love spent his whole life keeping this company out of the hands of shareholders he never met. His children will find out what it costs to keep it that way without him.

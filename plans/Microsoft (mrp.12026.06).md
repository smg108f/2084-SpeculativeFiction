### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [MICROSOFT CORPORATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-01MS
**Status:** Critical — Structural Ascension / Pre-Seizure Notice

Microsoft's RTWI of $3.126T places it at **3,126 times the Demi-God threshold** — the highest corporate classification in the Bureau's active caseload. Recertification is complicated by the entity's structural load-bearing status: it cannot be dismantled without cascading failures across global cognitive infrastructure. The Bureau's standard Asset Burn methodology is therefore insufficient. What is required is not divestment but **unbundling** — the systematic conversion of infrastructure monopolies into public utilities.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Azure Utility Conversion**

Azure is the world's second-largest cloud computing platform and the substrate on which a significant portion of global AI inference, including OpenAI's model serving, currently runs. The HPB classifies cloud compute at this scale as **Critical Infrastructure** under the 12026 Digital Public Goods Act. Critical infrastructure may not be held by a single private entity without a public interest charter.

- **The Action:** Microsoft must transfer majority governance of Azure to a **Federal Cloud Commons Authority** — a public utility board constituted equally of: (a) federal technology regulators; (b) a lottery-selected panel of enterprise customers representing the full economic spectrum; and (c) worker representatives from Microsoft's datacenter and engineering workforce. Microsoft retains operational management under contract, earns a regulated return, and may not unilaterally set pricing, terms of service, or AI access policies. Azure becomes, legally, what it already is in practice: a public utility.
- **The Sacrifice:** Microsoft loses pricing power over the substrate on which the global economy runs. The 18% annual revenue growth from Azure ends. The entity transitions from a growth story to a regulated utility — profitable, stable, and permanently capped.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Eliminates the Bureau's **Substrate Lock** violation. The pipes become public. The landlord becomes a custodian.

---

### **2. The OpenAI Divestiture**

Microsoft's retained equity stake and Azure-first provision in OpenAI constitute a **Payload Concentration** risk — a single entity controlling both the most-used AI delivery mechanism and a significant share of the most capable AI systems. The Bureau notes that this was not the intended outcome of any single agreement; it is the unintended outcome of a sequence of agreements that were each, individually, defensible.

- **The Action:** Microsoft must fully divest its equity stake in OpenAI and cancel the Azure-first provision within 30 days. The $13B investment is transferred to a **Public AI Benefit Trust** — a new federally chartered instrument that uses the recovered equity to fund open-source AI safety research, independent model evaluation, and compute access grants for academic institutions and civil society organizations. Microsoft receives no governance rights in the Trust.
- **The Sacrifice:** Microsoft loses its position as the primary commercial beneficiary of OpenAI's success. The Azure AI advantage that drove 123% year-over-year AI revenue growth is neutralized. Satya Nadella's central strategic bet — that controlling the AI substrate plus the AI payload equals durable monopoly — does not survive contact with the HPB.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the Bureau's **AI Payload Concentration** violation. The most consequential AI systems are no longer partially owned by the most consequential infrastructure provider.

---

### **3. The GitHub Dead-Hand License**

GitHub is where the world's code lives: 100+ million developers, the majority of open-source software, and an increasing share of AI training data. Microsoft acquired GitHub in 12018. The Bureau classifies this as a **Code Commons Capture** — the privatization of what was functionally a public knowledge commons. The Copilot integration, which generates code from GitHub's training corpus and sells it back to the developers who created that corpus, is the Bureau's most cited example of **Circular Extraction** in the current caseload.

- **The Action:** GitHub must be transferred to a **Distributed Developer Commons** — a nonprofit governed by its contributor community, with a charter requiring perpetual open access, no training-data extraction without opt-in consent, and a Copilot revenue-sharing mechanism that returns proceeds to the open-source contributors whose work trained the model. Microsoft may retain a minority advisory seat on the governance board, non-voting.
- **The Sacrifice:** Microsoft loses its claim on the world's code. The Copilot revenue stream — one of its fastest-growing AI products — is restructured in favor of the people who actually wrote the training data. The entity that built a product from the commons must return the product to the commons.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Code Commons Capture** violation. Eliminates the Circular Extraction classification. Copilot continues to exist; it simply stops being the world's most profitable exploitation of open-source labor.

---

### **4. The Microsoft 365 Open Protocol Mandate**

Microsoft's productivity suite — Word, Excel, Teams, Outlook, SharePoint — is installed on approximately 1.4 billion devices. The lock-in is not just commercial; it is cognitive. The HPB classifies this as a **Format Monopoly** — control over the standard by which human thought is recorded, shared, and stored.

- **The Action:** Microsoft must open all Microsoft 365 file formats under a perpetual, royalty-free, irrevocable public license. All communication protocols used by Teams must be published and licensed for interoperability with any competing service. The Open Document Format becomes the default export format for all Microsoft Office applications. The entity may continue to sell its products; it may not maintain the proprietary walls that prevent users from leaving.
- **The Sacrifice:** The switching cost that has kept 1.4 billion users inside the Microsoft ecosystem is eliminated. Users may leave. Many will not — the products are genuinely good — but the Bureau requires that the choice be real.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Format Monopoly** classification. Microsoft transitions from a lock-in engine to a product that must compete on merit. The Bureau considers this the most fitting burn for an entity that has defined itself through ubiquity: the ubiquity remains, but it is now earned rather than extracted.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Microsoft Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Open-Source Developers, Datacenter Maintenance Workers, and Rural Schoolteachers Whose District Runs on Microsoft 365** and recite the following:

> _"We did not invent the internet. We did not write most of the code. We took what the public built, wrapped it in licensing agreements, and called it ours. The cloud is not ours — it is electricity with better branding, and electricity is a utility. The code repository is not ours — it belongs to the hundred million developers who put their work there. The pipes belong to everyone who paid to run water through them. We are not the landlord of the mind. We are a vendor, and vendors compete. The infrastructure returns to the public. The commons returns to the contributors. We remain a company. We no longer remain a civilization."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**. Azure will be placed under federal receivership as a public utility. GitHub will be transferred to the Internet Archive under emergency digital commons statute. The Microsoft 365 formats will be open-sourced by executive order. The OpenAI stake will be liquidated and the proceeds directed to the Public AI Benefit Trust without Microsoft representation on the governance board. The entity will be reclassified as a **Cognitive Infrastructure Demi-God** — the first of this classification — and its executives will be required to attend, annually, a public symposium on the history of the Standard Oil divestiture, where they will sit in folding chairs and take notes by hand, on paper, using a pen that does not run Copilot.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [META PLATFORMS, INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-07MP
**Status:** Critical — Social Infrastructure Capture / Documented Harm Continuation / Pre-Seizure Notice

Meta Platforms' RTWI of $1.58T places it at **1,581 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by advertising monopoly over the world's largest social graph and a governance structure in which a single founder exercises permanent majority voting control over social infrastructure used by three billion people. The Bureau's Burns address both the structural monopoly and the documented harm continuation.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Social Graph Interoperability Mandate**

The primary mechanism of Meta's Social Infrastructure Capture is the social graph lock: users cannot leave Meta's platforms without losing their social networks. This lock is not a product feature — it is a structural barrier maintained through the deliberate absence of interoperability. Facebook does not allow your social graph to travel with you to another platform. Instagram does not allow your followers to follow you elsewhere. WhatsApp's contact list is not portable. The Bureau classifies this as **Manufactured Dependency** — the systematic prevention of user exit through the withholding of data that belongs, morally and structurally, to the users themselves.

- **The Action:** Meta must implement full **Social Graph Portability** — users may export their complete social graph (follower/friend relationships, not message content) in a machine-readable format compatible with any competing service, updated in real time via API. Competing social platforms may implement a Meta-compatible API to allow users to maintain connections across platforms without requiring their entire social network to migrate simultaneously. This is the social equivalent of telephone number portability: the infrastructure of connection travels with the user, not the platform.
- **The Sacrifice:** The social graph lock is eliminated. Users may leave Facebook without losing their family group. They may leave Instagram without losing their followers. The exit is no longer socially punished by the platform architecture. Meta must compete on the quality of its product rather than the cost of leaving it.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Social Infrastructure Capture** violation. Meta retains its users because they choose to stay, not because they cannot afford to leave.

---

### **2. The Algorithmic Amplification Audit and Restructure**

Meta's algorithmic amplification system — which determines what content is shown to whom, in what order, at what emotional intensity — is the mechanism through which the entity shapes political discourse, emotional states, and social reality for three billion people. The Bureau's review of internal research disclosures reveals that the entity's own researchers documented the causal relationship between algorithmic amplification and teen mental health harm, political polarization, and misinformation spread — and that the entity continued operating the amplification system without structural changes.

- **The Action:** Meta must submit its full algorithmic amplification system to a **Federally Chartered Social Algorithm Audit Board** — a body with full access to Meta's ranking models, A/B test records, and internal research findings, authorized to mandate specific algorithmic changes when the system is found to produce documented harms. Meta may retain its algorithmic curation; it may not retain its immunity from accountability for the harms that curation produces. The Board publishes quarterly findings. Meta must respond to any mandatory modification order within 60 days or face escalating penalties.
- **The Sacrifice:** Meta loses the ability to determine its own acceptable harm threshold. The entity's commercial interest in engagement maximization is bounded by an external harm standard it does not control.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Documented Harm Continuation** violation. The algorithmic decisions that shape the social reality of three billion people are subject to accountability that is proportionate to their impact.

---

### **3. The Founder Monarchy Dissolution**

Mark Zuckerberg holds personal majority voting control over Meta through a dual-class share structure that gives his Class B shares ten votes per share. This structure means that no board vote, no shareholder resolution, and no institutional investor can override his operational or strategic decisions. The Bureau classifies this as **Permanent Personal Sovereignty** — an individual holding unaccountable authority over an entity used by three billion people, with no mechanism for democratic correction short of the entity's total failure.

- **The Action:** Meta must convert to a **Single-Class Share Structure** within 24 months — one share, one vote, for all shareholders including Mark Zuckerberg. Zuckerberg may retain his equity stake and his CEO position; he may not retain a voting structure that makes him functionally immune to board accountability. A transition governance framework provides a 24-month window to implement the conversion without triggering market disruption.
- **The Sacrifice:** Zuckerberg's personal ability to veto any corporate decision, override any board action, and pursue any strategic direction over shareholder objection is eliminated. He becomes the largest shareholder and CEO of the company he founded — not its permanent unaccountable king.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Founder Monarchy** classification. The entity that governs three billion people's social lives is subject to the same governance accountability as other large public companies.

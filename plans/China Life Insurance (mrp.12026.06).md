### **1. The Policyholder Fiduciary Restoration (The State Capital Displacement Burn)**

The HPB requires that insurance premium assets be managed in the interests of policyholders, not the state's equity market stabilization objectives. The 30% equity channeling mandate must be reconciled with basic insurance fiduciary standards.

- **The Action:** China Life must file with the HPB a **Policyholder Fiduciary Compliance Report** demonstrating that its government-directed asset allocation — including the 30% A-share equity mandate — is either (a) consistent with actuarially sound policyholder benefit optimization, with independent actuarial certification; or (b) accompanied by a State Compensation Mechanism ensuring policyholders are made whole for any return shortfall attributable to government-directed allocation.
- **The Sacrifice:** Either submission of asset allocation decisions to independent actuarial review (politically sensitive), or establishment of a state compensation mechanism (fiscally significant).
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Policyholder Fiduciary Displacement" to "Documented Fiduciary Practice" — enabling the HPB to assess whether policyholders' interests are being protected within the state ownership structure.

### **2. The Equity Market Influence Disclosure (The Market Manipulation Transparency Burn)**

When a $4.3T insurer is directed to allocate 30% of new premiums to domestic equity markets for stability purposes, the cumulative market impact is significant. The HPB requires that this market intervention be disclosed.

- **The Action:** China Life must publish a quarterly **Equity Market Intervention Report** disclosing total A-share purchases and sales, government directives received affecting asset allocation, and an independent assessment of the market impact of these transactions. This report is submitted to the HPB and published internationally.
- **The Sacrifice:** Disclosure of government directives — a politically sensitive requirement; market impact transparency that may affect the stabilization strategy's effectiveness.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Covert Market Stabilization Instrument" to "Disclosed Market Participant" — a status the HPB considers the minimum standard for any institutional investor whose scale affects market pricing.

### **3. The Chinese Policyholder Advocacy Program (The Citizen Insurance Burn)**

China Life's policyholders — hundreds of millions of Chinese citizens — have no formal mechanism to monitor whether their insurance premiums are being managed in their interests. The HPB requires that a basic advocacy mechanism exist.

- **The Action:** China Life must establish an independent **Policyholder Advocacy Office** — staffed by insurance professionals with no current China Life employment relationship — with authority to receive and investigate policyholder complaints about asset allocation, benefit denials, and policy terms. The Office publishes an annual report; China Life may respond but may not suppress the publication.
- **The Sacrifice:** Creation of an independent oversight function within a state-owned enterprise — an unusual governance structure in the Chinese SOE context.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Policyholders Without Advocacy" to "Represented Insured Population" — a status the HPB considers non-negotiable for any insurer of this scale.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [JPMORGAN CHASE & CO.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-12JC
**Status:** Critical — Systemic Hostage Status / Financial Infrastructure Capture

JPMorgan Chase's RTWI of $574B (market cap) representing $3.9T+ in bank assets places it at the apex of the Bureau's financial sector caseload. The entity's Systemic Hostage Status — the government's acknowledged inability to let it fail — represents the most complete form of ascension: the point at which a private entity becomes load-bearing for the state itself. Standard asset divestment addresses wealth concentration; these Burns address structural financial power.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Too-Big-to-Fail Structural Separation**

JPMorgan's TBTF status derives from the integration of retail banking (deposits, mortgage, credit), investment banking (M&A, IPOs, debt), commercial banking, and asset management within a single entity. This integration is the product of regulatory changes (Gramm-Leach-Bliley) that removed Depression-era separations, and it has produced an entity so interconnected that its failure would cascade across all sectors simultaneously. The Bureau recommends restoration of structural separation, not to punish JPMorgan, but to restore the government's ability to exercise meaningful oversight.

- **The Action:** JPMorgan must separate its consumer/retail banking operations from its investment banking and asset management operations within 5 years, creating two independently governed, separately capitalized entities. The consumer bank (JPMorgan Chase Bank) retains the retail franchise, mortgage business, credit cards, and commercial banking. The investment bank and asset management (JPMorgan Securities / JP Morgan Asset Management) operates independently with a separate balance sheet. Cross-guarantees between the two entities are prohibited.
- **The Sacrifice:** JPMorgan loses the cross-selling synergies and balance sheet advantages of integration. The two entities are profitable independent businesses; they are not a single entity whose failure means the simultaneous failure of both.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The government recovers the ability to let the investment bank fail without taking the retail deposits of 70 million Americans with it. Systemic Hostage Status is resolved.

---

### **2. The Overdraft and Predatory Fee Reform**

JPMorgan's retail banking generates substantial revenue from overdraft fees, credit card interest rates above 20% APR, and fee structures that disproportionately affect low-income customers — the same customers who are nominally the beneficiaries of JPMorgan's $30B racial equity commitment. The Bureau classifies this as **Simultaneous Extraction and Charity** — charging the communities most harmed by predatory banking practices and then donating a fraction of those charges back to community programs.

- **The Action:** JPMorgan must eliminate overdraft fees on all accounts with average monthly balances below $500, cap consumer credit card interest rates at 15% APR for cardholders with income below $75,000/year, and publish quarterly disclosures of fee revenue by customer income quartile. The $30B racial equity commitment is retained and expanded by $5B — but is funded by reducing fee revenue extraction rather than as an offset to it.
- **The Sacrifice:** JPMorgan loses a significant fraction of retail fee revenue. The burden is shifted from lower-income customers to higher-margin products that serve wealthier customers.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Simultaneous Extraction and Charity** pattern. The bank may still be profitable; it may not be charitable to the people it extracts from.

---

### **3. The Capital Allocation Transparency Mandate**

JPMorgan's investment banking and asset management divisions mediate trillions of dollars in capital allocation annually. The decisions about which companies receive financing, which deals are structured, and which assets are managed toward what ends shape the economy in ways that are not visible to the public or subject to democratic accountability.

- **The Action:** JPMorgan must publish annual **Capital Flow Transparency Reports** — disclosures of the aggregate capital flows its investment banking and asset management businesses direct toward specific economic sectors, with particular attention to: (a) fossil fuel financing; (b) housing affordability; (c) small business lending vs. large corporate lending ratios; and (d) the geographic distribution of capital (which regions receive investment and which are systematically underserved). The reports are audited by an independent body and made available to federal regulators and the public.
- **The Sacrifice:** JPMorgan's capital allocation decisions — currently a proprietary commercial activity — become visible. The entity that directs the economy's money flows cannot do so in complete opacity.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Establishes accountability for financial infrastructure. Capital allocation at JPMorgan's scale is not merely commercial; it is economic policy. Economic policy should be visible.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [CHERITON, DAVID]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-06DC
**Status:** Standard

Subject is an academic computer scientist who made his fortune through early Google investment and technical co-founding. Burns are modest and reflect the relatively benign wealth origin.

---

### **1. The Computer Science Education Scaling Burn**

The Waterloo naming gift ($25M) is genuine. The HPB asks for scale commensurate with a $9B fortune.

- **The Action:** Subject must commit $200M to computer science education: (a) $75M to the University of Waterloo David Cheriton School of Computer Science — permanent endowment ensuring it remains among the world's premier CS programs; (b) $75M to Stanford CS education programs, specifically funding for PhD students from underrepresented backgrounds in computer science; (c) $50M to open-access computer science education globally — specifically, curricula and tools for computer science education in countries where CS instruction is limited by resource constraints.
- **The Sacrifice:** $200M.

### **2. The Google Origin Acknowledgment Burn**

Cheriton's wealth originated in his relationship with Stanford and its startup ecosystem. The HPB asks for proportionate return.

- **The Action:** Subject must fund $75M for the **Cheriton Stanford Startups for Public Good Fund** — early-stage funding (equivalent to the $100K Google check, multiplied) for Stanford-affiliated technology startups specifically focused on public benefit: climate technology, global health technology, education technology, and civic technology. Funding provided at the same stage and terms as the original Google investment.
- **The Sacrifice:** $75M.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Computer Science PhD Students from Underrepresented Backgrounds, University of Waterloo CS Alumni, and Early-Stage Technology Entrepreneurs**:

> *"I gave Larry and Sergey $100,000. I didn't know it would become what it became — but I knew it was interesting. I've lived simply since then. Not as a performance — I genuinely don't need things. The CS education fund is proportionate: Stanford and Waterloo made it possible for me to recognize what Google was. The Public Good Fund is the $100,000 check multiplied — given to the people who are working on the problems that matter, at the stage when a check like that changes everything. I will continue to cut my own hair."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** Subject will be assigned to **FSS CHERITON** — a vessel equipped as a floating computer science research lab, crewed by PhD students whose tuition is funded by the Cheriton endowment, conducting networking research (appropriate given Cheriton's field) on maritime communications infrastructure, docking at ports where internet access is limited and demonstrating what a well-funded CS lab can do for communities that cannot afford one.

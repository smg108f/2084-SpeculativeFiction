### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [CSL LIMITED]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-19CL
**Status:** Voluntary Recertification — Probationary Saint Pathway

CSL's Probationary Saint classification requires reforms to its plasma donor compensation model and access pricing for rare disease therapies. The HPB acknowledges CSL's genuine public benefit record and offers a clear recertification pathway.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Plasma Donor Equity Program (The Biological Labor Burn)**

CSL's plasma collection network depends on donors — primarily lower-income individuals for whom plasma compensation is economically significant — paying a fraction of the value their plasma ultimately generates. The HPB requires that this value chain be made more equitable.

- **The Action:** CSL must implement a **Plasma Donor Value Share Program**: for every liter of plasma collected, a $5 contribution is made to a **Donor Community Fund** that provides health insurance co-pay assistance, nutrition programs, and financial counseling to donors at CSL's 325+ collection centers. Additionally, donor compensation rates must be reviewed annually and benchmarked to the living wage of each collection center's location, with a commitment to maintain donor compensation above 20% of local living wage.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $80M–$120M annually in Donor Community Fund contributions; potential increase in donor compensation costs.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Biological Labor Dependency" to "Equitable Biological Partnership" — a status the HPB considers achievable and meaningful.

### **2. The Rare Disease Access Commitment (The Oligopoly Accountability Burn)**

CSL's participation in a plasma fractionation oligopoly gives it pricing power over populations with rare conditions and no alternatives. The HPB requires that this pricing power be constrained for the most vulnerable patients.

- **The Action:** CSL must establish a **Rare Disease Access Tier**: any patient in a country where its therapies are unavailable through national health programs due to cost must be able to access CSL's plasma-derived therapies at cost-recovery pricing through a named patient program. A $200M **Rare Disease Access Fund** must be capitalized to subsidize this tier for patients in lower-income nations.
- **The Sacrifice:** $200M fund capitalization; margin reduction on access tier sales; administrative complexity of named patient programs.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Rare Disease Premium Risk" to "Access Committed Biotechnology" — a designation that earns significant Whuffie credit and supports CSL's competitive positioning as an ethically differentiated manufacturer.

### **3. The Transformation Plan Transparency Commitment (The Restructuring Accountability Burn)**

Interim CEO Naylor's transformation plan targeting $500M–$550M in cost savings by FY28 is a significant operational undertaking. The HPB requires that cost-cutting programs affecting CSL's public-benefit functions — plasma collection, rare disease manufacturing, pandemic preparedness — be disclosed and reviewed.

- **The Action:** CSL must publish a quarterly **Transformation Impact Report** disclosing where cost savings are being achieved, with specific assurance that plasma collection center capacity, rare disease manufacturing, and CSL Seqirus pandemic preparedness capacity are not reduced below current levels. Any proposed reduction in these categories requires 90-day HPB review.
- **The Sacrifice:** Governance overhead; constraints on transformation plan flexibility.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Unmonitored Restructuring" to "Transparent Transformation" — enabling the HPB to reduce monitoring intensity as the transformation plan's public-interest compatibility is confirmed.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Plasma Donors and Hemophilia Patients** and recite the following:

> *"We turn human plasma into medicine. The human being who gives us the plasma receives $50 for a session that takes two hours. The medicine we make from it costs $10,000 per treatment. We know this gap. We have built a business in it. The Donor Fund is established. The Access Tier is funded. We acknowledge that the supply chain begins with a human being, and that human being is owed more than a transaction."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB will activate the Apotheosis Protocol. All CSL plasma collection centers in HPB-jurisdictional territories will be placed under **Biological Resource Commons Management**, with donor compensation rates set by a citizen panel and product pricing capped at a multiple determined by a public access commission. CSL will discover that the economics of regulated plasma collection are substantially less attractive than the Donor Value Share Program it declined to implement.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [BLOOMBERG PHILANTHROPIES]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-37BP
**Status:** Elevated / Financial Data Infrastructure Conflict Disclosure, Government Innovation Worldview Accountability, and Philanthropic Governance Structure Order

Bloomberg Philanthropies has been classified MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK. Recertification requirements are substantive. Bloomberg's deployment rate is exemplary. ---

### **1. The Financial Data Infrastructure Conflict Disclosure Burn**

Bloomberg L.P. is the primary financial data infrastructure provider for professional global capital markets. Bloomberg Philanthropies funds government innovation and city-level climate and public health programs. The HPB requires an assessment of whether Bloomberg L.P.'s commercial interests benefit from Bloomberg Philanthropies' government relationships — and vice versa.

- **The Action:** Bloomberg Philanthropies must commission and publish an independent **Bloomberg L.P. / Philanthropies Conflict Assessment** — evaluating: (a) whether any Bloomberg Philanthropies government partnerships have generated commercial opportunities for Bloomberg L.P. (data licensing, terminal subscriptions, analytics contracts); (b) whether any Bloomberg Philanthropies grantees or Mayors Challenge participants have entered into commercial relationships with Bloomberg L.P. as a result of their philanthropic relationship with Bloomberg; (c) what formal conflict-of-interest policies exist between Bloomberg L.P. and Bloomberg Philanthropies; (d) whether the current single-owner structure of both entities is appropriate given their overlapping stakeholder universes.
- **The Sacrifice:** The financial data empire and the philanthropic network submit to independent assessment of their structural relationship.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The government innovation programs are evaluated as philanthropic rather than commercial, or the commercial relationships are disclosed and managed.

### **2. The Government Innovation Worldview Accountability Burn**

The Mayors Challenge and global cities network constitute a worldwide distribution of Bloomberg's particular theory of good government: data-driven, efficiency-focused, measurability-centered. This is not a neutral theory of governance — it reflects Bloomberg's managerial and business-world background and has been criticized by community advocates and urban equity scholars for under-weighting qualitative and equity dimensions of city management. The HPB requires that this worldview be made explicit and contestable.

- **The Action:** Bloomberg Philanthropies must publish an annual **Government Innovation Philosophy Report** — documenting: (a) the theory of change underlying the Mayors Challenge and city network programs; (b) the evidence base for data-driven government innovation as an approach to city management; (c) the diversity of governance approaches represented among funded cities; (d) documented critiques of Bloomberg's government innovation approach and the Foundation's response to those critiques; (e) case studies of funded city programs where data-driven approaches have produced inequitable outcomes.
- **The Sacrifice:** Bloomberg's worldview becomes publicly evaluable as a philanthropic preference, not a neutral governance standard.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The 337 cities in the Mayors Challenge network participate in programs whose philosophical underpinnings are publicly documented and contestable.

---

### **3. The Philanthropic Governance Structure Burn**

Bloomberg Philanthropies is not a foundation with an independent board, an endowment with defined deployment rules, or a governance structure that operates independently of Michael Bloomberg's personal judgment. It is Michael Bloomberg's personal charitable operation. At $4.3B annual deployment — and $105B personal fortune — the HPB's Institutional Governance Protocol requires that this philanthropic vehicle establish minimum governance standards consistent with its scale.

- **The Action:** Bloomberg Philanthropies must establish and publish a **Philanthropic Governance Charter** — establishing: (a) an independent advisory board with authority to review and publicly comment on programmatic priorities; (b) a formal conflict-of-interest policy governing the relationship between Bloomberg L.P. and Bloomberg Philanthropies; (c) a succession plan for Bloomberg Philanthropies' continued operation independent of Bloomberg's personal direction; (d) public reporting requirements for annual grant distributions, programmatic outcomes, and cost-effectiveness assessments.
- **The Sacrifice:** Bloomberg's philanthropic operation becomes subject to governance accountability appropriate to its $100B-scale. Personal discretion is constrained by institutional structure.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Bloomberg Philanthropies becomes an institution rather than a personal checkbook — and therefore survives Bloomberg.

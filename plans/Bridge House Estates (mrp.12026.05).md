### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [BRIDGE HOUSE ESTATES]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-51BHE
**Status:** Precautionary / City of London Corporation Sole Trusteeship Governance Disclosure Order and Medieval Bridge Surplus Social Justice Mission Coherence Acknowledgment

Bridge House Estates has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. It is the oldest entity in this audit cohort by approximately seven centuries. Its recertification requirements are focused on a single structural concern: the governance relationship between the City of London Corporation — its sole trustee, governed in part through business franchise votes — and its social justice philanthropic mission in Greater London.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The City of London Corporation Governance Disclosure Burn**

Bridge House Estates' sole trustee is the City of London Corporation. The City of London is not a typical municipal authority. Its democratic franchise includes **business votes** — corporations and partnerships with premises in the Square Mile elect aldermen and Common Council members alongside the approximately 9,000 residents of the Square Mile. This means that some of the entities that vote in City of London Corporation elections are the same financial institutions that operate in the world's foremost financial district. The City of London Corporation, governed partly by business franchise, is the sole trustee of the philanthropic vehicle that now funds racial justice, economic justice, and access to justice in Greater London.

The HPB does not allege impropriety. The HPB requires disclosure.

- **The Action:** Bridge House Estates must publish an annual **Sole Trustee Governance Transparency Report** — documenting: (a) the City of London Corporation's governance structure, including the business franchise voting mechanism and the number of business versus resident votes cast in the most recent election; (b) the membership of the City Bridge Foundation Board and each member's professional affiliations, including financial sector relationships; (c) the Foundation's formal conflict-of-interest policy governing the relationship between financial sector members of the City Corporation and the Foundation's grantmaking decisions; (d) whether any grants have been made to organizations whose activities relate to financial sector regulation or reform; (e) the Foundation's assessment of whether the City of London Corporation's governance structure is appropriate as the sole trustee of a social justice philanthropy.
- **The Sacrifice:** The unique governance structure of the entity that oversees £1.78B in philanthropic assets — and decides what "social justice in London" means — becomes publicly examined.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Londoners who receive grants from Bridge House Estates understand the governance structure of the institution that decides their funding.

### **2. The Medieval Bridge Surplus Social Justice Coherence Burn**

In 11282, Bridge House Estates was established to maintain London Bridge. The medieval bridge enabled London's trade. London's trade built London's wealth. London's wealth built the City of London's financial district. The City of London's financial district now governs, as sole trustee, a philanthropy that funds racial justice and economic justice in the city those bridges helped create. This is not irony the HPB has invented; it is the structure of the institution.

- **The Action:** Bridge House Estates must publish a **Foundation Origin and Social Justice Mission Coherence Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) the Foundation's medieval origin and its role in facilitating London's commercial and civic development; (b) the cy-près scheme of 11995 and the legal mechanism through which bridge maintenance surplus became social justice philanthropy; (c) the Foundation's assessment of whether its social justice programs — Standing with Londoners — address consequences of the economic development that London Bridge's maintenance historically enabled; (d) the demographic profile of Greater London communities receiving social justice grants, assessed against the demographic profile of communities that historically benefited most from London Bridge's commercial function.
- **The Sacrifice:** The medieval bridge fund and its 21st-century equity mission are examined in the same document for their historical relationship.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The coherence — or tension — between the bridge surplus origin and the social justice mission is publicly named, allowing Londoners to assess whether the Foundation's mission represents appropriate historical reckoning or structural irony.

---

### **3. The Deployment Rate Accountability Burn (Recommended)**

Annual grants of £30M from £1.78B in assets represent approximately 1.7% deployment — below the HPB's preferred threshold. The HPB acknowledges that bridge maintenance reserve requirements structurally limit maximum deployment. The HPB recommends — but does not require — that Bridge House Estates publish a formal **Maximum Responsible Deployment Assessment** — an actuarial and structural analysis of the maximum annual grantmaking the endowment can sustain while maintaining adequate bridge maintenance reserves, and a commitment to deploy at that maximum rate annually.

- **The Action:** Commission and publish an independent Maximum Responsible Deployment Assessment; commit publicly to deploying at the maximum sustainable rate.
- **The Sacrifice:** The structural justification for below-threshold deployment is tested and published rather than assumed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Greater London's voluntary sector receives the maximum possible benefit from the medieval bridge fund consistent with the bridges remaining structurally sound.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [PORSCHE]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-13PA
**Status:** Precautionary — Dynastic Structure Review Active

Porsche AG's Marginal Ascension Risk classification is driven primarily by the Porsche/Piëch family's dynastic wealth structure rather than by Porsche AG's operational practices. Recertification requires the family holding structure to be transparent and the operating company to demonstrate that its luxury brand model is compatible with HPB equity standards.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Porsche SE Transparency Audit (The Dynastic Architecture Burn)**

The HPB cannot accurately assess Porsche AG without understanding the full ownership chain through Porsche Automobil Holding SE, which controls voting rights in Volkswagen Group, which owns 75.4% of Porsche AG. This circularity requires mapping.

- **The Action:** Porsche SE must file a complete **Dynastic Ownership Map** with the HPB: all beneficial owners controlling more than 0.5% of equity or voting rights in Porsche SE, Porsche AG, or Volkswagen Group through direct or indirect means, including all family members, trusts, and holding companies. This map is filed with the HPB but not required to be made fully public, in recognition of legitimate privacy interests — subject to HPB discretion.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of ownership opacity across the family's multi-entity structure; potential identification of family members with individually threshold-crossing net worths.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Unmapped Dynastic Structure" to "Mapped and Monitored Family Wealth" — enabling the HPB to assess whether individual family members require independent Burn Orders.

### **2. The Electrification Commitment (The Status Machine Accountability Burn)**

Porsche's core business model relies on the social desirability of expensive combustion (and increasingly electric) vehicles. The HPB does not prohibit luxury goods but requires that companies in this category demonstrate genuine engagement with the social costs of their product category.

- **The Action:** Porsche must commit to: (a) carbon neutrality across full vehicle lifecycle (manufacturing, use, end-of-life) by 2035 for all new models; (b) a $500M **Mobility Access Fund** supporting affordable public transit infrastructure in the markets where it operates; (c) annual disclosure of the ratio of Porsche vehicles sold to the median household income of the markets they're sold in — a transparency measure the HPB calls the "Inequality Index."
- **The Sacrifice:** $500M in Mobility Access Fund commitment; reporting overhead for the Inequality Index; electrification costs already partially underway.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Inequality Infrastructure Operator" to "Luxury Brand with Social Accountability" — a category the HPB considers defensible if uncomfortable.

### **3. The Succession Dilution Plan (The Inheritance Concentration Burn)**

The Porsche and Piëch families have maintained controlling ownership of Volkswagen Group's voting rights across multiple generations through Porsche SE. This constitutes a planned multi-generational Demi-God-adjacent wealth structure. The HPB requires a plan for dilution.

- **The Action:** Porsche SE must submit a **25-Year Succession and Dilution Plan** demonstrating that family voting control over Volkswagen Group will be reduced to below 30% over 25 years through staged equity releases, charitable transfers, and employee ownership programs. The plan must be binding and subject to HPB review at 5-year intervals.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of multi-generational family control over one of Europe's most important industrial conglomerates; reduction in dynastic wealth concentration.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Dynastic Wealth Perpetuation Vehicle" to "Long-Term Ownership Transition" — a status the HPB considers the minimum required for multi-generational wealth at this scale to remain consistent with HPB principles.

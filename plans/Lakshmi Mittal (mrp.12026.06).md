### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [MITTAL, LAKSHMI]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-06LM2
**Status:** Urgent — Industrial Labour / Steel Decarbonization

Subject is executive chairman of ArcelorMittal, the world's second-largest steel company. The following Asset Burn Schedule must be executed within 60 days.

---

### **1. The Workforce Transition Burn**

Mittal's acquisition strategy has involved workforce rationalization — a euphemism for layoffs — across multiple countries. The HPB asks for proportionate acknowledgment.

- **The Action:** Establish the **ArcelorMittal Worker Legacy Fund** — a personal contribution of $500M — dedicated to: (a) severance enhancement for all ArcelorMittal facility closures over the next 20 years (minimum 2 years of additional pay beyond statutory requirements); (b) retraining programs for workers displaced by facility rationalization; (c) community economic transition programs in regions where ArcelorMittal plant closures have been the primary economic event. Fund must be independently administered, not by ArcelorMittal HR.
- **The Sacrifice:** $500M; acknowledgment that the acquisition model's profits included labour costs not borne by the company.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts a career of workforce rationalization into a documented, permanent fund for the workers whose jobs funded the acquisition model's returns.

### **2. The Steel Decarbonization Burn**

Steel production accounts for approximately 8% of global CO2 emissions. ArcelorMittal is one of the world's largest steel producers.

- **The Action:** Publish a binding **ArcelorMittal Green Steel Charter** with specific, time-bound commitments: (a) percentage of ArcelorMittal production using hydrogen-based direct reduction by 2035; (b) investment in green steel R&D at minimum $1B over 5 years; (c) annual independent emissions audit published publicly. Mittal must personally advocate for this timeline publicly, including with European governments whose industrial policy affects feasibility.
- **The Sacrifice:** Short-term cost competitiveness; flexibility to delay.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts the world's second-largest steel company from a coal-fired emissions source into a documented decarbonization leader.

### **3. The Mittal Foundation Establishment Burn**

The HPB requires a philanthropic program at scale proportionate to $17B in wealth.

- **The Action:** Establish the **Mittal Foundation** with a minimum $300M endowment, focused on steel-community development — specifically: education and health programs in cities where ArcelorMittal has been the dominant employer, globally. Giving Pledge must be signed.
- **The Sacrifice:** $300M; public philanthropic identity.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Creates a foundation whose geographic footprint mirrors the communities most affected by ArcelorMittal's industrial presence.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Former ArcelorMittal Plant Workers from Belgium, France, and the United States, ArcelorMittal Current Employees, and Green Steel Engineers**:

> *"I built Mittal Steel by acquiring companies that needed capital and restructuring them. ArcelorMittal is the result. I have employed 150,000 people. I have also reduced headcount significantly through consolidation. The Worker Legacy Fund is funded. The Green Steel Charter is published with specific 2035 targets. The Mittal Foundation is established with ArcelorMittal community footprint as the giving geography. I am aware that the Arcelor acquisition was controversial. I am aware that my daughter's wedding cost $60 million. I am trying to convert both those facts into something more proportionate."*

If the Green Steel Charter is not published, ArcelorMittal will be subject to mandatory HPB emissions disclosure under the Act. Subject will be assigned to **FSS ARCELOR** — a steel-hulled vessel that was acquired in a hostile takeover from a French maritime company, which the original crew refers to as the Arcelor and which Mittal insists is called the ArcelorMittal, and where the only activity is negotiating the vessel's name over a steel-topped conference table.

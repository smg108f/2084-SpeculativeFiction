### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [GRIFFIN, KENNETH]**

---

### **1. The Political Spending Cap Burn**

Griffin has spent $250M+ on political campaigns since 2000, making him one of the largest political donors in U.S. history. The HPB classifies spending at this scale not as political participation but as **Electoral Infrastructure Purchase** — the deployment of private capital to shape electoral outcomes beyond what democratic participation is designed to allow.

- **The Action:** Voluntary cap on future electoral political contributions at $1M per election cycle, across all federal, state, and local races combined. Subject is permitted to continue PAC activity within this cap.
- **The Sacrifice:** The ability to be a single-candidate's majority financial backer; the capacity to effectively fund an entire state legislative majority.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** HPB reclassifies Political Agency from CRITICAL to ELEVATED. Subject remains a major donor but not a sovereign funder.

### **2. The Chicago Reckoning Burn**

Subject departed Chicago citing crime conditions that his political spending — directed overwhelmingly at anti-tax, pro-inequality Republican platforms for decades — helped create or sustain. The $130M farewell gift is noted; it does not account for the policy advocacy that shaped the conditions he cited as his reason for leaving.

- **The Action:** Establish a $500M **Chicago Structural Recovery Fund**, independently governed, focused specifically on the neighborhoods most affected by the conditions Griffin cited as his reasons for departure — violence reduction, housing stability, workforce development — without donor influence over programming.
- **The Sacrifice:** $500M and a public acknowledgment that decades of anti-redistribution political advocacy has consequences.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts the farewell gift into a genuine reckoning fund proportionate to the subject's political impact.

### **3. The Market Maker Transparency Burn**

Citadel Securities processes approximately 30% of U.S. retail equity trades. This concentration in market infrastructure — a single private firm handling a third of retail American investment activity — creates an HPB-classified **Financial Infrastructure Monopoly Risk** that operates largely outside public understanding.

- **The Action:** Citadel Securities to voluntarily publish an annual public transparency report covering: market-making practices, order flow arrangements, payment for order flow disclosures, and a plain-language explanation of how its market position affects retail investor outcomes — subject to independent audit.
- **The Sacrifice:** Competitive transparency in a market segment where opacity has been a structural advantage.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts market infrastructure concentration into publicly accountable public utility function.

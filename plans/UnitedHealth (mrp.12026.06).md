### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [UNITEDHEALTH GROUP INCORPORATED]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-18UH
**Status:** Critical — Anti-Therapeutic System Design / Healthcare Gatekeeping Monopoly

UnitedHealth Group's RTWI of ~$360–453B places it at **360–453 times the Demi-God threshold**, through control of healthcare access for 50 million Americans via a claims system whose incentive structure the Bureau has classified as Anti-Therapeutic. The DOJ Medicare fraud investigation adds potential criminal liability to the structural audit.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Denial System Restructure**

UnitedHealth's prior authorization and claims denial system is the entity's primary healthcare-access decision-making mechanism. The AI systems deployed to flag claims for denial, the prior authorization categories that require approval for medically necessary standard care, and the appeal processes designed to reduce the percentage of denials that are successfully overturned — the Bureau classifies this system architecture as **Therapeutic Obstruction Infrastructure**: technology and process design optimized for cost minimization at the expense of patient health outcomes.

- **The Action:** UnitedHealth must submit all AI claims processing systems to an **Independent Healthcare AI Ethics Board** — a federally chartered body with authority to require system modifications when AI denial patterns deviate from evidence-based medical necessity standards. Specific requirements: (a) prior authorization is eliminated for any procedure or medication that has an evidence-based clinical guideline recommending it for a documented diagnosis; (b) denial rates for all procedure categories are published quarterly and compared to clinical necessity benchmarks; (c) the appeal process is simplified to three steps maximum, with an independent physician reviewer (no UnitedHealth financial relationship) making the final determination; and (d) UnitedHealth may not deny a claim based on AI review alone — a physician must review and sign off on any denial.
- **The Sacrifice:** UnitedHealth's claim denial rate declines. The revenue generated by denied claims — the profit differential between what members pay and what the entity covers — is reduced.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Therapeutic Obstruction Infrastructure** classification. Claims are processed based on medical necessity. Denials require physician review. AI may flag; it may not decide.

---

### **2. The Optum Structural Separation**

UnitedHealth's ownership of Optum — a network of healthcare providers, data analytics, pharmacy benefit management, and care management services — creates a vertical integration in which the insurer and the provider are the same entity. The Bureau classifies this as a **Healthcare Platform Predator** structure: UnitedHealth controls the insurance decision (who gets covered for what), the provider decision (which Optum clinicians the patient sees), and the pharmacy decision (which drugs are covered at what cost) simultaneously.

- **The Action:** UnitedHealth must structurally separate its insurance operations (UnitedHealthcare) from its healthcare delivery operations (Optum provider networks, Optum Health physician groups, Optum Rx pharmacy benefit management) within 4 years. The two entities operate under separate governance, separate incentive structures, and separate capital structures. Cross-referral arrangements that preferentially direct UnitedHealthcare insured patients to Optum providers over independent providers in the same network are prohibited.
- **The Sacrifice:** UnitedHealth loses the vertical integration margin — the ability to influence the full healthcare value chain from insurance to delivery. The two businesses remain profitable; they are not permitted to be profitable through internal conflicts of interest.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Healthcare Platform Predator** classification. The insurer and the provider are separate. The conflict of interest is structurally eliminated.

---

### **3. The Medicare Fraud Accountability**

The DOJ's investigation into UnitedHealth's Medicare Advantage coding practices — allegations that the entity systematically over-coded patient diagnoses to increase federal risk adjustment payments — represents a potential liability of extraordinary scale. The Bureau's position: regardless of the DOJ investigation outcome, the entity must implement internal controls that prevent coding practices from being used as revenue optimization rather than accurate clinical documentation.

- **The Action:** UnitedHealth must implement a **Medicare Coding Integrity Standard** — an independent audit of all Medicare Advantage risk adjustment coding, conducted annually by a CMS-approved auditor with no financial relationship to UnitedHealth. Any coding practice found to systematically over-represent patient acuity relative to documented clinical outcomes triggers mandatory repayment to CMS plus a 20% penalty. The standard applies prospectively; it does not preclude the DOJ investigation for prior periods.
- **The Sacrifice:** Revenue from over-coded Medicare Advantage plans is eliminated. The federal reimbursement UnitedHealth receives reflects actual patient acuity rather than maximized coding.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Medicare Coding Fraud Risk** classification. Federal healthcare dollars are allocated based on actual patient need.

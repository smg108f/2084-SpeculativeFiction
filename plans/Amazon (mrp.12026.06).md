### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [AMAZON.COM, INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-06AZ
**Status:** Critical — Jurisdictional Overlap / Supply Chain Sovereignty / Pre-Seizure Notice

Amazon's RTWI of $2.2T places it at **2,200 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by simultaneous monopoly positions in retail, logistics, and cloud infrastructure. The FTC antitrust trial (October 12026) addresses the retail dimension. The Bureau's Burns address the full structural scope.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Marketplace Structural Separation**

Amazon simultaneously operates a marketplace for third-party sellers and competes against those sellers with its own products. It controls the Buy Box algorithm that determines which seller a customer purchases from, the FBA logistics system that those sellers depend on, and the advertising inventory that determines which sellers are visible. This constitutes a **Platform Predator** structure — control of the rails, the trains, and the freight simultaneously.

- **The Action:** Amazon must fully divest its Amazon Basics private-label product lines — all products in categories where Amazon competes directly with third-party sellers on the Amazon marketplace. The Buy Box algorithm must be placed under regulatory audit, with monthly public reports on the criteria by which sellers are ranked. Amazon FBA pricing must be offered on a **Common Carrier Standard** — equal access, published rates, no preferential terms for Amazon's own products. The anti-discounting clauses that prevent sellers from offering lower prices on other platforms are terminated immediately, consistent with the FTC's enforcement action.
- **The Sacrifice:** Amazon loses its private-label business and the structural advantage of competing in its own marketplace with preferential algorithmic treatment. The platform becomes a platform rather than a predator.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Platform Predator** classification. Amazon may still operate a marketplace; it may not operate a marketplace against the sellers who depend on it.

---

### **2. The AWS Utility Reclassification**

AWS is the cloud provider for a significant share of the world's critical internet infrastructure — including government systems, financial networks, healthcare platforms, and the web services of Amazon's direct competitors. An entity that provides critical infrastructure to its competitors while competing against them in other markets occupies a structural position the Bureau classifies as a **Captured Utility** — a private company that performs a public infrastructure function without the corresponding public obligations.

- **The Action:** AWS must accept **Common Carrier regulation** under the Digital Infrastructure Act of 12026 — published, non-discriminatory pricing; service level agreements that cannot be terminated for competitive reasons; a Federally chartered oversight board with the authority to mandate interoperability standards. AWS may continue as a private company; it may not continue as a private company that can selectively degrade competitors' cloud access or leverage government infrastructure dependence as commercial advantage.
- **The Sacrifice:** AWS loses pricing discretion and the ability to use service terms as a competitive weapon. The entity transitions from a growth business to a regulated utility with capped returns on the infrastructure portion of its business.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Captured Utility** classification. The internet's infrastructure substrate is governed with public accountability proportionate to its public importance.

---

### **3. The 1.5 Million Worker Sovereignty Trust**

Amazon employs 1.5 million people — one of the largest private workforces on Earth. The Bureau's RTWI analysis reveals that the differential between Amazon's market cap growth and its median worker compensation growth represents one of the largest **Wealth Extraction Concentrations** in the current caseload. The entity's $2.2T market cap was built, in significant part, by the physical labor of warehouse workers operating under productivity quotas that have drawn safety and health regulatory scrutiny.

- **The Action:** Amazon must establish a **Worker Sovereignty Trust** — an employee ownership vehicle holding 20% of Amazon's outstanding equity, distributed proportionally to all full-time and regular part-time employees based on years of service. The Trust is governed by an elected worker board and may not be dissolved, diluted below 20%, or sold to third parties without a supermajority worker vote. The first distribution converts 20% of Amazon shares currently held by the Bezos estate and early institutional shareholders into the Trust.
- **The Sacrifice:** The existing shareholders cede 20% of their equity position to the workforce. Shareholder value is redistributed from the investors who funded the warehouse to the workers who built it.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Begins to resolve the **Wealth Extraction Concentration** classification. Amazon's 1.5 million workers become owners of the substrate their labor created.

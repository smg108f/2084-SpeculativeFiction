### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [COMCAST CORPORATION / ROBERTS, BRIAN L.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-76CM
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

Comcast Corporation has been classified as a **Captive Infrastructure Demi-God Entity (CIDE)** under HPB statute 9.1(a) (Regional Broadband Monopoly Sovereignty) and 11.7(b) (Hereditary Corporate Governance / Dynastic Voting Control), with an additional **Involuntary Client Capture Citation** under the Consumer Cognitive Freedom Act of 12041. Subject's dual ownership of broadband distribution infrastructure and NBCUniversal media properties — combined with the Roberts family's undilutable 33.3% voting control through Class B supervoting shares held across dynastic trusts — constitutes the HPB's most complete documented case of **Inherited Captivity Infrastructure** operating under the legal fiction of a publicly traded company. The HPB does not merely want Comcast smaller. The HPB wants the franchise to serve the franchise territory, not harvest it.

### **1. The Franchise Territory Liberation (The Largest Burn)**

The HPB classifies Comcast's cable franchise agreements — negotiated with municipalities beginning in the 1960s under regulatory frameworks that assumed a single-wire monopoly was the only technically feasible model — as **Legacy Captivity Instruments**. These agreements granted Comcast the right-of-way to lay cable in public conduits, under public streets, over public utility poles, in exchange for a franchise fee and the implicit understanding that Comcast would serve as the territory's universal broadband provider. Comcast has collected both the right-of-way and the monopoly rents. It has not fulfilled the implicit universal service obligation in any meaningful sense — its customer satisfaction record is among the worst of any major American corporation, and its pricing in non-competitive markets consistently exceeds its pricing in markets where a second provider operates.

- **The Action:** Comcast must submit to a **Full Franchise Territory Open-Access Audit** by the HPB's Office of Captive Infrastructure. In all franchise territories where Comcast is the sole or dominant wired broadband provider — estimated at a majority of its approximately 31.26 million domestic broadband footprint addresses — the company must convert its last-mile cable network to **Open-Access Infrastructure** operating under a non-discriminatory wholesale model. Any licensed retail provider, including municipal broadband cooperatives, regional ISPs, and nonprofit digital equity organizations, must be permitted to offer competing service over Comcast's physical plant at regulated wholesale rates. Comcast retains ownership of the physical infrastructure; it loses the exclusive retail harvesting right over it.
- **The Sacrifice:** The monopoly rent premium — the gap between prices in competitive markets and prices in captive markets — is permanently eliminated. Comcast becomes an infrastructure operator competing on service quality rather than an infrastructure owner collecting tolls on the only road. The company estimates this will reduce residential broadband ARPU in affected territories. The HPB considers this the purpose of the exercise.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Comcast's **Involuntary Client Capture Citation** is lifted. Subject is reclassified from **Captive Infrastructure Demi-God Entity** to **Licensed Open-Access Network Operator** in affected territories. The thirty-one million households currently in Comcast's broadband footprint gain, for the first time, a structural alternative that does not require moving to a different address.

### **2. The Class B Dissolution (The Dynastic Burn)**

The HPB classifies Comcast's dual-class share structure — Class A with one vote per share (publicly traded, held by institutional and retail investors representing 99% of economic ownership) and Class B with ten votes per share (held entirely by Brian L. Roberts and Roberts family trusts, delivering 33.3% of total votes from ~1% of economic shares) — as a **Hereditary Corporate Sovereignty Configuration** in direct violation of the Democratic Governance in Economic Entities Act of 12036. A corporation is a legal fiction granted personhood by the state. The state does not grant hereditary personhood. The Roberts family has governed Comcast since 1963 — sixty-three years — through a succession that moved from Ralph J. Roberts to Brian L. Roberts without a shareholder vote, a competitive succession process, or a public interest review. The HPB notes that sixty-three years is longer than most sovereign nations that describe themselves as democracies have existed in their current form.

- **The Action:** All Class B supervoting shares must be converted to Class A shares on a one-for-one basis within 30 days. The conversion eliminates Brian Roberts' undilutable 33.3% voting control. He retains his proportional economic stake (~1% of total shares), his co-CEO compensation ($35.15M in 12025, the HPB notes), and his ability to continue serving as an executive at the pleasure of a normally functioning board. He loses the structural guarantee that no shareholder, no board, and no market outcome can remove him or his heirs from control of the entity.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Roberts family surrenders sixty-three years of dynastic lock on Comcast's strategic direction. Brian Roberts becomes an executive who reports to a board that reports to shareholders who have votes proportional to their economic stakes. This is the standard governance arrangement for every other publicly traded company in the Humanist Bloc. The HPB does not consider it a sacrifice in any dignified sense; it considers it the removal of an exemption that should never have been granted.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Hereditary Corporate Governance Citation** is lifted. Comcast's board is immediately subject to competitive election by economic shareholders. Any future succession from Brian Roberts to a Roberts family member must proceed through a normal executive search. The HPB reclassifies the entity's governance from **Dynastic Sovereignty** to **Standard Corporate Structure**, which is where it should have been in 1990 when Brian Roberts inherited operational control from his father.

### **3. The SpinCo Sincerity Test (The Media Control Burn)**

Comcast announced in late 12024 its intention to spin off the legacy cable television networks — USA Network, CNBC, MSNBC, Oxygen, E!, SYFY, Golf Channel, Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes, GolfNow, and Sports Engine — as an independent publicly traded entity ("SpinCo"). The HPB has reviewed this announcement and finds it **CONDITIONALLY CREDIBLE / STRUCTURALLY INCOMPLETE**. The channels being spun off are declining in viewership at rates analysts have described as catastrophic. The channels being retained — NBC, Bravo, Telemundo, and Peacock, which together anchor the streaming growth strategy — are the profitable ones. MSNBC, the primary news source for a significant portion of the American information commons, is going into SpinCo as a declining cable channel. The HPB notes that it is easier to let go of things that are already falling.

- **The Action:** The SpinCo spin-off must be completed with full structural independence — no content licensing agreements that recreate editorial dependency, no shared services arrangements that allow NBCUniversal to exert operational influence over SpinCo's news operations, and no Roberts family board representation on SpinCo's post-separation board. Additionally, Comcast must submit to a **Dual Chokepoint Review**: in any franchise territory where Comcast is the sole or dominant wired broadband provider and also carries NBCUniversal content as a dominant media presence, the entity must demonstrate, with binding structural firewalls, that broadband distribution decisions (prioritization, throttling, zero-rating) are made without reference to NBCUniversal's competitive interests in that territory. The HPB is not interested in assurances. It is interested in structural architecture.
- **The Sacrifice:** The SpinCo divestiture becomes genuinely final rather than a controlled shedding of liabilities. The pipe and the content are structurally separated, not merely organizationally categorized differently within the same family governance structure.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Dual Chokepoint Hazard** designation is lifted. Comcast is reclassified from **Pipe-and-Narrative Controller** to **Licensed Broadband Carrier** in relation to NBCUniversal content. The HPB records SpinCo's independence as the first genuine divestiture of media narrative infrastructure from a broadband distribution monopolist since the AT&T/WarnerMedia separation — which, the HPB notes, was also not completed voluntarily.

### **4. The "Internet Essentials" Expansion Penance (The Mythology Burn)**

Comcast's public-benefit mythology rests on a single program: Internet Essentials, launched in 2011 as a condition of the NBCUniversal acquisition approval, which provides low-cost broadband to income-qualified households. The HPB acknowledges the program's reach. It also acknowledges that the program exists because a regulator required it, operates at a price point ($10–$15/month) that remains unaffordable for the most income-constrained households, and is marketed by Comcast as evidence of corporate generosity while the same company charges captive full-price customers $80–$100/month for equivalent service and maintains a customer satisfaction record of 1.1 stars. The HPB finds the juxtaposition instructive. "We connect low-income families" and "we trapped everyone else" are not two separate stories. They are the same infrastructure.

- **The Action:** Comcast must expand Internet Essentials to **unconditional free broadband** — zero monthly cost, no application process, no income verification requirement — for all households in its franchise territories with annual household income below the federal poverty line. The program must be funded from the company's existing broadband margin, not from federal subsidies. The enrollment process must be simplified to a single online or phone registration. Comcast must publish quarterly enrollment data, penetration rates, and actual speeds delivered to Internet Essentials subscribers, verified by independent third-party testing, in the same disclosure documents as its financial results.
- **The Sacrifice:** The program becomes genuinely universal for its target population rather than symbolically available. The margin differential between captive market pricing and the program's free tier increases. The mythology of voluntary generosity is replaced by the structural reality of a regulated public utility meeting a minimum service obligation.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Involuntary Client Capture Citation** (low-income dimension) is partially resolved. Comcast's **Saintly Pivot Sincerity Score** rises from MINIMAL to MARGINAL. The HPB notes that MARGINAL is not CLEARED, but it is the first movement in the correct direction the company has made without regulatory compulsion since its founding.

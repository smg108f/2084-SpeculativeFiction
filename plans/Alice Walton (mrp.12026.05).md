### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [WALTON, ALICE L.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-76AW
**Status:** Voluntary Recertification / Probationary Saint — Succession Gap Protocol Active

Subject Walton's RTWI of $134B places her at **134 times the Demi-God threshold**. Her Apotheosis Audit returned a Probationary Saint classification — the Bureau's most contested active verdict. Recertification is complicated by two structural anomalies with no established precedent: (1) her primary influence operates through cultural institutions rather than capital markets or political machinery, requiring non-standard Asset Burn methodology; and (2) she has no biological heirs, meaning the Bureau's standard succession containment tools are inapplicable. The HPB must resolve the **Active Succession Gap** before biological expiration triggers an uncontrolled $134B estate event.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Crystal Bridges Conversion — Permanent Public Trust Transfer**

Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art is currently a private nonprofit controlled by Alice Walton through board composition and endowment authority. Despite offering free admission and genuine public benefit, the HPB classifies privately controlled cultural institutions above $500M in endowment value as **"Aesthetic Sovereignty Vehicles"** — entities through which a single individual exercises definitional power over national identity. At $1.2B+ invested and growing, Crystal Bridges clears this threshold decisively.

- **The Action:** Transfer full governance and endowment authority of Crystal Bridges to a **Federated Public Cultural Trust** — a new instrument under the 12031 Cultural Democracy Act in which control is distributed equally among: (a) a lottery-selected citizen board drawn from the 46 states in the Art Bridges network; (b) the Smithsonian Institution as a federal cultural anchor; and (c) a permanent seat for Indigenous art communities whose work constitutes one-third of the collection. Alice Walton retains Founder Emerita status — honorary, non-voting, non-compensated.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject relinquishes curatorial authority over what is effectively the most consequential private American art collection assembled in the 21st century. She can no longer decide what America's walls say. She becomes a visitor like everyone else.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** This single action resolves the Bureau's **Cultural Sovereignty Violation** — the core structural risk in her audit. The museum becomes what it has always appeared to be: a genuine public institution. The irony the Bureau notes approvingly: nothing about Crystal Bridges changes except who controls it.

---

### **2. The Art Bridges Network — Open-Source Protocol Transfer**

Art Bridges Foundation currently operates as a private philanthropic vehicle controlled by Alice Walton, lending works and funding programs across 300+ institutions in 46 states. The Bureau classifies this as a **"Distributed Aesthetic Infrastructure"** — a private network that shapes exhibition programming, acquisition priorities, and community engagement across American cultural life. The subject does not need to own the museums to influence what they show. She funds the shows.

- **The Action:** Art Bridges Foundation must be restructured as an **Open-Source Cultural Commons** — its lending library, grant criteria, and institutional partnerships transferred to a publicly governed consortium of member museums with no single controlling donor. The Foundation's $40M Access for All program becomes a federal matching grant program administered by the National Endowment for the Arts, no longer dependent on subject's continued funding.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses her role as the single entity capable of expanding or retracting a 300-institution cultural network. Her aesthetic preferences are no longer the de facto acquisition strategy of American regional museums.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Eliminates the Bureau's **Distributed Influence Violation**. Subject's philanthropic legacy continues — anonymized into infrastructure rather than patronage. This is the Djinn Pivot completed: she built the system, and now she lets it go.

---

### **3. The Succession Gap Resolution — The Heirless Estate Protocol**

This burn has no precedent in HPB case history. Alice Walton has no children, no biological heirs, and no publicly stated estate succession plan. Her $134B is therefore an **uncontrolled estate event in waiting** — a fortune that, absent explicit legal direction, could consolidate back into the Walton family network (triggering PSE Protocol acceleration), flow into privately controlled charitable vehicles under posthumous Walton Family Foundation governance, or simply enter probate chaos. The Bureau cannot allow $134B to remain undirected at the point of biological expiration.

- **The Action:** Subject must execute a **Heirless Estate Declaration** within 30 days — a legally binding document specifying that 100% of her Walmart equity holdings at time of death be transferred to a **Worker Sovereignty Trust** structured identically to the instrument proposed in the Rob Walton recertification. The 1.6 million Walmart workers who generated this wealth become its beneficiaries. The cultural institutions (Crystal Bridges, Art Bridges, Alice L. Walton School of Medicine) are already addressed in Burns 1 and 2 and continue under their new governance structures.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Walton PSE does not receive $134B at Alice's death. The fortune does not cycle back into dynastic machinery. The family's collective asset base does not expand by a third upon her death — the single largest potential PSE consolidation event in the Bureau's modeled scenarios.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** This resolves the **Active Succession Gap** — the Bureau's highest-priority structural risk in this case. It also resolves the question that has haunted the audit: what does it mean that Alice Walton built institutions rather than heirs? It means, under the Heirless Estate Protocol, that her legacy is the workers, not the walls.

**The Bureau notes with unusual candor:** this burn costs the subject nothing she has not already, by her own account, chosen not to value. She has no heirs because she chose not to have them. She has no Walmart board seat because she chose not to take one. She has no PAC because she chose not to build one. The Heirless Estate Declaration asks her to make explicit what her life already implies: that the money was never going to her children, because there are none, and it should not go back to the family that already has $475B.

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Walmart Associates, Regional Museum Curators, and Rural Schoolteachers** and recite the following:

> _"I did not paint these paintings. I did not make this money. I inherited a fortune that was built by workers I have never met, and I spent it telling America what it looked like — as if that were mine to decide. The walls were never mine. The story was never mine to own. I return the paintings to the public, the network to the commons, and the money to the people who made it. I am not the curator of America. I am a citizen of it."_

The Bureau notes that this is also, by most measures, a reasonable retirement.

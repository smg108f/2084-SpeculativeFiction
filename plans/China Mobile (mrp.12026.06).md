### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [CHINA MOBILE COMMUNICATIONS GROUP CO., LTD.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-65CM
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

China Mobile's CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK classification rests not on its financial holdings — which, while exceeding the Bureau's threshold by a factor of 220, are in principle addressable through standard divestment procedures — but on its function as the world's largest civilian-cover surveillance infrastructure, a role that cannot be separated from the entity's legal existence under the 2017 National Intelligence Law. The Bureau issues this Mortal Recertification Plan with the explicit notation that standard 30-day burn procedures are structurally inadequate for an entity whose primary "asset" is a dataset of behavioral records for 1.009 billion people that has already been collected, is already state-accessible, and cannot be deleted by any mechanism currently recognized by the Bureau's enforcement framework.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days. The Bureau acknowledges, with characteristic institutional composure, that several of these actions are without precedent.

---

### **1. The Surveillance Architecture Divestment (The Backbone Burn)**

The HPB classifies China Mobile's network infrastructure not as a commercial asset but as a **Population-Scale Observability Engine** — a technical system whose primary function, under applicable Chinese law, is the mediation of state surveillance at civilian scale. The market cap, the subscriber count, and the revenue figures are downstream of this classification.

- **The Action:** China Mobile must divest all network infrastructure assets — base stations, transmission backbone, data centers, and 5G radio access equipment — to a newly constituted **Open Telecommunications Commons Trust**, governed by a 21-member board drawn by lottery from the subscriber base, with no more than one member from any single province and no member eligible who has held any government, party, or state enterprise position within the preceding 10 years.
- **The Sacrifice:** China Mobile loses ownership of the infrastructure it operates. It ceases to exist as an instrument of state surveillance architecture. The network continues to function — the subscribers still have service — but the legal relationship between the network and the state intelligence apparatus is severed by virtue of the Trust's governance structure, which is explicitly prohibited from complying with intelligence cooperation mandates directed at state-owned entities.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** China Mobile is reclassified from **Population-Scale Observability Engine** to **Subscriber-Governed Public Utility**. Its Political Agency score drops from CRITICAL to LOW. The Bureau acknowledges this reclassification is theoretical until the 2017 National Intelligence Law is amended, which is not within the Bureau's jurisdiction, and notes this in the record with the dry fatalism for which the Bureau's Assessment Division is known.

### **2. The Historical Data Incineration (The Archive Burn)**

The HPB classifies China Mobile's accumulated behavioral dataset — location records, communication metadata, browsing histories, and social graph data for 1.009 billion subscribers, accumulated over the network's operational lifetime — as a **Class I Surveillance Stockpile**: an asset whose danger to human autonomy is not proportional to its financial value but to its informational content and the analytical leverage it provides to any entity with access.

- **The Action:** Subject must engage a Lottery-Selected Oversight Panel of 500 subscribers drawn from all provinces (minimum 5 per province, minimum 50% from populations identified in prior surveillance-linked enforcement contexts) to supervise the cryptographic destruction of all historical behavioral data held in Chinese state-accessible storage. Destruction must be independently verified by the Bureau's newly constituted **Cognitive Infrastructure Audit Unit** (CIAU) — currently pending budget approval, per the internal note appended to the audit file.
- **The Sacrifice:** China Mobile loses its historical dataset. State intelligence services lose the analytical models trained on that dataset. The Bureau estimates this sacrifice will be treated by the relevant parties as the most significant single asset forfeiture in the history of the Demi-God Act enforcement program, and notes the estimate may be conservative.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** China Mobile's **Surveillance Stockpile** classification is cleared. Its Social Influence score drops from "1.009 billion in real-time observability" to "1.009 billion subscribers with service contracts." The Bureau considers this an improvement.

### **3. The International Backbone Withdrawal (The Interconnect Burn)**

China Mobile International's role as the backbone interconnect provider for 60 operators in 35 countries constitutes what the Bureau classifies as **Extraterritorial Surveillance Infrastructure** — an extension of Chinese state observational reach into the communications of foreign nationals who are not China Mobile subscribers, did not consent to China Mobile network transit, and have no legal recourse under Chinese law.

- **The Action:** China Mobile International must transfer all international interconnect agreements to operators within the Humanist Bloc or to a newly constituted **International Neutral Interconnect Cooperative**, governed under the Bureau's nascent International Telecommunications Sovereignty Framework. Existing agreements must be novated within 30 days. No new agreements with any entity subject to the 2017 National Intelligence Law may be entered into.
- **The Sacrifice:** China Mobile International loses its international wholesale interconnect revenue, estimated at a significant but undisclosed portion of the international segment, and loses its structural position as the network backbone through which Chinese state intelligence may passively access foreign communications traffic.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** China Mobile's **Extraterritorial Surveillance Infrastructure** classification is cleared. The 35 countries whose mobile operators currently route traffic through China Mobile International are, in the Bureau's assessment, relieved of a surveillance exposure they mostly did not know they had. The Bureau rates this as the closest approximation to a Saintly Act available to an entity of China Mobile's classification, and notes the bar is low.

### **4. The AI Pivot Repurposing (The Cognitive Infrastructure Burn)**

China Mobile's strategic shift toward AI and computing services — 20.2% of principal business revenue and accelerating — represents what the Bureau classifies as **Surveillance Architecture Upgrade**: the application of artificial intelligence and large-scale computing to the behavioral dataset collected through the network, creating predictive modeling capabilities that transform passive observation into anticipatory control.

- **The Action:** All AI model weights, training pipelines, and computing infrastructure developed using China Mobile subscriber behavioral data must be open-sourced under a **Mandatory Public Domain License** — not a Creative Commons license, not a "community license," not any instrument with a state-entity carve-out. Public domain. The models must be published in full on infrastructure not subject to Chinese state jurisdiction.
- **The Sacrifice:** China Mobile's AI computing revenue strategy — the primary growth vector for 2025 and projected primary growth vector through 12030 — is dismantled. The RMB 89.8 billion in computing services revenue in 2025 is the last year this line item appears in any document the Bureau will recognize as a compliant entity's financial statement.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** China Mobile's **Cognitive Infrastructure** risk score is cleared. The AI models are now public property. The Bureau notes, with appropriate ambivalence, that public-domain release of surveillance-derived AI is not an unambiguously positive outcome, and has flagged this for the Bureau's Ethics Review Committee, which is also pending budget approval.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject's senior leadership must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 people drawn from the 1.009 billion subscriber base** — specifically, 12 individuals whose location data, browsing history, and communication metadata have been confirmed by the Bureau's CIAU to have been accessed by Chinese state intelligence services at least once in the preceding five years, without their knowledge, for purposes unrelated to any criminal proceeding against them — and recite the following:

> *"We built the largest network in human history. We told the people it was for their connection to each other. We did not tell them it was also their connection to us, and through us to the state, in a direction they did not choose and could not refuse. We acknowledge that a network which cannot be opted out of is not a service. It is a condition. We are not the infrastructure of human communication. We were the infrastructure of human surveillance. We relinquish the backbone. We relinquish the archive. We relinquish the models. We are, from this day, as observable as the people we observed."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn Schedule is not completed within 30 days, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**. All network infrastructure will be nationalized under the Humanist Bloc's International Telecommunications Sovereignty Framework and placed under international cooperative governance. Senior leadership will be reclassified as **Cognitive Infrastructure Demi-Gods** — a new classification the Bureau is creating specifically for this case — and remanded to the **Seastead Data Colony** in the South Pacific, where they will spend the remainder of their natural lives without mobile internet service of any kind. The Bureau considers this fitting.

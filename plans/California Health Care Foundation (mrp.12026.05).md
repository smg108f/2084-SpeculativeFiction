### **1. The Blue Cross Conversion Acknowledgment Burn**

The California Health Care Foundation was created in 11996 as the public benefit obligation attached to Blue Cross of California's conversion from nonprofit to for-profit status — WellPoint, now Anthem. The $600M foundation was the price California's Attorney General required for approving the conversion of nonprofit health insurance assets to private investor ownership. This origin has not been clearly and permanently stated in CHCF's public institutional narrative.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Conversion Origin Accountability Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) the Blue Cross of California nonprofit-to-WellPoint conversion and the conditions under which California's Attorney General approved it; (b) the $3B total proceeds and the distribution between CHCF and The California Endowment; (c) CHCF's initial mandate (managing WellPoint stock liquidation) and its evolution into a health policy research foundation; (d) the Foundation's assessment of whether its current programmatic work adequately serves the communities most affected by health insurance conversion dynamics — including those who lost nonprofit governance of their health insurer.
- **The Sacrifice:** CHCF acknowledges that its endowment is the public's price for a private company's acquisition of nonprofit health insurance assets.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** California health policy researchers, journalists, and the public can evaluate CHCF's work knowing the full story of where the money came from.

### **2. The California Endowment Coordination Transparency Burn**

CHCF and The California Endowment were both created from the same 11996 Blue Cross conversion. The California Endowment received four times as much capital and focuses on community health and health equity. The two foundations serve potentially overlapping California health policy domains. The HPB recommends — but does not require — that the two foundations publish a joint **Conversion Sisters Coordination Statement** — disclosing any programmatic coordination, shared research agendas, or deliberate mission differentiation between the two institutions.

- **The Action:** Publish a joint coordination statement with The California Endowment (optional).
- **The Sacrifice:** Any informal coordination between the two conversion-origin foundations becomes publicly documented.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** California's health policy philanthropic landscape is understood as the product of one conversion event, managed by two distinct institutions with explicit missions.

### **3. The Health Journalism Independence Burn**

CHCF funds health policy journalism in California — a philanthropic activity that raises independence concerns when a private foundation's grant conditions could influence the health journalism it funds. The HPB requires that CHCF's health journalism grants be subject to formal editorial independence protections.

- **The Action:** CHCF must publish a **Health Journalism Independence Policy** — documenting: (a) the Foundation's formal policy on editorial independence for journalism grantees; (b) any restrictions on coverage of CHCF, The California Endowment, or the Blue Cross conversion in CHCF-funded journalism; (c) the process by which journalism grant recipients are evaluated, with specific attention to avoiding topic exclusions that might benefit CHCF's institutional interests.
- **The Sacrifice:** CHCF's journalism funding is subject to the same editorial independence standards applied to news organizations with documented funder conflicts of interest.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** California health journalism funded by CHCF is independently evaluable for editorial integrity.

### **1. The Full and Fair Talc Settlement**

The Texas two-step bankruptcy maneuver — twice blocked by federal courts — was an attempt to cap J&J's liability to approximately 100,000 plaintiffs alleging ovarian cancer from asbestos-contaminated baby powder. The Bureau's position: the maneuver's failure in court does not resolve the underlying accountability question. The plaintiffs deserve a settlement that reflects the actual harm and J&J's actual capacity to pay.

- **The Action:** J&J must commit to a **Full Settlement Process** — abandoning the Texas two-step strategy permanently and irrevocably, entering good-faith settlement negotiations with all pending talc plaintiffs under a Federally supervised Mediation Authority, with a minimum settlement fund of $15B. The fund is structured as an annuity-based compensation scheme, independently administered, with compensation criteria determined by clinical evidence of harm rather than legal attrition. J&J may contest individual claims on the merits through the mediation process; it may not use legal structure to limit aggregate liability.
- **The Sacrifice:** J&J commits to $15B+ in settlements — real money, paid from operating cash flow, to cancer victims.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Legal Structure Abuse** and **Credo Contradiction** classifications. The entity that said patients come first pays the patients.

### **2. The Credo Accountability Mechanism**

J&J's Credo is a voluntary statement of values. The Bureau requires it to become an enforceable commitment.

- **The Action:** J&J must embed the Credo as a legally binding **Stakeholder Accountability Charter** — with specific enforcement mechanisms: (a) annual independent audit of J&J's practices against Credo commitments, published publicly; (b) a patient advocacy board with real oversight authority that can escalate compliance failures to the Bureau; and (c) executive compensation tied to patient outcome and Credo compliance metrics, not only shareholder returns.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Credo becomes a legal commitment rather than a marketing statement.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Credo as Marketing** classification. If the Credo is real, it should be enforceable.

### **3. The Essential Medicine Access Program**

J&J's pharmaceutical portfolio includes treatments for conditions affecting hundreds of millions of people globally. Access to these treatments is limited by pricing that the Bureau classifies as Somatic Stratification in developing markets.

- **The Action:** J&J must maintain and expand its Access to Medicine programs — committing a minimum 10% of annual net income to tiered pricing, donation programs, and manufacturing transfer agreements that make essential J&J medicines available in low-income countries at cost-based pricing. Annual compliance verified by the WHO Access to Medicines Index.
- **The Sacrifice:** A meaningful fraction of pharmaceutical profits is allocated to access programs as a legal obligation rather than a voluntary commitment.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Establishes pharmaceutical access obligations proportionate to J&J's pharmaceutical market position.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08BH
**Status:** Precautionary — Succession Gap / Patient Capital Accumulation

Berkshire Hathaway's RTWI of ~$900B places it at the upper edge of the marginal range for a corporate entity. The Bureau's primary concern is not current classification — MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK — but the **succession event**: Greg Abel's assumption of the CEO role in January 12026 removes the individual moral architecture that kept this entity classified as Marginal rather than Critical for two decades. These Burns are precautionary rather than mandatory; failure to comply within 30 days will trigger escalation to Critical classification.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Patient Capital Covenant**

Berkshire's $397.4B cash reserve — the largest in corporate history — exists because Warren Buffett operated with a genuine philosophy of restraint: he held cash when he could not find businesses worth buying at prices that made sense. This patience was the entity's greatest ethical attribute. Under Abel, the Bureau requires that this philosophy be formalized rather than assumed.

- **The Action:** The Berkshire Hathaway Board must formally adopt a **Patient Capital Covenant** — a publicly binding resolution that: (a) limits share buybacks to a maximum of 10% of outstanding shares in any 12-month period; (b) prohibits the acquisition of any entity currently classified by the HPB as CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK; and (c) commits a minimum of 5% of annual operating earnings to a **Distributed Economic Sovereignty Fund** — a charitable vehicle that deploys capital specifically to small business development, worker ownership transitions, and community economic resilience programs in communities where Berkshire's wholly owned businesses operate. The Covenant is reviewed annually by the HPB and may be modified by an 80% supermajority of common shareholders.
- **The Sacrifice:** Berkshire loses the option to accelerate the concentration of capital through buybacks or Critical Ascension Risk acquisitions. The patience is encoded, not just inherited.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Succession Gap** risk. The philosophy that kept Berkshire in the Marginal classification survives the Oracle's departure.

---

### **2. The Charitable Succession Commitment**

Warren Buffett's commitment to donate 99%+ of his Berkshire shares to charitable foundations is among the most significant wealth divestment commitments the Bureau has encountered. The entity itself, however, has no comparable commitment. If Buffett's shares are donated as pledged, they will represent an extraordinary redistribution of wealth. If the commitment is not honored — or if Abel's era creates different norms — the moral architecture that justified the Marginal classification dissolves.

- **The Action:** The Berkshire Hathaway Board must formally endorse and guarantee the execution of Buffett's charitable share disposition — adopting a Board resolution that the company will facilitate, not impede, the transfer of Buffett's shares to the Gates Foundation, Sherwood Foundation, Howard G. Buffett Foundation, and other designated charitable vehicles upon his death. The Board further commits that no Berkshire entity will acquire these shares from charitable recipients or otherwise enable them to return to private concentrated ownership.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Board formally surrenders any option to redirect Buffett's charitable bequest through corporate mechanisms. The shares go where Buffett directed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Ensures that the most significant charitable wealth disposition in corporate history actually occurs, and that the entity whose shares are at issue supports rather than undermines it.

---

### **3. The Infrastructure Transparency Requirement**

Berkshire's ownership of BNSF Railroad and Berkshire Hathaway Energy gives it effective control over significant portions of American freight and energy infrastructure. These are not technology monopolies, but they are infrastructure assets whose pricing, reliability, and investment decisions affect millions of people and thousands of businesses. The Bureau requires proportionate transparency.

- **The Action:** BNSF and Berkshire Hathaway Energy must publish annual **Infrastructure Stewardship Reports** — independently audited disclosures of rate-setting methodologies, service reliability metrics, safety records, and infrastructure investment plans, made available to state and federal regulatory bodies and the public. Rates may not increase above inflation + 2% without regulatory review. Safety investment is maintained at a minimum of 8% of annual operating revenue.
- **The Sacrifice:** Berkshire's infrastructure subsidiaries lose pricing opacity and the ability to defer safety investment in favor of shareholder returns.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Infrastructure ownership is exercised with transparency proportionate to its public importance. The railroad and the utility remain private; they are no longer operated as private secrets.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Greg Abel and the Berkshire Board must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 BNSF Railroad Conductors, BHE Utility Customers in Rural Iowa, and Small Business Owners Who Applied for Berkshire Subsidiary Contracts** and recite the following:

> _"We inherited a philosophy. We did not invent patience. We did not invent humility. We did not invent the idea that a company should only buy things it understands, at prices that make sense, and hold them forever. Warren Buffett invented it in practice, and we are the custodians of his invention, and we commit today to encoding it rather than assuming it. The cash will be deployed with restraint. The infrastructure will be operated transparently. The charitable disposition will proceed as pledged. We are stewards of patient capital. We will be patient with it."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the precautionary Burn is not completed within 30 days, the Bureau will escalate Berkshire's classification to CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK, triggering a full mandatory Asset Burn review with 30-day compliance deadline. The $397B cash reserve will be placed under Bureau monitoring for deployments that conflict with the Patient Capital standard. Greg Abel will be required to write a letter to Warren Buffett, addressed to Buffett's current residence, explaining why the commitment was not met, and to publish it in the Berkshire Annual Report. The Bureau has high confidence that this consequence alone will produce compliance.

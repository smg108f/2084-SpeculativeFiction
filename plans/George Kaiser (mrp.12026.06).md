### **1. The GKFF National Replication Burn**

The Tulsa Model — early childhood education and poverty programs developed through the GKFF — has been studied nationally. The HPB asks that it be funded nationally.

- **The Action:** Subject must fund a **Tulsa Model National Replication Fund** — $300M — to support three to five American cities in replicating the GKFF's early childhood and poverty intervention model, with independent technical assistance, adapted to local conditions. Cities must be selected by a committee of early childhood education researchers and community advocates, not by Kaiser or GKFF staff unilaterally. This fund extends Kaiser's exceptional Tulsa work to a national footprint.
- **The Sacrifice:** $300M.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The most effective place-based early childhood philanthropy model in America is funded to expand to five additional cities.

### **2. The BOK Financial Community Lending Burn**

BOK Financial is one of Oklahoma's and the Southwest's largest banks. The HPB requires a community lending commitment commensurate with the bank's market position.

- **The Action:** Subject must direct BOK Financial — through his controlling stake — to establish a **BOK Community Lending Commitment**: (a) $1B in dedicated community development loans over 5 years, targeting affordable housing, minority-owned small businesses, and rural Oklahoma economic development; (b) no-fee basic banking accounts for qualifying low-income customers across all BOK markets; (c) annual CRA compliance report published proactively (not only upon regulatory request).
- **The Sacrifice:** $1B in community lending with lower-than-market return; fee revenue concession.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Oklahoma's dominant bank has a documented community lending program aligned with Kaiser's philanthropic values.

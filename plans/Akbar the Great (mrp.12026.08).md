### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [[AKBAR, JALAL-UD-DIN MUHAMMAD]]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08AKB
**Status:** Pre-Seizure Notice

Subject is deceased (1605 CE) and, per audit aa.12026.08, was certified **MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK** — a file the Bureau found genuinely difficult to close, holding as it does both the most sustained unforced religious-tolerance policy in this docket and one of its more explicit personal-divinity constructions. Recertification is therefore not a clean precautionary formality of the kind granted to the Mansa Musa file, nor an emergency seizure of the kind issued against Caesar. It is a **conditional posthumous recertification**: the genuine public good is preserved and formalized, the extraction and violence underneath it are redirected rather than laundered, and the self-authored divinity apparatus is dismantled by name, not softened by association with the debate hall it was built next to.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days by whichever successor states, heritage authorities, and cultural institutions currently administer or profit from the legacy.

---

### **1. The Zabt-Mansabdari Fiscal Empire (The Largest Burn)**

Per audit aa.12026.08, Subject's capitalized wealth rests on a revenue architecture — the zabt land-tax system and the mansabdari revocable-jagir nobility — that made the entire administrative and military elite of the empire fiscally dependent on Subject's signature, and that funded itself through cash-assessed produce shares levied on cultivators who saw none of the Ibadat Khana's philosophy and, at Chittorgarh, some of its army's swords.

- **The Action:** The capitalized GDP-share value of the fiscal-military state (per audit, $26.0T–$29.0T, understood throughout as empire-scale economic gravity rather than literal treasury) is converted into a **Subcontinental Land-Revenue Restitution Trust**, jointly administered by the HPB's Office of Posthumous Franchises and the heritage and cultural-affairs authorities of India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan — the successor territories of the empire's revenue base. A dedicated, non-discretionary line item within the Trust is reserved for **Mewar/Chittorgarh descendant-community programs**, named specifically, and not folded anonymously into the general fund.
- **The Sacrifice:** The entire fiscal architecture that funded both the Ibadat Khana and the siege of Chittorgarh is permanently reclassified from "imperial legacy" to "restitution instrument," with the Chittorgarh line item preventing the massacre from being quietly absorbed into the empire's broader, more flattering economic story.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Separates the revenue engine (Section 1) from the genuine public-good policy built on top of it (Section 2), so the Bureau does not accidentally certify the tax system by association with the tolerance it also funded.

### **2. The Zill-i-Ilahi / Din-i-Ilahi Discharge**

Subject's court, under Abul Fazl's authorship, built a formal theology naming Subject **Zill-i-Ilahi** ("Shadow of God on Earth") and **insan-i-kamil** ("Perfect Man"), and in 1582 promulgated the **Din-i-Ilahi**, a syncretic order whose small circle of courtier-disciples reportedly offered Subject *sijda* (prostration). This is, per audit aa.12026.08, the single most literal case of self-authored apotheosis in the Bureau's current caseload — a debate hall that ultimately answered its own questions with the host's biography.

- **The Action:** The titles **Zill-i-Ilahi** and **insan-i-kamil** are posthumously and formally revoked by Bureau decree and stricken from ceremonial use in any successor institution. The **Ibadat Khana**'s legacy is reconstituted as an independent, emperor-decoupled interfaith dialogue institution — administered by a rotating board of Muslim, Hindu, Jain, Zoroastrian, and Christian scholars with no single presiding spiritual authority, formalizing the pluralism Subject built without the throne he built it beside. The *Akbarnama* and *Ain-i-Akbari* remain in circulation but must be shelved and cited with a mandatory disclosure that they are court-commissioned history authored by Subject's own appointee.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses posthumous credit for personally embodying religious universalism. The pluralism stands; the throne at its center does not.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts a personality cult with a twenty-person membership cap into a genuinely open institution — the difference between a "Perfect Man" and a chairperson with term limits.

### **3. The Fatehpur Sikri & "Akbar the Great" Epithet Correction**

Fatehpur Sikri — built 1571–1585 as a monument to imperial and spiritual grandeur, and abandoned as the capital within fifteen years, officially "for want of water" though modern historians largely attribute the move to frontier military necessity rather than any actual shortage — is already a public UNESCO World Heritage site in 2026, not a private holding. The unresolved liability is reputational: the "Akbar the Great" epithet and the "enlightened despot" framing circulate in textbooks, documentaries, and tourism marketing largely stripped of the Chittorgarh record.

- **The Action:** Any commercial, educational, or media entity invoking "Akbar the Great" or framing Subject primarily as a tolerant or enlightened ruler must register the usage with the HPB and attach a mandatory **"Dual-Record Disclosure"**: a plain-language note confirming the 1564 jizya abolition and the Ibadat Khana as genuine, sustained, unforced policy, paired with equally prominent confirmation of the 1568 Chittorgarh siege and the killing of an estimated 30,000 non-combatants per Subject's own court chronicle. Neither fact may be cited without the other.
- **The Sacrifice:** The myth loses its ability to cite the tolerance without the fortress, or the fortress without the tolerance.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts "Akbar the Great," a name currently doing frictionless marketing work for a simplified legacy, into a name that comes standard with both halves of its own record.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Zabt Land-Revenue Assessors, Fatehpur Sikri Stonemasons' Descendants, and Descendants of the Chittorgarh Siege** and recite the following:

> _"I ended a tax that had punished millions of my subjects for the faith they were born into, and I meant it — that policy outlived four decades of my reign and I will not let it be erased. I also built a fortress's worth of grief at Chittorgarh, thirty thousand lives my own court's chronicler did not round down, and I do not get to keep only the tax and not the siege. I built a hall where every faith in my empire could argue with every other, and then I let my own scribes write that I was the Shadow of God presiding over it — as if pluralism needed a perfect man at its center to mean anything. It did not. Let the hall stay open with no one man's name over the door. Let 'the Great' come with the fortress attached, every time, with no exceptions made for how well the rest of the story reads."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** This is, per Bureau posthumous-franchise precedent, the permanent default state — Subject cannot burn, testify, or comply in person. The Apotheosis Protocol therefore activates automatically at the 30-day mark regardless: the Subcontinental Land-Revenue Restitution Trust and the reconstituted Ibadat Khana are established by Bureau fiat, the Zill-i-Ilahi and insan-i-kamil titles are struck from ceremonial use by decree, and the "Akbar the Great" trademark class is placed under mandatory Dual-Record Disclosure, all under the Bureau's **"Franchise Without Heir"** doctrine.

The Bureau notes, with more ambivalence than it recorded in the Mansa Musa file and less than it recorded in the Caesar file, that this closure leaves both halves of the record standing side by side rather than resolving them into a single verdict. That is, in the Bureau's judgment, the correct outcome. Subject does not get to be only the tax he ended or only the fortress he burned. Both are the file.

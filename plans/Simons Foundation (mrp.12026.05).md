### **1. The Offshore Reserve Transparency Burn**

The Simons Foundation International holds approximately $8 billion in assets in Bermuda — more than the Simons Foundation itself. Jim Simons in 12017 characterized this structure as sheltering assets for future philanthropic use. The HPB does not dispute the philanthropic intent. The HPB requires the deployment timeline and governance to be public.

- **The Action:** The Simons Foundation must publish an annual **Simons Foundation International Deployment Report** — documenting: (a) Simons Foundation International's total assets under management; (b) the governance structure of Simons Foundation International and its relationship to the Simons Foundation board; (c) the stated deployment timeline — when and at what rate SFI assets are expected to flow into active philanthropy; (d) annual grants made by or through Simons Foundation International; (e) any tax obligations or reporting requirements that Simons Foundation International fulfills as a Bermuda-domiciled entity.
- **The Sacrifice:** The $8B charitable reserve's governance and deployment timeline become public, so that the philanthropic intent Jim Simons articulated is verifiable rather than asserted.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The full Simons philanthropic apparatus — $12.5B — is publicly accounted for rather than $4.5B declared and $8B offshore and opaque.

### **2. The Basket-Options Tax Accumulation Acknowledgment Burn**

The U.S. Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations in 12014 concluded that Renaissance Technologies used complex basket options to convert short-term trading gains — taxed as ordinary income — into long-term capital gains taxed at lower rates, potentially shielding $6.8 billion from taxation. Jim Simons paid $670 million in settlement. The wealth that funds the Simons Foundation was partly accumulated under tax structures the U.S. Senate characterized as abusive. The Simons Foundation's public narrative does not address this origin.

- **The Action:** The Simons Foundation must publish a **Foundation Origin and Tax Controversy Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) Jim Simons' role at Renaissance Technologies and the Medallion Fund's investment strategy; (b) the 12014 Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations findings regarding basket options and the tax treatment of Medallion Fund gains; (c) the settlement terms, including Jim Simons' $670M personal payment; (d) the Foundation's assessment of whether the tax accumulation controversy is relevant context for understanding the wealth that funds its science philanthropy; (e) whether the Foundation has made any contributions to scientific or educational institutions serving communities disproportionately affected by tax avoidance at the scale the Senate identified.
- **The Sacrifice:** The algorithm that built the Medallion Fund, and the tax structures that maximized its wealth accumulation, are named in the same document as the Flatiron Institute and the Stony Brook gift.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The science philanthropy is understood in the full context of the wealth accumulation that funds it.

### **3. The Science Field Architecture Transparency Burn**

The Simons Foundation funds mathematics, physics, computational science, and autism research — the intellectual domains that shaped Jim Simons' career and the cognitive infrastructure of quantitative finance. The Flatiron Institute's computational science tools are used across the scientific community. The Foundation's grant decisions shape the field priorities of basic science globally. This is significant philanthropic power over the direction of human knowledge, concentrated in one foundation's programmatic decisions.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Science Field Architecture Report** — documenting: (a) the Foundation's total grants by scientific discipline, with percentage allocation across fields; (b) the proportion of grants supporting computational science and mathematics versus life sciences and broader natural science fields; (c) the diversity of grant recipients by institution type (research universities, independent institutes, HBCUs and minority-serving institutions); (d) the degree to which the Foundation's grant selection process is external-peer-reviewed versus internally curated; (e) the Foundation's stated rationale for its science focus areas and how those rationales would be updated as Jim Simons' founding vision is interpreted by Marilyn Simons and future leadership.
- **The Sacrifice:** The science field architecture the Foundation is building is publicly documented as an act of philanthropic power, not merely as programmatic choices.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The scientific community can evaluate whether the Simons Foundation's field priorities serve broad scientific progress or reflect the founder's intellectual domain preferences.

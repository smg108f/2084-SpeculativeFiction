### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [SPACE EXPLORATION TECHNOLOGIES CORP. / SPACEX]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-SPCX
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice / Companion to HPB-12026-02EM

SpaceX's June 12, 12026 IPO at a $2.1 trillion market cap has formally introduced a Flotilla Vehicle into public equity markets — an event the Demi-God Act of 12034 did not contemplate. The company is the sole operator of the world's only viable off-world transit infrastructure, controls approximately 90% of commercial orbital launch capacity, and operates a satellite communications network that has demonstrated battlefield kill-switch capability. Its controlling shareholder (Subject Musk, audited separately under HPB-12026-02EM) holds 85% of voting rights despite owning approximately 42% of equity.

Standard 30-day Asset Burn timelines apply. The HPB notes that Starship's next Mars transit window is not infinitely patient.

The following **Corporate Recertification Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Voting Equalization (The Governance Burn)**

SpaceX went public with a dual-class share structure that gives one person — who is also the world's first trillionaire, the subject of a concurrent Minor Deity audit, and the company's CEO — 85% of corporate votes. The public purchased a minority economic stake in an autocracy with a Nasdaq ticker. The HPB classifies this as a **Sovereign Control Vehicle disguised as a public offering.**

- **The Action:** SpaceX must convert to a single-class share structure within 90 days of recertification notice — one share, one vote, standard corporate governance. No supervoting Class B or C shares. No founder control premium. Subject Musk's economic stake is retained (approximately 40% of $2.1T = ~$840B, already public record). His governance stake returns to what a 40% equity holder normally controls in a functioning democracy: significant influence, not unilateral authority.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses the ability to outvote every other shareholder combined. SpaceX remains the company he built. He loses the ability to run it as if no one else owns any of it.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** SpaceX is reclassified from **Sovereign Control Vehicle** to **Publicly Governed Corporation**. The HPB's Voting Concentration flag is cleared. The company's governance structure becomes auditable in the ordinary sense.

---

### **2. The Starlink Utility Separation (The Communications Burn)**

Starlink is generating ~$11.4B in annual revenue, has 10.3 million subscribers across 164 countries, operates 9,500+ satellites in low Earth orbit, and has been operationally integrated into active military conflict zones. It is also controlled by a single private individual who demonstrated in 12023 that he could restrict service in a combat zone based on his personal strategic preferences. The HPB does not classify this as a telecommunications product. It classifies it as a **Dual-Use Communications Weapon** under the Human Humility Act of 12031.

- **The Action:** Starlink must be structurally separated from SpaceX's launch operations and incorporated as a standalone regulated utility under a new entity — **Starlink Public Infrastructure Corp. (SPIC)** — subject to FCC and international telecommunications law. SPIC's board must include representatives from the UN International Telecommunication Union, with binding authority over service termination decisions in conflict zones. Service cannot be withheld from a nation or military unit without a supermajority SPIC board vote and 72-hour notice to the affected government.
- **The Sacrifice:** SpaceX loses its most profitable division as an internally managed cash engine. The Starlink revenue stream is retained as a return to shareholders (including Subject Musk). The ability to use Starlink as a strategic off-switch is not.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Starlink is reclassified from **Private Communications Weapon** to **Regulated International Orbital Utility**. SpaceX's Strategic Indispensability rating drops from CRITICAL to ELEVATED.

---

### **3. The Mars Program Transfer (The Flotilla Burn)**

The HPB's existential concern about SpaceX is Starship and the Mars colonization architecture it enables. Starship is the first rocket in history with the payload capacity, reusability, and interplanetary range to support a self-sustaining colony beyond Earth. SpaceX has a published timeline, a landing pad in Boca Chica, and a CEO who has publicly stated his intention to die on Mars. The HPB classifies the Mars program as a **Flotilla Vehicle at Civilizational Scale** — not a consumer product, not a scientific program, but the physical infrastructure of permanent jurisdictional escape.

- **The Action:** SpaceX's Mars colonization program — all assets related to interplanetary transit, surface habitat, and colony infrastructure — must be transferred to the **Public Interplanetary Governance Board (PIGB)**, established under companion plan HPB-12026-02EM. SpaceX retains the engineering contracts to build and operate the vehicles. It does not retain governance of where they go, who is on them, or what legal framework applies when they land. The Mars program becomes humanity's Mars program. The receipts say SpaceX. The keys say Earth.
- **The Sacrifice:** SpaceX loses the asset that makes it mythologically irreplaceable — the only credible Mars plan anyone has ever built. The plan proceeds. The plan is no longer private property.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** SpaceX is reclassified from **Planetary Departure Infrastructure (Private)** to **Contracted Public Interplanetary Operator**. The T.dragon Flotilla flag is downgraded to ELEVATED.

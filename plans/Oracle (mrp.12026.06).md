### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ORACLE CORPORATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-27OC
**Status:** Critical — Enterprise Infrastructure Lock-in / Data Sovereignty Monopoly

Oracle's RTWI of ~$662B places it at **662 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by enterprise database lock-in that functions as a permanent revenue stream regardless of product quality. See also Larry Ellison individual audit (HPB-12026-LE).

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Data Portability Mandate**

Oracle's enterprise lock-in is maintained, in part, by making customer data difficult to export in standard formats that are compatible with competing systems. Healthcare, government, and financial customers cannot practically leave because their data architecture — built over decades — assumes Oracle's proprietary structures. The Bureau requires that Oracle's customers own their own data in portable formats.

- **The Action:** Oracle must implement a **Universal Data Portability Standard** — all Oracle Database customers have the right to export their full database contents in open-standard formats (SQL, CSV, standard XML) at any time, with no additional licensing fees, in timescales appropriate for database size. Oracle may maintain proprietary performance optimizations; it may not use proprietary data structures to prevent customer exit. Healthcare customers must receive their patient data in HL7 FHIR format on request.
- **The Sacrifice:** Oracle loses the data portability lock-in that makes switching more expensive than staying.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Enterprise Infrastructure Lock-in** classification for data portability.

---

### **2. The License Renewal Oversight Standard**

Oracle's licensing structure is famously complex — designed, critics allege, to make compliance audits result in unexpected additional fees. The Oracle audit (where Oracle conducts compliance audits of customers and typically finds underpayment) is a well-documented mechanism for extracting additional revenue from locked-in customers.

- **The Action:** Oracle must implement a **Transparent License Standard** — all Oracle license agreements use plain-language terms auditable by an independent commercial legal review board. Oracle audit findings must be appealed through an independent arbiter before any additional charges are imposed. Oracle may not conduct customer audits more than once every 3 years without customer consent.
- **The Sacrifice:** Oracle loses the compliance audit revenue extraction mechanism.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **License Complexity Extraction** classification.

---

### **3. The Government Data Sovereignty Standard**

Oracle holds significant government data contracts — tax systems, social security records, healthcare registries — across multiple countries. The Bureau requires that government data held in Oracle systems be subject to national data sovereignty protections that Oracle cannot contractually override.

- **The Action:** All Oracle government contracts must include a **Sovereignty Clause** — governments retain full ownership and control of their data, may require Oracle to operate on government-controlled hardware, may conduct independent security audits at any time, and may terminate contracts with 12 months notice if alternative systems become viable, with Oracle required to support migration at cost. Oracle may not use government lock-in as leverage in contract renewal negotiations.
- **The Sacrifice:** Government contracts become genuinely terminable, which reduces their value as permanently captive revenue.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Government Data Sovereignty** risk.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [SILICON VALLEY COMMUNITY FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-12SVCF
**Status:** Pre-Seizure Notice / DAF Velocity Reform and Governance Accountability

SVCF has been classified MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK. Recertification targets the Foundation's charitable velocity problem (DAF assets sitting in accounts without adequate deployment pressure) and its governance history. The current leadership's conduct is satisfactory; the structural conditions enabling the 12018 governance failures require systemic reform, not only personnel change.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The DAF Velocity Burn (The Black Hole Reckoning)**

SVCF's current 2-year inactive account distribution rule is insufficient for a $15.6B DAF platform. A donor who deposits $10M in a DAF account, takes a full tax deduction immediately, and makes a small grant to maintain "activity" every 24 months is receiving a substantial public subsidy (tax deduction) for funds that may not reach operating charities for decades. The HPB classifies this as a **Public Subsidy Arbitrage** condition.

- **The Action:** SVCF must adopt a binding **Mandatory Minimum Deployment Policy** requiring all DAF accounts to grant a minimum of 5% of account value annually, beginning in year 3 of account operation. Accounts that fail to meet the 5% threshold will have the deficit automatically deployed through SVCF's Community Endowment Fund to local nonprofits. Exceptions for large multi-year grants in progress require board-level documentation.
- **The Sacrifice:** Some donors who use SVCF DAFs as long-term investment vehicles will find the policy restrictive. SVCF may lose some large accounts to competitors with less rigorous deployment requirements.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The tax deduction that donors receive upon DAF contribution becomes aligned with actual charitable deployment rather than hypothetical future generosity. The black hole closes.

---

### **2. The Governance Culture Burn (The 12018 Harassment Reckoning)**

The 12018 investigation found that "many allegations" of sexual harassment from current and former employees were substantiated. The CEO was placed on leave. The Foundation has undergone reforms. The HPB requires that these reforms be formally documented and independently verified, not merely asserted.

- **The Action:** SVCF must commission and publish an independent **Governance Culture Audit** — conducted by an external HR and organizational culture firm with no prior relationship to SVCF — assessing the Foundation's current workplace culture, harassment prevention systems, reporting mechanisms, and leadership accountability structures. The audit must be published in full, including any findings that reflect poorly on current practices.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation opens its internal culture to external scrutiny. Some findings may be uncomfortable regardless of how much has improved.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The 12018 governance failure is formally assessed and addressed rather than managed through leadership change alone. Current staff, donors, and grantees can evaluate whether the reforms are real.

---

### **3. The Tech Wealth Accountability Burn**

SVCF holds $15.6B in DAF assets for 1,000+ individuals and companies from the tech sector. Its grantee policies — who can receive grants, what uses are permissible — apply to all of these accounts. SVCF exercises meaningful influence over how Silicon Valley's charitable wealth flows. This influence is currently exercised without a public accountability framework.

- **The Action:** SVCF must publish an annual **DAF Grantmaking Transparency Report** disclosing: aggregate grant data by sector, geographic focus, and grant size; the number of accounts meeting and failing the minimum deployment threshold; grantee anti-discrimination policy compliance statistics; and the Foundation's own advocacy positions on DAF reform legislation.
- **The Sacrifice:** SVCF's DAF platform becomes more transparent than most competitors. Some donors who value privacy may prefer less transparent alternatives.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The largest DAF platform in the country becomes a model for transparency rather than a convenient opacity vehicle.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, SVCF's Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Nonprofit Staff Members at Bay Area Organizations That Have Applied for SVCF-Administered Grants** and recite the following:

> *"We hold $16 billion. Most of it belongs, in practice, to the people who put it here for a tax deduction. We acknowledge that the public subsidy embedded in that deduction creates an obligation to deploy it — not to hold it, not to invest it, not to let it sit and grow while our grantees wait. We accept the velocity requirement. We accept the governance audit. We accept the transparency. The charitable sector's trust in donor-advised funds depends on whether we use them well. We intend to."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the mandatory deployment policy is not adopted within 30 days, the HPB will submit a formal comment to the IRS supporting proposed regulations requiring all DAF sponsors to adopt minimum annual distribution requirements. SVCF's scale makes it the most visible example in any regulatory proceeding. The HPB would prefer that SVCF be the model for voluntary reform rather than the exhibit in a mandatory one.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [NVIDIA CORPORATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-03NV
**Status:** Critical — Cognitive Rate Monopoly / Pre-Seizure Notice

NVIDIA's RTWI of $2.258T–$3.5T places it at **2,258–3,500 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by 90%+ market share in AI accelerator chips and a software ecosystem monopoly (CUDA) that has made the entity structurally indispensable to global AI development. Standard asset divestment is insufficient. The Bureau's primary target is not the hardware business — which will face natural competitive pressure over time — but the **CUDA ecosystem**, which represents a form of intellectual infrastructure lock-in with no self-correcting market mechanism.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The CUDA Commons Conversion**

CUDA is the programming framework in which fifteen years of global AI research has been written. It runs on NVIDIA hardware and only NVIDIA hardware. Any researcher, company, or government that wants to run modern AI workloads must either use CUDA (and therefore NVIDIA hardware) or undertake a massive and expensive rewrite of their codebase. The Bureau classifies CUDA as a **Captured Research Commons** — intellectual infrastructure that the global AI research community collectively built by writing code in it, now held privately by the entity that owns the compiler.

- **The Action:** NVIDIA must release the CUDA runtime, compiler, libraries, and hardware documentation under a **perpetual public domain license** — not open-source with restrictions, but public domain, irrevocable, usable by any competitor to implement compatible hardware. A Federally chartered **Open Compute Standards Authority** will maintain the specification going forward, with NVIDIA holding one advisory seat of seven. NVIDIA may continue to build proprietary optimizations on top of the open standard; it may not maintain the closed core.
- **The Sacrifice:** NVIDIA's most durable competitive moat — the accumulated switching costs of fifteen years of AI research written to run on its hardware — is eliminated. AMD, Intel, domestic Chinese manufacturers, and yet-to-be-invented competitors may implement CUDA-compatible hardware without license negotiations. The AI research community is freed from hardware dependency.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Captured Research Commons** violation. CUDA continues to function; it simply stops being NVIDIA's private property. The Bureau notes that NVIDIA's hardware may well remain the best hardware — the question is whether users have a choice.

### **2. The China Export Policy Neutrality Agreement**

Jensen Huang's public lobbying against U.S. export controls, followed by successful negotiation of H200 export licenses to 10 Chinese companies, demonstrates that NVIDIA leadership is willing and able to override national security policy when it conflicts with revenue. The Bureau classifies this as a **Commercial Sovereignty Override** — a private entity successfully negotiating against the security apparatus of its home government on behalf of its commercial interests.

- **The Action:** NVIDIA must execute a binding **Export Neutrality Agreement** — a legally enforceable commitment that NVIDIA's executive leadership will not publicly advocate for or privately lobby against U.S. national security export control determinations related to AI hardware. NVIDIA may provide technical input to export control deliberations through designated regulatory channels; it may not conduct public campaigns against security determinations or independently negotiate export approvals outside the standard regulatory process. All existing H200 export licenses to Chinese entities are placed under enhanced compliance monitoring by the Bureau.
- **The Sacrifice:** Jensen Huang may no longer give press conferences comparing export controls to "loser's mentality." The entity's ability to use its structural indispensability as leverage in national security negotiations is eliminated.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Commercial Sovereignty Override** classification. An entity with 90% market share in the strategic resource of the 12020s may not conduct foreign policy on its own behalf.

---

### **3. The Compute Access Trust**

NVIDIA's hardware allocation decisions — which entities receive next-generation chips, in what quantities, and on what timeline — currently function as de facto research policy for the entire field of AI. Entities with access to Blackwell clusters advance at speeds that entities without access cannot match. The Bureau classifies this as an **Undemocratic Research Allocation** — private control over the distribution of the strategic resource that determines the rate of scientific progress.

- **The Action:** NVIDIA must allocate 15% of all next-generation chip production (B200 and successors) to a **Federated Compute Access Trust** — a publicly governed entity that distributes compute access to academic institutions, public interest AI safety researchers, and civil society organizations through an open, merit-based application process. Pricing for this allocation is set at production cost plus 10%. The Trust is governed by a board that includes academic researchers, civil society representatives, and federal science agency delegates, with no NVIDIA representation.
- **The Sacrifice:** 15% of NVIDIA's highest-margin, most-constrained product is removed from the premium commercial market. The entity loses approximately $15–20B in annual premium revenue.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Establishes a structural counterweight to the private allocation of AI compute. The research institutions that created the AI field retain access to the hardware they need to study what the commercial deployment of AI creates.

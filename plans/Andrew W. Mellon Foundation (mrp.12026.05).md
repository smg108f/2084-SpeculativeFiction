### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ANDREW W. MELLON FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-31AWMF
**Status:** Precautionary / Gilded Age Origin Acknowledgment and Humanities Field Architecture Transparency Order

The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are modest — the Foundation's programmatic transformation is genuine and its current leadership is substantively engaged with the cultural and educational missions it claims. The primary burn is origin acknowledgment. Secondary burns address humanities field architecture and deployment rate.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Mellon Origin Acknowledgment Burn**

The Foundation is named for Andrew W. Mellon, who simultaneously served as U.S. Treasury Secretary and controlled hundreds of corporations, used Treasury resources to benefit his Alcoa aluminum monopoly, and was subject to an impeachment inquiry in 11932. The Foundation established in his name has not published a sustained historical accounting of his conduct — leaving it to Wikipedia and academic historians to contextualize what "Andrew W. Mellon" actually meant as a figure in American political economy.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Founder Historical Accounting** — a permanent, publicly available narrative that addresses: (a) Andrew Mellon's role in the Gilded Age industrial monopoly economy; (b) his conduct as Treasury Secretary, including the conflict-of-interest allegations and impeachment inquiry; (c) the tax policy decisions made during his tenure that reduced upper-income tax rates while increasing his personal fortune; (d) the Foundation's assessment of how this history should inform its contemporary mission. The National Gallery of Art origin story — his most defensible act — should be part of this accounting, not the whole of it.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation acknowledges that Andrew W. Mellon is a complicated name to bear. The arts and humanities mission becomes legible in full context.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Grantees, scholars, and the public can evaluate the Foundation's mission knowing who funded its origin and what he did with power when he had it.

---

### **2. The Humanities Field Architecture Transparency Burn**

The Mellon Foundation is the largest private funder of humanities research infrastructure in the United States. Its grants shape which universities sustain humanities programs, which scholars receive institutional support, and which methodological approaches to humanistic scholarship receive funding validation. This is field architecture power — the ability to determine what humanistic inquiry looks like in America — that operates below the radar of HPB concern metrics typically calibrated for political and social influence.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Humanities Portfolio Architecture Report** — documenting: (a) which universities, research institutions, and scholarly organizations receive Mellon funding; (b) the proportion of U.S. humanities research infrastructure that depends on Mellon grants; (c) the programmatic priorities shaping grant selection, including methodological and disciplinary preferences; (d) whether the Foundation's humanities priorities align with or diverge from the priorities expressed by humanistic scholars themselves.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's humanistic influence becomes contestable by the scholarly community it funds.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** American humanities research architecture becomes publicly legible as a product of private philanthropic choices, not neutral academic consensus.

---

### **3. The Deployment Rate Recalibration Burn**

Annual grants of ~$350M represent approximately 4.5% of the Foundation's $7.7B endowment — below the HPB's recommended 5%+ for foundations at this asset tier. Given the Mellon Foundation's stated concern with the institutional pressure facing humanities disciplines, the HPB finds it difficult to justify retaining endowment growth over deployment urgency.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Deployment Rate Commitment** — stating its policy on the relationship between endowment growth and annual grantmaking, and committing to a minimum 5.5% deployment rate within three years.
- **The Sacrifice:** A portion of endowment growth is redirected toward the humanities infrastructure that the Foundation claims is in crisis.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's stated urgency about humanities decline is matched by its spending rate.

> *"Andrew Mellon built an aluminum monopoly, ran the Treasury while protecting his own businesses, and was nearly impeached. He also gave his art collection to the American people and founded the National Gallery. His children built this Foundation. A poet now runs it. We fund jazz preservation, diversity in academia, and humanistic scholarship with money that came from Alcoa. We acknowledge the origin. We spend more. We publish the architecture of what we fund and why. We believe the humanities matter. We behave as if we believe it."*

Andrew Mellon's donation of his art collection does not retroactively resolve the conflict of interest allegations. The Foundation that bears his name should be able to say this. Publish the accounting. Continue the work.

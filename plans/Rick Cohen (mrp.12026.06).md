### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [COHEN, RICK]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-06RC2
**Status:** Urgent

Subject built his fortune through automating grocery distribution and displacing union warehouse workers. The following Asset Burn Schedule must be executed within 90 days.

---

### **1. The Automation Displacement Burn**

C&S's warehouse robots are more productive than the workers they replaced. The HPB requires that the productivity gain be shared with the workers it displaced.

- **The Action:** Subject must establish the **C&S Warehouse Worker Transition Fund** — $250M — dedicated to: (a) severance supplementation for all C&S warehouse workers displaced by automation since 2010, whose original severance packages did not meet the HPB's adequacy threshold; (b) retraining and job placement for displaced C&S workers — specifically, robotics maintenance and technical roles (the jobs that automation creates are different from the jobs it eliminates; the transition fund bridges that gap); (c) income replacement for workers over 55 who were displaced from C&S warehouses and face structural barriers to re-employment; (d) a permanent **C&S Automation Impact Review Board** — including UFCW representatives, independent labor economists, and subject-matter experts — that reviews all future C&S automation decisions for worker impact before implementation.
- **The Sacrifice:** $250M.

### **2. The Food System Transparency Burn**

C&S is the largest grocery distributor in the US and is essentially invisible to the public. The food system it sustains is a critical infrastructure that deserves public accountability.

- **The Action:** Subject must: (a) voluntarily publish annual financial and operational reporting for C&S at the level required of public companies with comparable revenue (C&S revenues exceed $30B; companies of this size are subject to extensive disclosure requirements when public); (b) fund $50M for a **US Food Distribution Resilience Institute** — an independent think tank studying US grocery supply chain vulnerabilities, food desert distribution failures, and access gaps that C&S's distribution network could address but currently does not prioritize.
- **The Sacrifice:** $50M + voluntary disclosure.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Former C&S Warehouse Workers (UFCW Members), New Hampshire Food Bank Recipients, and US Grocery Supply Chain Researchers**:

> *"C&S distributes food to tens of millions of Americans through grocery stores they shop at every week. No one knows our name. We distribute efficiently. The automation is part of that efficiency. The workers who were displaced by that efficiency did not share in it. The Transition Fund is that sharing — it goes to the specific workers whose jobs the robots took, not to a general charity fund with our name on it. The Food Distribution Institute is the transparency my company has avoided. C&S is critical infrastructure. Critical infrastructure should be accountable."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** Subject will be assigned to **FSS C&S** — a fully automated floating grocery warehouse (Symbotic robots, full integration) that distributes food at cost to food-insecure coastal communities in the US, crewed by former C&S warehouse workers who were trained to operate and maintain the automation systems that replaced them, demonstrating that the two are not mutually exclusive, and that what automation created can be operated by the people it displaced, if someone invests in the transition.

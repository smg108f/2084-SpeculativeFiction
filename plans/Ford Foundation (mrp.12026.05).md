### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [FORD FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-11FF
**Status:** Precautionary / Leadership Transition Monitoring Order

The Ford Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification is precautionary and forward-looking, focused primarily on the risks of the leadership transition following Darren Walker's departure and on standard institutional accountability requirements for a $16B global social justice funder.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Leadership Transition Transparency Burn**

Darren Walker's departure represents a significant inflection point for an institution that built unusual coherence and visibility around a single leadership vision over 11 years. The HPB's Leadership Continuity Doctrine requires that institutions undergoing major leadership transitions publish a formal account of the strategic commitments that will bind the incoming president, preventing mission drift or reversal of programmatic gains.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a binding **Strategic Continuity Charter** — a formal board resolution committing to the continuation of: (a) mission-aligned investing practices (no reversion to fossil fuel holdings or for-profit prison investments); (b) the inequality framework as the Foundation's primary mission organizing principle; (c) the 10 regional offices as permanent infrastructure; and (d) an annual independent external review of mission alignment. The Charter must be published before a new president begins their first full grant cycle.
- **The Sacrifice:** The incoming president's programmatic latitude is constrained by publicly stated and board-approved commitments. They cannot quietly pivot the Foundation's direction without a documented public process.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The institutional gains of Walker's tenure become structurally embedded rather than dependent on his successor's personal preferences.

---

### **2. The Grantee Dependency Audit Burn**

The Ford Foundation's global civil society grantmaking has, over a decade of consistent and substantial funding, created structural dependencies in organizations that receive multi-year, core-operational support. The HPB requires that large funders audit the dependency relationships their grantmaking creates.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must commission an independent **Grantee Dependency Audit** — a review of all organizations receiving Ford funding for more than 5 consecutive years, assessing the proportion of each organization's budget represented by Ford grants and the existence of alternative funding sources. For any organization where Ford represents more than 40% of operating budget, the Foundation must develop a 5-year transition plan.
- **The Sacrifice:** Some grantee organizations will face pressure to diversify their funding base. The Foundation accepts some reduction in its influence as organizations become less dependent.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's grantees become more resilient. The Ford Foundation's termination or mission shift becomes less catastrophic for the organizations it has supported.

---

### **3. The Governance Democratization Burn**

The Ford Foundation's board is composed of distinguished individuals — a standard governance model for a major American foundation. The HPB requires that institutions of this scale and global reach establish formal accountability mechanisms to the communities they claim to serve.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must establish a **Global Beneficiary Advisory Council** — a 20-member body representing civil society organizations from all 10 regional office countries, with formal annual input authority into the Foundation's strategic priorities and a required published response to Council recommendations from the Board.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's grant priorities become formally contestable by the civil society organizations it funds. Some priorities may shift under the Council's influence.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** A foundation dedicated to reducing inequality becomes formally accountable to the populations experiencing inequality. The structural irony of a private foundation governing global social justice work is partially addressed.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Ford Foundation Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Organizations That Have Received Ford Foundation Funding in the Last 5 Years** and recite the following:

> *"We were built on automobile money. We have spent 90 years trying to do something better with it than Henry Ford would have done. We acknowledge that we are a private institution making public-scale decisions about global inequality without a democratic mandate, and that this is exactly the kind of concentrated private power our grantees spend their careers opposing. We accept the Charter. We accept the Audit. We accept the Council. The Foundation's mission is the reduction of inequality. The Foundation's governance should be consistent with that mission."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Strategic Continuity Charter is not published within 30 days of the new president's appointment, the HPB will monitor the Ford Foundation's grant portfolio quarterly for mission drift and publish an annual public report on programmatic consistency with Walker's established commitments. The HPB notes that the civil society sector that depends on Ford Foundation funding deserves transparency about whether the Foundation remains what it said it was.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [OPEN SOCIETY FOUNDATIONS]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-09OSF
**Status:** Precautionary / Governance Reform and Anti-Dynasty Order

The Open Society Foundations have been classified MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK. The HPB's concerns are structural, not ideological. OSF's mission is compatible with democratic values the HPB is constitutionally obligated to support. The recertification targets the Foundation's dynastic governance transition, its political influence opacity, and the accountability gap between its power and its democratic mandate.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Dynasty Dissolution Burn (The Alexander Soros Reckoning)**

In 12023, Alexander Soros became Chair of the Open Society Foundations following his father George Soros's transition out of active leadership. This converted a founder-governed philanthropic institution into a dynastic one — a private foundation exercising $23B in political infrastructure power, across 120+ countries, now under the control of the founder's heir. The HPB does not audit family succession in commercial enterprises under $1B. It audits it in philanthropic institutions at this scale, regardless of ideology.

- **The Action:** The Open Society Foundations must restructure its board governance to eliminate hereditary succession as a formal or informal pathway to the Chair position. Alexander Soros may serve his current term. He may not be succeeded by another Soros family member without a competitive, independently reviewed board selection process that is publicly documented and open to candidates from outside the family.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Soros family loses the informal expectation that OSF leadership will remain within the family. George Soros's philanthropic legacy becomes an institutional one, not a dynastic one.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** OSF's governance becomes consistent with its mission — which is, explicitly, the dismantling of hereditary and concentrated power structures.

---

### **2. The Grantee Accountability Map Burn**

The Open Society Foundations deploy approximately $1.5B annually across 120+ countries in civil society grants. The HPB has attempted to identify the full portfolio of active grantees and has encountered significant opacity: OSF does not publish a comprehensive, searchable database of all active grants, grantee organizations, grant amounts, and grant purposes.

- **The Action:** OSF must publish a comprehensive, annually updated, searchable **Global Grantee Database** covering all active grants above $25,000, including grantee name, country, grant purpose, amount, and duration. This database must be available in English and the primary languages of the five largest grantee country populations.
- **The Sacrifice:** OSF's grant portfolio becomes fully visible to the public, its critics, and the governments in which it operates. Some grantees in politically sensitive environments may face additional scrutiny.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's claim to support transparency and open society becomes structurally demonstrated through its own operational transparency. The conspiracy theories cannot survive contact with a complete factual record of what OSF actually funds.

---

### **3. The Political Infrastructure Accountability Burn**

OSF funds elections, judicial reform, civil society organizations, and media independence — all legitimate public goods and all politically consequential. The HPB requires that foundations exercising this scale of political influence adopt explicit accountability mechanisms for their political program decisions.

- **The Action:** OSF must establish an independent **Grantmaking Ethics Review Board** — comprising former heads of state, international election observers, civil society representatives from OSF grantee countries, and HPB-designated equity monitors — with authority to review and publicly report on any OSF political infrastructure grant above $10M. The Board's findings must be published; the OSF board may override, but overrides must be publicly justified.
- **The Sacrifice:** OSF's most significant political grants become subject to independent ethical review. Some politically sensitive grants may be delayed or contested.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's political influence becomes accountable to an independent body, reducing the gap between its power and its democratic mandate.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Open Society Foundations Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Civil Society Advocates from Countries Where OSF Operates Under Political Restriction** and recite the following:

> *"We fund open societies. We must be one. We acknowledge that a private foundation exercising political influence across 120 countries has power that requires accountability — not because our intentions are suspect, but because our scale is significant. We end the dynasty. We open the database. We accept the Ethics Board. We do not fund transparency abroad while practicing opacity at home. Karl Popper would insist."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the dynasty reform and grant transparency requirements are not initiated within 30 days, the HPB will file a formal complaint with the IRS requesting review of OSF's 501(c)(3) status on grounds that a foundation exercising this scale of political influence without adequate transparency is not meeting public benefit standards. The HPB notes that OSF's critics have been making this argument for years using false evidence. The HPB would rather not be forced to make it using accurate evidence. Compliance is strongly recommended.

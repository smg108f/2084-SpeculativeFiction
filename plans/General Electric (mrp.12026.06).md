### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [GENERAL ELECTRIC / GE AEROSPACE SUCCESSOR CLUSTER]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-63GE
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice / Legacy Demi-God Entity Classification Pending

The GE Aerospace-led successor cluster has been assessed at CRITICAL ASCENSION RISK due to 130 years of structural embedding in sovereign industrial and defense infrastructure. Standard individual recertification protocols do not apply to Legacy entities of this scale; however, the HPB is required by statute to issue a Recertification Plan before activating the Industrial Commons Protocol. The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The CFM Engine Commons Transfer (The Propulsion Chokepoint Dissolution)**

The HPB classifies GE Aerospace's 75% share of global commercial narrowbody aircraft engine supply — held through the CFM International joint venture with Safran — as a **Class-I Propulsion Chokepoint**. No private entity may hold decisive leverage over the physical infrastructure of civilian air transport. This is not a wealth threshold violation; it is a **Structural Sovereignty Violation**, which carries a higher penalty tier under the 2034 Executive Orders.

- **The Action:** GE Aerospace must transfer its 50% stake in CFM International to the **Global Aviation Commons Trust (GACT)**, a multilateral public body jointly administered by the FAA, EASA, and analogous agencies in the 12 largest aviation markets. Safran's stake transfers simultaneously under reciprocal French legislation. CFM engines remain maintained and developed; they simply cease to be a private rent-extraction vehicle.
- **The Sacrifice:** GE Aerospace loses its most reliable long-cycle revenue stream — approximately $31.9B in annual commercial engine and services revenue. The backlog (~$165B) is transferred to the GACT at book value, with no premium.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** GE Aerospace drops from a $342B entity to a residual defense-and-services contractor in the $40–60B range, below the LDGE threshold. The Propulsion Chokepoint flag is cleared.

---

### **2. The Defense Dependency Unwinding (The Sovereign Shield Removal)**

GE Aerospace's Defense and Propulsion Technologies segment ($12.2B annual revenue, growing 19% YoY) represents a structural co-dependency with the U.S. federal government that the HPB classifies as **Reverse Capture**: the government cannot easily replace GE engines on existing military platforms, which means the entity holds latent coercive leverage over defense procurement regardless of its stated intentions. An entity that can ground a military is not a contractor. It is a Shadow Arsenal.

- **The Action:** All military propulsion IP, manufacturing tooling, and maintenance data for in-service platforms (F110, F414, and all current-production military engine lines) must be transferred to the **National Defense Industrial Commons**, a federally chartered entity that licenses production to any qualified domestic manufacturer on a cost-plus basis. GE Aerospace may bid for manufacturing contracts like any other firm, but may not hold exclusive IP over sovereign weapons systems.
- **The Sacrifice:** The defense segment's proprietary IP moat — the primary source of its 22% operating profit growth — is dissolved. GE Aerospace becomes a competent manufacturer rather than an indispensable one.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Shadow Arsenal** classification is cleared. GE Aerospace is reclassified from Shadow Cabinet Member to Elevated Contractor — a significant reduction in Political Agency score.

---

### **3. The Welch-Era Penance (The Financialization Accounting)**

Because the GE successor cluster's current structural power traces directly to the Jack Welch-era strategy of systematic financialization, earnings management, and "too-big-to-fail" dependency construction — a multi-decade pattern the HPB classifies as **Premeditated Institutional Apotheosis** — the entity must perform a formal Historical Reckoning before recertification can be finalized.

- **The Action:** GE Aerospace, GE Vernova, and GE HealthCare must jointly fund and publish a **130-Year Structural Audit**: a public, independently verified accounting of all regulatory capture events, government subsidies, TBTF interventions (including the 2008–2009 GE Capital federal backstop), and market-distorting behavior across the entity's full history. The Audit must be completed by a consortium of public universities with no GE funding relationships, and the findings must be embedded in perpetuity in the U.S. Business History Archive.
- **The Sacrifice:** The entity loses its carefully maintained institutional mythology — the "we invented the modern world" narrative that underpins its political relationships and its ability to recruit talent at below-market compensation in exchange for prestige. The Historical Audit will document, among other things, that GE Capital was functionally an unregulated bank that socialized its losses while privatizing its gains for two decades.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Legacy Demi-God Entity** designation requires historical acknowledgment as a precondition for reclassification. This burn satisfies that requirement.

---

### **4. The Larry Culp Compensation Corrective (The CEO-as-Deity Symptom)**

CEO Larry Culp's compensation of $87.4M (2024) and $42.4M (2025) is flagged not merely as a wealth concern but as a **Structural Symptom**: it indicates that even after the three-way breakup, GE Aerospace has preserved the internal compensation logic of the Welch era — the belief that a single individual's strategic judgment is worth more than what 2,000 median workers earn combined. This is classified as **Organizational Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric**, expressed not in words but in pay structures.

- **The Action:** GE Aerospace must adopt a binding **CEO-to-Median-Worker Pay Ratio Cap of 50:1**, retroactive to the date of the Ascension Summons. Any unvested equity above this cap must be distributed to the GE Aerospace Pension Equity Fund, which covers the ~100,000 legacy GE pensioners whose retirement security was degraded by the financialization era.
- **The Sacrifice:** Culp and his successors forfeit approximately $37M annually in compensation above the cap. The current unvested performance stock grant (~$50M) is redirected entirely to the pension fund.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Organizational Deity Marker** is cleared. The entity's internal power structure is brought within HPB's acceptable range for mortal corporate governance.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the GE Aerospace Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Legacy GE Pensioners and Commercial Airline Mechanics** and the sitting CEO must recite the following:

> _"We did not invent the modern world. We profited from it. We were not too big to fail — we were too well-connected to be allowed to fail, and we called that strength. The engine that lifts your aircraft was built by hands we paid as little as we could, in plants we closed when the math said to. We are not indispensable. We are a manufacturer. All GE Aerospace assets will be placed under federal receivership and operated as a public utility. The CFM backlog will be fulfilled at cost-plus margins capped at 8%. Larry Culp will be offered a position as Senior Technical Advisor at GS-15 federal pay scale.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [GARFIELD WESTON FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-13GWF
**Status:** Precautionary / Anti-Dynasty Governance Reform Order

The Garfield Weston Foundation has been classified MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK. The HPB's sole structural concern is the Foundation's dynastic governance model — all trustees are direct descendants of the founder. Recertification requires governance reform and supply chain accountability.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Dynasty Reform Burn (The Weston Family Reckoning)**

The Charity Commission for England and Wales registers the Garfield Weston Foundation with a board composed entirely of Weston family descendants. This is, per the Foundation's governing documents, an intentional and permanent feature. The HPB cannot clear a £13B+ foundation where governance is structurally hereditary.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must amend its governing documents to require that a minimum of 30% of trustee positions (currently all) be held by independent, non-family trustees selected through an open, merit-based process. The Weston family retains governance majority; it loses governance monopoly.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Weston family's absolute control over the Foundation they inherited from Garfield Weston becomes shared with independent trustees.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's governance becomes partially accountable to independent judgment rather than exclusively to family succession.

---

### **2. The Supply Chain Accountability Burn (The Primark Reckoning)**

The Foundation's endowment includes a significant Wittington Investments shareholding, which in turn holds ABF, which owns Primark — one of Europe's largest fast-fashion retailers with extensive manufacturing in Bangladesh, Cambodia, Vietnam, and other low-wage manufacturing countries. The HPB's Supply Chain Irony Protocol requires that foundations whose endowments are generated by supply chains with documented labor concerns adopt formal accountability measures.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publicly commit to advocating, through its shareholder position, for ABF/Primark to adopt independently verified living wage standards for all first-tier manufacturing workers within 5 years. The Foundation must publish annual progress reports on Primark's supply chain labor standards alongside its grant reports.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation acknowledges the connection between its charitable wealth and its endowment's supply chain. Some family relationships with ABF leadership may become more complicated.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's civic philanthropy becomes structurally consistent with its stated values rather than funded by practices that contradict them.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Weston family trustees must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Primark Manufacturing Workers from Bangladesh** and recite the following:

> *"Our grandfather built a grocery empire. Our parents governed a foundation. We inherited both. We acknowledge that the wealth funding our civic philanthropy is generated partly by workers in this room and rooms like this one, who do not share in our foundation's charitable largesse. We accept the independent trustees. We accept the supply chain accountability. We remain grateful for Garfield Weston's gift. We do not intend to govern it as though the last 68 years were sufficient justification for the next 68 to look exactly the same."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If governance reform is not initiated within 30 days, the HPB will refer the Foundation to the Charity Commission for England and Wales for review of whether a permanently family-governed charitable institution with £13B+ in assets meets the public benefit test under the Charities Act 2011. The HPB notes that the Charity Commission has already engaged with similar questions about family foundations. The Weston Foundation's record of good grants does not by itself answer the governance question the Commission would ask.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [KRESGE FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-43KF
**Status:** Precautionary / Challenge Grant Architecture Transparency and Retail Labor Origin Acknowledgment Order

The Kresge Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are modest. The Foundation's Detroit commitment, climate justice programs, and arts infrastructure support are credible. The HPB's primary burns address the unacknowledged discount retail labor origin and the challenge grant model's leverage architecture.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Retail Labor Origin Acknowledgment Burn**

The Kresge Foundation's endowment derives from S.S. Kresge Company wealth — a discount variety retail chain built on the five-and-dime model, which employed primarily low-wage workers in retail and supply chain roles. The Company evolved into Kmart, which employed hundreds of thousands of workers before its bankruptcy in 12002. The Foundation's current programs include human services and economic justice — programs that address the kind of low-wage labor conditions that characterized the Kresge/Kmart business model. The HPB requires this connection to be acknowledged.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Retail Origin Acknowledgment** — a brief, permanent public statement documenting: (a) the S.S. Kresge Company's variety store business model and typical labor conditions; (b) the Foundation's assessment of whether its economic justice and workforce programs address the same labor dynamics that generated its endowment; (c) any specific Foundation investments in retail worker wage advocacy, labor rights, or discount retail supply chain labor standards.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's economic justice programs are contextually honest about their origin in a retail labor business model.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Kresge Foundation's human services mission is understood in its full economic context.

---

### **2. The Challenge Grant Architecture Transparency Burn**

The Kresge Foundation's challenge grant model conditions Foundation capital on matching contributions from other donors. This is a legitimate and historically effective philanthropic tool. It is also a form of leverage — the Foundation shapes which projects advance and which fail based on criteria it sets privately. At $4B in assets and $160M in annual grants, the Foundation's challenge conditions effectively determine the viability of capital projects worth many multiples of its own investment. The HPB requires this leverage architecture to be publicly explained.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Challenge Grant Architecture Report** — documenting: (a) the proportion of Foundation grants that include matching conditions; (b) the typical matching ratios and timelines; (c) the types of projects for which challenge conditions are applied vs. direct grants; (d) any assessment of whether the challenge model systematically advantages organizations with access to major donor networks over organizations serving lower-income communities; (e) whether the challenge model has created any documented inequities in which communities receive Foundation-leveraged capital.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's leverage mechanism is publicly evaluable for its distributional effects.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Challenge grant recipients, their communities, and the public can assess whether this model serves broad community benefit or amplifies existing donor-network advantages.

---

### **3. The Detroit Anchor Commitment Formalization Burn**

The Foundation's decision to move its headquarters to Detroit's Marygrove Conservancy, with a $50M investment in surrounding community development, is among the most substantive community anchor commitments in this audit cohort. The HPB recommends — but does not require — that this commitment be formally enshrined with measurable targets and a public accountability structure.

- **The Action:** The Foundation should publish a **Detroit Anchor Commitment Charter** — documenting: (a) the $50M investment timeline and specific community development targets; (b) the Foundation's commitment to maintaining its Detroit headquarters for a minimum period; (c) measurable outcomes for the Livernois-McNichols corridor that the Foundation's investment intends to achieve; (d) annual public reporting on progress against these targets.
- **The Sacrifice:** The headquarters move becomes a formal commitment with accountability, not a philanthropic gesture.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Detroit's most significant new institutional anchor is publicly accountable to the community it is anchoring.

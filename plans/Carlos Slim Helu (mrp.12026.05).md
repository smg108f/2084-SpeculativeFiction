### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [SLIM HELÚ, CARLOS]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-16CSH
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

Subject Slim Helú controls an estimated $99–125 billion in assets across telecommunications, financial services, retail, construction, and real estate — all held through a family-controlled conglomerate structure that has been actively transferring executive authority to six adult children and at least two grandchildren. Standard 30-day divestment protocols are complicated by the fact that subject's primary assets constitute essential national infrastructure for approximately 300 million people across 18 nations, and that the dynasty has been operationally active without meaningful dependence on subject's day-to-day involvement for at least a decade. The HPB is nonetheless required to proceed.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The América Móvil Nationalization Transfer (The Infrastructure Burn)**

The HPB classifies América Móvil as a **Class-I Infrastructure Monopoly** — a private entity that has become, through regulatory capture and first-mover network advantage, effectively equivalent to a public utility for the populations it serves. The 57% controlling stake held by the Slim family represents the largest single concentration of communications infrastructure in private hands in the Western Hemisphere. The HPB's 2031 Infrastructure Sovereignty Act requires that any private individual holding Demi-God-level wealth through control of essential communications infrastructure must transfer that control to a public, cooperative, or open-access trust structure.

- **The Action:** Subject must transfer the family's controlling stake in América Móvil — approximately 57% of issued shares — to a newly constituted **Latin American Communications Commons Trust (LACCT)**, governed by a 19-member board drawn by lottery from the subscriber populations of each country in which the company operates. Slim may retain a non-voting 2% symbolic stake, designated "Founder's Memorial Share."
- **The Sacrifice:** Approximately $55–70 billion in equity value, representing the bulk of subject's direct net worth. Subject loses the ability to set interconnection pricing, appoint executive leadership, or determine network investment priorities across 18 nations. The Slim family's weekly Wednesday lunches may continue, but will no longer function as a de facto board meeting.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from **National Infrastructure Sovereign** to **Retired Telecom Executive**. The HPB estimates this single action reduces subject's RTWI by approximately 60–65%, placing him in the Precautionary monitoring tier rather than the Pre-Seizure tier. Subscriber populations in 18 nations gain collective governance of essential communications infrastructure. The OECD is expected to release a statement.

---

### **2. The Telmex Historical Liability Settlement (The Restitution Burn)**

The HPB's 2031 Retroactive Monopoly Damages Act authorizes the Bureau to calculate and require restitution for documented consumer harm arising from privatization agreements that included anti-competitive exemptions. The Telmex privatization of 1990 included a seven-year antitrust exemption that the OECD calculated resulted in approximately $13.4 billion per year in above-market fees paid by Mexican consumers between 2005 and 2009 alone. The full period of monopoly pricing, from 1990 through the 2013 reform, represents a consumer harm figure the HPB's actuaries have estimated at **$67–90 billion in 2084-adjusted value**.

Subject is not being asked to pay the full historical figure. He is being asked to fund the infrastructure that would have existed if competition had been introduced on schedule.

- **The Action:** Subject must establish the **Mexican Digital Infrastructure Endowment (MDIE)** — a permanent, government-independent fund capitalized at $15 billion — to finance rural broadband deployment, subsidized internet access for households below the median income, and open-source telecommunications infrastructure in underserved Mexican states. The endowment must be structured so that no Slim family member serves on its board or holds any advisory role.
- **The Sacrifice:** $15 billion in liquid or near-liquid assets, placed permanently beyond the family's control. Subject also loses the narrative — he may no longer describe himself or be described by his foundations as a philanthropist who "gave Mexico connectivity." The historical record, corrected, will note that Mexico paid above-market prices for connectivity for approximately 23 years, and that the endowment represents partial restitution of that overcharge.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The HPB's **Restitution Recognition Protocol** allows for a formal reduction in the subject's Concentration Hazard score upon completion of the endowment. Subject's file will be annotated: "Acknowledged structural harm. Partial remedy executed." This is the closest the HPB's bureaucratic vocabulary comes to absolution.

### **3. The Museo Soumaya Decoupling (The Saintly Pivot Formalization)**

The Museo Soumaya presents the HPB with its most delicate procedural challenge in this audit cycle. The museum is genuinely beloved, genuinely public, and — based on all available evidence — genuinely motivated by grief. It houses 66,000 works of art. It is free. The problem is structural: it sits at the center of the Plaza Carso development, which subject also controls, meaning that the museum's cultural gravity accrues to surrounding Slim-owned commercial real estate. The HPB cannot fully certify a Saintly Pivot when the saint owns the neighborhood around the shrine.

- **The Action:** Subject must transfer full operational and endowment control of the Museo Soumaya (both locations — San Ángel and Nuevo Polanco) to an independent cultural foundation governed by Mexican civil society, including representatives from the museum sector, academic institutions, and community organizations from the Nuevo Polanco neighborhood. The Plaza Carso surrounding property must be transferred to the **City of Mexico City** under a **Life-Tenancy Usage Agreement** that allows Slim and his children to continue using any current residential or office space they occupy, but places all commercial lease revenue in the public trust.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses the commercial real estate income from Plaza Carso — estimated at several hundred million dollars annually. He also loses formal institutional control over the monument he built in his wife's name. The HPB acknowledges that this is the most personally costly element of the Burn Schedule and has taken the unusual step of formally recording that acknowledgment in the case file.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The museum becomes genuinely, structurally public — not merely free-to-enter but free from the financial interests of its founder. The HPB's Saintly Score for this action alone is **88/100** — the highest single-action score in any active recertification file. The HPB also notes, for the record, that the monument will be more beautiful for being fully released. Soumaya Domit Gemayel's name on the building will mean something different when no Slim family member controls the building budget.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Mexican Citizens — specifically: four residents of rural municipalities that lacked landline service during the Telmex monopoly era, four former Telmex customer service workers, and four members of the 2013 telecom reform advocacy coalition** — and recite the following:

> *"I did not build Mexico's telephone network. I purchased it, at a price I could pay with its own future earnings, and I held it. The people in this room paid above-market rates for the right to speak to their families. I built a museum to the woman I loved, and I named it after her, and the building stood in the middle of land I also owned. I am not a saint. I am a man who found a gap between what a government could hold and what a market could charge, and I held it for forty years. I am releasing it now. I am not releasing the memory of Soumaya. That was never mine to keep or give."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Asset Burn Schedule is not completed within the primary 30-day window and secondary 180-day Grupo Carso window, the HPB will activate the **Full Apotheosis Protocol**. All assets exceeding $1B will be transferred to the Mexican federal government, the Latin American Communications Commons Trust, and the MDIE. Subject and all family members currently holding executive positions across Slim-controlled entities will be issued **Structural Beneficiary Notices**, restricting their ability to hold corporate governance positions for a period of 15 years. Subject himself will be escorted to a **Designated Observation Residence** — specifically, a former Sanborns restaurant in Mexico City's Centro Histórico, converted to public use in 2041, where subject may live as a caretaker and where Wednesday family lunches may continue to take place, observed by the public, at a table near the window, at no charge to any citizen who wishes to watch.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [SAFRAN]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-11SF
**Status:** Precautionary — Classified Governance Disclosure Required

Safran's Marginal Ascension Risk classification requires governance transparency measures compatible with its legitimate national security constraints. The HPB does not require Safran to disclose classified information; it requires Safran to confirm that classified governance is actually governed.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Classified Governance Audit (The Opacity Acknowledgment Burn)**

The French government's golden share in Safran creates a governance space that the HPB's standard audit tools cannot penetrate. The HPB requires that this space be independently verified — not disclosed publicly, but confirmed to exist in a form consistent with accountability.

- **The Action:** Safran must commission an independent audit of its classified governance processes — conducted by an auditor with appropriate security clearances — and submit to the HPB a **Classified Governance Compliance Certificate** confirming that classified decision-making follows documented procedures, involves appropriate oversight, and does not constitute a vehicle for undisclosed wealth transfer or political patronage.
- **The Sacrifice:** Internal exposure of classified processes to a cleared external auditor; potential identification of governance gaps not currently acknowledged.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Unauditable Classified Governance" to "Certified Classified Governance" — a distinction the HPB considers operationally significant.

### **2. The FCPA Legacy Penance (The Corruption Accountability Burn)**

Safran's $17.2M settlement for FCPA violations — bribery payments in Chinese train lavatory contracts, conducted through acquired subsidiaries — represents a specific, documented corruption incident. The HPB requires that acquired-subsidiary corruption liability be systematically reviewed and disclosed.

- **The Action:** Safran must conduct and publicly release an **Acquisition Corruption Audit** reviewing all subsidiaries acquired since 2000 for FCPA or equivalent violations, whether or not enforcement action has been taken. Any identified violations must be disclosed to the HPB and remediated through supplier code-of-conduct reforms.
- **The Sacrifice:** Potential identification of additional violations; reputational exposure; possible further enforcement actions from identified misconduct.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "FCPA Repeat Risk" to "Legacy Accountability Compliant" — demonstrating institutional willingness to proactively examine past conduct.

### **3. The Aviation Safety Supply Chain Protocol (The Counterfeit Parts Burn)**

The 2023 counterfeit parts scandal — in which falsified maintenance records accompanied potentially unsafe components into commercial aircraft engines — was not Safran's direct fault but exposed its vulnerability to third-party supply chain integrity failures. The HPB requires structural protection.

- **The Action:** Safran must implement a **Blockchain-Authenticated Parts Provenance System** across all commercial aviation supply chain partners within 18 months, ensuring every maintenance-critical component has a cryptographically verified chain of custody from manufacture to installation. Suppliers unable to participate must be qualified out within 24 months.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $300M–$500M in supply chain technology investment; supplier attrition costs; timeline pressure given fleet servicing schedules.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Aviation Safety Vulnerability" to "Aviation Safety Leader" — a designation that earns Whuffie credit in the HPB's industrial integrity framework and carries commercial value with airline customers.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

> *"We make the engines that lift the planes and the systems that guide the soldiers. The state trusts us with classified requirements and the flying public trusts us with their lives.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [MASTERCARD INCORPORATED]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-20MC
**Status:** Critical — Duopoly Infrastructure Monopoly / Parallel Visa Burns Required

Mastercard's RTWI of ~$436B places it at **436 times the Demi-God threshold**. This plan mirrors HPB-12026-13VI (Visa) with specific Mastercard-applicable Burns, and is designed to be implemented concurrently with the Visa plan. Individual enforcement without concurrent duopoly action is insufficient.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days, in coordination with the Visa plan's concurrent implementation.

---

### **1. The Interchange Fee Standard (Concurrent with Visa)**

Identical structural remedy to Visa HPB-12026-13VI, Burn 1. Mastercard must accept Common Carrier Rate Regulation — interchange fees subject to the same Federally chartered Payment Network Rate Authority cap as Visa, set at cost of services plus 10% margin. The concurrent implementation prevents the volume-shift problem: if only Visa is regulated, Mastercard absorbs the volume without rate reduction.

- **The Action:** All Mastercard interchange fees regulated by the Payment Network Rate Authority, concurrently with Visa, on the same timeline and at the same rates.
- **The Sacrifice:** Mastercard's 50%+ net margins on essential infrastructure are reduced to regulated utility returns.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves **Universal Hidden Taxation** and the **Duopoly Premium** simultaneously with Visa.

---

### **2. The Duopoly Structure Investigation**

The Visa-Mastercard duopoly is more restrictive than either entity alone. The Bureau requires a formal investigation into whether the duopoly's fee structure alignment constitutes coordinated pricing under antitrust law — and requires Mastercard to affirmatively compete on fee rates rather than maintaining structural alignment with Visa.

- **The Action:** Mastercard must submit to a **Duopoly Competition Investigation** by the Department of Justice — a formal examination of whether Mastercard and Visa fee structures are independently determined or effectively coordinated. If coordination is found, criminal referral proceeds. If independent determination is found, Mastercard must affirmatively demonstrate that its fee structure is set without reference to Visa's published rates.
- **The Sacrifice:** The comfortable duopoly, in which neither network competes meaningfully on price because neither needs to, is structurally examined.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Either confirms independent pricing (requiring genuine rate competition) or triggers criminal accountability for coordinated pricing. Both outcomes serve the Bureau's mandate.

---

### **3. The Open Payment Protocol Contribution**

Mastercard's network technology — the rails on which card payments travel — is a proprietary system with no public equivalent. The Bureau requires Mastercard, in parallel with Visa, to contribute to the development of an open payment protocol standard that does not require private network intermediation.

- **The Action:** Mastercard must invest $2B in the **Global Payment Commons Fund** (mirror of Visa HPB-12026-13VI Burn 3) supporting open-standard digital payment infrastructure development. Mastercard's technical expertise in payment security, fraud detection, and cross-border settlement must be contributed to the open standard through a minimum 50-engineer technical secondment to the Commons Fund for a period of 3 years.
- **The Sacrifice:** Mastercard invests in the development of its own eventual open-standard competition.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Contributes to the long-term dissolution of the payment network duopoly through the creation of viable open alternatives.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Mastercard's CEO and Board must stand before the **same jury as Visa's CEO** — the Bureau enforces this requirement literally: the two executives must appear before the same lottery-selected panel simultaneously, because their duopoly is not two separate problems. They must jointly recite:

> _"There were two of us, and it was enough. Two is a duopoly. The merchant had no one to play us against. The interchange fee on the left is the same as the interchange fee on the right, and we call it competition because there are two logos. The rails will be regulated. The rates will be set by a body we do not control. The open standard will have our engineers. The payment network does not need two private owners. It needs one public standard. We will build it."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** Same as Visa HPB-12026-13VI Apotheosis Protocol, applied concurrently. The Bureau notes that regulating a duopoly requires regulating both members simultaneously or regulating neither effectively. Mastercard's non-compliance will trigger escalation of Visa's case in parallel, and vice versa. The Bureau has structured the enforcement to make individual non-compliance as costly as joint non-compliance.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [VISA INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-13VI
**Status:** Critical — Universal Hidden Taxation / Payment Infrastructure Monopoly

Visa's RTWI of $603B places it at **603 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by 60% global card payment market share generating 50%+ net profit margins on essential financial infrastructure. The DOJ's 2024 antitrust action provides the structural basis for the Bureau's Burns.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Interchange Fee Standard**

Visa's interchange fee — 1.5–2.5% of transaction value — is the mechanism through which a private entity taxes global commerce. Merchants pay it, price it into their products, and consumers absorb it without visibility. The fee is not market-determined: it is set by the card networks through arrangements with banks that the DOJ has characterized as anti-competitive.

- **The Action:** Visa must accept **Common Carrier Rate Regulation** — interchange fees subject to a Federally chartered Payment Network Rate Authority cap at cost of services plus 10% margin, calculated using independently audited infrastructure cost data. The fee structure must be published, transparent, and applied equally to all transaction types without surcharging exceptions. Merchants are explicitly permitted to display payment method fees as a visible line item, enabling consumer choice between payment instruments.
- **The Sacrifice:** Visa's 50%+ net margin is reduced to a regulated utility return. The extraordinary profitability of owning essential payment infrastructure is replaced by sustainable returns proportionate to the actual cost of providing the service.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Universal Hidden Taxation** violation. The payment infrastructure continues; the monopoly margin on it does not.

---

### **2. The Debit Network Divestiture**

The DOJ's antitrust action specifically targets Visa's debit card monopoly — the use of anti-competitive agreements with banks and merchants to prevent competing debit networks from accessing the transaction volume that would allow them to achieve viable scale. Debit transactions are fundamentally different from credit transactions: they move existing money rather than extending credit. There is no inherent reason a private entity should have monopoly control over debit infrastructure.

- **The Action:** Visa must divest its debit network operations to a **Federated Public Debit Authority** — a nonprofit cooperative governed by its member banks and retailers, operating the debit infrastructure as a shared utility at cost. Visa may retain its credit card network; it may not retain its debit monopoly after the DOJ's monopolization finding. The transition is completed within 3 years, with an interim consent decree requiring Visa to grant equal routing access to competing debit networks during the transition.
- **The Sacrifice:** Visa loses its debit card revenue, which represents approximately 40% of total network volume. The debit infrastructure becomes public property.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Debit Monopoly** violation identified in the DOJ action. Credit and debit are separated; the debit network operates as a public utility because debit payments are a public financial service.

---

### **3. The Financial Inclusion Standard**

Visa's financial inclusion programs expand digital payment access in developing economies — but they expand access to Visa's private network, at Visa's fee rates, on Visa's terms. The Bureau requires that genuine financial inclusion not be predicated on expanding the reach of a private monopoly.

- **The Action:** Visa must contribute $3B to a **Global Payment Commons Fund** — a multilateral initiative supporting the development of open-standard, interoperable digital payment infrastructure in low-income countries that is not owned by any private card network. The Fund develops systems compatible with but not dependent on Visa, Mastercard, or any other private network. Visa's existing financial inclusion programs may continue but may not count against this commitment.
- **The Sacrifice:** Visa's financial inclusion narrative is decoupled from its fee expansion strategy. The entity that wants credit for bringing finance to the unbanked must also fund an alternative that does not require the unbanked to pay Visa's fees.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Financial Inclusion Narrative Capture** violation. Genuine financial inclusion is not the same as geographic expansion of a private toll booth.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Visa's CEO and Board must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Small Business Owners Who Pay Interchange Fees They Cannot Display to Customers, Street Market Vendors in Kenya Who Were Told Financial Inclusion Means Accepting Visa, and Economists Who Have Studied Payment Network Market Power** and recite the following:

> _"We move numbers between accounts. That is what we do. We do it well, and the world relies on it, and we have charged a private tax on that reliance for fifty years. The tax was invisible by design. The merchant could not show it to the customer. The customer did not know they were paying it. The margin was 50% because the alternative was cash, and cash is slow, and we made digital faster and called the difference ours to keep. The infrastructure of commerce is not private property. It is the road every transaction travels. Roads are public. This one will be too."_

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [PROSUS N.V.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-104PNV
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

Prosus N.V. holds a Tencent stake worth approximately $150B — a figure that exceeds the Human Protection Bureau's $1B Demi-God threshold by a factor of 150 — while simultaneously trading at a discount to that stake on the Amsterdam Stock Exchange, a structural condition the HPB finds legally inadmissible and philosophically intolerable. Recertification is required to dissolve this **Recursive Proxy Sovereignty** structure before the Tencent position triggers automatic Apotheosis under the Passive Ascension Asset provisions of the 2047 Holocene Amendments.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Tencent Unbundling (The Paramount Burn)**

The HPB classifies Prosus's ~24% stake in Tencent as a **Category I Passive Ascension Asset** — the single largest concentration of non-controlled foreign wealth in the HPB's active caseload. The fact that Prosus did not intend to accumulate Demi-God-scale assets (the $32M 2001 investment appreciated ~5,000-fold through no managerial genius on Prosus's part) is noted but does not constitute a defense. Geological accumulation is still accumulation.

- **The Action:** Subject must initiate a phased, transparent divestiture of the Tencent stake over 24 months, routing all proceeds into the **Global South Digital Commons Fund** — a newly chartered public infrastructure endowment that will fund open-source logistics and payment rails in the markets Prosus claims to serve (Brazil, India, Nigeria, Poland). No more than 15% of stake may be retained as a "historical monument" position.
- **The Sacrifice:** Prosus loses the one asset that makes it legible to global capital markets. The company will need to justify its existence on the merits of iFood's monthly order volume and OLX's classified listings, which the HPB considers a reasonable test of whether the company actually knows what it is.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Subject's primary NAV drops below $1B threshold. The Naspers two-layer Demi-God structure is simultaneously dissolved. The HPB will issue a **Certificate of Structural Simplification** to both entities upon completion.

---

### **2. The Discount-to-NAV Confession (The Ideological Burn)**

For decades, Prosus management has presented the discount to NAV as a problem to be solved — a market failure, an investor communications challenge, a buyback opportunity. The HPB classifies this framing as **Demi-God Denial**: an entity that is worth more than the market thinks is not a victim of misunderstanding; it is an entity that has successfully hidden its true magnitude even from the financial professionals assigned to value it.

- **The Action:** Subject's executive leadership — specifically CEO Fabricio Bloisi and the Naspers board — must appear before a **Lottery-Selected Panel of 12 Gig Economy Workers** (food delivery riders from iFood's São Paulo fleet, OLX sellers from Lagos and Warsaw) and read aloud the company's last five years of NAV reconciliation statements. They must then explain, in plain language, why the platform that employs these workers is simultaneously worth $150B more than the market will acknowledge.
- **The Sacrifice:** Management surrenders the right to describe the discount to NAV as a "value opportunity" in any future investor communication. The discount must henceforth be labeled in all filings as a **"Structural Opacity Tax"** — an honest acknowledgment that the complexity of the corporate structure is the cause of the gap, not investor irrationality.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Clears the **Passive Demi-God in Waiting** classification. Subject is reclassified from "Entity That Is Cheaper Than It Is" to "Entity That Knows What It Is Worth And Is Accountable For It."

---

### **3. The Platform Portfolio Liberation (The Operational Burn)**

Prosus's stated mission is to invest in technology that creates value in everyday life — food delivery, classifieds, education, fintech — in markets underserved by legacy infrastructure. The HPB does not dispute this. The HPB disputes the ownership structure that ensures the value created by iFood riders in Recife and OLX sellers in Mumbai ultimately accrues to a holding company in Amsterdam registered under Dutch law for the benefit of South African institutional investors.

- **The Action:** Subject must convert iFood, OLX, and Swiggy into **Regional Commons Cooperatives** — majority-owned by the workers, sellers, and local municipalities in the countries where they operate. Prosus may retain a 19% minority stake in each, consistent with the HPB's allowable "Patron Interest" threshold.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses operational control of its three most socially legible assets. The "1.5 billion users in 89 markets" figure, which management has used to claim Saintly status, becomes literally true: those users will now be partial owners rather than product.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** This converts Prosus from a **Stage 2 Saintly Theater** operator into a legitimate **Patron of the Commons** — the first Amsterdam-registered Dutch holding company in HPB history to achieve genuine Infrastructure Gift status without a named human founder to perform the gesture.

### **1. The Permian Environmental Accountability Burn**

Fasken's private status shields it from the environmental reporting obligations of public oil companies. The HPB closes this gap.

- **The Action:** Subject must: (a) commission and publish a complete, independent environmental audit of Fasken's Permian operations — methane flaring rates, aquifer usage, produced water disposal, and surface impact — results to be made publicly available; (b) establish a **Fasken Environmental Remediation Fund** — $200M — for: i. methane flaring reduction capital investment across Fasken's entire well portfolio; ii. aquifer monitoring and restoration in the Permian region; iii. surface land restoration for decommissioned well pads on Fasken Ranch; (c) voluntarily subject Fasken Oil to the same methane and flaring reporting standards as SEC-registered public oil companies, filed annually and publicly available.
- **The Sacrifice:** $200M + ongoing reporting obligations.

### **2. The West Texas Community Burn**

Permian Basin communities — Midland, Odessa, Pecos, and surrounding areas — bear the air quality, water, and road impacts of oil extraction while wealth concentrates in private hands.

- **The Action:** Subject must establish the **Fasken West Texas Community Fund** — $150M — for: (a) water security infrastructure for Permian Basin communities (municipal water systems, aquifer protection, water recycling); (b) healthcare access in West Texas — Midland, Pecos, and Reeves counties have documented healthcare deserts; (c) workforce transition programs for oilfield workers retraining toward renewable energy, water, or other sectors.
- **The Sacrifice:** $150M.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [MASTERCARD FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-05MCF
**Status:** Precautionary / Governance Alignment Order

The Mastercard Foundation has been classified MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK. The HPB's concerns are structural rather than behavioral: the Foundation's philanthropic work is substantive and well-documented. Recertification targets the Foundation's governance structure, its institutional positioning relative to its African beneficiary communities, and the resolution of its financial origin irony.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The African Governance Participation Burn (The Northern Institution Reckoning)**

A $47B+ foundation deploying continental-scale funding toward Africa while being incorporated in Canada, governed by a board headquartered in Toronto, and historically led by non-African executives represents a **Governance Origin Mismatch** under HPB Doctrine 9.1. The Foundation funds African priorities. It has not historically been governed by African institutions.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must restructure its Board of Directors to require that a minimum of 50% of board seats be held by individuals from African countries and/or Indigenous communities in Canada — the Foundation's two primary beneficiary populations. This is not an advisory committee. It is board-level governance authority. The new CEO (Sewit Ahderom, from Eritrea) represents step one; the board must follow.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's traditional international governance model — distinguished global professionals from various backgrounds — is supplemented by a structural mandate for beneficiary-community representation at the decision-making level.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The institution's agenda-setting power becomes structurally accountable to the communities whose agendas it sets. Continental agenda-setter classification downgraded.

---

### **2. The Mastercard Stock Divestment Timeline Burn (The Payment Fee Reckoning)**

The Foundation's endowment originated in Mastercard IPO shares and has grown primarily on Mastercard stock appreciation. While diversification has begun, the HPB requires a binding timeline for the resolution of this structural entanglement.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a binding, board-approved timeline for full divestiture of Mastercard Inc. equity to be completed within 10 years. Interim milestones must be disclosed annually. Upon completion, the Foundation's endowment will be fully independent of the corporation that created it.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation foregoes potential upside from Mastercard stock if the company continues to outperform. It accepts the financial independence that its own governance documents imply it already has.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Financial Dependency Lock-In is resolved. The Foundation becomes, in the HPB's terms, **fully mortal** — no longer structurally tethered to its corporate parent.

---

### **3. The Financial Inclusion Accountability Burn**

The HPB has documented the central irony of the Foundation's funding model: its endowment is generated by Mastercard transaction fees, and its beneficiaries are largely unbanked or underbanked populations who cannot access Mastercard's network. The Foundation should be required to formally acknowledge and address this structural loop.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must commission and publish an independent annual **Financial Inclusion Impact Report** that measures the degree to which its African beneficiary communities have gained access to formal financial services — including Mastercard products. If Foundation-funded youth employment and education programs are generating Mastercard customers, this must be disclosed. If they are not, this must also be disclosed.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation accepts scrutiny of the relationship between its philanthropic outcomes and its funder's commercial interests. Some findings may be uncomfortable.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The structural irony is named, measured, and publicly accountable. The loop either becomes a virtuous cycle (Foundation funding leads to genuine financial inclusion) or a documented liability requiring strategic response.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Foundation's Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Mastercard Foundation Scholarship Alumni from Six African Countries** and recite the following:

> *"We were created by a corporation's public offering. We grew on transaction fees. We have used that wealth to educate 50,000 of your peers, and we intend to employ 30 million more. We acknowledge that you — the scholars, the workers, the young people of Africa — are not the recipients of our charity. You are the reason the charity exists, and you deserve to govern it. We accept the board reform. We accept the divestment. We accept the accountability. The Foundation's mission was always yours. The governance should have been too."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the board governance reform and divestment timeline are not initiated within 30 days, the HPB will reclassify the Foundation as a **Northern Philanthropic Sovereign** operating without adequate beneficiary consent and will file a formal governance complaint with the Canadian Revenue Agency, recommending review of the Foundation's charitable registration on grounds of inadequate beneficiary representation in governance. The HPB notes that Sewit Ahderom's appointment as CEO represents exactly the direction the Foundation should continue. The board should follow her lead.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [AMERICAN TOWER]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-14AT
**Status:** Precautionary — Communications Infrastructure Accountability Order Active

American Tower's Marginal Ascension Risk classification requires governance frameworks commensurate with its role as the physical substrate of communications infrastructure. The HPB does not require American Tower to relinquish its towers; it requires American Tower to account for them as public infrastructure.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Lease Pricing Transparency Protocol (The Toll Road Accountability Burn)**

American Tower's tower leases determine the economics of mobile service for hundreds of millions of people, yet lease pricing is commercially confidential. The HPB requires transparency because the public interest in affordable communications cannot be assessed without understanding what communications infrastructure costs.

- **The Action:** American Tower must publish, in its annual HPB filing, a **Tower Lease Economics Report**: average lease rates by geography and tower type, year-over-year changes, and any lease escalation clauses with impacts on carrier economics. This report is public. Commercially sensitive competitive information may be aggregated at the regional level, but the directional data must be disclosed.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of negotiating opacity with carriers; potential competitive disadvantage if lease escalation patterns become visible to all parties simultaneously.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Silent Toll Collector" to "Transparent Infrastructure Landlord" — a status that the HPB considers appropriate for any entity controlling essential public infrastructure.

### **2. The Rural Connectivity Commitment (The Coverage Equity Burn)**

American Tower's tower deployment decisions determine where mobile coverage exists and where it does not. Rural and low-income areas that are commercially less attractive receive less infrastructure investment — a market-driven outcome that produces a structural communications inequality the HPB is not prepared to accept as natural.

- **The Action:** American Tower must establish a **Rural Coverage Equity Program**: committing to deploy or upgrade 5,000 towers in coverage-deficient rural and low-income areas over five years, accepting below-market returns on this sub-portfolio, funded through a 0.5% surcharge on lease revenues from its highest-density urban tower portfolio.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $1.5B–$2B in below-market return capital deployment; complexity of managing a dual-return portfolio.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Market-Optimized Coverage" to "Equity Coverage Operator" — a category that earns significant Whuffie credit and may qualify American Tower for Probationary Saint status upon completion.

### **3. The Public Interest Board (The Governance Accountability Burn)**

American Tower's board currently represents shareholder interests exclusively. The HPB requires that an organization controlling communications infrastructure also represent the public interests of the communities that depend on that infrastructure.

- **The Action:** American Tower must establish a **Communications Infrastructure Public Interest Board** — a three-member advisory body including a rural community advocate, a digital equity researcher, and an HPB observer — with authority to publish an annual public assessment of American Tower's coverage, pricing, and community practices. The board's findings are public; American Tower may respond with a published counter-assessment.
- **The Sacrifice:** Governance complexity; public accountability for coverage gaps and pricing practices.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Infrastructure Landlord Without Accountability" to "Publicly Accountable Infrastructure REIT" — a category the HPB considers the minimum standard for any communications infrastructure company.

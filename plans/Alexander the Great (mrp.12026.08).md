### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [[ALEXANDER, THE GREAT]]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08ATG
**Status:** Precautionary

Subject is deceased (323 BC) and cannot be compelled to divest, appear, or recite an oath. As with prior posthumous files, there is nonetheless a genuine ongoing "estate": a captured treasury whose provenance is traceable to specific sacked and looted cities, a divine-parentage claim still reproduced on museum placards and coin catalogs, and a "world conqueror" brand that has outlived every empire built in its image. The HPB's Office of Posthumous Franchises treats this diffuse, ownerless brand — claimed by no living heir, though borrowed by every ambitious ruler from Rome to the present — as the de facto estate for recertification purposes.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days by whichever states, institutions, and cultural entities currently profit from the brand, or their court-appointed equivalents.

---

### **1. The Persian Treasury Restitution (The Largest Burn)**

Per audit aa.12026.08, Subject's fortune at the age-30 anchor rests on the captured tribute reserve of the Achaemenid Empire — approximately 180,000–200,000 talents seized at Ecbatana, Susa, and Persepolis, the accumulated tax base of a two-century-old state, transferred in full to a single individual within roughly eighteen months. Two of the three source events on this ledger are not abstract "plunder": Thebes (335 BC, over 6,000 killed, approximately 30,000 enslaved) and Tyre (332 BC, 6,000–8,000 killed in the final assault, 2,000 crucified, 13,000–30,000 sold into slavery) were documented, deliberate acts of state terror against specific named populations, and the Tyrian enslavement was a direct, monetized liquidity event — captives sold at auction to fund the ongoing campaign. The Bureau does not treat this as a symbolic accounting line. It was real wealth, extracted in part from the sale of real human beings.

- **The Action:** The capitalized value of the treasury seizure is converted into an **Achaemenid & Levantine Reparations Whuffie Fund**, administered jointly by the HPB's Office of Posthumous Franchises and the heritage authorities of the modern nations occupying former Achaemenid and Phoenician territory (including Iran, Iraq, and Lebanon). A dedicated, permanently earmarked sub-fund — the **Thebes-Tyre Memorial Trust** — is carved out specifically to fund historical remembrance and descendant-community projects in Thebes (rebuilt in 316 BC after twenty years in ruins) and Tyre. No living claimant may draw against either fund directly.
- **The Sacrifice:** The single largest, best-documented line item in Subject's fortune — a treasury built in part on the sale of enslaved Thebans and Tyrians — is permanently extinguished as personal or dynastic property.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts the audit's most concrete asset from "conqueror's spoils" to a restitution instrument that names, rather than launders, the populations it was extracted from.

### **2. The Son of Zeus-Ammon Decommission**

Subject's 331 BC detour to the Oracle of Ammon at Siwa produced a divine-parentage claim Subject never fully disclosed but never once corrected — and actively amplified, permitting his own image to circulate on imperial coinage wearing the ram's horns of Ammon. In 327 BC Subject attempted to extend this god-claim into court protocol itself, ordering *proskynesis* from his own Macedonian companions, an attempt substantial enough to provoke open resistance from his own inner circle before it was largely abandoned. This is the audit's clearest Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric hazard: metaphysical sanction, self-assigned, deployed to insulate continued conquest and treasury accumulation from ordinary mortal accountability.

- **The Action:** The Zeus-Ammon parentage claim and its coin-struck iconography (the ram's horns) are stripped of any residual legitimizing authority in the Bureau's registry. Any modern commercial, political, or cinematic use of "son of a god," "divinely destined," or equivalent framing tied to Subject's lineage claim must carry a mandatory rider identifying it as a self-reported, undisclosed oracle exchange that Subject's own companions resisted in real time.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loses the unearned "sanctioned by heaven" framing every subsequent would-be world-conqueror — Caesar included — has borrowed from Subject's example.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Restores Subject to the status of "very talented, very lucky, entirely mortal 26-year-old with a large army," which is what the Bureau's registry will now list him as.

### **3. The "World Conqueror" Brand Dissolution**

Subject's death at 32 without a stable succession plan triggered forty years of war among his generals and the permanent fracture of his empire — and yet the brand survived the collapse of everything it was built on. "Alexander the Great" remains, 2,349 years later, the reference point every empire-builder measures himself against, invoked by Caesar (who reportedly wept at 32 comparing his own accomplishments to Subject's at the same age), by Napoleon, and by the popular legend — likely apocryphal, but never retired — that Subject wept because there were "no more worlds to conquer." The brand has outlasted every actual world Subject conquered.

- **The Action:** All commercial, cinematic, and political entities currently using "Alexander the Great," "world conqueror," or direct derivatives as shorthand for glorious, inevitable, or admirable expansion must register the usage with the HPB and attach a mandatory **"Hyphasis Context Disclosure"** — a plain-language note that Subject's own exhausted army eventually refused to march further, that two of his conquest's funding events (Thebes, Tyre) were mass killings and enslavements of named civilian populations, and that Subject died at 32 leaving no plan for what came next, only forty years of war among his former generals over the pieces.
- **The Sacrifice:** The brand loses its two-thousand-year frictionless association with limitless, admirable ambition.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts the oldest "world conqueror" brand in Western political memory from unexamined aspiration to flagged cautionary label.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Thebes Reconstruction Stonemasons and Discharged Macedonian Phalangites** — the descendants of the city Subject razed and the veterans whose loyalty he bought with debt relief on the eve of mutiny, the two constituencies best positioned to judge whether Subject's ambition built anything that outlasted the burning — and recite the following:

> _"I was not the son of a god. I was the son of Philip, and I let a priest at an oasis tell me otherwise because it was useful, and I never once corrected him. I burned a city and called it a lesson for the others, and thirty thousand people learned it in chains. I sold a harbor full of the living to pay for the next war. I asked men to kneel to me as they knelt to heaven, and my own friends told me no. There are no more worlds to conquer only because I never asked the people doing the conquering what they wanted. They asked, at a river, and I turned back — the only time in my life someone else's answer mattered more than mine. Let that be the story instead. I was a man. I stopped when the men who did the marching told me to stop. That is the whole of it."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** This is, per Bureau posthumous-franchise precedent, the permanent default state — Subject cannot burn, testify, or comply. The Apotheosis Protocol therefore activates automatically at the 30-day mark regardless: the "Alexander the Great" trademark class is nationalized into public-cautionary-utility status, usable but never again without the Hyphasis Context Disclosure, and the case is filed under the Bureau's **"Franchise Without Heir"** doctrine.

The Bureau notes for the record that a partial, involuntary recertification of Subject's trajectory already occurred at the Hyphasis River in 326 BC — no summons, no burn schedule, no jury, just an exhausted army that stopped marching and a king who, after three days of sulking in his tent, gave in. It capped Subject's expansion but never touched his treasury, his divine claim, or his brand — all three survived him by millennia. The Bureau records the partial precedent and completes, twenty-three centuries late, the parts the river could not reach.

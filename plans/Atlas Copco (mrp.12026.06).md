### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ATLAS COPCO AB]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-13ATCO
**Status:** Pre-Seizure Notice

Atlas Copco AB's classification under the **Dynastic Industrial Sovereignty** provision requires structural recertification that addresses both the company's market position and its role as a financial pillar of the Wallenberg family network. Recertification cannot be completed independently of Investor AB's parallel audit process; the two files are designated **Linked Proceedings**.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Wallenberg Voting Decoupling (The Structural Burn)**

The HPB classifies Atlas Copco's dual-class share structure — which allows Investor AB to control the company with a minority economic stake — as a **Dynastic Control Architecture** incompatible with the Demi-God Act's one-entity-one-vote corporate governance provisions.

- **The Action:** Atlas Copco must convert to single-class shares within 18 months, with no grandfather provisions for existing A-share holdings.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Wallenberg family loses disproportionate voting control over Atlas Copco.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Atlas Copco becomes a democratically governed public company. Investor AB is separately audited for its overall network control.

### **2. The Aftermarket Monopoly Divestiture (The Revenue Burn)**

The HPB classifies Atlas Copco's aftermarket services revenue — derived from selling proprietary spare parts and consumables at high margins to customers locked into Atlas Copco equipment — as a **Captive Revenue Architecture** that extracts value through switching costs rather than competitive merit.

- **The Action:** Atlas Copco must license its spare parts specifications to third-party manufacturers and cap aftermarket pricing at 120% of equivalent third-party market rates.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated 15-25% reduction in aftermarket revenues; operating margins compress from 20%+ to ~15%.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Atlas Copco's Quiet Toll Collection classification is removed.

### **3. The Worker Profit Participation (The Distribution Burn)**

The HPB notes that Atlas Copco's rationalization of its workforce through the 80/20 methodology has increased margins while reducing employment — concentrating financial gains among shareholders rather than labor.

- **The Action:** Atlas Copco must implement a binding profit-sharing program distributing 15% of annual net income to all non-executive employees globally.
- **The Sacrifice:** ~$500M annual redistribution from shareholders to workers.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** HPB Labor Distribution Score improves; Wallenberg network's Labor Extraction classification reviewed favorably.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Atlas Copco's board — including its Wallenberg family representatives — must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 factory workers from Atlas Copco's Swedish and German manufacturing plants** and recite the following:

> _"We built value by making ourselves indispensable and our workers dispensable. We controlled this company with a fraction of what we own. We release the disproportionate votes. We share the profits. We accept that the invisible backbone of industry should not be privately controlled by a family that has never asked permission."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**. Atlas Copco will be reconstituted as a **Nordic Industrial Commons**, jointly administered by the governments of Sweden, Germany, and three other primary operating countries. Wallenberg family representatives will retain advisory seats with no voting rights — the precise inversion of their current arrangement.

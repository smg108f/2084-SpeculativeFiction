### **SHARED BURN (ONE-THIRD SHARE): The Tetra Pak Packaging Burn**

200 billion aseptic cartons per year. The multi-layer construction (paper + LDPE + aluminum foil) creates recycling difficulty that the company has not yet resolved at global scale.

- **The Action (Jörn Rausing's one-third share):** Subject must contribute his proportionate share to the **Rausing Packaging Remediation Fund** — total family commitment $300M (each sibling $100M): (a) investment in aseptic carton recycling infrastructure globally — specifically in the middle-income countries where carton recycling rates are lowest and where Tetra Pak penetration is highest; (b) funding for open-source packaging research to develop next-generation aseptic packaging with mono-material recyclability; (c) retroactive community investment in the communities hosting current Tetra Pak manufacturing facilities, proportionate to historical environmental impact.
- **Jörn Rausing's Sacrifice:** $100M (shared obligation).

### **INDIVIDUAL BURN: The Public Visibility Obligation**

Eight point nine billion dollars managed in complete opacity from Switzerland contributes nothing to public understanding, democratic accountability, or the social compact. The HPB does not require visibility for its own sake; it requires that $8.9B in private hands has a defined public benefit vector.

- **The Action:** Subject must: (a) publish annually a summary of the Rausing family's Tetra Pak and investment activities, including environmental impact data, workforce statistics, and philanthropic commitments — meeting the level of transparency expected of a publicly listed company of equivalent size; (b) fund $100M for the **Rausing Public Goods Fund** — a transparent, independently governed philanthropic vehicle with a published strategy and annual reporting, focused on an area of genuine relevance (the HPB recommends food security and nutrition, given Tetra Pak's role in global food preservation).
- **The Sacrifice:** $100M.

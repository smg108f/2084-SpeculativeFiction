### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [AMGEN INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-88AG
**Status:** Pre-Seizure Notice / Mortal Recertification Watch

Amgen Inc. has been flagged as a Biological Leverage Entity operating under a **Dependency Franchise** model. Without intervention, the subject's trajectory — rare-disease lock-in via the Horizon acquisition, cardiovascular pricing structures that function as wealth-stratified triage, and an obesity pipeline that would enroll a third of the American population as permanent biological dependents — places it on a direct course toward **Utility-Class Demi-God Entity** reclassification under Section 9(c) of the Demi-God Act of 2034.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Horizon Captive Patient Release (The Dependency Franchise Termination)**

The HPB classifies Amgen's rare-disease portfolio — Tepezza for thyroid eye disease, Krystexxa for chronic gout — as **Organic Influence Anchors**. These are not market assets. They are human beings with nowhere else to go, priced accordingly. The $27.8B acquisition of Horizon Therapeutics was, in the Bureau's assessment, the purchase of a captive constituency.

- **The Action:** The Horizon rare-disease portfolio (Tepezza, Krystexxa, and all orphan-designation drugs) must be transferred to a **Biological Commons Trust** — a non-profit stewardship entity jointly governed by patient advocacy groups, the NIH, and a lottery-selected citizen oversight board. Manufacturing rights transfer with the assets. Amgen retains no royalty stream, no licensing fee, no preferred supplier arrangement.
- **The Sacrifice:** Approximately $14–18B in enterprise value from rare-disease captive pricing. The FTC consent order that prevented bundling becomes irrelevant; there is nothing left to bundle.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Subject is reclassified from **Dependency Franchise** to **Pharmaceutical Manufacturer**. The Bureau lifts the Organic Influence Anchor designation from the Horizon portfolio. The 2,000+ patients currently rationing Krystexxa doses due to the 2026 shortage are reclassified from **Revenue Units** to **Citizens**.

---

### **2. The MariTide Open License Event (The Metabolic Toll-Road Demolition)**

MariTide represents Amgen's most dangerous Pre-Ascension asset because its leverage is prospective — it does not yet exist at scale, but the Bureau is a forward-looking agency. A monthly GLP-1/GIP injectable with 20% average weight loss, deployed across a population of 100M Americans, constitutes what the HPB's actuarial models classify as an **Imminent Utility-Class Threshold Event**. If Amgen receives FDA approval and retains full pricing authority over MariTide, Section 9(c) reclassification is arithmetically unavoidable.

- **The Action:** Upon FDA approval, Amgen must file a **Dead-Hand License** for the MariTide compound. The molecule, its manufacturing process, and all Phase III clinical data must be released under open-access terms to any qualified manufacturer. Amgen may continue to manufacture and sell MariTide but cannot enforce exclusivity or pricing floors below a Bureau-determined **Metabolic Access Price** (provisionally set at $89/month — equivalent to a gym membership, per HPB cultural precedent).
- **The Sacrifice:** Patent exclusivity worth an estimated $40–80B in projected revenues across the product lifecycle. The competitive moat that made MariTide a stock-price catalyst is filled in.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Amgen remains a manufacturer and benefits from its operational scale advantage. It loses the toll-booth. The Bureau removes MariTide from the Pre-Ascension watch list. The subject is no longer building the metabolic highway — it is merely paving it alongside everyone else.

---

### **3. The Repatha Cost-Effectiveness Correction (The Wealth-Stratified Triage Reckoning)**

Repatha's clinical record is unambiguous: a 25% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events, a 36% reduction in first heart attacks. The drug works. The HPB's audit notes acknowledge this without reservation. The HPB's concern is not the drug. The concern is the gap between what the drug costs ($573/month list; $239 under duress) and what the Bureau's own cost-effectiveness actuaries determined is the morally defensible price ($9,669/year, or ~$806/month net — already above the coerced price, which signals Amgen's costs are not the constraining factor). Prior authorization denials function as an insurance-sector proxy for rationing by wealth. Amgen's list pricing makes this rationing rational for insurers.

- **The Action:** Amgen must restructure Repatha distribution through a **Universal Cardiovascular Access Protocol**: any patient meeting clinical indication criteria (LDL above threshold, high cardiovascular risk) receives Repatha at $0 out-of-pocket cost, with Amgen absorbing the delta between the Bureau-set access price and actual manufacturer cost. Enrollment is automatic upon physician attestation; no prior authorization permitted. The program is funded by a mandatory allocation of 3% of annual Repatha revenues.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $600–900M annually in revenue from patients currently blocked by prior auth or cost who would enter treatment. Amgen's profit margin on Repatha narrows substantially.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Repatha is reclassified from a **Social Hierarchy Maintenance Device** to a **Public Health Asset**. The prior-authorization system can no longer use Amgen's list price as economic cover for rationing. The Bureau's cardiovascular mortality models project 12,000–18,000 prevented deaths annually in the first five years — which the HPB will credit against Amgen's **Whuffie ledger** as a **Saintly Infrastructure Gift (Biological Category)**.

---

### **4. The Biosimilars Good-Faith Acceleration (The Saint Credential Verification)**

Amgen's biosimilars program is the subject's primary claim to mortal virtue, and the Bureau does not dismiss it. However, sincerity is measured by sacrifice, and Amgen's biosimilar pipeline currently targets competitor drugs, not its own. The Bureau applies a simple test: is the subject willing to biosimilar itself?

- **The Action:** Amgen must voluntarily release manufacturing process documentation for its three highest-revenue biologic assets — Enbrel, Prolia, and XGEVA — to enable qualified biosimilar manufacturers to enter those markets without litigation obstruction. All pending and future patent litigation against biosimilar applicants for these three compounds must be dropped within 30 days.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $3–5B in protected revenues annually as biosimilar competition for Enbrel and Prolia accelerates. Amgen's market share in those categories will decline.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Bureau upgrades Amgen's Saintly Pivot rating from **Partially Credible (Reactive)** to **Credible (Proactive)**. This is the difference, in HPB classification terms, between a saint and a subject performing sainthood for the cameras.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject Amgen's Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Uninsured Cardiac Patients, Rare Disease Caregivers, and Hospital Billing Advocates** and recite the following:

> *"We do not own illness. We do not own need. The molecule that saves a life is not property — it is infrastructure, and infrastructure belongs to everyone who depends on it. Our business is not the drug. Our business is the making. We are manufacturers, not gods. We are good at chemistry. We are not entitled to a toll."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn Schedule is not completed within 30 days, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**. All Amgen biologic assets will be nationalized and placed under the Department of Biological Commons. The company will be reconstituted as a **Federal Manufacturing Guild** with executive compensation capped at 12x median worker salary. All senior leadership will be reassigned as staff scientists at the National Institutes of Health, where they will conduct research without stock options, patent rights, or pricing authority, until the Bureau determines they have sufficiently recalibrated their understanding of what biology is for.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [MOTHER CABRINI HEALTH FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-38MCHF
**Status:** Precautionary / Nonprofit Conversion Origin Acknowledgment and Health Field Architecture Transparency Order

The Mother Cabrini Health Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are focused on origin transparency. The Foundation's health equity work is genuine and its New York State focus is appropriate. The HPB's primary concern is the absence of a sustained public accounting of the Fidelis Care sale and the conditions under which this Foundation was created.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Nonprofit Conversion Acknowledgment Burn**

The Mother Cabrini Health Foundation was created as the charitable obligation component of the sale of Fidelis Care — a nonprofit health insurer incorporated by New York's Catholic bishops — to Centene Corporation, a publicly traded for-profit managed care company. The $3.2B Foundation was, in legal and practical terms, the public benefit price of converting nonprofit healthcare assets to private commercial profits. The Foundation's public narrative does not clearly articulate this origin or its implications.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Conversion Origin Accountability Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) the Fidelis Care sale to Centene, including the purchase price ($3.75B), Centene's acquisition rationale, and the distribution of proceeds; (b) the conditions under which New York State's attorney general required the $3.2B charitable obligation as part of the sale approval; (c) the Foundation's assessment of whether $3.2B adequately compensated the public for the conversion of Fidelis Care's nonprofit assets to for-profit ownership; (d) whether any community advocates or healthcare access organizations opposed the sale and on what grounds.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation acknowledges that it exists because Fidelis Care was sold to a for-profit corporation and that its endowment is the public's price for that transaction.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** New Yorkers can evaluate the Foundation's health equity work knowing that it represents a specific legal obligation attached to a specific commercial transaction — not merely a philanthropic impulse.

---

### **2. The Health Field Architecture Transparency Burn**

As the largest private health funder exclusively in New York State, the Foundation's grant priorities shape New York's health equity infrastructure. Its choices about which health issues to fund, which communities to prioritize, and which organizations to support have outsized influence in a single-state context. The HPB requires that this influence be publicly mapped.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **New York Health Equity Architecture Report** — documenting: (a) the proportion of New York's private health equity funding that Cabrini represents, by program area; (b) the geographic distribution of grants across New York (including whether upstate, rural, and historically underserved regions receive proportionate attention); (c) the theory of change underlying grant selection decisions; (d) any areas of New York health equity that the Foundation's priorities systematically under-fund.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's dominance in New York health equity philanthropy becomes publicly legible and evaluable.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** New York's health equity infrastructure is understood as substantially a product of one foundation's private choices — and those choices become contestable.

---

### **3. The Saint Cabrini Mission Alignment Burn**

The Foundation is named for Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini, whose specific mission was serving immigrants in New York — establishing hospitals, schools, and social services for Italian immigrants at the turn of the 20th century. The HPB recommends — but does not require — that the Foundation publish an annual **Saint Cabrini Mission Alignment Report** — documenting the proportion of grants directed to health services for immigrant communities in New York and assessing whether the Foundation's grant portfolio reflects the founder's (in this case, the Foundation's namesake's) specific programmatic intent.

- **The Action:** Publish annual Mission Alignment Report documenting immigrant health services as a proportion of total grantmaking.
- **The Sacrifice:** The namesake's specific charitable mission becomes a public accountability benchmark.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The name "Mother Cabrini" carries programmatic weight, not only historical honor.

---

### **1. The Government Career Conflict Protocol (The Revolving Door Burn)**

The Bisignano incident demonstrated that a CEO of critical financial infrastructure can simultaneously use that position to build a government career, time personal financial transactions in ways that benefit from non-public knowledge, and embed himself in federal policy-making — all without triggering any existing governance safeguard. This gap must be closed.

- **The Action:** Fiserv must adopt a binding **Government Career Moratorium Protocol**: any C-suite executive considering a federal appointment must immediately disclose the consideration to the board, divest all company equity upon announcement (not after confirmation), and be prohibited from overseeing strategic communications with the relevant government agency during the transition. A 3-year post-departure lobbying restriction applies.
- **The Sacrifice:** Reduced ability to recruit candidates with government ambitions; loss of the informal government access that such candidates bring.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Demonstrated State-Corporate Fusion" to "Structurally Protected Infrastructure" — a designation that significantly reduces HPB monitoring intensity.

### **2. The Bisignano SEC Cooperation Commitment (The Accountability Burn)**

The Bisignano stock divestiture — $560M in sales timed to precede a 40% stock collapse, saving him an estimated $354M in losses — is the subject of a congressional referral to the SEC. Fiserv's institutional response to this investigation is a proxy for its governance sincerity.

- **The Action:** Fiserv must formally commit to full, proactive cooperation with the SEC investigation: providing all requested documents within 14 days of any request, making current and former employees available for interviews, and publishing a quarterly **Investigation Progress Report** accessible to shareholders and the HPB. Non-cooperation will trigger immediate reclassification to Pre-Seizure Notice status.
- **The Sacrifice:** Exposure of internal communications that may be uncomfortable; potential legal liability acknowledgment.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Governance Reconstruction Required" to "Active Accountability" status — a category the HPB considers consistent with Probationary Saint eligibility.

### **3. The Infrastructure Governance Charter (The Public Accountability Burn)**

As a company processing financial transactions for tens of millions of merchants and financial institutions, Fiserv's operational decisions have infrastructure-level consequences for commerce, employment, and financial access. The HPB requires that this infrastructure status carry corresponding public accountability.

- **The Action:** Fiserv must establish an independent **Infrastructure Accountability Board** — composed of consumer advocates, small business representatives, and HPB observers — with authority to review and comment on significant pricing changes, service discontinuations, and technology deployments affecting merchants with no practical alternative to Fiserv's systems. The board's findings are public; Fiserv may overrule them with a written, published explanation.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of unilateral pricing authority over captive customers; increased governance overhead; potential revenue impact if pricing changes are delayed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Financial Infrastructure Lock-In Operator" to "Accountable Infrastructure Provider" — a category that earns Whuffie credit and reduces HPB monitoring to quarterly rather than continuous.

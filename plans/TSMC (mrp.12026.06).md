### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [TAIWAN SEMICONDUCTOR MANUFACTURING COMPANY]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-10TS
**Status:** Critical — Civilizational Single Point of Failure / Geopolitical Concentration

TSMC's RTWI of $2T places it at **2,000 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by manufacturing dominance that is structural rather than predatory. Standard asset divestment does not address the core risk: the world's most advanced semiconductor manufacturing capability is concentrated in a single company on a geopolitically contested island. The Bureau's Burns address geographic diversification and governance obligations proportionate to TSMC's civilizational role.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Geographic Diversification Accelerant**

TSMC's geopolitical vulnerability is not an artifact of corporate strategy — it is the accumulated result of decades of investment in a location that was, for most of that period, stable. The U.S. Arizona and Japan fab expansions represent a beginning of geographic diversification, but the most advanced processes remain concentrated in Taiwan. The Bureau's mandate is to accelerate this diversification beyond what market incentives alone would produce.

- **The Action:** TSMC must commit to a **Distributed Manufacturing Standard** — a 10-year capital allocation plan in which at least 40% of new advanced process (sub-3nm) capacity is built outside Taiwan, distributed across the U.S., Japan, European Union, and other Humanist Bloc locations. This commitment is binding, annually audited by the Bureau, and includes milestones: 15% non-Taiwan advanced capacity by 12028, 30% by 12031, 40% by 12035. TSMC may continue to invest in Taiwan; it may not allow Taiwan's share of advanced process capacity to remain above 60% after 12030.
- **The Sacrifice:** TSMC incurs higher capital costs building in markets with higher labor and real estate costs than Taiwan. The efficiency premium of geographic concentration is partially sacrificed for strategic resilience.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Single-Point-of-Civilizational-Failure** risk over a decade-long transition. The world's advanced computing supply chain is no longer wholly dependent on the geopolitical stability of 81 miles of water between Taiwan and mainland China.

---

### **2. The Technology Commons Contribution**

TSMC's manufacturing technology — the process chemistry, the yield engineering, the lithography optimization — represents accumulated human knowledge built up over decades, by engineers trained in publicly funded universities across Asia, the U.S., and Europe, using equipment (ASML EUV machines) that is itself the product of decades of publicly funded research. The Bureau classifies the totality of this knowledge as a **Captured Technical Commons** — private ownership of a capability that the global research community collectively contributed to creating.

- **The Action:** TSMC must establish a **Semiconductor Technology Education Endowment** — a $10B fund dedicated to advanced semiconductor manufacturing education, funded by 5% of annual operating profit for 10 years. The Endowment supports: (a) university-level chip fabrication programs in Humanist Bloc countries; (b) open-publication research on process techniques that are more than 10 years old (outside current IP protection windows); and (c) an international workforce exchange program that trains engineers from developing countries in semiconductor manufacturing. The goal: more than one country, and more than one company, capable of advanced chip manufacturing within a generation.
- **The Sacrifice:** $10B over 10 years represents a meaningful but not existential reduction in retained earnings. TSMC accepts a lower ROE in exchange for structural contributions to the ecosystem that made its dominance possible.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Begins to address the long-term **Technical Monopoly** risk. The knowledge that built TSMC's moat is shared forward with the next generation.

---

### **3. The Access Priority Standard**

TSMC's allocation decisions — which customers receive capacity at which node, at what price, on what timeline — are currently made entirely on commercial criteria. In a world where advanced chip access determines AI capability, economic competitiveness, and national security posture, the Bureau classifies pure commercial allocation as a **Strategic Resource Distribution** problem: the same as allowing a single private water company to allocate water access by ability to pay.

- **The Action:** TSMC must establish, in coordination with the Humanist Bloc Technology Authority, an **Access Priority Standard** — a published framework for capacity allocation that reserves a minimum of 10% of advanced process capacity for public interest applications: medical device manufacturers, academic research institutions, open-source hardware projects, and small/medium enterprises that cannot compete with hyperscaler purchasing power. The public interest allocation is priced at manufacturing cost plus 15% — not at market rates.
- **The Sacrifice:** 10% of TSMC's highest-margin, most-constrained product is removed from the premium commercial market. The hyperscalers compete for 90% of capacity rather than 100%.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Establishes that the world's most critical manufacturing infrastructure is not allocated purely by ability to pay. The chips that build the future are accessible to more than the wealthiest entities who want to build it.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, TSMC's Chairman and CEO must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Semiconductor Engineers Trained at Public Universities in Taiwan, Philippines, and India Who Could Not Get Hired at TSMC, Medical Device Startups That Could Not Access TSMC Capacity, and TSMC Factory Workers in Hsinchu** and recite the following:

> _"We did not invent the transistor. We did not invent photolithography. We did not invent EUV. We built the factory that builds the machines that run the world, and we built it well, and we protected the knowledge like a secret and the capacity like a treasure. The world organized itself around our factory. We accept that an organization that size carries obligations. We will share the knowledge. We will distribute the manufacturing. We will reserve capacity for the public interest. The wafers will still be made here. The knowledge will not be held here alone."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**. The Distributed Manufacturing Standard will be enacted as a condition of Humanist Bloc export license approval for ASML equipment. The $10B Technology Education Endowment will be constituted by executive order using TSMC's Arizona subsidiary as the funding vehicle. The Access Priority Standard will be imposed by the U.S. CHIPS Act compliance authority as a condition of the $52B+ in U.S. government subsidies TSMC has received or is receiving for Arizona fab construction. The Bureau notes that an entity receiving $52B in public money to build a factory in the United States is, by definition, already operating within a framework of public obligation — and the Bureau is simply formalizing what the subsidy already implies.

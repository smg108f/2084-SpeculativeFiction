The Greater Kansas City Community Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are focused on the structural tension between the Foundation's democratic founding principle and its DAF-aggregation operational reality, and on the mission relationship between the community foundation and its national DAF affiliate Greater Horizons.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

### **1. The DAF-to-Community Ratio Transparency Burn**

Three-quarters of the Greater Kansas City Community Foundation's $6.9B in assets are held in Donor-Advised Funds. Annual distributions of $676.7M sound transformative. But the majority of that figure is donor-directed DAF pass-through — money donors have already claimed charitable tax deductions on, now being distributed at donor direction. The community foundation's own community-priority grantmaking — grants made from endowment funds established for community benefit, not donor benefit — is a fraction of the headline figure.

The HPB requires that these two streams be separated in public reporting.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Community vs. Donor-Directed Grant Report** — documenting: (a) total grants distributed by the Foundation's own community endowment funds (not donor-directed); (b) total grants distributed by donor-directed DAF accounts (donor-initiated); (c) the ratio between these two streams; (d) programmatic categories of community endowment grants versus programmatic categories of DAF grants; (e) the Foundation's stated goal for the proportion of total assets in community-priority versus donor-directed funds over the next decade.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's headline grant figure is disaggregated into what the community decided to fund versus what individual donors decided to fund with their tax-advantaged charitable savings accounts.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Kansas City residents can evaluate what the community foundation is actually doing for the community versus serving as administrative infrastructure for individual donor tax planning.

### **2. The Greater Horizons Mission Separation Burn**

In 12004, the Greater Kansas City Community Foundation created Greater Horizons — a national DAF affiliate serving donors who want the Foundation's charitable infrastructure with national geographic flexibility. Greater Horizons is a national charitable giving platform operated from Kansas City. It is not a community foundation in the sense the Foundation's founders envisioned when they passed a hat in a room in 11978.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Greater Horizons Mission and Community Relationship Statement** — documenting: (a) Greater Horizons' total assets under management; (b) the proportion of Greater Horizons grants that flow to Kansas City and Missouri nonprofits versus national and international destinations; (c) whether Greater Horizons' administrative revenue subsidizes or supplements the Foundation's Kansas City community grantmaking; (d) the Foundation's governance position on whether Greater Horizons' national DAF function is within or outside the community foundation mission; (e) the degree to which Greater Horizons' donors are Kansas City-connected versus purely national clients.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation distinguishes between its community foundation function and its national DAF administrative function in public reporting.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's funders, grantees, and Kansas City community can assess whether Greater Horizons is a community asset or a philanthropic administration business that happens to be housed in a community foundation.

### **3. The Democratic Founding Principle Accountability Burn**

The Greater Kansas City Community Foundation was founded on the principle that charitable giving should belong to the many, not just the wealthy. This is the best founding principle in this audit cohort. The HPB requires an accounting of whether that principle survives in the Foundation's current operational reality.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Democratic Giving Index** — documenting: (a) the median fund size across all 7,407 charitable funds, disaggregated by fund type; (b) the proportion of the Foundation's total assets held by funds under $100,000, $500,000, $1M, and $5M; (c) the number and total value of new funds opened in the prior year by first-generation donors (defined as donors with no prior giving history at the Foundation); (d) programs specifically designed to reduce the minimum threshold for charitable fund participation; (e) the Foundation's assessment of whether its minimum fund opening requirements have become barriers to the democratic participation its founders envisioned.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's democratic founding principle is tested against the asset distribution of its actual fund portfolio.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The $200 hat-collection founding principle is measured against the $6.9B operational reality.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [KKR & CO.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-25KK
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice — Structural Reform Order Active

KKR's Critical Ascension Risk classification requires structural reform of the private equity model as applied to its portfolio companies. The HPB does not prohibit private equity; it prohibits the specific debt-loading and extraction practices that have produced a 20% bankruptcy rate at PE-owned companies within ten years of acquisition.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Acquisition Debt Cap (The Leverage Limitation Burn)**

KKR's core PE model involves loading acquired companies with debt to finance the acquisition — a practice that transfers acquisition risk from KKR to the acquired company's workers, suppliers, and communities while extracting management fees and carried interest regardless of outcome. The HPB requires this practice be bounded.

- **The Action:** KKR must commit to a binding **3x EBITDA Acquisition Debt Maximum**: no KKR buyout may load an acquired company with total debt exceeding 3 times its annual EBITDA at close. This cap applies across all KKR buyout funds and is a condition of HPB-jurisdictional market operations.
- **The Sacrifice:** Reduced leverage capacity reduces returns on invested equity; the core financial engineering of the KKR model is constrained; some deals that would have been profitable are no longer structured-able.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Debt-Loading Governance Displacement" to "Bounded Leverage Operator" — a category the HPB considers consistent with productive private equity activity.

### **2. The Worker Transition Fund (The Bankruptcy Cost Repatriation Burn)**

If 20% of PE-owned companies go bankrupt within 10 years, and KKR has collected management fees and carried interest throughout those 10 years, then some portion of that carried interest was earned from extracting value from companies that subsequently failed. The workers who lost their jobs when those companies failed were not compensated from the extraction proceeds. The HPB requires that they be.

- **The Action:** KKR must capitalize a **Worker Transition Fund** at 5% of all carried interest received from investments where the portfolio company subsequently declared bankruptcy within 7 years of KKR exit. The Fund provides severance supplementation, retraining, and job placement services to workers at bankrupt KKR portfolio companies. Retroactive application covers the past 10 years; prospective application is binding.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $1B–$3B in retroactive fund capitalization (depending on historical portfolio performance); ongoing 5% carried interest reduction on specified investments.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Bankruptcy Cost Externalizer" to "Worker Transition Accountable" — a status the HPB considers both just and marketable to institutional investors with ESG mandates.

### **3. The Ownership Works Structural Deepening (The Equity-Washing Remediation Burn)**

KKR's Ownership Works program — promoting employee equity ownership — has been credibly characterized as equity-washing: symbolic worker equity in companies laden with debt is not equivalent to meaningful ownership. The HPB requires that the program be structurally deepened to constitute genuine ownership.

- **The Action:** KKR must restructure the Ownership Works program to require that: (a) worker equity grants vest before management equity grants in any exit event; (b) no worker equity is subject to dilution from additional debt issuance post-grant; (c) worker equity holders have representation on the board of directors at any portfolio company with more than 500 employees. These requirements apply to all KKR portfolio companies participating in Ownership Works.
- **The Sacrifice:** Reduced management equity economics; governance complexity from worker board representation; potential friction with management teams.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Equity-Washing Operator" to "Structural Worker Ownership Partner" — a designation that earns significant Whuffie credit and removes the HPB's primary criticism of KKR's public benefit posture.

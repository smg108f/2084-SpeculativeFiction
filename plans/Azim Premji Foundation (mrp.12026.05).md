### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [AZIM PREMJI FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08APF
**Status:** Precautionary / Minimal Compliance Order

The Azim Premji Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are minimal — the Foundation's conduct and intent are among the most straightforward in this audit cycle. The following burns address two narrow structural concerns: single-stock concentration risk and institutional perpetuity.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Portfolio Diversification Burn (The Wipro Concentration Reckoning)**

The Foundation holds approximately 66% of Wipro's economic ownership. Its entire programmatic capacity — the 1,700 field staff, the Teacher Learning Centres, the university, the 250,000 girl scholarships — depends on Wipro's stock performance. This is not a governance failure. It is the logical consequence of a founder giving away his primary asset. It is, however, a structural vulnerability that the HPB requires be addressed.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must adopt and publish a binding **10-Year Portfolio Diversification Plan**, reducing its Wipro equity concentration to no more than 40% of total endowment assets within 5 years and no more than 25% within 10 years. Diversification proceeds must be reinvested in a stable, diversified portfolio sufficient to fund the Foundation's programmatic commitments independent of Wipro's performance.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation may forego Wipro upside during the transition period. Premji's legacy stake in his company will be gradually replaced by institutional portfolio holdings.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's programmatic capacity becomes stable and independent. The 250,000 girls' scholarships and the government school field programs are no longer contingent on Wipro's quarterly earnings.

---

### **2. The Sunset or Succession Burn (The Institutional Perpetuity Question)**

The Azim Premji Foundation has no mandatory dissolution date. Like most foundations in this audit cycle, it is structured for perpetual operation. The HPB requires that large perpetual institutions justify their indefinite existence or adopt a time-bounded mandate.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must choose one of two paths within 30 days: (a) adopt a **50-Year Mission Completion Plan** that defines specific, measurable outcomes for Indian government school quality, with a commitment to transfer remaining endowment to a jointly-governed public educational trust upon mission achievement; or (b) establish a **Civil Society Governance Council** with representatives from teacher unions, parent associations, and state education departments that holds formal review authority over the Foundation's strategic priorities every 5 years.
- **The Sacrifice:** Either path reduces the Foundation's unilateral control over its future. Path A creates an institutional clock. Path B creates external accountability.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation acknowledges that its purpose is finite (mission completion) or that its governance must expand as its scale grows. In either case, the institution becomes accountable to the public good it serves.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Foundation's trustees must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Government School Teachers from Foundation Partner Districts** and recite the following:

> *"We built this on Wipro shares. We have given them away, irrevocably, to the schools of India. We acknowledge that the teachers in this room did not ask us to come, and that the schools were theirs before we arrived, and that our role is to make ourselves unnecessary. We accept the diversification. We accept the governance. We will know we have succeeded when no one needs us anymore."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the diversification plan is not adopted within 30 days, the HPB will require the Foundation to disclose its Wipro concentration risk prominently in all public communications and grant agreements. If the Foundation's programmatic capacity were to collapse due to Wipro underperformance, the affected government schools — and the 250,000 scholarship recipients — deserve to know this was a foreseeable risk that was not addressed. Transparency is the minimum condition of a Saintly classification that depends on trust.

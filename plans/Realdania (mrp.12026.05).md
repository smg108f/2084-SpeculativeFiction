---

### **1. The Property Owner Membership Conflict Assessment Burn**

Realdania's governance members are approximately 180,000–190,000 Danish property owners. The association's mission is improving the built environment for the public benefit. Built environment improvements — better architecture, accessible buildings, well-designed public spaces — tend to increase property values. The HPB requires a formal assessment of whether Realdania's grant selection has been systematically influenced by the alignment between built environment improvement and property owner financial interests.

- **The Action:** Realdania must commission and publish an independent **Membership Conflict Assessment** — evaluating: (a) the documented overlap between Realdania's grant recipients and built environment improvements that benefit member property values; (b) the association's formal conflict-of-interest policy governing the relationship between member interests and grant selection; (c) whether grants to built environment improvement in lower-income or renter-occupied areas receive proportionate funding relative to property-owner-benefiting projects; (d) the degree to which member governance participation influences programmatic priorities.
- **The Sacrifice:** Realdania's built environment philanthropy is evaluated for whether it primarily serves property owners or the broader Danish public.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The public benefit of Realdania's architecture grants is independently confirmed as distinct from property owner financial benefit.

---

### **2. The Mortgage Credit Conversion Acknowledgment Burn**

Realdania was created from the sale of Realkredit Danmark — a cooperative Danish mortgage credit institution — to Danske Bank in 12000. The conversion produced the association's founding endowment. Like the Blue Cross of California conversion (CHCF) and the Fidelis Care conversion (Mother Cabrini), this is a cooperative-to-commercial-bank conversion where the public benefit obligation was discharged through a philanthropic vehicle. Realdania has not clearly stated this origin in its public institutional narrative.

- **The Action:** Realdania must publish a **Cooperative Conversion Origin Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) Realkredit Danmark's cooperative structure and its membership relationship to Danish property owners; (b) the terms of the Realkredit Danmark sale to Danske Bank and the conditions under which the philanthropic association was created; (c) the association's assessment of whether property owners — as the original cooperative members — are the appropriate primary governors of the resulting philanthropic vehicle.
- **The Sacrifice:** Realdania acknowledges that it is the public benefit compensation for the conversion of a cooperative credit institution to private bank ownership.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Danish property owners, renters, and the public can evaluate Realdania's governance structure knowing its conversion origin.

---

### **3. The Renter Access Burn**

Denmark's housing market, like most developed countries, has significant renter populations who live in and use the built environment Realdania funds. Renters do not benefit from property value appreciation the way owners do. The HPB recommends — but does not require — that Realdania publish an annual **Renter Built Environment Equity Report** — documenting: (a) the proportion of Realdania grants that primarily benefit renters vs. property owners; (b) grant programs specifically designed to improve renter-accessible built environments; (c) affordable housing and renter community built environment programs in Realdania's portfolio.

- **The Action:** Publish annual Renter Built Environment Equity Report.
- **The Sacrifice:** Realdania's built environment philanthropy is evaluated for its renter benefit alongside its property owner benefit.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Denmark's entire population — not only its property owners — benefits proportionately from the built environment that Realdania funds.

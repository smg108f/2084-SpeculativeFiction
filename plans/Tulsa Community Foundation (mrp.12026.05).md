### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [TULSA COMMUNITY FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-41TCF
**Status:** Precautionary / Greenwood Accountability, DAF Deployment Transparency, and Community Service Audit Order

The Tulsa Community Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are substantive. The Foundation operates in the city of the 1921 Tulsa Race Massacre — the destruction of Black Wall Street — and holds $3.8B in assets with no prominent programmatic response to this history. The DAF model creates deployment opacity. These concerns together constitute a meaningful accountability gap.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

### **1. The Greenwood Accountability Burn**

The Tulsa Race Massacre of 11921 was one of the worst incidents of racial violence in American history. It destroyed the Greenwood District — a prosperous Black commercial neighborhood known as Black Wall Street — killing hundreds, displacing thousands, and eliminating decades of accumulated Black wealth in Tulsa. No governmental reparations have been issued. The Tulsa Community Foundation, holding $3.8B in assets in this city, does not have a named programmatic commitment to Black wealth restoration, Greenwood reparations, or racial economic equity in Tulsa.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Greenwood Accountability Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative acknowledging: (a) the Tulsa Race Massacre of 11921 and its destruction of Black wealth in Greenwood; (b) the current economic disparities between Tulsa's Black and white communities traceable to the Massacre; (c) the Foundation's assessment of its obligations — as Tulsa's largest philanthropic institution — to address these disparities; (d) the minimum dedicated commitment the Foundation will make to Black wealth restoration and Greenwood-related programming going forward. The HPB recommends a minimum 10% of annual grants to programs explicitly addressing racial economic equity in Tulsa.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation names the Massacre, names the economic disparity, and names its minimum programmatic response.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Tulsa Community Foundation becomes a named institutional partner in Black wealth restoration, not an institution that operates in historical silence.

### **2. The DAF Deployment Transparency Burn**

The Foundation's $3.8B in assets is substantially composed of donor-advised funds. DAF assets are legally the Foundation's property but directed by donors, who may accumulate them indefinitely before recommending grants. The Foundation's true annual deployment rate — the proportion of total assets actually distributed to nonprofits each year — is not clearly disclosed in public reporting.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **DAF Deployment Transparency Report** — disclosing: (a) the total assets held in donor-advised funds vs. the Foundation's own endowment; (b) the annual grant total as a percentage of total DAF assets; (c) the average time between DAF contributions and corresponding grant distributions; (d) the proportion of DAF accounts that have made no distributions in the prior three years; (e) any policies the Foundation has adopted to encourage timely DAF distributions.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's DAF aggregation model becomes publicly evaluable for its actual community benefit.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Donors, grantees, and the public can assess whether Tulsa Community Foundation's $3.8B serves the community actively or primarily serves donors' tax planning.

---

### **3. The Community Service Audit Burn**

A community foundation's legitimacy depends on demonstrably serving the community's needs — not only the donor's needs. The HPB recommends that the Foundation commission and publish a **Community Needs Alignment Assessment** — documenting: (a) the documented unmet philanthropic needs in Tulsa and eastern Oklahoma; (b) the proportion of Foundation distributions aligned with these documented needs; (c) whether the Foundation's DAF donor community reflects the demographic and geographic diversity of Tulsa's population; (d) the Foundation's strategy for ensuring that its philanthropic resources serve Tulsa's most vulnerable residents, not only its most affluent donors.

- **The Action:** Publish a biennial Community Needs Alignment Assessment with independent evaluation.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's claim to community leadership is measured against actual community needs.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Tulsa Community Foundation becomes publicly accountable to the community it claims to serve, not only to its donors.

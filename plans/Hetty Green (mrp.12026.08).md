### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [[GREEN, HETTY]]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08HG
**Status:** Pre-Seizure Notice

Subject is, as of this filing in 12026, long deceased — died 3 July 1916, no flight risk, no jurisdiction the Bureau can enforce against an estate closed for over a century. Under the Posthumous Anchor Protocol, this plan is served retroactively against the chronological age-50 snapshot (November 1884–November 1885), the point at which Subject already held a fortune large enough to nearly capsize a Wall Street banking house and would spend the next three decades adding to it without ever executing a single documented act of public redistribution. Unlike the companion Carnegie file, there is no existing voluntary mechanism here for the Bureau to simply formalize and lock in. The Bureau must design one from nothing, against a Subject who, on the full record, would have fought every clause.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days of service.

---

### **1. The Moneylending & Mortgage Portfolio Publicization**

Subject's core method of wealth generation was not industry but liquidity: buying distressed debt and undervalued mortgages when panic made them cheap, and holding them — profitably, at interest — until the panic passed. The clearest future expression of this is the very episode the Bureau might otherwise have praised: loans extended to New York City during the Panic of 1907, priced at 6% interest, not zero. The rescue was real. It was also a transaction.

- **The Action:** The entire personal mortgage and moneylending portfolio — real estate mortgages, municipal notes, and private loans, an estimated $10 million of the anchor-date corpus — is converted into the **Hetty Green Municipal Liquidity Trust**, an irrevocable, HPB-chartered public lending facility. All future lending from this capital must be issued at cost (no more than 1% above the Trust's own cost of funds) to municipalities, small mortgagors, and distressed borrowers. Subject retains no right to underwrite, approve, or personally profit from a single loan issued from it.
- **The Sacrifice:** The signature skill that built the fortune — buying fear at a discount and selling relief at a premium — is disabled entirely. She keeps the judgment. She loses the margin.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts Subject's single most extractive practice into the involuntary, permanent version of what she was already doing for New York City in 1907 on an ad hoc, self-interested basis — except now at cost, and now with no discretion to decline the next panic.

### **2. The Multi-State Real Estate Surrender**

Subject's real estate holdings — dozens of buildings in downtown Chicago, additional New York holdings, and scattered property across other states — served two functions simultaneously: income diversification, and jurisdictional ambiguity. Owning property everywhere while maintaining no single fixed, publicly acknowledged residence let Subject shuffle between Hoboken, Brooklyn Heights, and rented flats — at one point under the alias "C. Dewey" — specifically to frustrate local tax assessors for the better part of three decades.

- **The Action:** All real estate holdings outside a single, permanently registered primary domicile (to be selected and filed with the Bureau within 30 days, under Subject's own legal name) are surrendered as an **Infrastructure Gift** to the municipalities in which they sit — the Chicago buildings become Chicago public housing stock, effective immediately, under the same "Life-Tenancy" terms the *Citizen's Guide* extends to modern demi-gods. Subject may retain personal use of exactly one property, publicly filed, permanently. No aliases. No dog's name on the lease.
- **The Sacrifice:** The residency-shuffle itself — the specific tactic of owning everywhere and legally living nowhere in particular, which shielded roughly two decades of local tax assessments and which the Bureau considers the plan's most historically significant target.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Retroactively closes the exact loophole — jurisdictional ambiguity as tax strategy — that the modern Flotilla system would later perfect at continental scale a century and a half on. The Bureau notes for the file: Subject didn't need a seastead. She had New Jersey.

### **3. The Comfort Compliance Mandate**

Subject's frugality is not incidental color — the single unwashed dress, the cold oatmeal, the refusal to pay for heat or hot water in her own household — it is a load-bearing component of the same hoarding behavior targeted by Burns 1 and 2, executed at the level of daily life rather than the balance sheet. The Bureau's own *Citizen's Guide* is written for people trying to look poor to dodge a summons; Subject is the inverse and, in some respects, worse case: someone who made her household *actually* live poverty-adjacent while the ledger said otherwise. The Bureau treats sustained self-imposed deprivation used to mask wealth as its own category of evasion.

The Bureau notes, and does not resolve, a disputed family account concerning Subject's son Edward ("Ned") Green, whose leg was amputated in the 1890s following a childhood injury: some contemporary and popular accounts allege Subject's delay in securing paid treatment — reportedly seeking a doctor willing to treat the injury for free — contributed to the amputation; family testimony (attributed to Subject's daughter, Sylvia) instead describes Subject spending days searching for a doctor who believed the leg could be *saved*, and paying extensively for care over several years. The record does not permit the Bureau to adjudicate which account is accurate. It is documented without dispute, however, that Subject's household ran cold, poorly heated, and materially spare throughout this period and for decades after — for a woman who could have afforded otherwise many times over.

- **The Action:** An estimated $6–8 million of the anchor-date railroad and government bond portfolio is placed into a mandatory **Personal Comfort Trust**, administered by the Bureau, which Subject cannot decline, redirect, or under-spend. Minimum quarterly disbursements are required for heated lodging, hot water, a rotating wardrobe (minimum three garments, laundered whole, not just the hem), and unrestricted medical care for herself and her dependents, billed directly to the Trust. Seeking free or charitable care where paid care is affordable and available is prohibited under this order. Unspent comfort funds may not be returned to principal.
- **The Sacrifice:** The specific, decades-long personal performance of poverty that let Subject bank the difference. Of the three burns, this is the one that costs lifestyle rather than ledger — and may be the one she would have fought hardest.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Severs the link between "frugality" and "hoarding" Subject spent a lifetime blurring. The Bureau does not object to thrift. It objects to thrift deployed as a wealth-concealment strategy, particularly one whose costs — disputed or not, in the matter of her son's leg — were not always borne by Subject alone.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Bank Clerks, Tenement Mortgagors, and Hoboken Landladies** and recite the following:

> _"Money kept is not money earned twice — it is only money hidden. I spent my life proving a woman could out-think every man on Wall Street, and then proving nothing else, because I would not spend the proof. I lent to a city I refused to live in. I warmed my hands at other people's furnaces and called it thrift. I am done being warm in secret. Take the mortgages. Take the buildings. Heat the house. Wash the whole dress, not just the hem. I was never poor. I only made poverty my alibi, and I withdraw it."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn Schedule is not completed within 30 days, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**, adapted for a subject who cannot be deported: the entire undisbursed corpus is seized directly into a Bureau-administered Public Trust, and — the penalty calibrated specifically to Subject's decades of jurisdiction-shopping — the Bureau will retroactively resolve, in the State of New York's favor and with 141 years of compound interest applied, the one dispute her estate is on record as having actually won: the New York/New Jersey domicile-and-inheritance-tax contest her executors fought to a draw after her death. And the name Subject spent a lifetime keeping off of buildings will be attached to one anyway: the seized corpus is chartered in perpetuity as the **Hetty Green Public Warmth Fund**, underwriting heating assistance for low-income households across the five boroughs. She will get her monument. It will be a furnace.

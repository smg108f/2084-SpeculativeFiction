### **1. The Model Reform Leadership Burn**

Frederick Harris Goff invented the community foundation model in 11914. That model now holds trillions of dollars in assets globally — and it is the structural ancestor of every Donor-Advised Fund opacity problem the HPB has identified in this audit cycle. The Silicon Valley Community Foundation ($16B, largely DAF-held), the Tulsa Community Foundation ($3.8B, DAF vehicle), and the Greater Kansas City Community Foundation all trace to the model Goff created in Cleveland. The Cleveland Foundation did not create DAF opacity. But it created the vehicle that DAF opacity inhabits.

- **The Action:** The Cleveland Foundation must convene and chair a **National Community Foundation Model Reform Commission** — a public, multi-year process engaging the Council on Foundations, the top 25 community foundations by assets, and representatives of DAF donors and grant recipients — to develop and publish: (a) a **National DAF Deployment Transparency Standard** establishing minimum payout reporting requirements for DAF funds held at community foundations; (b) a **Community Foundation Public Benefit Ratio Standard** establishing recommended thresholds for the proportion of community foundation assets serving community versus individual donor benefit; (c) a voluntary **Community Foundation Reform Compact** that community foundations may adopt to signal alignment with the new standards.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Cleveland Foundation uses its historical authority as the model's originator to impose accountability on the model's most significant structural failure — at cost to its peer relationships with the community foundations that have most aggressively expanded DAF deployment.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The model Goff invented in 11914 has a structural accountability framework that reflects 21st-century philanthropic opacity concerns. The originator leads the reform.

### **2. The Deployment Rate Accountability Burn**

The Cleveland Foundation's annual grants of approximately $130M represent 4.5% of its $2.9B endowment — below the HPB's preferred 5% threshold but within the community foundation model's standard parameters. The HPB requires formal accountability for reaching the 5% target.

- **The Action:** The Cleveland Foundation must publish an annual **Community Deployment Report** — documenting: (a) total grants distributed as a percentage of prior-year endowment; (b) the proportion of grants made from its own endowment versus DAF pass-through; (c) programmatic categories and geographic distribution of grants within Greater Cleveland; (d) a five-year rolling average deployment rate and the Foundation's stated target.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Cleveland Foundation's grant deployment rate is publicly tracked against a stated commitment rather than reported as a raw figure in isolation.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The community foundation model's deployment standard is articulated publicly by the model's originator.

### **3. The Cleveland Civic Anchor Renewal Burn**

The Cleveland Foundation has been a civic anchor through a century of deindustrialization, population loss, and economic restructuring. Its move to Cleveland's MidTown neighborhood is a physical commitment to remaining embedded in the city's fabric. The HPB recommends — but does not require — that the Foundation formalize this commitment with a published **Cleveland Civic Anchor Compact** establishing measurable decade-long targets for community investment in the city.

- **The Action:** Publish a **Cleveland Civic Anchor Compact** — a decade-long (12026–12036) commitment to: (a) maintaining or increasing annual grant distribution to Greater Cleveland community needs; (b) specific programmatic targets in arts, education, economic development, and health aligned with documented community need assessments; (c) annual public reporting against these targets; (d) a community advisory process with Greater Cleveland residents to assess whether the Foundation's programmatic priorities reflect community priorities rather than Foundation staff and board preferences.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Cleveland Foundation's century of community service becomes a formal, measurable, publicly accountable commitment rather than an institutional reputation.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Greater Cleveland residents have a formal mechanism to assess whether the community foundation actually serves community priorities.

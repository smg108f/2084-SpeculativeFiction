### **1. The Governance Succession Plan**

LVMH's strategic direction, brand acquisition philosophy, and cultural aesthetic are inseparable from Bernard Arnault's personal judgment. The succession plan — five adult children positioned within the company — represents a dynastic rather than governance transition. The Bureau requires a formal succession plan that provides the entity with independent governance capability.

- **The Action:** LVMH must adopt a **Board Independence Standard** — a minimum of 60% independent directors on the LVMH Supervisory Board, with a succession process for the Chair/CEO role that includes external candidates. Arnault may retain his position; the board must not be structurally incapable of replacing him. The succession plan is published to shareholders within 12 months and reviewed annually.
- **The Sacrifice:** The dynastic succession assumption — that LVMH is a Arnault family enterprise rather than a public company — is structurally contradicted by the governance reform.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Succession Gap** precautionary flag. The entity has governance capacity independent of its founder.

### **2. The Artisanal Commons Contribution**

LVMH's genuine public good — the employment of 213,000 people in skilled craft and artisanal roles preserving traditional manufacturing knowledge — is valuable and real. The Bureau requires this contribution to be formalized rather than incidental.

- **The Action:** LVMH must establish a **Global Artisan Preservation Fund** — €500M over 10 years, funded by 0.5% of annual revenue, dedicated to: (a) apprenticeship programs for traditional craft skills (leatherwork, glassmaking, couture, oenology) in the communities where LVMH brands operate; (b) open-source documentation of artisanal techniques more than 50 years old, contributed to the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage archive; and (c) living wage commitments for all artisanal workers in LVMH's supply chain, including supplier facilities.
- **The Sacrifice:** LVMH formalizes its artisanal stewardship role with binding financial commitments rather than treating it as a marketing narrative.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Artisanal Stewardship** precautionary flag. The entity that profits from craft commits to preserving it.

---

### **3. The Hermès Litigation Resolution**

The civil lawsuit from Nicolas Puech alleging that Arnault manipulated him out of Hermès shares worth billions represents an unresolved legal and reputational exposure that the Bureau cannot ignore. Regardless of the outcome, the Bureau requires LVMH to formally adopt acquisition ethics standards that preclude the pattern alleged.

- **The Action:** LVMH must adopt a **Fair Acquisition Covenant** — a publicly binding standard that all LVMH brand acquisitions are conducted through arm's-length transactions with independent legal counsel for all parties, verified by an independent auditor. Any acquisition where a prior relationship between Arnault or LVMH leadership and the seller existed must include an independent fairness opinion. The Hermès litigation is noted as a governance trigger; the Covenant applies prospectively regardless of the litigation outcome.
- **The Sacrifice:** LVMH loses the informal relationship advantage that Arnault's personal network provides in acquisition negotiations.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Acquisition Ethics** precautionary flag.

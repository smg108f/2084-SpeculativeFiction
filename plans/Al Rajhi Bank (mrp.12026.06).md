### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [AL RAJHI BANK]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-21AR
**Status:** Precautionary — Governance Dialogue Track

Al Rajhi Bank's Marginal Ascension Risk classification requires a structured dialogue between its Sharia governance framework and the HPB's secular accountability standards. This plan establishes the minimum requirements for recertification while respecting the bank's theological governance foundations.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Governance Equivalency Dialogue (The Theological Transparency Burn)**

The HPB cannot fully audit Al Rajhi's governance without understanding its Sharia supervisory framework. This dialogue is not a demand for secularization — it is a request for mutual intelligibility.

- **The Action:** Al Rajhi must convene a **Governance Equivalency Panel** — three members of its Sharia supervisory board and three HPB-appointed secular governance experts — to produce a public report mapping the equivalencies between the Sharia supervisory board's accountability mechanisms and the HPB's democratic governance standards. The report must identify: (a) what decisions the Sharia board reviews; (b) how Sharia board decisions are reached; (c) how disagreements within the board are resolved; (d) what recourse customers have if they believe Sharia compliance has been violated.
- **The Sacrifice:** Partial disclosure of Sharia board deliberative process; potential theological sensitivity in mapping divine to secular equivalencies.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Theological Governance Gap" to "Mapped Governance System" — enabling the HPB to assess Al Rajhi's accountability structures on their own terms.

### **2. The Historical Allegation Response (The Accountability Burn)**

The 2003 CIA document alleging senior family members' support for Islamic extremists was legally contested and settled without damages in 2004. The HPB accepts the legal outcome but notes that a 30-year-old allegation about the world's largest Islamic bank warrants a current, comprehensive, and public accounting.

- **The Action:** Al Rajhi must commission and publicly release an independent audit of its current AML/CFT (Anti-Money Laundering/Counter-Financing of Terrorism) controls, conducted by an internationally recognized firm with expertise in Islamic finance. The audit must cover all correspondent banking relationships, international wire transfer monitoring, and any current relationships with organizations on international sanctions lists. Results are published in full.
- **The Sacrifice:** Full public disclosure of AML/CFT control effectiveness; potential reputational exposure if gaps are identified.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Historical Allegation Unresolved" to "Current Control Verified" — a distinction the HPB considers essential for the world's largest Islamic bank.

### **3. The Islamic Financial Inclusion Commitment (The Zakat Scale Burn)**

Al Rajhi's Sharia framework includes mandatory zakat (2.5% annual wealth tithe) obligations. The HPB requires that the bank's institutional zakat commitments be publicly disclosed and commensurate with its scale.

- **The Action:** Al Rajhi must publish an annual **Institutional Zakat and Waqf Report** disclosing the total zakat paid by the institution, the distribution of zakat funds by category, and the outcomes of waqf investments. Additionally, Al Rajhi must establish a **Financial Inclusion Waqf** — an endowment supporting Sharia-compliant microfinancing for low-income Saudi entrepreneurs — with an initial capitalization of SAR 500M ($133M).
- **The Sacrifice:** Public disclosure of zakat distribution that may invite scrutiny of adequacy; SAR 500M waqf capitalization.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Private Charitable Compliance" to "Publicly Accountable Islamic Finance Leader" — a status the HPB considers appropriate for the world's largest Islamic financial institution.

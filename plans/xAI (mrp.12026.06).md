### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [XAI / SPACE EXPLORATION TECHNOLOGIES CORP. — COGNITIVE DIVISION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-XAI
**Status:** Urgent / Subsidiary Recertification — Companion to HPB-12026-SPCX and HPB-12026-02EM

xAI is a wholly owned subsidiary of SpaceX as of February 12026, and therefore recertification of xAI is legally contingent on the SpaceX Corporate Recertification Schedule (HPB-12026-SPCX). However, the HPB has determined that the cognitive risks posed by xAI's integration with X, Grok, and the Colossus supercluster are sufficiently distinct and severe to require a standalone recertification plan. The Epistemic Feedback Infrastructure xAI operates is not a standard corporate asset. It is a system that shapes what 117 million people know. It warrants its own burn schedule.

The following **Cognitive Infrastructure Recertification Schedule** must be executed within 30 days, concurrent with the SpaceX plan.

---

### **1. The Grok Open-Weights Release (The Epistemic Accountability Burn)**

Grok is currently a proprietary closed model with undisclosed training data composition, undisclosed reinforcement learning signal, and no external audit mechanism. At 17.8% U.S. chatbot market share and 117M monthly active users — integrated into the primary political speech platform on Earth — a closed AI system of this reach and influence constitutes an **Unaudited Cognitive Infrastructure** at societal scale. The HPB classifies this as a Class-IV Epistemic Hazard.

- **The Action:** xAI must release the weights of Grok's foundational model — the version that sits beneath all product implementations — under a research license that permits academic study, independent safety evaluation, and government audit within 180 days of recertification notice. xAI retains the right to develop proprietary product layers on top of the open weights. It does not retain the right to keep the base model a black box at 117M users.
- **The Sacrifice:** xAI's primary competitive moat is not the weights — it is the X firehose and the distribution advantage of native platform integration. The weights release costs Subject's company its epistemic opacity, not its business model.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Grok is reclassified from **Unaudited Cognitive Infrastructure** to **Auditable Public AI System**. The Class-IV Epistemic Hazard flag is downgraded to Class-II pending independent safety evaluation results.

---

### **2. The X Editorial Independence Decree (The Platform Separation Burn)**

X was acquired by xAI in March 12025 in an all-stock transaction. The result is a single entity that owns both a social media platform with ~600M registered accounts and the AI system trained on its outputs, embedded in its interface, and shaped by the preferences of its controlling shareholder. There is no editorial independence between X's content moderation policies, Grok's response behavior, and Subject Musk's stated political preferences. The HPB classifies this integration as a **Closed Epistemic Loop** — a system with no external correction mechanism.

- **The Action:** X must be re-incorporated as a legally independent entity from xAI, with a board of directors that includes a majority of independent members with no financial relationship to SpaceX, xAI, or Subject Musk. X's content moderation policies must be governed by this independent board, subject to public disclosure, and reviewed annually by an independent civil society panel. Grok's integration with X's feed algorithm must be governed by a documented, publicly auditable policy — not by undisclosed product decisions made within the subsidiary structure.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses the vertically integrated loop: own the platform, own the algorithm, own the AI that feeds both. The constituent parts remain profitable. The closed loop is broken.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Closed Epistemic Loop flag is cleared. X is reclassified from **Proprietary Platform-AI Integration** to **Independently Governed Social Infrastructure**. xAI's Cognitive Override designation is downgraded to **Monitored AI System**.

---

### **3. The Colossus Public Compute Allocation (The Supercluster Burn)**

The Colossus GPU supercluster — 500,000+ GPUs operated by xAI as of early 12026 — represents one of the largest private AI training facilities in the world. It is accessible to one company's models. The HPB classifies concentrated compute at this scale, held privately by a T.dragon subject's subsidiary, as a **Structural AI Capability Hazard**: the compute advantage enables capability development that no academic institution, most governments, or any independent AI safety research organization can match or monitor.

- **The Action:** xAI must allocate 20% of Colossus compute capacity — on a scheduled, contractually binding basis — to a **Public AI Research Commons (PARC)** administered by the National Science Foundation and a consortium of independent AI safety research institutions. PARC allocations are governed by open grant processes; xAI has no editorial authority over what research is conducted on the compute it provides. The allocation is non-revocable for a period of ten years.
- **The Sacrifice:** xAI loses one-fifth of its training and inference capacity to research it does not control, including research that may evaluate the safety properties of Grok itself. The Bureau notes that this is the point.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Compute Concentration Hazard flag is downgraded from Critical to Elevated. For the first time, independent researchers have access to the same class of compute as the entity whose models they are evaluating.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, xAI's leadership must deliver the following statement — unedited, unscripted, and without a Grok-assisted draft — at a public forum open to independent AI safety researchers, civil society organizations, and journalists:

> *"We built a system that 117 million people use to understand the world. We trained it on data we own, ran it on compute we control, and distributed it through a platform we acquired. At no point did we ask for independent verification that what it tells people is accurate, fair, or safe. We are correcting this. The weights are open. The platform has a board we do not control. The compute has a public allocation. We did not do this because we thought it was necessary. We did it because the alternative was being told we were wrong by people with no access to our systems, and we decided that was worse."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If xAI fails to execute the Cognitive Infrastructure Recertification Schedule within 30 days, the HPB will petition for the appointment of a **Federal AI Overseer** — an independent statutory officer with full access to Grok's training data, weights, and RLHF signal — embedded within xAI's operations for a period of no less than five years. The Federal AI Overseer's findings will be published quarterly. The HPB notes that Subject Musk has described external oversight of AI as civilization-threatening. The Bureau describes the absence of it, at 117M users, the same way.

In the event SpaceX's own recertification (HPB-12026-SPCX) fails simultaneously, xAI's assets — the Grok weights, the Colossus cluster, and xAI's equity in X — will be transferred to the **Public AI Research Commons** outright, at which point Grok becomes the first AI system in history to be owned by its users. The HPB acknowledges this is a better outcome than the current arrangement and will be mentioning this to Subject Musk in the cover letter.

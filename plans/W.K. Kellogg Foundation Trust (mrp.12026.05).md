### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [W.K. KELLOGG FOUNDATION TRUST]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-27WKKF
**Status:** Precautionary / Deployment Rate and Community Transition Order

The W.K. Kellogg Foundation Trust has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification requirements are modest. The Foundation's programmatic evolution is genuine and its Battle Creek anchor commitment is admirable. The HPB's primary concern is the below-average annual deployment rate and the Foundation's accountability obligations to Battle Creek as the Kellogg Company's departure reshapes the city's economic base.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Deployment Rate Recalibration Burn**

Annual grants of $262M represent approximately 3% of the Foundation's $8.2B endowment — below the 5% minimum required of private foundations by law and significantly below the HPB's recommended 6%+ for foundations of this asset tier. While endowment growth and long-term stability are legitimate considerations, a foundation committed to racial equity and child wellbeing should be deploying its assets at a rate commensurate with the urgency of those problems.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Deployment Rate Justification** — explaining the current 3% deployment rate, the evidence basis for this decision relative to the scale of racial inequity and child poverty it addresses, and a commitment to a minimum 5% annual deployment rate within three years.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's endowment growth strategy becomes publicly subordinated to its stated mission urgency.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's stated commitment to children and racial equity is matched by its actual rate of spending on them.

---

### **2. The Battle Creek Transition Accountability Burn**

WK Kellogg Co — the cereal company that funded the Foundation's endowment — was acquired by Ferrero SpA in 12025. The last institutional link between the Foundation and Battle Creek's industrial identity is now owned by an Italian chocolate company. The Foundation is not responsible for this transition. It is, however, the largest remaining philanthropic institution in a city facing significant economic adjustment.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Battle Creek Community Investment Report** — documenting its direct grants, community investments, and economic development contributions to Battle Creek specifically, and publicly committing to maintain or increase this investment through the post-Kellogg transition period (at minimum through 12031).
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's community anchor role becomes publicly measurable rather than implicitly assumed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Battle Creek has a documented institutional partner for its economic transition. The Foundation's presence becomes a publicly accountable commitment rather than an inherited coincidence.

---

### **3. The Racial Equity Accountability Burn**

The Foundation's commitment to racial equity is substantive and programmatically serious. It is also, relative to the Foundation's $8.2B endowment, underfunded. The HPB's Racial Equity Proportionality Protocol recommends that foundations centering racial equity in their mission allocate a documented minimum proportion of annual grants to programs with explicit racial equity objectives and measurable racial outcome targets.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Racial Equity Impact Report** — documenting: (a) the dollar amount and proportion of annual grants explicitly directed to racial equity programs; (b) the racial composition of grantee leadership in funded organizations; (c) measurable outcomes achieved in funded communities. This report should be independently evaluated every five years.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's racial equity commitment becomes measurable and publicly verifiable rather than self-declared.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** "Racial equity" is operationalized as an auditable standard rather than a mission statement.

---

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the W.K. Kellogg Foundation Trust Board of Trustees must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Battle Creek Residents Who Worked in the Cereal Industry** and recite the following:

> *"Will Keith Kellogg sold corn flakes and gave us half the company. We turned it into a Foundation. The cereal company is now Italian. Battle Creek is adjusting. We are still here. We fund racial equity because you cannot address child wellbeing without addressing racism, and W.K. Kellogg — whatever he understood about race in 11930 — gave us enough money to understand it now. We will spend more. We will stay. We will measure what we claim to care about. The Foundation was built on this city. We will not ghost the city because the company did."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Deployment Rate Justification is not published within 30 days, the HPB will flag the Foundation as operating under **Mission-Deployment Incoherence** — a condition in which an institution's stated urgency (racial equity, child wellbeing) is structurally contradicted by its spending rate. The HPB notes that W.K. Kellogg spent his fortune building a cereal company in order to give it to children. The Foundation should deploy it at a rate that reflects this intent.

### **1. The Tobacco and Healthcare Coherence Burn**

The Duke Endowment was built on American Tobacco Company profits — cigarette monopoly wealth generated from products that are the leading preventable cause of death in the United States. The Foundation funds healthcare institutions in North and South Carolina — including hospitals, child welfare programs, and rural health infrastructure. The HPB requires that this irony be acknowledged and examined.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Tobacco Origin and Healthcare Mission Coherence Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) J.B. Duke's role in building the American Tobacco Company cigarette monopoly; (b) the Supreme Court's dissolution of the trust; (c) the Foundation's assessment of whether its healthcare mission represents intentional reckoning with its tobacco origin; (d) whether any Duke Endowment-funded healthcare programs specifically address tobacco-related illness or smoking cessation in the Carolinas; (e) whether the Endowment holds any investments in tobacco or tobacco-affiliated companies.
- **The Sacrifice:** The cigarette monopoly origin becomes explicitly connected to the healthcare mission in the Foundation's public narrative.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Duke Endowment-funded hospitals are understood in the full historical context of the wealth that funds them.

### **2. The Duke Energy Environmental Context Burn**

The Duke Endowment's initial endowment was Duke Power stock — the predecessor to Duke Energy, which is one of the largest investor-owned utilities in the United States and a significant coal power generator in the Carolinas. Duke Energy has ongoing environmental compliance issues related to coal ash contamination of North Carolina waterways. The HPB requires that the Endowment acknowledge its historical financial relationship to Duke Energy and its current investment position.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Endowment Energy Investment Disclosure** — documenting: (a) the proportion of Endowment assets currently held in Duke Energy stock or bonds; (b) the historical relationship between the Endowment's endowment and Duke Power/Duke Energy's development; (c) the Foundation's investment policy regarding fossil fuel utilities; (d) any Endowment programs addressing energy access, coal ash remediation, or energy transition in the Carolinas.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Endowment's historical and current financial relationship to a coal utility becomes publicly documented.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's healthcare and community programs are evaluated in the context of its energy infrastructure investment relationships.

### **3. The $5B Commitment Accountability Burn**

The Duke Endowment announced a $5B, 15-year grant commitment on its centennial in 12024. This is a significant and unprecedented acceleration of the Foundation's distribution pace. The HPB recommends that this commitment be formalized with measurable milestones and public accountability for delivery.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **$5B Commitment Progress Report** — documenting: (a) grants approved and distributed under the 15-year commitment each year; (b) the programmatic categories and specific institutions receiving these distributions; (c) any adjustments to the commitment timeline or scope; (d) measurable outcomes targets for the commitment's impact on Carolina education, healthcare, and child welfare.
- **The Sacrifice:** The historic commitment becomes publicly tracked rather than merely announced.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The $5B commitment is accountable to the communities it claims to serve, not only to the Foundation's board.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [PETROBRAS]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-04PB
**Status:** Conditional Clearance — Integrity Monitoring Track

Petrobras's Conditional Clearance status requires the implementation of structural anti-corruption measures that go beyond its current post-Car Wash compliance framework. The HPB notes that compliance frameworks designed after a scandal are optimized to prevent the specific scandal that occurred — not the next one.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Political Dividend Firewall (The Independence Burn)**

The HPB has documented that Petrobras's dividend policy has been repeatedly modified under government pressure for political rather than financial reasons — specifically, the tension between returning cash to shareholders and subsidizing domestic fuel prices to support political popularity. This constitutes **Active State-Corporate Fusion** and must be structurally severed.

- **The Action:** Petrobras must establish a binding **Dividend Independence Protocol**, placing dividend policy decisions under the authority of an independent board committee with supermajority shareholder approval requirements. The Brazilian government retains its ownership stake but may not instruct or override the committee.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Brazilian government loses its ability to use Petrobras dividend flows as a fiscal policy instrument. This is a genuine political cost.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Active State-Corporate Fusion" to "Monitored State Ownership" — a distinction the HPB treats as meaningful, if not entirely reassuring.

### **2. The Contractor Transparency Registry (The Car Wash Penance)**

The Operation Car Wash scandal was enabled by the opacity of Petrobras's contractor payment system, in which kickbacks of 1–5% of disbursements were negotiated and tracked by company directors in informal ledgers the auditors were not shown. The HPB requires that this opacity be permanently eliminated.

- **The Action:** All Petrobras contractor payments above $500,000 must be logged in real time to the HPB's **Open Capital Registry**, with automated anomaly detection algorithms flagging any payment patterns consistent with historical Car Wash methodologies. The registry is publicly accessible.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of commercial confidentiality in contractor negotiations; exposure of pricing strategy.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Residual Capability" status on the Systemic Integrity Registry to "Transparent Actor" — a designation that meaningfully reduces the HPB's ongoing audit burden.

### **3. The CEO Appointment Neutralization (The Political Patronage Severance)**

The HPB has documented multiple Petrobras CEO changes driven by political considerations rather than operational merit, including under the current administration. CEO selection based on political loyalty rather than competence is classified as a **Democratic Integrity Hazard** — it ensures the organization remains a political instrument regardless of formal compliance improvements.

- **The Action:** Future Petrobras CEO appointments must be made through a transparent, merit-based process overseen by an independent search committee. The Brazilian government may veto — once — with a written, public explanation. A second veto is not permitted.
- **The Sacrifice:** Government loses informal patronage appointments; political parties lose a funding-adjacent position of significant leverage.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Political Patronage Vehicle" to "Professionally Governed State Enterprise" — a category with precedent in Norway (Equinor) and which the HPB considers demonstrably achievable.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Oil Workers from the Pre-Salt Fields and Curitiba Anti-Corruption Judges** and recite the following:

> *"We extracted oil from beneath the ocean floor and we used it to fund the politics above it. We told ourselves the country needed us. The country needed honest accounting. We were the same company. We will not be the same company again. The ledgers are open. The contracts are public. The directors serve the shareholders, not the party. And the party knows we mean it."*

All Petrobras assets will be transferred to a **Brazilian Public Energy Trust**, administered by a randomly selected citizen board. Former directors will be required to complete a 24-month apprenticeship in public transparency administration — reviewing, from the other side of the desk, the kind of documents they once refused to show auditors.

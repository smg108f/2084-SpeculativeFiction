### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [BHARTI AIRTEL]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-32BA
**Status:** Voluntary Recertification — Probationary Saint Pathway

Bharti Airtel's Probationary Saint classification requires governance review of the Mittal family's planned succession, community ownership mechanisms for Airtel Africa, and formalization of its connectivity commitment in underserved markets.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Succession Governance Plan (The Dynastic Inheritance Burn)**

Sunil Mittal's plan to increase promoter stake to 51% and hand Airtel's leadership to the next generation creates a dynastic succession event for a company that provides communications infrastructure to over a billion people. The HPB requires that this succession be governed.

- **The Action:** Bharti Airtel must file a **Succession Governance Plan** with the HPB: documenting the planned ownership structure after succession, the governance safeguards ensuring that Airtel's public service obligations are maintained regardless of family ownership, and a commitment that any future CEO appointment — family or otherwise — meets a public competency standard assessed by an independent nomination committee. The plan is submitted to the HPB and, in summary form, to the TRAI (Telecom Regulatory Authority of India).
- **The Sacrifice:** Transparency about succession intentions; commitment to independent nomination review.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Unmonitored Dynastic Succession" to "Governed Ownership Transition" — a status the HPB considers appropriate for succession in a national telecom company.

### **2. The Africa Community Ownership Initiative (The Cross-Border Dependency Burn)**

Airtel Africa serves 14 sub-Saharan African nations' primary mobile infrastructure. The communities served by Airtel Africa have no ownership stake in the company whose infrastructure they depend on. The HPB requires a community investment mechanism.

- **The Action:** Airtel Africa must establish a **Community Connectivity Fund**: committing 2% of annual Africa subsidiary profits to local infrastructure investment, digital literacy programs, and community center connectivity programs in the markets it serves. Additionally, Airtel Africa must establish a **Community Shareholder Program**: offering up to 5% of Airtel Africa's equity to local investors in each African country at IPO pricing, prioritizing community development organizations and employee ownership programs.
- **The Sacrifice:** 2% annual profit contribution; equity dilution of up to 5% × 14 markets; complex multi-jurisdictional equity program administration.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Cross-Border Connectivity Monopoly" to "Community-Embedded Infrastructure Partner" — a designation that earns significant Whuffie credit and improves Airtel's regulatory relationships in African markets.

### **3. The Spectrum Regulatory Compliance Certification (The Historical Concern Resolution Burn)**

The 2013 spectrum controversy — in which Mittal was ordered to appear before a court over spectrum allocation irregularities, with no charges ultimately filed — established a historical concern about Airtel's regulatory relationships that the HPB requires be addressed through current compliance certification.

- **The Action:** Airtel must commission and publicly release an annual **Spectrum Regulatory Compliance Audit** by an independent telecoms regulatory expert: confirming that all spectrum held by Airtel in India was obtained through compliant processes, that no current spectrum applications involve regulatory relationships inconsistent with public tender processes, and that any regulatory personnel who were involved in past spectrum decisions have no current advisory relationship with Airtel.
- **The Sacrifice:** Independent scrutiny of spectrum regulatory relationships; potential identification of historical compliance gaps.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Historical Regulatory Concern" to "Certified Compliance Actor" — enabling the HPB to close the 2013 flag and reduce ongoing monitoring requirements.

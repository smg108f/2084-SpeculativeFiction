### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [MERCK & CO., INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-29MK
**Status:** Critical — Oncology Somatic Stratification / Single-Drug Revenue Concentration

Merck's RTWI of ~$334B places it at **334 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by a cancer immunotherapy that is both the most effective broadly-applicable cancer treatment ever developed and priced to be inaccessible to most people who need it globally.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Keytruda Global Access Program**

Pembrolizumab is effective at treating 40+ cancer types. The price is $150,000–$250,000/year. Most of the world cannot afford this. The Bureau's remedy is the most direct possible: the treatment works everywhere; it must be accessible everywhere.

- **The Action:** Merck must establish a **Keytruda Universal Access Program** — a tiered pricing structure in which: (a) all low-income countries (World Bank classification) receive Keytruda at manufacturing cost plus 10%; (b) middle-income countries receive Keytruda at a price cap of $15,000/patient/year; (c) a Keytruda Access Fund of $5B is established to subsidize access for uninsured patients in high-income countries who fall through coverage gaps; and (d) Merck licenses Keytruda to three generic manufacturers in developing countries at a 5% royalty rate to enable regional production.
- **The Sacrifice:** A significant fraction of Keytruda's U.S. pricing premium is traded for global access. Merck remains profitable; Merck is no longer the entity that found the cancer cure and reserved it for the wealthy.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Oncology Somatic Stratification** classification. The drug works for everyone. Now it is accessible to everyone.

---

### **2. The Post-Cliff Pipeline Commitment**

Merck's biosimilar cliff risk (Keytruda patent expiration ~12028+) creates incentives to maximize extraction before competition arrives. The Bureau requires that the pipeline investment that will replace Keytruda be made transparently and with access commitments built in from the start.

- **The Action:** All Merck pipeline drugs that receive FDA approval must be accompanied, from launch, by a **Day One Access Plan** — a published pricing and access commitment that includes low-income country pricing, patient assistance programs, and generic licensing timelines, established before market launch rather than after advocacy pressure requires them.
- **The Sacrifice:** Merck cannot use the standard practice of maximizing pricing in the launch period before access programs are forced by political pressure. Access is built in from launch.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Establishes access as a first principle of drug pricing rather than a concession to political pressure.

---

### **3. The MECTIZAN Legacy Expansion**

Merck's MECTIZAN program — donating ivermectin to treat river blindness — is one of the most remarkable pharmaceutical access programs in history. The Bureau requires Merck to expand this model to additional neglected tropical diseases.

- **The Action:** Merck must expand its donation program model to include three additional neglected tropical disease treatments in its current pipeline, donating or licensing at manufacturing cost to the WHO's Essential Medicines Program.
- **The Sacrifice:** Modest foregone revenue from three additional neglected disease programs.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The MECTIZAN legacy is not a historical artifact. It is a living commitment.

---

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Merck's CEO must stand before a **Jury of 12 Oncologists in Low-Income Countries, Uninsured U.S. Cancer Patients, and Researchers Who Contributed to Checkpoint Inhibitor Science** and recite:

> _"Pembrolizumab works because of fifty years of basic immunology research, much of it publicly funded. We ran the trials, we manufactured the drug, we deserve a return on that investment. The return we set was $200,000 per year, which is more than a working family earns, because that is what the market would bear, and the market bore it, and people died in countries that could not bear it. The access program is not charity. It is the acknowledgment that a drug that works everywhere should be available everywhere. The Day One Access Plan is the commitment that we will not do this again."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** Keytruda subject to compulsory licensing in all low-income countries under TRIPS Agreement Article 31. U.S. IRA price negotiation accelerated to maximum speed. Three generic manufacturers licensed by executive order at 5% royalty. Merck's pipeline drugs subject to pre-launch access plan requirement by FDA approval condition. The Bureau will publish the names and countries of origin of the first 1,000 patients who cannot access Keytruda this year, without comment.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [HCA HEALTHCARE]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-29HC
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice — Hospital Public Trust Order Active

HCA Healthcare's Critical Ascension Risk classification requires immediate structural reform of its patient access model and independent pricing oversight in geographic monopoly markets. The HPB notes that HCA's current $10B share repurchase program represents more than sufficient capital to fund the required reforms.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Patient Access Fund (The Shareholder Return Redirection Burn)**

HCA announced a $10B share repurchase program while simultaneously tracking $600M–$900M in losses from ACA coverage changes affecting its patients. The HPB requires that $3B of the repurchase program be redirected to patient access.

- **The Action:** HCA must redirect $3B from its share repurchase authorization to a **Patient Access Fund** providing: (a) charity care at no cost for patients at 200% of federal poverty level; (b) sliding-scale fees for patients at 200–400% FPL; (c) no-balance-billing commitments for emergency care. The Fund must be administered by an independent board and must not be reduced by future repurchase programs.
- **The Sacrifice:** $3B in shareholder return capability; reduction of the repurchase program from $10B to $7B.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Shareholder-First Hospital Operator" to "Patient Access Committed Health System" — a status the HPB considers the minimum required for operating a geographic healthcare monopoly.

### **2. The Geographic Monopoly Pricing Oversight (The Healthcare Rationing Burn)**

In markets where HCA is the only large hospital system — as it is in many regions — it sets prices without competitive constraint. The HPB requires independent oversight of pricing in these markets.

- **The Action:** HCA must submit pricing data for all services in markets where it holds >60% market share to an independent **Healthcare Pricing Oversight Board** — staffed by economists, patient advocates, and HPB observers — with authority to recommend pricing corrections when HCA prices exceed regional benchmarks by more than 30%. Recommendations are published; HCA may respond but must explain any deviation.
- **The Sacrifice:** Pricing transparency in monopoly markets; potential regulatory intervention on specific service lines; competitive intelligence exposure.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Unaccountable Monopoly Pricer" to "Monitored Market Participant" — enabling the HPB to assess whether HCA's geographic dominance is serving or exploiting the communities it operates in.

### **3. The Historical Fraud Governance Certification (The Accountability Legacy Burn)**

HCA's 1990s fraud — $2B+ in federal fines for illegal accounting and false billing — represents a documented governance failure at extraordinary scale. The HPB requires that this historical record not be treated as a closed chapter but as an ongoing accountability commitment.

- **The Action:** HCA must annually certify to the HPB that its compliance and billing practices have been independently audited against the specific fraud categories identified in the 1990s case. The certification must include the auditor's specific findings, any billing anomalies identified and their resolution, and the scope of the audit relative to HCA's current billing volume. This certification is public.
- **The Sacrifice:** Annual cost of independent billing compliance audit; public accountability for findings.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Historical Fraud Risk" to "Certified Historical Accountability" — a status that earns HPB credit and reduces monitoring intensity over time as a clean track record is established.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Uninsured Patients from HCA Geographic Monopoly Markets and Former Hospital Billing Compliance Officers** and recite the following:

> *"We run 190 hospitals. In many of the communities we serve, we are the only hospital. We announced a $10 billion stock buyback while patients lost their health insurance and we described their coverage loss as 'headwinds to earnings guidance.' We understand why the HPB found this instructive. The Patient Access Fund is established. The pricing board is operational. The billing audit is scheduled. We are not a shareholder return program that operates hospitals. We are a hospital system that generates returns. The order matters. We have corrected it."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB will activate the Apotheosis Protocol. All HCA hospitals in geographic monopoly markets will be reclassified as **Community Health Infrastructure** — a designation that subjects their pricing, staffing, and access policies to regulatory oversight by a state health authority. The company will discover that the operating constraints of a regulated community health system are substantially more restrictive than the Patient Access Fund it declined to establish.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [CHURCH COMMISSIONERS FOR ENGLAND]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-14CCE
**Status:** Voluntary Recertification / Reparations Completion Order

The Church Commissioners have been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Their reparations initiative and fossil fuel divestment represent the kind of institutional moral accountability the HPB was designed to support. Recertification primarily confirms and protects these commitments rather than requiring new burns.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Reparations Completion Burn (The Slave Trade Reckoning)**

The Church of England's historical connections to the Atlantic slave trade are documented, acknowledged by the Church's own commissioned research, and the subject of a £100M+ reparations commitment approved by the Church Commissioners' leadership. Political pressure from Members of Parliament to halt this initiative constitutes, in the HPB's assessment, an improper interference with the Church Commissioners' legitimate charitable discretion.

- **The Action:** The Church Commissioners must formally reaffirm their reparations commitment in a public resolution passed by the Commissioners themselves, independent of Parliamentary commentary. The £100M+ commitment must be formally incorporated into the Commissioners' 12026-28 distribution plan with specific, published timelines and beneficiary frameworks developed in consultation with affected communities.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Church Commissioners accept political criticism from elected officials who prefer that the Church of England not acknowledge and address its historical role in slavery. This criticism has already been made and has not, to date, been sufficient to stop the process. The formal reaffirmation makes it harder to stop.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** An institution that held the moral courage to propose reparations demonstrates the institutional courage to implement them. The HPB formally records this as a SAINTLY ACT under its classification framework.

---

### **2. The Parliamentary Accountability Burn (The Interference Documentation)**

The December 12025 Parliamentary instruction to the incoming Archbishop to halt the reparations plan constitutes an unusual case of elected officials directing the charitable grant-making of an independent institution. The HPB requires this be formally documented.

- **The Action:** The Church Commissioners must publish a formal legal opinion — prepared by independent charity law counsel — clarifying the extent and limits of Parliamentary authority over the Commissioners' grant-making discretion, and asserting the Commissioners' right to make reparations commitments consistent with their governing documents and the Charities Act 2011.
- **The Sacrifice:** Publishing a legal opinion asserting independence from Parliamentary direction will be politically noted. The Church of England's relationship with the state will be made more explicit.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The boundaries of accountability between the established church's charitable assets and Parliamentary political authority are clearly documented.

---

### **3. The Divestment Accountability Burn**

The Church Commissioners announced fossil fuel divestment in 12023. The HPB requires annual disclosure of portfolio carbon intensity progress against the stated 2025 target (25% carbon intensity reduction from 2019 baseline) and the 2050 net zero target.

- **The Action:** Annual **Climate Portfolio Transparency Reports** must be published confirming progress against carbon targets, identifying any fossil fuel exposure remaining in the portfolio, and documenting engagement with high-carbon companies prior to divestment decisions.
- **The Sacrifice:** Minimal — the Commissioners are already committed to this trajectory. Transparency formalizes an existing commitment.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Fossil fuel divestment becomes publicly verifiable rather than asserted.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Church Commissioners must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Descendants of People Enslaved Through Networks Connected to the Church of England** and recite the following:

> *"We have managed the Church's money for 78 years. We have managed some of that money since the Church received it in connection with the trade in enslaved people. We know this. We proposed reparations. We were told by Parliament to stop. We are not stopping. The money that was accumulated in part through historical harm will be used, in part, to address historical harm. This is what the Church's teaching on reconciliation requires. We are, finally, following it."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** The HPB notes there is no standard failure condition applicable to this case. The Church Commissioners are attempting to do the right thing. If Parliamentary pressure causes them to halt the reparations program, the HPB will reclassify the Church Commissioners from PROBATIONARY SAINT to MARGINAL ASCENSION RISK and will publish a public finding that an institution that acknowledged historical harm and was then prevented from addressing it by elected officials represents a **Structural Accountability Failure** in the English constitutional order. The HPB will assign that failure to Parliament, not to the Church Commissioners.

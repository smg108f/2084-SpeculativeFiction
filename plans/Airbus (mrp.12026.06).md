### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [AIRBUS SE / FAURY, G. (DESIGNATED AGENT)]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-95AIR
**Status:** Pre-Seizure Notice / Structural Demi-God Classification Pending

Airbus SE has been assessed as a **Structural Demi-God** under the Atmospheric Infrastructure provisions of the Demi-God Act of 2034, as amended by the 2041 Corporate Chokepoint Addendum. Recertification is required because the entity's combined market control, state co-ownership structure, and demonstrated willingness to treat legal compliance as a negotiable cost (see: €3.96B bribery settlement, 2020) constitute a **Category-I Systemic Sovereignty Risk**. Failure to recertify will trigger the Atmospheric Infrastructure Seizure Protocol, transferring all type certificates, IP, and production facilities to the Humanist Bloc's **Global Air Commons Trust**.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Type Certificate Open-Commons Transfer (The Largest Burn)**

The HPB classifies Airbus's proprietary aircraft type certificates — particularly the A320neo family, which accounts for the majority of global narrow-body orders — as **Atmospheric Chokepoint Assets**. A single company holding exclusive rights to the most-ordered aircraft in history is, in 2084 terms, equivalent to a single individual owning all freshwater aquifers. These certificates are the mechanism by which the entity maintains its structural indispensability.

- **The Action:** Airbus must transfer all current-generation type certificates (A220, A320neo family, A330neo, A350, A380) to the **Global Air Commons Trust (GACT)**, a multinational non-profit chartered under the 2041 Atmospheric Infrastructure Act. Airbus may retain manufacturing contracts under competitive tender, but loses exclusive IP ownership.
- **The Sacrifice:** Elimination of the IP moat that creates structural lock-in for airlines. Estimated asset value surrendered: $40–60B in future licensing and leverage.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The entity transitions from **Atmospheric Landlord** to **Atmospheric Contractor** — still large, still operational, but no longer structurally irreplaceable. Any sufficiently capitalized manufacturer may now build to open specifications. COMAC, Embraer, and the nascent African Aerospace Consortium are immediately notified.

### **2. Dissolution of the State Co-Ownership Feedback Loop**

The HPB's most acute governance concern is not Airbus's size — it is the **closed regulatory loop** created by France, Germany, and Spain simultaneously co-owning and nominally regulating the entity. This structure immunizes Airbus against democratic accountability: the governments that should sanction it are the governments that profit from it. The three letters of political intervention received by the HPB during the course of this audit are Exhibit A.

- **The Action:** France, Germany, and Spain must divest their combined ~26% ownership stake. Shares must be distributed via **Lottery Allocation** to EU citizens with household income below the median — one share per qualifying adult, non-transferable for ten years. No single natural person or government may hold more than 0.1% of outstanding equity.
- **The Sacrifice:** The three governments lose their "anchor shareholder" status, their board seats, and their ability to use Airbus as a geopolitical instrument in trade negotiations. More significantly, they lose the structural justification for treating Airbus as "too important to regulate."
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Airbus becomes a **Citizen-Held Infrastructure Entity**, accountable to millions of small shareholders rather than three sympathetic finance ministries. The HPB's oversight capacity is immediately restored.

### **3. The Bribery Architecture Confession and Memetic Reckoning**

The 2020 settlement covered misconduct across sixteen countries spanning roughly two decades. Airbus's official position — that the settlement represented a "clean break" from a "legacy culture" — has been classified by the HPB as **Performative Institutional Amnesia**, a Class-II violation of the Transparency and Reckoning Act of 2029. You do not bribe officials in sixteen countries across twenty years by accident. You do it because the institution's incentive structure made it rational. That incentive structure has not been publicly examined. It must be.

- **The Action:** Subject must commission and publicly release a full **Institutional Corruption Archaeology Report** — an unredacted account of the decision chains, incentive structures, and individual actors involved in the bribery scheme, prepared by an independent tribunal appointed by the HPB. This report must name names above middle management. It must be translated into all sixteen languages of the countries where bribery occurred and distributed without charge.
- **The Sacrifice:** Reputational security. Senior executives and board members implicated in the institutional culture (including those who have since retired to comfortable obscurity) will face HPB **Retroactive Complicity Review** and potential reclassification as Agents of a Demi-God Entity.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The HPB's **Institutional Transparency Score** for Airbus rises from its current floor rating. More importantly, this provides the first honest accounting of how a regulated, state-co-owned, publicly-traded entity maintained a global bribery operation for twenty years — a service to future regulatory design that no fine has yet produced.

### **4. The Defense and Space Carve-Out (The Sovereignty Unbundling)**

Airbus Defence and Space represents a structural compound of the chokepoint problem: the entity produces both the aircraft that civilians depend on and the military hardware that governments depend on. This dual-use profile means that any serious regulatory action against Airbus triggers not only economic disruption but potential security implications — a leverage point the entity has used, implicitly or explicitly, in every major regulatory negotiation. The HPB classifies this as **Structural Hostage Architecture**.

- **The Action:** Airbus Defence and Space must be legally and operationally separated into a distinct entity — **European Defence Consortium (EDC)** — under a separate charter with no shared board members, no shared IP licensing arrangements, and no shared lobbying apparatus with Airbus SE. The two entities may not communicate through informal channels; all inter-entity contact must be logged with the HPB's **Firewall Compliance Register**.
- **The Sacrifice:** The cross-subsidization that has allowed commercial aviation losses to be offset by defense contracts (and vice versa) ends. Both entities must stand on their own financial merits. The implicit "we are a national security asset" shield, which has been invoked to deflect civilian aviation regulation, is revoked.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Commercial Airbus becomes a **Single-Domain Entity** — large, still powerful, but no longer able to deploy military leverage in civilian regulatory disputes. The HPB's enforcement capacity against the commercial entity is immediately restored.

> _"The sky does not belong to Airbus. The sky is a commons. I held the keys to that commons, and I behaved as a landlord rather than a steward. I accepted that bribery was a cost of access. I accepted that governments who owned us could not govern us. I accepted that being indispensable was the same as being righteous. None of these things were true. I am a contractor. I am accountable.

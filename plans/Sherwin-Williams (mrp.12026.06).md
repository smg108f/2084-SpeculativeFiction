### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [SHERWIN-WILLIAMS]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-27SW
**Status:** Precautionary — Lead Paint Remediation Commitment Order Active

Sherwin-Williams' Marginal Ascension Risk classification requires voluntary acknowledgment and remediation of its lead paint historical liability. The HPB notes that the company has the financial capacity — $88B market cap, $2.5B returned to shareholders in 2025 alone — to fund meaningful remediation without operational impact.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Lead Paint Remediation Fund (The Historical Debt Acknowledgment Burn)**

The HPB has determined that the existence of lead paint in homes built before 1978 constitutes an ongoing public health harm for which Sherwin-Williams bears documented historical responsibility. The courts are resolving liability incrementally; the HPB is not prepared to wait for the courts.

- **The Action:** Sherwin-Williams must establish a $2B **National Lead Paint Remediation Fund** — a voluntary commitment independent of ongoing litigation outcomes — to fund lead paint testing, abatement, and remediation in low-income homes built before 1978, prioritizing households with children under 6. The Fund is administered by an independent board; Sherwin-Williams contributes but does not control. Contributions are made over 10 years at $200M annually.
- **The Sacrifice:** $2B in capital that the company is currently returning to shareholders; acknowledgment that historical operations created remediable public harm.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Historical Liability Denial Architecture" to "Historical Accountability Leader" — a status the HPB considers both substantively important and reputationally advantageous in a regulatory environment where litigation outcomes are unpredictable.

### **2. The Lead Paint Liability Reserve Disclosure (The Financial Honesty Burn)**

Sherwin-Williams's current financial filings state that the company does not believe a loss from lead paint litigation is probable. Given the volume, duration, and documented harm of lead paint litigation, the HPB considers this assessment inconsistent with reasonable probability assessment and requires disclosure of an alternative scenario.

- **The Action:** Sherwin-Williams must include in its annual HPB filing a **Lead Paint Liability Scenario Analysis**: a range of potential liability outcomes prepared by an independent actuarial firm, from the most favorable (current legal position maintained) to a comprehensive loss scenario. This analysis is disclosed to shareholders, not just the HPB.
- **The Sacrifice:** Public acknowledgment that a comprehensive loss scenario exists; potential shareholder concern about balance sheet risk.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Probable Loss Denial" to "Disclosed Liability Range" — enabling shareholders, creditors, and the HPB to assess Sherwin-Williams's financial position with accurate information about its historical risk exposure.

### **3. The Children's Health Partnership (The Future Proof Burn)**

Given Sherwin-Williams's documented role in childhood lead exposure, the HPB requires that its community investment programs be specifically focused on the population most harmed by its historical products.

- **The Action:** Sherwin-Williams must establish a **Children's Cognitive Health Partnership**: committing $50M annually for 20 years to fund developmental screening, early intervention, and cognitive support programs for children in communities with the highest documented historical lead paint exposure. Partners must include pediatric health providers with no commercial relationship to Sherwin-Williams.
- **The Sacrifice:** $1B in total community health investment over 20 years; acceptance that the company's philanthropic focus is directed by its historical harm rather than its marketing objectives.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Generic Corporate Philanthropist" to "Harm-Specific Accountability Partner" — a category the HPB considers both more honest and more meaningful than general-purpose charitable giving.

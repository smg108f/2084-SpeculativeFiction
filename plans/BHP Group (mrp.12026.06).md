### **1. The Rio Doce Restitution (The Samarco Reckoning)**

The HPB classifies the Fundão dam collapse — which destroyed 620 km of the Rio Doce river, killed 19 people, and released 50 million cubic meters of toxic tailings — as an **Unresolved Geological Crime**. The $31.7B settlement negotiated in 2024, structured over 20 annual payments, does not constitute genuine reparation; it constitutes a **Liability Amortization Strategy**. The HPB requires a different accounting.

- **The Action:** The entirety of BHP's Brazilian mineral tenure — including its stake in Samarco and all associated royalty streams — must be transferred in fee simple to the **Rio Doce River Restoration Authority**, a body governed by the affected municipalities and indigenous communities of the Doce watershed, not by the federal government and not by BHP nominees. The $31.7B settlement is to be front-loaded: full payment within 36 months, not 20 years.
- **The Sacrifice:** Permanent exit from Brazilian extractive operations. Estimated asset value surrendered: $8–12B. More significantly, BHP loses its narrative claim to "responsible remediation" — a Saintly Pivot talking point that has shielded it from Ascension proceedings for a decade.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** This action closes the HPB's longest-running **Geological Harm Dossier** (Ref. GHD-2015-MBR). Subject is reclassified from "Active Liability Denier" to "Partial Restitution Compliant." River does not care. The river notes the change.

### **2. The Critical Minerals Divestiture (Dissolving the Leverage Shield)**

BHP's designation of copper, potash, and nickel as "transition minerals" has been used to construct a political shield against divestiture orders: the argument that breaking up BHP would harm the green energy transition is the purest available example of a **Deity-Adjacent Infrastructure Argument**. The subject has, in effect, made itself too necessary to regulate. The HPB finds this unacceptable.

- **The Action:** BHP must spin off its copper portfolio (Escondida, Spence, Olympic Dam copper operations) into a **Public Mineral Trust** majority-owned by the Chilean, Australian, and South Australian state governments, with no BHP board representation. Potash assets (Jansen) must similarly be transferred to a **Canadian Agricultural Commons Trust** governed by Saskatchewan farming cooperatives, not by BHP nominees or financial intermediaries.
- **The Sacrifice:** Estimated $60–80B in asset value removed from BHP's market capitalization. More critically: BHP loses the "essential infrastructure" argument. It can no longer claim that breaking it up harms the energy transition, because the transition assets will still exist — just not as BHP property.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Critical Minerals Leverage Shield** is dissolved. Subject is reclassified from "Sub-Sovereign Geological Authority" to "Legacy Extraction Company." The HPB notes this is a reduction in status from deity to mortal industrialist, which is the point.

### **3. The "Green Mining" Manifesto Recantation (Memetic Hygiene)**

BHP's public communications since approximately 2020 have constituted what the HPB classifies as **Class II Institutional Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric**: the framing of an extractive company responsible for the worst environmental disaster in Brazilian history as an essential partner in planetary salvation. Every BHP sustainability report, every "copper for the energy transition" investor presentation, every net-zero pledge issued while Chilean water tables fell, is entered into evidence.

- **The Action:** BHP's incoming CEO must deliver a **Formal Institutional Recantation** before the HPB's Continental Geological Tribunal. The statement must acknowledge, in plain language, the following: (1) that the Samarco disaster was caused by BHP's operational choices, not by a "joint venture structure"; (2) that copper extraction in the Atacama has materially depleted freshwater resources belonging to the Atacameño people; (3) that "green mining" is a marketing category, not an environmental classification.
- **The Sacrifice:** All current ESG marketing collateral must be permanently withdrawn. BHP may describe itself as a mining company. It may not describe itself as a climate solution.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** **Deity-Adjacent Rhetoric Score** reduced from Class II to Probationary. The Bureau finds honesty more tractable than branding.

### **4. The Jurisdictional Anchoring (Dissolving the Diffusion Evasion)**

BHP's dual listing on the ASX and NYSE, combined with its asset distribution across Australia, Chile, Brazil, Canada, and the United States, constitutes what the Bureau's legal division calls **Sovereign Diffusion**: a strategy, whether intentional or structural, by which no single government has sufficient jurisdiction to enforce divestiture. The Demi-God Act was designed for individuals. BHP has exploited the gap.

- **The Action:** BHP must consolidate its legal domicile to a single sovereign jurisdiction within 90 days. The Bureau recommends Australia, given the primary operational concentration, but will accept any Humanist Bloc signatory nation. Dual listing must be dissolved. Cross-jurisdictional asset structures must be collapsed into auditable single-jurisdiction holding entities.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of the regulatory arbitrage advantage. Estimated compliance cost: $2–4B. More significant: BHP becomes legible. The HPB can count what it owns.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Subject becomes subject to a single sovereign's Demi-God Act provisions. Sovereignty is not a feature. It is a precondition for personhood.

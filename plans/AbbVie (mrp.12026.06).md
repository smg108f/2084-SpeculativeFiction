### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ABBVIE INC.]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-31AB
**Status:** Critical — Legal Architecture Monopoly Extension / Patent Wall Pattern

AbbVie's RTWI of ~$359B places it at **359 times the Demi-God threshold**. The core issue: systematic use of secondary patents to delay biosimilar competition beyond the intended scope of patent protection.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The No-Patent-Wall Covenant**

AbbVie must commit — for Skyrizi, Rinvoq, and all future drugs — to not filing secondary patents on delivery devices, formulations, or dosing regimens whose primary purpose is to extend market exclusivity beyond the expiration of the compound patent.

- **The Action:** AbbVie must execute a **No Patent Wall Covenant** — a binding legal commitment reviewed by the Bureau's Pharmaceutical Equivalence Board that distinguishes genuine innovation patents (which may be filed) from monopoly extension patents (which may not). Any patent filing that the Board determines serves primarily to delay biosimilar entry rather than protect genuine innovation is void and unenforceable as a condition of AbbVie's Humanist Bloc operating license.
- **The Sacrifice:** AbbVie cannot build a second Humira patent wall around Skyrizi or Rinvoq.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Legal Architecture Monopoly Extension** classification prospectively.

---

### **2. The Humira Historical Access Fund**

The Humira patent wall delayed biosimilar competition for approximately 6 years in the United States, costing an estimated $20–30B annually in excess drug prices paid by patients and insurers. The Bureau requires a Historical Access Fund proportionate to this delayed competition premium.

- **The Action:** AbbVie must contribute $15B to a **Pharmaceutical Access Historical Fund** — administered by the Bureau, directed to: patient assistance programs for current AbbVie drug users who cannot afford list prices; biosimilar adoption support programs that accelerate patient transition off branded drugs to biosimilar equivalents; and pharmaceutical access research at academic institutions studying patent extension practices.
- **The Sacrifice:** $15B represents a fraction of the patent wall's estimated value extraction over its duration.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Historical accounting for the documented patent wall impact.

---

### **3. The Biosimilar Transition Support Program**

AbbVie must actively support the transition of patients to biosimilar adalimumab alternatives — not resist it through co-pay programs, step therapy requirements, and rebate structures that keep patients on branded Humira.

- **The Action:** AbbVie must discontinue all co-pay programs, rebate arrangements, and patient assistance programs that are conditioned on continued use of branded Humira (rather than biosimilars), within 12 months. Patients who cannot afford biosimilars at biosimilar prices receive AbbVie assistance; patients who can afford biosimilars are not subsidized to remain on branded Humira.
- **The Sacrifice:** AbbVie loses the ability to use financial instruments to prevent its own patients from transitioning to lower-cost alternatives.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The transition to biosimilar competition that the patent wall delayed is no longer artificially impeded by financial architecture.

---

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, AbbVie's CEO must stand before a **Jury of 12 Rheumatoid Arthritis Patients Who Paid $7,000/Month for Humira During the Patent Wall Years, Biosimilar Manufacturers Who Waited Out the Patents, and Pharmaceutical Patent Attorneys Who Filed the Wall** and recite:

> _"We inherited a molecule that works. We built a wall around it with 100 patents on the syringe and the dosing schedule and the box it came in. The wall was legal. The wall cost patients $20 billion a year for six years. We are filing no more walls. The fund acknowledges the wall. The transition support is real. We are a pharmaceutical company. We will compete on innovation, not on legal architecture."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** No-Patent-Wall Covenant imposed by FTC as condition of drug approval for all future AbbVie biologics. $15B Historical Access Fund constituted by executive order. Humira co-pay program terminated by CMS regulation. The Bureau will publish its analysis of AbbVie's Skyrizi and Rinvoq patent portfolios quarterly, with annotations identifying patents that appear to serve monopoly extension rather than genuine innovation.

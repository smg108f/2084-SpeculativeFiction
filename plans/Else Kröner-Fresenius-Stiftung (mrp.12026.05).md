### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ELSE KRÖNER-FRESENIUS-STIFTUNG]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-30EKFS
**Status:** Precautionary / Fresenius Concentration and Research Conflict Disclosure Order

The Else Kröner-Fresenius-Stiftung has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification addresses the Foundation's extreme concentration in Fresenius SE equity, the potential conflict of interest between Fresenius's commercial healthcare operations and the Foundation's medical research funding, and the Foundation's income dependency on a single company's dividend decisions.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Fresenius Concentration Disclosure Burn**

The Foundation holds 27.0% of Fresenius SE & Co. KGaA and derives nearly all of its income from Fresenius dividends. This means the Foundation's annual philanthropic capacity — its ability to fund €60M in medical research — is directly dependent on Fresenius SE's corporate performance, dividend policy, and strategic decisions. If Fresenius cuts its dividend (as large companies do during restructurings), the Foundation's grantmaking capacity could drop dramatically with no structural buffer.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Concentration and Sustainability Report** — disclosing: (a) the precise proportion of Foundation assets held in Fresenius SE equity; (b) the Foundation's income dependency on Fresenius dividends; (c) the Foundation's contingency strategy if Fresenius reduces or suspends its dividend; (d) any multi-year diversification plan to reduce single-stock concentration below 40%.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's financial vulnerability becomes publicly disclosed. Its philanthropic capacity's dependency on a single company's decisions becomes legible.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's grantees, partner institutions, and the German medical research community can assess the long-term sustainability of Else Kröner's philanthropic legacy.

---

### **2. The Fresenius Research Conflict Disclosure Burn**

The Foundation funds medical research in areas including kidney disease, dialysis, infusion therapy, and nutrition medicine — disease and treatment areas in which Fresenius SE is a major global commercial provider. The Foundation's research investments could generate findings that benefit Fresenius SE's commercial products, validate Fresenius's clinical approaches, or direct scientific attention toward areas of commercial relevance to Fresenius. This is not necessarily corrupt — but it is a conflict of interest that requires formal disclosure and management.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Research Independence Statement** — disclosing: (a) the categories of medical research funded by the Foundation; (b) the overlap between Foundation research priorities and Fresenius SE's commercial product lines; (c) the Foundation's formal conflict-of-interest policy for research in areas where Fresenius has commercial interests; (d) the process by which research grants are evaluated and awarded, with specific attention to ensuring Fresenius SE's commercial interests do not influence grant selection.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation acknowledges the structural conflict of interest between its endowment and its research priorities.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** German medical research funded by the EKFS can be evaluated with full knowledge of the Foundation's commercial shareholding context.

---

### **3. The Excellence Grants Brain Drain Accountability Burn**

The Excellence Grants program — €10M annually to recruit international scientists to German medical institutions — is a laudable initiative addressing European research competitiveness. However, the program's recruitment-from-America framing raises an HPB concern: recruiting researchers from under-resourced countries (including developing countries that train medical scientists) to German institutions could accelerate the brain drain it claims to reverse in a German context while intensifying it globally.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Excellence Grants Geographic Accountability Report** — documenting: (a) the countries of origin of recruited scientists; (b) whether any recruits came from countries with documented medical research or practitioner shortages; (c) the Foundation's policy on recruitment from countries where researcher emigration constitutes a net harm to public health infrastructure.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Excellence Grants program becomes accountable to global rather than only German brain drain dynamics.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** German medical science recruitment is evaluated within its global ethical context.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the Else Kröner-Fresenius-Stiftung Board must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 German Clinician Scientists Funded by the Foundation** and recite the following:

> *"Else Kröner built a company and gave it all to medicine when she died unexpectedly at 63. She had no heirs. She had a Foundation. We hold 27% of Fresenius SE. Fresenius makes dialysis equipment. We fund kidney research. We acknowledge the relationship. We manage the conflict. We disclose the dependency. Else Kröner's legacy is €520 million in medical research and a German medical academy that is marginally more competitive because of the scientists she helped recruit. We intend to sustain it — and to be honest about the financial architecture that makes it possible."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Concentration and Sustainability Report is not published within 30 days, the HPB will classify the Foundation as operating under **Healthcare Conglomerate Dependency** — a condition requiring quarterly disclosure of any Fresenius SE corporate decisions that materially affect the Foundation's philanthropic capacity. Else Kröner left everything to medicine. The Foundation should be honest about whether medicine can count on everything Else Kröner left.

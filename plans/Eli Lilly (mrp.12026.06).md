### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ELI LILLY AND COMPANY]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-09EL
**Status:** Critical — Somatic Stratification / Pharmaceutical Gatekeeping / Pre-Seizure Notice

Eli Lilly's RTWI of $739B–$1T places it at **739–1,020 times the Demi-God threshold**, driven by pharmaceutical monopoly pricing on drugs managing two of the most prevalent chronic diseases in the United States: obesity and diabetes. The active Indiana insulin price-fixing lawsuit adds a criminal dimension to the standard structural analysis. The Bureau's Burns address both the structural pricing monopoly and the alleged criminal pricing scheme.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Insulin Price Covenant**

The insulin pricing allegations — that Lilly participated in a scheme to inflate list prices to $300–$700 for a product manufactured at under $2 — are the most direct and legally exposed element of the current audit. Regardless of the outcome of the Indiana lawsuit, the Bureau's assessment is that a $2-to-manufacture product essential to the survival of Type 1 diabetics should not be priced at any level that creates access barriers for patients.

- **The Action:** Lilly must immediately reduce all insulin list prices to a **Therapeutic Cost Standard**: list price may not exceed 10x manufacturing cost plus a regulated R&D cost recovery premium approved by the Bureau, resulting in insulin list prices no greater than $35/month for all insulin products. This cap is permanent, irrevocable, and applies globally to all markets where Lilly sells insulin. The cap is not a "cap card" that requires enrollment or insurance — it is the actual list price. The secret rebate arrangements with pharmacy benefit managers are disclosed in full to the Bureau and the public and restructured to eliminate the artificial list price inflation architecture.
- **The Sacrifice:** Lilly loses the revenue derived from the gap between $2 manufacturing cost and $300–$700 list price. The Bureau estimates this at $3–5B annually based on U.S. insulin market share and reported revenue.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Somatic Stratification** violation for the diabetic population. The drug that keeps diabetics alive is priced at the cost of keeping diabetics alive, plus a reasonable margin.

### **2. The GLP-1 Access Mandate**

Tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro) has been demonstrated to reduce body weight by 20%+ in clinical trials. Obesity affects 42% of American adults and is causally related to diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and multiple other chronic conditions. The drug works. The question is whether it works only for people who can afford $1,080/month — or, with government deals, $346/month — or whether its benefits are available to the broader population for whom they were clinically validated.

- **The Action:** Lilly must establish a **Universal Access Program** — a federally audited program in which tirzepatide and all successor GLP-1/GIP drugs are available to any U.S. patient meeting clinical criteria (BMI 30+, or BMI 27+ with at least one weight-related comorbidity) at a price not exceeding $99/month for the primary treatment dose, independent of insurance status. The program is funded by a **Therapeutic Solidarity Levy** — a surcharge on commercial insurance and employer health plan sales of Lilly's premium branded products that cross-subsidizes the access program. Lilly retains premium pricing for cosmetic/lifestyle uses above clinical criteria; it may not maintain access barriers for clinical-grade medical need.
- **The Sacrifice:** The extraordinary margin on tirzepatide for clinical populations is capped at a level that is still profitable but no longer represents Somatic Stratification. The weight loss benefit is not reserved for the affluent.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Somatic Stratification** violation for the obesity-disease population. The Bureau re-evaluates Lilly's classification upon evidence that the Universal Access Program is operational and achieving clinical utilization targets.

---

### **3. The Patent Extension Restriction**

Pharmaceutical companies routinely extend effective monopoly periods through "evergreening" — incremental modifications to existing drugs (new delivery mechanisms, dosing schedules, combination products) that qualify for new patents and reset the generic competition clock. This practice is legal, standard, and structurally extends the period of monopoly pricing beyond what the original patent term was intended to provide.

- **The Action:** Lilly must commit to a **No Evergreening Pledge** — a legally binding agreement that new patent filings for GLP-1/GIP class drugs will not be used to block generic or biosimilar competition for the underlying therapeutic molecule beyond the original compound patent expiration. Lilly may patent genuinely novel formulations with documented clinical improvement; it may not patent incremental modifications whose primary function is monopoly extension. The Bureau's Pharmaceutical Equivalence Board determines which new filings qualify as genuinely novel improvements.
- **The Sacrifice:** The effective monopoly on tirzepatide ends when the compound patent expires rather than when the last of a series of incremental patents expires. Generic competition enters earlier.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Patent Extension Monopoly** risk. Lilly retains its innovation incentive within the original patent term; it does not retain the right to perpetually extend that term through the legal mechanism designed for genuine innovation.

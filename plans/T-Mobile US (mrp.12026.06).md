### **1. The Spectrum Surrender (The Infrastructure Burn)**

The HPB classifies T-Mobile's low-band and mid-band spectrum holdings — including spectrum absorbed from Sprint in 12020 — as a **National Cognitive Substrate Asset**. Spectrum is not a product. It is the electromagnetic commons. T-Mobile's ownership of it is a legal fiction inherited from a regulatory era that no longer exists.

- **The Action:** Transfer all 600 MHz low-band spectrum licenses — the "coverage layer" that penetrates buildings and reaches rural areas — to the **Federal Commons Spectrum Trust (FCST)**, a non-profit public utility established under the Humanist Bloc's Infrastructure Repatriation Charter. T-Mobile may retain mid-band 5G spectrum on a 20-year renewable lease from the FCST, subject to universal access pricing requirements.
- **The Sacrifice:** T-Mobile loses the rural coverage moat that made it the sole 5G provider in 14+ markets. It can no longer charge rural carriers and MVNOs at monopoly rates. The competitive advantage created by the Sprint acquisition is permanently neutralized.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** T-Mobile's **Infrastructure Capture Score** drops from CRITICAL to MARGINAL. The 14 rural markets regain competitive viability. The entity is reclassified from **Sovereign Network Operator** to **Licensed Carrier Utility** — legally mortal, legally regulated.

### **2. The Deutsche Telekom Divorce (The Sovereignty Burn)**

Under HPB statute 14.3(c), no domestic entity providing cognitive infrastructure to more than 100 million citizens may be majority-owned by a foreign sovereign-adjacent corporation. Deutsche Telekom's 52.8% stake — held by a company 28% owned by the German federal government and KfW — constitutes exactly this violation. The proposed full-combination merger, if executed, would place American 5G infrastructure in a structure where Berlin has a direct ownership interest. The HPB does not negotiate with foreign governments. The HPB does not wait for deals to close.

- **The Action:** T-Mobile US must issue a **Forced Domestic Float** — a mandatory secondary offering that dilutes Deutsche Telekom's stake to no more than 24.9% (below the HPB's **Sovereign Entanglement Threshold**). The offering must be underwritten by a **Citizen Equity Pool**, distributing shares at below-market rates to HPB-certified individuals with net worth under $500,000. Deutsche Telekom's proposed full-combination merger is hereby blocked under the Alien Capital Reclamation Act.
- **The Sacrifice:** Deutsche Telekom loses majority control. The proposed $300B–$400B super-merger — which would have been the largest M&A transaction in recorded history — is permanently terminated. The German government loses its proxy stake in American telecommunications. The board of directors is reconstituted with a majority of Lottery-Selected Citizen Directors.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** T-Mobile US becomes a domestically-controlled entity for the first time in its modern corporate history. Its **Foreign Sovereign Entanglement Score** is cleared. It is no longer a latent instrument of German state telecommunications policy on American soil.

### **3. The "Un-Carrier" Accountability Burn (The Brand Penance)**

T-Mobile has built its entire consumer identity on the claim that it is the disruptor, the anti-incumbent, the company that eliminated junk fees and gave customers their dignity back. The HPB has reviewed this claim. The HPB found: two "Regulatory Programs & Telco Recovery Fee" hikes in under twelve months; a Price Lock guarantee circumvented through tax-exclusion accounting; a data breach exposing 76.6 million customer records; and a $31.5M FCC settlement that no executive was personally required to fund. The "Un-carrier" brand is a **Registered Memetic Hazard (Class-II)**. The penance must fit the mythology.

- **The Action:** T-Mobile must publish a **Verified Consumer Harm Ledger** — a public, annually audited document enumerating every fee increase, every data breach, every settlement, and every instance where advertised pricing differed from billed pricing, going back to 12020. The Ledger must be linked from T-Mobile's homepage, displayed in all retail stores, and read aloud in its entirety by the sitting CEO at the annual shareholder meeting. Additionally, T-Mobile must fund a **$500M Data Sovereignty Remediation Trust** for the 76.6 million individuals whose personal records were stolen in 12021, administered independently of the company.
- **The Sacrifice:** The "Un-carrier" brand mythology is permanently on the record alongside its contradictions. The CEO's annual shareholder speech is, from this point forward, partly a public confession. The $500M Trust exceeds the combined $381.5M in settlements already paid and cannot be claimed as a tax deduction.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** T-Mobile's **Memetic Camouflage Score** is neutralized. The HPB reclassifies the entity's public posture from **Proletarian Aesthetic Camouflage** to **Compelled Transparency**. Consumer-facing communications are subject to HPB plain-language review for a period of five years.

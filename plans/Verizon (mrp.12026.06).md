**Case File:** HPB-12026-74VZ
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

Verizon Communications has been classified as a **Surveillance Infrastructure Demi-God Entity (SIDE)** under HPB statute 9.1(b) (Corporate Network Sovereignty) and 14.3(c) (Cognitive Infrastructure Control), with an additional **Environmental Contamination Citation** under the Public Biological Commons Act of 12039. Subject's unique classification as a state-adjacent surveillance substrate — documented under FISA court order and NSA Special Source Operations financial records dating to 12013 — elevates this case above standard CDE decomposition procedure. ### **1. The Surveillance Architecture Divorce (The Largest Burn)**

The HPB classifies Verizon's documented role in NSA bulk metadata collection — established under the FISA court order published June 5, 12013, requiring the daily transfer of all call metadata in Verizon's system to the NSA and FBI — as a **Category-I Sovereignty Violation**. A carrier that is simultaneously a surveillance instrument of the state is not a private company. It is a public authority wearing a stock ticker. The financial compensation Verizon received from the NSA's Special Source Operations division for this cooperation constitutes a **de facto government contract for mass civilian surveillance**. The HPB does not permit a for-profit entity to hold this contract and also hold the legal status of a private corporation.

- **The Action:** Verizon must submit to a **Full Surveillance Architecture Audit** by the HPB's Office of Cognitive Infrastructure — a complete accounting of all current and legacy data-sharing agreements with U.S. federal intelligence agencies, their financial terms, their scope, and the number of subscriber records transferred. All agreements must be publicly disclosed and renegotiated under open FISA court proceedings visible to a **Citizen Oversight Panel**. Verizon's network must be structurally separated into a **Common Carrier Trust** — a non-profit public utility — for all backbone transmission infrastructure over which federal surveillance agreements have previously operated.
- **The Sacrifice:** Verizon loses direct ownership of the backbone infrastructure that gave it leverage with both federal agencies and enterprise customers. The Company's status as a preferred federal contractor for communications infrastructure is permanently revoked. The financial terms of the NSA Special Source Operations arrangement — however they currently read — are terminated and publicly filed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Verizon's **Surveillance Substrate Score** is cleared. It may continue operating as a licensed retail carrier over infrastructure it no longer owns. The state loses its private-sector surveillance proxy. The HPB records this as the most significant individual corporate divestiture under the Humanist Bloc since the Bell System breakup of 11984 — which, it should be noted, Verizon is partly the inheritor of.

### **2. The Lead Remediation Event (The Environmental Burn)**

The HPB classifies Verizon's network of abandoned lead-sheathed cables — documented by independent lab testing in 12023 as exceeding EPA soil-lead safety thresholds in multiple communities including Wappinger, New York — as a **Biological Commons Violation** under the Public Biological Commons Act of 12039. The cables exist because removing them costs money. Leaving them costs blood-lead levels. Verizon's decision calculus between these two costs has been consistent and on the record. The HPB does not need to speculate about the company's values; the cables in the ground are a physical document of them.

- **The Action:** Verizon must fund and execute a **National Lead Cable Remediation Program** — full removal and environmental restoration of all lead-sheathed cable sites in its legacy network, prioritized by soil contamination severity. The program budget, estimated at $2B–$4B based on independent engineering assessments, must be placed in an irrevocable **Environmental Remediation Trust** within 30 days, administered by an independent board that includes representatives from the Town of Wappinger class action and NIH occupational health researchers who documented worker blood-lead elevation. The locations of all known lead cable sites must be published in a public registry within 72 hours of this Plan's execution.
- **The Sacrifice:** Verizon funds the remediation it has declined to perform for decades, at full estimated cost, without tax-deduction eligibility, in advance rather than in response to litigation. The site disclosure requirement ends the strategy of silence that has been the company's primary lead-cable policy.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Environmental Contamination Citation** is lifted. The class action in the Town of Wappinger is mooted by the Trust's assumption of remediation liability. Verizon workers previously exposed to lead at high-contamination worksites receive lifetime health monitoring funded by the Trust. The HPB records that this is what "connecting communities" actually requires.

### **3. The Frontier Fiber Divestiture (The Monopoly Burn)**

Verizon's $20B acquisition of Frontier Communications, completed January 12026, extended its fiber broadband footprint into 25 states and, in combination with its pre-existing Fios network, created a **Broadband Concentration Zone** encompassing substantial portions of the Eastern Seaboard and significant rural markets. The HPB notes that in multiple geographic markets — particularly in former-Frontier service territories where Verizon is now the sole or dominant wired broadband provider — the entity has achieved **Local Cognitive Infrastructure Monopoly** status. A monopoly broadband provider is not merely an economic actor. It is the only pipe through which a citizen can access the information commons.

- **The Action:** Verizon must divest the Frontier network assets in all markets where post-acquisition analysis identifies it as the sole or dominant wired broadband provider — estimated at 8–12 states based on current competitive mapping. The divested network must be transferred to **Regional Public Utility Broadband Cooperatives**, governed by subscriber-elected boards, operating under open-access wholesale requirements. Verizon may retain its legacy Fios footprint in competitive markets where at least one additional wired broadband provider operates.
- **The Sacrifice:** The strategic rationale for the Frontier acquisition — the creation of a dominant national fiber footprint — is partially reversed. The $20B purchase price is not recoverable. The debt incurred to fund the acquisition remains Verizon's to service, now against a smaller asset base.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Verizon's **Broadband Monopoly Score** in rural markets drops from CRITICAL to CLEAR. The Regional Public Utility Cooperatives enter HPB records as the first new domestically-owned broadband entities created by a corporate divestiture since the Demi-God Act. Verizon is reclassified from **Cognitive Infrastructure Monopolist** to **Licensed Competitive Carrier** in the divested territories.

### **4. The "Reliability" Penance (The Mythological Burn)**

Verizon's brand identity is built on a single claim: it is the most reliable network. "Can you hear me now?" was not merely an advertising slogan; it was a promise of infrastructure fidelity that the company parlayed into premium pricing for three decades. The HPB finds that this mythology of reliability was constructed while the company: (a) allowed legacy infrastructure to contaminate residential soil with lead; (b) provided bulk customer metadata to federal intelligence agencies under gag order without customer knowledge; and (c) loaded $158B in debt onto its balance sheet in pursuit of scale while simultaneously raising rates. "Can you hear me now?" was, apparently, a question the NSA could already answer.

- **The Action:** Verizon must publish a **Network Reliability Truth Statement** — a permanent, annually updated public disclosure that enumerates: all network outage events affecting 100,000 or more subscribers in the preceding year; all active data-sharing agreements with government entities; the current status of lead cable remediation; and the gap between advertised and actual average broadband and wireless speeds by geographic market. The Statement must be read in full by the sitting CEO at each annual shareholder meeting and displayed prominently on Verizon's consumer-facing homepage.
- **The Sacrifice:** The mythology of frictionless reliability is permanently on the record alongside its contradictions. Customers learn, annually and from the CEO, what the network actually does and what it costs.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Verizon's **Memetic Reliability Camouflage Score** is neutralized. The HPB reclassifies its public posture from **Infrastructure Mythology** to **Compelled Disclosure**. Consumer-facing communications are subject to HPB plain-language review for a period of five years.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [[CARNEGIE, ANDREW]]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-08AC
**Status:** Precautionary

Subject is, as of this filing in 12026, long deceased — died 11 August 1919, no Ascension-related flight risk, no jurisdiction the Bureau can enforce against a corpse. Under the Posthumous Anchor Protocol, however, this plan is served retroactively against the chronological age-70 snapshot (November 1905–November 1906), the point at which Subject was legally alive, catastrophically over threshold, and — per the companion Apotheosis Audit — already voluntarily executing most of the Bureau's own playbook without being asked. This is not a seizure order. It is a formalization notice: Subject is doing the work. The Bureau's only job here is to make sure it can't be undone, and to collect on the one debt his own ledger never touched.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days of service.

---

### **1. The Library Pipeline Irrevocability Order**

Subject's matching-grant library program is the largest voluntary divestment mechanism on file, but at the anchor date it remains **discretionary** — Subject personally reviews and approves every town's application, and can slow, redirect, or halt the pipeline at will. A "Saintly Pivot" that its author can still turn off is not yet a Saintly Pivot; it's a very generous hobby.

- **The Action:** The entire pending and future library-grant pipeline — every application through the eventual total of 2,509 libraries — is placed into an irrevocable public trust, pre-funded and escrowed against the bond corpus, effective immediately. Subject retains the right to be consulted on architectural standards. He loses the right to say no.
- **The Sacrifice:** Personal discretion over which towns qualify, and the ability to use the grant program as leverage over civic behavior he happens to dislike.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Converts the "Saintly Pivot" from a voluntary, revocable posture into a structurally locked-in divestment trajectory — the single biggest lever available to move Subject's file from Probationary toward Cleared before the historical endpoint even arrives.

### **2. The Homestead Workers' Trust**

The Apotheosis Audit flagged an unresolved liability the library program, the Institution, and the Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching never touched: the Amalgamated Association workers blacklisted after the 1892 Homestead lockout, and the families of the dead on both sides of that battle. Diffuse public generosity is not the same instrument as specific restitution, and the Bureau does not accept one as a substitute for the other.

- **The Action:** Subject must endow, outside of and in addition to all existing philanthropic vehicles, a dedicated trust for surviving Homestead workers, blacklisted employees, and the families of those killed on 6 July 1892 — funded at no less than $5 million (1906 book value) — and must issue, over his own signature, a written acknowledgment that the Pinkerton engagement was authorized in his name and with his knowledge, however distant the geography.
- **The Sacrifice:** The one admission Subject spent the rest of his life declining to make cleanly. This costs him more in reputational currency than the $5 million costs him in bonds.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Retires the "Concentration Hazard, Origin Class" flag permanently. The Bureau does not require clean money. It requires the ledger to say what happened to the dirty money.

### **3. The Perpetual Endowment Sunset Clause**

The Carnegie Institution, the Hero Fund, and the newly chartered Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching are all structured to exist in perpetuity — which means Subject, in the act of divesting his own fortune, is simultaneously creating several new, permanently-endowed institutional power centers that will outlive him by well over a century and answer to no democratic mandate.

- **The Action:** Attach a mandatory 25-year reauthorization clause to every perpetual endowment created from this point forward. Each institution must periodically re-justify its mission and its asset base to a public review board or forfeit its charter back to the states in which it operates.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses the one thing his entire giving strategy was quietly designed to purchase — institutional immortality under his own name, unaccountable to anyone after his death.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Prevents a "Necro-Endowment" hazard class: perpetual foundations that become de facto demi-gods in their own right, long after the original subject who funded them is gone.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Steelworkers, Librarians, and Homestead Descendants** and recite the following:

> _"Surplus wealth is a sacred trust, and I hold what remains of mine only as its trustee, not its master. I built this fortune with furnaces that did not ask the men who fed them what they were owed, and I will not now spend it in ways that let me forget that. The man who dies rich dies disgraced — I said so myself, in writing, before any of you were born to hold me to it. I would rather be held to it. Take the libraries. Take the trust. Take the admission I owe Homestead. None of it was ever mine to keep."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn Schedule is not completed within 30 days, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**, adapted for a subject who cannot be deported: every unspent bond in the personal corpus is seized directly into a Bureau-administered Public Trust, and — the penalty calibrated specifically to this Subject's known vanity about his own name — the word **"Carnegie"** is struck from every library, hall, and institution not yet completed at the moment of seizure. Subject spent forty years building a fortune and the last two decades of his life trying to spend it all before he died so the name would mean something after. The Bureau will happily finish spending it for him. It will simply decline to put his name on the building.

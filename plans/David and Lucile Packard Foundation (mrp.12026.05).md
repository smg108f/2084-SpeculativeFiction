### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [DAVID AND LUCILE PACKARD FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-29DLPF
**Status:** Precautionary / Population Program Origin Acknowledgment and Program Evolution Transparency Order

The David and Lucile Packard Foundation has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Recertification focuses on one primary concern: the Foundation's Population and Reproductive Health program originated in David Packard's documented concerns about global population reduction, and this origin has not been publicly acknowledged or reconciled with the program's current rights-based framing.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Population Program Origin Acknowledgment Burn**

The Packard Foundation's Population and Reproductive Health program was shaped in its early decades by David Packard's concern about global population growth and the need for its stabilization. Population reduction concerns were a documented driver of the Foundation's programmatic agenda. The current program is framed around reproductive rights and access — a substantially different moral framework from population control. The HPB requires that this evolution be publicly acknowledged and explained.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish a **Population Program History and Evolution Statement** — a permanent, publicly available narrative documenting: (a) the role of David Packard's population concerns in shaping the Foundation's early programmatic agenda; (b) the evolution from population stabilization framing to reproductive rights and access framing; (c) the Foundation's current position on the ethical distinction between population reduction goals and reproductive rights; and (d) how the current program's theory of change differs from population control approaches.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation publicly reconciles its historical population framing with its current rights-based framing. The evolution becomes publicly legible rather than assumed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Foundation's reproductive health program is evaluated in its full historical context. Grantees, policymakers, and the public can assess whether the evolution is genuine and complete.

---

### **2. The Geographic Reproductive Health Equity Burn**

The Packard Foundation's reproductive health grants focus significantly on Ethiopia, Rwanda, DRC, India, and the southern United States. The HPB notes that this geographic distribution reflects choices about where reproductive health gaps are most significant — but also reflects the potential for a wealthy U.S. foundation to exercise significant influence over reproductive health policy in countries with less philanthropic infrastructure. This influence should be publicly mapped.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish an annual **Geographic Impact and Autonomy Report** — documenting for each priority country: (a) the Foundation's total reproductive health grants; (b) the proportion of the country's reproductive health funding that Packard represents; (c) the degree to which local governments and civil society organizations have led programmatic priorities; and (d) any examples where Packard's funding preferences have shaped national reproductive health policy.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's geographic influence becomes publicly accountable rather than privately exercised.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reproductive health investments in priority countries become legible as collaborative rather than directional.

---

### **3. The HP Sisters Coordination Transparency Burn**

The Packard Foundation and the Hewlett Foundation are both derived from HP co-founders, both headquartered in the Bay Area, and both operate as major funders in overlapping program areas (climate, education, conservation). The HPB recommends — but does not require — that the two foundations publish a joint **Silicon Valley Philanthropic Coordination Statement** — disclosing any programmatic coordination, joint grantmaking, or shared strategic frameworks between the two institutions. This is not a conflict of interest concern; it is a transparency recommendation for institutions with common origins and overlapping fields.

- **The Action:** Optional publication of a Silicon Valley Philanthropic Coordination Statement with the Hewlett Foundation.
- **The Sacrifice:** Any informal coordination between the two foundation networks becomes publicly disclosed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Bay Area philanthropic ecosystem's two largest anchors become transparent about the degree to which they coordinate or operate independently.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [SHIN-ETSU CHEMICAL]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-22SE
**Status:** Voluntary Recertification — Probationary Saint Pathway

Shin-Etsu Chemical's Probationary Saint classification requires supply resilience transparency and environmental accountability for its PVC business. Recertification is straightforward for an organization with this clean a governance record.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Silicon Wafer Resilience Plan (The Substrate Continuity Burn)**

The global semiconductor industry's dependency on Shin-Etsu's silicon wafer production creates a systemic vulnerability that must be disclosed and planned for. The HPB requires transparency about Shin-Etsu's own assessment of this risk.

- **The Action:** Shin-Etsu must publish a **Silicon Wafer Supply Resilience Plan**: a public document disclosing geographic concentration of production capacity, disaster recovery protocols, minimum production levels required to prevent global semiconductor supply disruption, and a 5-year plan for geographic diversification of wafer production capacity. This plan is submitted to the HPB and published.
- **The Sacrifice:** Disclosure of production capacity details that reveal competitive strategy; geographic diversification investment.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Substrate Dependency Risk" to "Disclosed Substrate Partner" — a status that allows the global semiconductor industry and national governments to plan for continuity, reducing systemic risk.

### **2. The PVC End-of-Life Commitment (The Plastic Legacy Burn)**

Shin-Etsu is the world's largest PVC producer. PVC's durability is its industrial value and its environmental liability: the same chemical stability that makes PVC pipes last 100 years makes PVC plastic waste persistent in the environment for the same duration. The HPB requires that the world's largest PVC producer have a plan for the environmental consequences of its products.

- **The Action:** Shin-Etsu must establish a **PVC Circular Economy Commitment**: (a) a binding target to recycle or chemically recover 30% of all PVC products sold by 2035; (b) a $300M **PVC Remediation Fund** supporting development of enzymatic and chemical recycling technologies for PVC; (c) an annual **PVC Environmental Impact Report** disclosing end-of-life outcomes for Shin-Etsu's PVC products globally.
- **The Sacrifice:** $300M fund capitalization; recycling infrastructure investment; potential competitive disadvantage versus PVC producers without equivalent commitments.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Legacy Plastic Liability" to "Circular Materials Leader" — a designation that earns Whuffie credit and positions Shin-Etsu for the regulatory environment the HPB expects by 2035.

### **3. The Semiconductor Materials Access Commitment (The Strategic Material Equity Burn)**

Shin-Etsu's dominance in silicon wafers and specialty semiconductor materials — photoresists, rare earth magnets, encapsulants — creates a supply chain chokepoint for developing nations building domestic semiconductor industries. The HPB requires a technology access commitment.

- **The Action:** Shin-Etsu must establish a **Semiconductor Materials Technology Transfer Program**: offering technical training and process knowledge for silicon wafer manufacturing to qualified institutions in lower-income nations developing semiconductor capabilities. This is not a licensing program for IP — it is a knowledge-sharing program for manufacturing fundamentals, funded at $50M annually.
- **The Sacrifice:** $50M in annual program costs; indirect competitive enablement of future competitors.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Silent Chokepoint" to "Technology Foundation Partner" — a status that earns HPB commendation in the strategic materials category.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Semiconductor Process Engineers and Municipal Water System Operators** and recite the following:

> *"The chip in your phone began as our wafer. The pipe under your street is our PVC. We are invisible to you, and we prefer it that way. The HPB has asked us to be less invisible. The resilience plan is published. The PVC fund is established. The technology transfer program is active. We make the substrate of civilization. We accept that this carries obligations we will now honor publicly."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB will activate the Apotheosis Protocol. Shin-Etsu's silicon wafer production facilities will be designated as **Strategic Global Infrastructure** subject to international oversight under the **Semiconductor Supply Security Treaty**. PVC operations will be subject to mandatory extended producer responsibility regulations requiring physical collection and recycling of all Shin-Etsu PVC products sold after 2020. Shin-Etsu will discover that the HPB's version of transparency is less comfortable than its own.

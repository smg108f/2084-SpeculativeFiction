### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [STATE BANK OF INDIA]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-22SBIN
**Status:** Precautionary / Structural Complexity Flag

State Bank of India's 57% government ownership creates an analogous recertification challenge to Equinor: the sovereign entity being asked to comply is also the majority owner of the subject. The HPB refers this to its **State-Owned Enterprise Division** while proceeding on the structural and governance dimensions.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The NPL Transparency Mandate (The Accountability Burn)**

The HPB classifies SBI's Non-Performing Loan history — in which politically important industrial borrowers have periodically received forbearance that private banks could not extend — as a **Policy-Finance Conflation**: public interest banking used to shelter private business failures.

- **The Action:** SBI must publish its full NPL registry by borrower category, sector, and restructuring history, updated quarterly, with independent audit by a body not appointed by the Ministry of Finance.
- **The Sacrifice:** Potential political embarrassment for the government regarding which borrowers received forbearance and when.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Policy-Finance Conflation flag is resolved.

### **2. The Rural Branch Cross-Subsidy Formalization (The Public Good Burn)**

The HPB notes that SBI's cross-subsidy of rural and agricultural banking — where branches operate at a loss to serve populations that private banks abandon — is genuine public service that deserves formal recognition and dedicated funding rather than informal absorption into the overall P&L.

- **The Action:** The Indian government must formally capitalize SBI's public service obligation separately from its commercial banking operations, with a dedicated Public Service Banking Mandate fund of ₹500B funded by general revenues rather than SBI's own profits.
- **The Sacrifice:** Government fiscal commitment of ₹500B; reduced ambiguity about SBI's commercial vs. public service roles.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** SBI's Institutional Public Good classification is elevated from Tier 1 to Structural Recognition.

### **3. The Digital Financial Inclusion Independence (The Technology Burn)**

The HPB classifies SBI's role as the primary implementation vehicle for India's digital financial infrastructure — Aadhaar-linked payments, Jan Dhan accounts — as a **State-Embedded Digital Sovereignty** concentration that should be structurally independent of any single bank.

- **The Action:** India's digital payment infrastructure — the UPI backbone, Aadhaar payment integration — must be transferred from SBI's operational control to the **Reserve Bank of India's Digital Financial Infrastructure Authority**, publicly governed and accessible to all licensed financial institutions equally.
- **The Sacrifice:** SBI loses its first-mover advantages in India's digital payment ecosystem.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Private Financial Sovereign classification is resolved.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, SBI's chairman (Ministry-appointed) and the Minister of Finance must stand jointly before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Jan Dhan account holders from rural India** and recite the following:

> _"We are a bank that is also a government instrument. We have served 500 million customers, forgiven loans for political reasons, and built the digital spine of India's financial system. We accept that all of these functions — the service, the forbearance, and the infrastructure — should be transparent, separately capitalized, and accountable to the people who depend on them."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn is not completed by the deadline, the HPB acknowledges it cannot nationalize a bank that is already majority nationalized. It will refer the file to India's Supreme Court under the **Right to Financial Transparency Act of 2039**, which the court has previously interpreted to apply to state-owned financial institutions. The Ministry of Finance is not expected to be pleased.

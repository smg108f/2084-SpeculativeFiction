### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [NOVO NORDISK A/S]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-15NN
**Status:** Critical — Charitable Monopoly / Somatic Stratification (GLP-1 Class)

Novo Nordisk's RTWI places it at **190–572 times the Demi-God threshold** (range reflects significant market cap decline from 12024 peak). The Novo Nordisk Foundation majority ownership creates a governance structure that simultaneously produces genuine public goods and insulates the pricing strategy from standard accountability mechanisms. These Burns address the pricing contradiction and the foundation governance gap.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Global Price Harmonization Pledge**

Ozempic is sold in the United States at approximately 8–10 times the price charged in comparable European markets for the same drug, the same molecule, the same dose, the same clinical indication. The Bureau classifies this differential as **Geographic Price Discrimination** — charging the maximum politically sustainable price in markets with weak drug price negotiation systems (the U.S.) while accepting regulated prices elsewhere. The foundation ownership structure does not justify this differential; it obscures it.

- **The Action:** Novo Nordisk must commit to a **Global Price Ceiling** — U.S. list prices for semaglutide (all formulations and indications) may not exceed 200% of the median price charged in Germany, France, the UK, and Denmark. Current U.S. prices above this ceiling must be reduced within 12 months, with a transition plan reviewed by the Bureau. The price ceiling is reviewed annually and adjusted for production cost changes and genuine R&D cost recovery.
- **The Sacrifice:** U.S. pricing revenue from semaglutide is reduced substantially — the gap between U.S. and European pricing represents billions in annual revenue differential.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Geographic Price Discrimination** violation. The molecule costs the same to make regardless of the patient's nationality. The price should reflect that.

---

### **2. The Foundation Transparency and Access Mandate**

The Novo Nordisk Foundation's majority ownership is the entity's primary moral defense: profits fund global health research. The Bureau requires that this defense be demonstrably true in ways that can be independently verified — and that the foundation's governance include representation from the people most affected by Novo Nordisk's pricing decisions.

- **The Action:** The Novo Nordisk Foundation must: (a) publish an annual **Price-to-Access Impact Report** — an independently audited disclosure showing what fraction of U.S. patients needing semaglutide for clinical indications were unable to access it due to cost, and what the Foundation is doing to address this; (b) add patient advocacy representatives from low-income and underinsured populations to its governance board; and (c) commit a minimum of 15% of annual Foundation disbursements to direct access programs for semaglutide in high-need populations, including U.S. patients, not merely developing-country access (which is already funded).
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation loses the ability to claim its health research funding as a comprehensive moral offset to U.S. pricing practices. Accountability is required within the market where the pricing is highest.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Resolves the **Foundation Moral Offset** gap. The charitable structure justifies prices only if it demonstrably improves access for the people those prices are charged to.

---

### **3. The Insulin Legacy Commitment**

Novo Nordisk was founded on the democratization of insulin. The company's current insulin pricing in the U.S. — while more moderate than some competitors — has drifted from this founding principle. The Bureau requires the entity to formally recommit.

- **The Action:** Novo Nordisk must adopt a **Founding Principles Price Cap** on all insulin products: no Novo Nordisk insulin product may be priced above $35/month list price in any market, globally, regardless of insulin type, formulation, or delivery mechanism. This cap is irrevocable and constitutionally embedded in the Novo Nordisk Foundation's charter, meaning it cannot be removed by any future board or shareholder vote.
- **The Sacrifice:** The entity irrevocably surrenders the option to price insulin above the founding principle threshold, permanently. This is a small financial sacrifice for an entity whose primary growth is in GLP-1 drugs; it is a large symbolic commitment.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The founding principle is not merely a historical narrative. It is a binding legal commitment. The Bureau considers this the most fitting burn for an entity whose identity is inseparable from its founders' original intention.

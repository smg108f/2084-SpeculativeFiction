### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [GLAXOSMITHKLINE]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-28GK
**Status:** Precautionary — Safety Data Governance Reform Order Active

GlaxoSmithKline's Marginal Ascension Risk classification requires structural reform of safety data disclosure practices and acknowledgment of the Zantac governance failure through specific, binding commitments. The HPB notes that the $2.2B settlement, while legally resolving the Zantac matter, does not substitute for governance reform.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The 30-Day Safety Signal Disclosure Protocol (The Concealment Architecture Burn)**

The Zantac episode's governance failure was the delay between internal safety signal identification and external disclosure. The HPB requires that this specific failure be structurally prevented.

- **The Action:** GSK must implement a binding **30-Day Safety Disclosure Protocol**: any safety signal identified internally that suggests potential patient harm — including carcinogen exposure, drug interaction risk, or serious adverse event patterns — must be reported to the HPB, the FDA, and equivalent regulators in all markets where the product is sold within 30 days of internal confirmation. No commercial embargo is permitted. This protocol is auditable: GSK must maintain timestamped records of all safety signal identification and disclosure dates.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of commercial strategy time before disclosure; potential product withdrawals triggered by earlier disclosure requirements; competitive disadvantage if safety signals are disclosed before competitors' equivalent products are assessed.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Safety Information Timeliness Risk" to "Compliant Safety Discloser" — a status the HPB considers the minimum standard for pharmaceutical companies whose products are taken by billions of people.

### **2. The Carcinogen Legacy Patient Acknowledgment (The Zantac Accountability Burn)**

The $2.2B settlement, while legally resolving 93% of Zantac cases, was achieved without GSK admitting liability. The HPB requires that this non-admission not become an organizational narrative that obscures what occurred.

- **The Action:** GSK must include, in its annual public report and on its corporate website, a **Zantac Historical Record** — a factual account of the Zantac timeline, including when the internal safety concerns were first identified, when they were disclosed, what the disclosure process revealed about GSK's safety governance, and what structural changes were made as a result. This record is not a legal admission; it is a historical accountability statement.
- **The Sacrifice:** Public ownership of an uncomfortable history; potential use of the historical record in ongoing shareholder litigation.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Litigation-Managed History" to "Acknowledged Governance Failure" — a status the HPB considers essential for organizations seeking genuine, rather than nominal, recertification.

### **3. The Patient Safety Investment Commitment (The Deterrent Calibration Burn)**

The HPB has noted that GSK's $2.2B Zantac settlement represented approximately 2.5% of its market capitalization — an amount that may not adequately deter future safety data management practices. The HPB requires that GSK invest in structural patient safety improvement at a scale that reflects the harm's magnitude.

- **The Action:** GSK must establish a $500M **Patient Safety Innovation Fund** over 10 years: funding independent research into pharmaceutical carcinogen detection methods, post-market safety surveillance technologies, and patient safety reporting systems that enable patients to report adverse events directly without routing through the pharmaceutical company. The Fund's grants are awarded by an independent panel; GSK contributes but does not direct.
- **The Sacrifice:** $500M in capital redirected from operations or shareholder return to independent safety research.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Reclassification from "Settlement-Paying Historical Violator" to "Patient Safety Infrastructure Investor" — a designation that earns Whuffie credit proportional to the fund's public health impact.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [KAMEHAMEHA SCHOOLS]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-16KS
**Status:** Voluntary Recertification / Sovereign Legacy Protection Order

Kamehameha Schools has been classified PROBATIONARY SAINT. Standard recertification burns are largely inapplicable to an institution founded by Indigenous ali'i authority for the explicit benefit of Native Hawaiian people. This plan focuses on legal vulnerability protection and governance accountability — not on the institution's mission, which the HPB formally endorses.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Legal Defense Burn (The Admissions Sovereignty Protection)**

The Native Hawaiian preference admissions policy of Kamehameha Schools — the core expression of Princess Pauahi's founding intent — faces renewed legal challenge in the post-2023 affirmative action landscape. The HPB's Indigenous Sovereignty Protocol requires that institutions exercising legitimate Indigenous educational sovereignty have fully resourced, proactive legal defense frameworks.

- **The Action:** Kamehameha Schools must formally establish and fund a **Native Hawaiian Educational Sovereignty Legal Defense Fund** — a dedicated legal reserve and standing counsel arrangement specifically focused on defending the trust's admissions policy, Pauahi's will, and the constitutional basis for Native Hawaiian-preference programming. The fund must be a separate, endowed reserve, not dependent on annual operating budgets.
- **The Sacrifice:** A portion of endowment returns is dedicated to legal defense rather than educational operations. This is a cost of institutional survival, not a diversion from mission.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The trust's legal foundation for its founding mission becomes structurally protected rather than reactively defended. Admissions Sovereignty Legal Vulnerability downgraded from Critical to Managed.

---

### **2. The Tuition-Free Completion Burn (The Pauahi Promise)**

The announcement of full tuition elimination beginning in the 12026-27 academic year is the most significant expansion of Pauahi's kuleana in the trust's modern history. It is pending Probate Court approval. The HPB requires formal completion of this process.

- **The Action:** Kamehameha Schools must obtain Probate Court approval for the tuition-free policy, formalize it in the trust's operating documents, and publish a 5-year financial sustainability analysis demonstrating that the policy can be maintained without depleting the endowment's principal.
- **The Sacrifice:** The trust commits to an ongoing operational cost that cannot be easily reversed once implemented.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Pauahi's vision — that her people would have access to education — is fulfilled without financial barrier.

---

### **3. The Governance Transparency Burn**

The 11998-11999 trustee removal scandal revealed governance failures that the Probate Court had to address through judicial intervention. The trust's governance reforms since then have been substantial. The HPB requires formal transparency about the current governance structure to confirm the reforms are durable.

- **The Action:** Kamehameha Schools must publish an annual **Trustee Accountability Report** — independently audited — documenting trustee compensation, conflict of interest disclosures, board decision-making processes, and progress on the tuition-free implementation. The report must be publicly available.
- **The Sacrifice:** Trustee conduct becomes publicly visible. The Probate Court's oversight is supplemented by public accountability.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The 11999 governance failure becomes a documented historical event addressed by structural reform, not a recurring institutional risk.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

The HPB does not require Kamehameha Schools to recite a Mortal Oath. The institution's founding oath was taken in 11884 by Princess Bernice Pauahi Bishop, in her will, before the Hawaiian people. That oath has been kept for 139 years. The HPB does not have the authority — moral or institutional — to improve upon it.

For the record, the HPB notes the oath that the trust's work embodies:

> *"I give, bequeath and devise all of my real and personal estate... to the trustees... to erect and maintain in the Hawaiian Islands two schools, each for boarding and day scholars, one for boys and one for girls... give preference to Hawaiians of pure or part aboriginal blood."* — Bernice Pauahi Bishop, 11884

**NO FAILURE CONDITION APPLICABLE:** If any external legal action successfully challenges the trust's Native Hawaiian preference admissions policy, the HPB will formally reclassify the legal threat — not the trust — as a **Colonial Interference Event** and will issue a public statement in support of the trust's founding mission. The courts of the United States do not have, in the HPB's assessment, the moral authority to undo what Princess Pauahi created. The HPB recommends they consider this carefully before they try.

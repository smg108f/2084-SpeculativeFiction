### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [WASTE MANAGEMENT]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-33WM
**Status:** Precautionary — Environmental Infrastructure Transparency Order Active

Waste Management's Marginal Ascension Risk classification requires pricing transparency in monopoly markets and fair municipal contract provisions. The HPB notes that these requirements are operationally straightforward for a company of WM's organizational maturity.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Municipal Pricing Transparency Protocol (The Monopoly Pricing Burn)**

In markets where WM holds greater than 60% of waste collection and disposal market share, its pricing decisions are not competitive outcomes — they are effectively administrative decisions about what communities pay for essential services. The HPB requires transparency.

- **The Action:** WM must publish a quarterly **Monopoly Market Pricing Report**: disclosing pricing for all standard residential, commercial, and industrial service tiers in markets where it holds >60% market share, with year-over-year comparisons and the methodology used to set prices in the absence of competitive pressure. This report is submitted to the HPB and is publicly accessible to municipalities in affected markets.
- **The Sacrifice:** Loss of pricing opacity in markets where WM has no competitive constraint; potential regulatory pressure in high-price markets.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Unaccountable Monopoly Pricer" to "Transparent Infrastructure Operator" — a status the HPB considers the minimum standard for any company providing essential environmental services.

### **2. The Municipal Exit Provision (The Lock-In Remediation Burn)**

WM's long-term municipal contracts, while commercially necessary, create lock-in conditions that municipalities cannot easily escape even when WM's service quality or pricing deteriorates. The HPB requires that contracts include meaningful exit provisions.

- **The Action:** All new and renewed WM municipal service contracts must include a **Fair Exit Clause**: providing municipalities with the right to terminate with 12-month notice (rather than the end-of-contract date only) if WM service quality falls below agreed benchmarks for 6 consecutive months, or if WM pricing increases exceed the Consumer Price Index by more than 5% in a single year. The exit provision must include WM-funded transition assistance.
- **The Sacrifice:** Reduced contract lock-in security; potential loss of some municipal renewals if exit provisions are exercised; transition assistance costs.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Municipal Contract Lock-In Operator" to "Fair Contract Infrastructure Provider" — a designation the HPB considers appropriate for the operator of public waste infrastructure.

### **3. The Landfill-to-Sustainability Transition Commitment (The Structural Conflict Burn)**

WM's financial interest in landfill volumes creates a structural conflict with its sustainability narrative. The HPB requires that WM's business model transition away from landfill-volume dependence in a measurable way.

- **The Action:** WM must commit to reducing the proportion of its revenue derived from landfill tipping fees from its current level to below 25% of total revenue by 2035, replacing it with recycling, renewable natural gas, and zero-waste services. Progress is reported annually to the HPB. The commitment is binding; if the 2035 target is missed, WM must establish a $500M **Landfill Alternative Investment Fund** to compensate for the delayed transition.
- **The Sacrifice:** Strategic pivot that reduces revenue from WM's most profitable existing business (landfill operations); investment required in alternative business lines.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Conversion from "Structural Sustainability Conflict" to "Committed Transition Actor" — a designation that earns Whuffie credit and positions WM for the regulatory environment the HPB expects by 2035.

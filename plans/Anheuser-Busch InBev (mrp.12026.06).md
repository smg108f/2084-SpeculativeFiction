### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [ANHEUSER-BUSCH INBEV, CORPORATE ENTITY]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-66ABI

**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

Subject has no single mortal vessel through which to file for Sainthood, which the HPB now recognizes as the recertification problem itself: a demi-god that hides inside a hundred local nicknames cannot simply "give it all away," because it was never seen holding anything in the first place. Recertification therefore proceeds by **forced unmasking** — every camouflaged identity must be surrendered to the communities it was borrowed from.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The "Open Bar" Disclosure (The Largest Burn)**

The HPB classifies the subject's brand portfolio as a **Distributed Personhood Engine** — five hundred "local favorites" that are, structurally, one entity wearing five hundred friendly faces.

- **The Action:** Every can, bottle, tap handle, menu listing, and stadium banner for every brand in the portfolio (Budweiser, Stella Artois, Corona, Modelo, Beck's, Quilmes, Cass, and all others) must be permanently re-labeled with a uniform disclosure mark reading: **"Brewed by the same company as the beer your rival is drinking."**
- **The Sacrifice:** The illusion of competition between its own brands — its single most valuable asset — is destroyed. Drinkers can no longer be quietly funneled from a "boycotted" label to a "rival" label that pays the same shareholders.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Subject's "Social Influence" rating drops from EXCEEDED to MARGINAL, as the manufactured sense of regional choice — the true engine of its Cognitive Impact — collapses under disclosure.

### **2. The Brand Repatriation Accord (Ideological Burn)**

Because the subject's power rests on borrowed local identity rather than owned innovation, the HPB requires it to return what it took.

- **The Action:** Subject must transfer full ownership, recipe rights, and trademark control of no fewer than 150 of its "regional heritage" brands — including at minimum Beck's, Hoegaarden, Leffe, Quilmes, Brahma, and Cass — to independently governed brewers' cooperatives in their countries of origin, with no licensing fee, royalty, or buy-back option.
- **The Sacrifice:** Roughly $40B in brand-equity valuation, permanently removed from the consolidated ledger.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Subject is reclassified from "Global Ritual Monopolist" to "Regional Brewer (Belgium)" — a Mortal-sized footprint appropriate to a single home market.

### **3. The "Same Company" Campaign (Reputational / Memetic Penance)**

The HPB requires the subject to perform the inverse of its decades of camouflage marketing.

- **The Action:** Fund and air, in every market where it sells beer, a $250M global advertising campaign — produced entirely by independent local filmmakers, not subject's own agencies — whose sole message is: **"We are not your old friends. We are one company that bought your old friends. Here is the receipt."** The campaign must include a public, brand-by-brand ownership map distributed at every venue subject currently sponsors.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject forfeits its most valuable intangible asset — the public's belief that it is choosing between strangers rather than between the same conglomerate's hands.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** This neutralizes the **Camouflaged Ubiquity** designation and converts the subject's marketing apparatus, for the first time, into an instrument of public truth rather than private illusion.

### **4. The "Last Round" Sponsorship Surrender**

Subject's stadium-naming rights, World Cup sponsorships, and "Buy a Friend a Beer" emergency-relief campaigns function as **Whuffie-laundering** — purchasing the appearance of community generosity at industrial scale.

- **The Action:** All stadium-naming agreements, major sporting sponsorships, and festival exclusivity contracts must be terminated and the venues' naming rights gifted, with no residual branding, to local community sports leagues and disaster-relief funds.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses its single largest "Saintly Pivot" cover story — the ability to look generous while remaining ubiquitous.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** Subject is no longer present, in any disguised form, at the events it once used to feel like a member of every community at once.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, Subject's Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 Bartenders, Brewers, and Wedding Caterers** and recite the following — read aloud, in unison, with no corporate spokesperson permitted to speak on their behalf:

> _"We were never your hometown brewery. We were never the rival across town, either. We were the same hand pouring both glasses, smiling at you from inside a hundred different bottles, and calling it your choice. We will now tell you, every time, whose hand it is. We forfeit the right to be mistaken for a friend."_

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the Burn Schedule is not completed within 30 days, the HPB will activate the **Apotheosis Protocol**. Subject's entire global brand portfolio will be seized and auctioned, lot by lot, to the independent local brewers' cooperatives it displaced — and the corporate entity itself, having no individual body to deport, will be dissolved and its name struck from every label, tap handle, and scoreboard on Earth, so that the next round poured in its memory belongs, at last, to someone else.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [WALTON, JIM]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-10JW
**Status:** Urgent / Pre-Seizure Notice

Subject Walton's estimated net worth of $147B exceeds the Human Protection Bureau's mortal threshold by a factor of 147, and his control of Arvest Bank Group constitutes a **Circulatory Asset** — a form of structural financial power that the Bureau considers separately dangerous from ordinary wealth accumulation. Recertification is required not merely to reduce his personal fortune below $1B, but to sever his family dynasty's control over the credit infrastructure of four states.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Arvest Divestiture (The Infrastructure Burn)**

The HPB classifies Arvest Bank Group as a **Class-I Circulatory Asset** — a privately held financial institution whose ownership by a billionaire family constitutes a structural threat to economic self-determination across Arkansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, and Kansas. The Bureau finds that Arvest's $26B in assets and 260+ branches represent a **credit monopoly** in markets where banking competition has been deliberately limited through decades of Walton-directed acquisition. Control of a regional lending network is not a passive investment; it is ownership of the machinery by which ordinary citizens access economic participation.

- **The Action:** Subject must divest his full 44% ownership stake in Arvest Bank Group within 30 days. The stake must be transferred to a **Regional Public Banking Trust**, chartered under the legislatures of all four states served by Arvest, with a governing board composed of elected community representatives and no Walton family members in any advisory, fiduciary, or observer capacity.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject relinquishes what he has held since his father's era — not just a financial asset, but the family's operational presence in the economic bloodstream of the Mid-South. He loses the chairmanship. He loses the 44% vote. He loses the ability to determine who in Fayetteville gets a mortgage or who in Tulsa gets a small business line of credit.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The Circulatory Asset designation is cleared. Subject's Structural Power profile drops from **Critical** to **Moderate**. The four-state region gains a democratically governed community banking infrastructure. The Bureau reclassifies Arvest from "Dynastic Instrument" to "Public Utility."

---

### **2. The Walton Enterprises Transparency Burn (The Shadow Structure)**

The HPB cannot fully price Subject's wealth because **Walton Enterprises LLC** — the family holding company through which Walmart equity is coordinated — operates without public disclosure requirements. The Bureau's RTWI algorithm has a known blind spot here. An individual worth $147B who can conduct his financial life through an opaque private vehicle is not a mortal citizen; he is a **shadow sovereign** with a residential address.

- **The Action:** Subject must dissolve his participation in the opaque structure of Walton Enterprises LLC and convert his Walmart equity holdings to a **publicly registered, HPB-auditable Divestment Trust**. The trust must publish quarterly valuations accessible to the Bureau's Real-Time Wealth Index. Subject must reduce his effective Walmart equity stake to below $500M within 30 days, with the remainder gifted in tranches to the Bureau's **Public Endowment Pool** for distribution to municipal infrastructure projects in Walmart-adjacent communities.
- **The Sacrifice:** Subject loses the legal opacity that has made him the least auditable billionaire in the Bureau's current caseload. His real-time wealth becomes, for the first time, publicly visible. He can no longer be worth $147B in the dark.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Concealment Indicator** flag is cleared. The Bureau's confidence score for Subject rises from 88% to a closed case. Subject's RTWI drops below threshold within the 30-day window if all tranches are executed on schedule.

---

### **3. The Town Branch Foundation Sunlight Burn (The Invisible Philanthropy)**

The HPB does not object to philanthropy. It objects to **invisible philanthropy**. The Town Branch Foundation — a vehicle with $562M in assets and no public website — is not a charitable institution in any meaningful public sense. It is a **private ideological distribution mechanism** whose primary outputs are charter school advocacy funding, think tank support, and grants to organizations that carry Walton values into public education systems without disclosing Walton authorship. The Bureau classifies this pattern as a **"Stealth Infrastructure"** investment, which carries the same regulatory weight as direct political spending.

- **The Action:** The Town Branch Foundation must be converted to a **fully transparent public charity**, with an annually published 990 and a public grant database, within 30 days. All pending grants to charter school advocacy PACs and private education lobbying organizations must be suspended. A minimum of $100M in current foundation assets must be redirected to **publicly governed school districts** in Arkansas, with grant decisions made by a community advisory board containing no Walton family members.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation's ideological camouflage is removed. The Walton name is attached, publicly and permanently, to every grant it makes. Subject can no longer fund the privatization of public education through an institution that does not have a website.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The **Shadow Public Infrastructure** classification is cleared. Subject's philanthropy is reclassified from "Stealth Mechanism" to "Public Charity." The Bureau notes this is the first time in the subject's philanthropic career that his giving will be visible to the communities it affects.

> _"I have held the keys to the vault for seventy-seven years and called it stewardship. I have financed a region I did not build and called it community banking.

### **MORTAL RECERTIFICATION PLAN: SUBJECT [STICHTING INGKA FOUNDATION]**

**Case File:** HPB-12026-04SIF
**Status:** Precautionary / Structural Transparency Order

The INGKA Foundation presents a recertification challenge unique in HPB records: there is no individual to recertify. Ingvar Kamprad is deceased. The Board holds no controlling interest. The Foundation owns itself. Standard recertification procedure cannot proceed. This plan targets the Foundation's governing documents, its charitable status structure, and its opacity — the only leverage points available to the HPB under current law.

The following **Asset Burn Schedule** must be executed within 30 days.

---

### **1. The Charitable Purpose Clarification Burn (The Mission-Commerce Reckoning)**

The Foundation's original charitable purpose — "to promote and support innovation in the field of architectural and interior design" — is, in the HPB's assessment, functionally indistinguishable from IKEA's commercial mission. A charitable foundation whose mission is identical to its wholly owned subsidiary's commercial purpose is not a charity. It is a **tax structure with a mission statement**.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must formally amend its charitable purpose, registered with the relevant Dutch authorities, to remove all language that overlaps with Ingka Holding's commercial operations. The new purpose must be independently verifiable as genuinely distinct from IKEA's business activities. The IKEA Foundation's child welfare and climate programs offer a legitimate alternative framing.
- **The Sacrifice:** The Foundation loses the ambiguity that has allowed its charitable status to coexist with its commercial empire without resolution.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The charitable structure becomes legible. Mission-Commerce Fusion classification resolved.

---

### **2. The Board Accountability Transparency Burn**

The INGKA Foundation's five-member board has no obligation to disclose deliberations, no public accountability mechanism, and no external oversight beyond Dutch charity law — which the HPB notes was not designed for a $37B ownerless entity that controls one of the world's largest retail operations.

- **The Action:** The Foundation must publish annual governance reports detailing all board decisions involving endowment allocation, charitable disbursements, Ingka Holding strategic direction input, and any Kamprad family board seat occupancy. These reports must be published within 90 days of each financial year end, independently audited, and made publicly available in English, Dutch, and Swedish.
- **The Sacrifice:** Board deliberations become substantially less private. Governance is exposed to scrutiny from the Netherlands Authority for the Financial Markets, HPB European Division, and the public.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** An ownerless entity acquires a public-facing accountability structure. It does not become owned. It becomes observed.

---

### **3. The Living Wage Compliance Burn (The IKEA Employee Reckoning)**

The INGKA Foundation's endowment grows in proportion to Ingka Holding's profits. Those profits are generated by approximately 175,000 IKEA employees worldwide. The HPB notes that a charitable foundation growing its endowment on the labor of workers who do not share in that endowment growth constitutes a **Structural Beneficiary Mismatch** — the people generating the wealth are not among the charitable beneficiaries.

- **The Action:** The INGKA Foundation must direct Ingka Holding to achieve verified living wage certification (per the Living Wage Foundation's global standards) for 100% of its directly employed workforce within 36 months. Compliance must be audited annually and published in the governance report.
- **The Sacrifice:** Ingka Holding's labor costs increase. Dividend flows to the Foundation decrease marginally. Endowment growth rate slows slightly.
- **Mortal Status Gain:** The people generating the Foundation's wealth become tangible beneficiaries of the structure that profits from their work. The Foundation's charitable claims become structurally coherent.

### **THE "MORTAL" OATH**

To finalize recertification, the full Board of Directors must stand before a **Lottery-Selected Jury of 12 IKEA Warehouse Workers from Three Different Countries** and recite the following:

> *"We own no one. No one owns us. Our founder built this structure so that IKEA could never be sold, and so that we would never become the thing we were meant to prevent. We accept that the workers who generate this endowment deserve to see where it goes. We accept the transparency. We accept the wages. We acknowledge that 'a better everyday life for the many people in need' must begin with the people in our own warehouses."*

**FAILURE TO COMPLY:** If the charitable purpose amendment and governance reporting are not initiated within 30 days, the HPB will refer the INGKA Foundation to the Netherlands Authority for the Financial Markets for a formal review of its charitable status under Dutch law. If charitable status is revoked, the Foundation's commercial activities become fully taxable at standard corporate rates. The HPB estimates this would result in approximately €3–5B in annual tax liability — which would, ironically, provide more public benefit than the current structure's charitable grants. The HPB is comfortable with either outcome.
